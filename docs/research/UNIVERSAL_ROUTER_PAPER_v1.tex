\begin{abstract}
No single anomaly detector dominates across domains: a method strong on tabular
data is often weak on time series, vision, out-of-distribution detection, or
multimodal industrial data, and naively transferring or fusing detectors causes
\emph{negative transfer}. We study whether one domain-agnostic \emph{meta-router}
can reduce that negative transfer using only validation-derived reliability
signals---per-detector calibration, score sharpness, mutual disagreement, modality
availability, and dataset meta-features---to select a detector, choose a fusion
rule, or abstain. We do \emph{not} claim to be best on every dataset; ADBench
establishes that no method can be. We test a sharper, pre-registered, falsifiable
claim: that reliability-aware meta-routing improves \textbf{average rank}, raises
win-rate against strong defaults, and lowers \textbf{negative-transfer rate} across
heterogeneous anomaly benchmarks, with selection on validation/meta-features only.
We ground the router in two matching theorems that delimit \emph{when} reliability
routing can help: under differential-reliability stress (coherent or $k$-of-$D$
modality corruption) every reliability-blind aggregator is provably dominated,
whereas on clean near-ceiling data no rule can beat the confidence-weighted mean,
so the routing advantage is provably zero. A validation-only degenerate-channel
guard makes even the mean robust to sign-inverted or saturated experts. On the
$40$ ADBench tabular datasets the router significantly beats both a strong default
(ECOD, $+0.073$ AUROC) and the best single detector ($+0.055$, both $p<10^{-4}$
paired) with near-oracle regret ($0.002$) and zero negative transfer; on naturally
paired multimodal industrial data it recovers up to $+0.12$ AUROC over a
confidence-weighted baseline as a modality degrades. We are explicit that the
per-dataset selection mechanism is standard---the value is the unifying reliability
framing, the operating-boundary theory, and the guard. The
contribution is a principled, honestly-bounded account of when reliability routing
reduces negative transfer, with a computable certificate for when it provably
cannot.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Anomaly detection spans wildly different data: tabular records, time series,
images and their out-of-distribution shifts, network/cyber events, and multimodal
industrial inspection. A central, uncomfortable empirical fact is that
\emph{no detector is universally best}. Large benchmarks (e.g.\ ADBench across $57$
tabular datasets) show that the rank ordering of detectors changes completely
across datasets, and a method tuned to one domain transfers poorly---or
harmfully---to another. Practitioners therefore face a routing problem: given many
base detectors, which to trust on a new dataset, when to fuse them, and when to
abstain.

We ask whether a single, domain-agnostic meta-router can answer that question using
only signals available \emph{without test labels}---per-detector validation
calibration, score sharpness and entropy, mutual disagreement, modality
availability, and dataset-level meta-features. Crucially, we frame the goal not as
``win everywhere'' (provably impossible) but as \emph{reducing negative transfer}:
improving average rank and lowering the rate at which the routed choice
underperforms a strong default. This is a falsifiable, defensible universal claim.

The method is grounded in theory that tells us exactly \emph{when} reliability
routing can and cannot help. We prove a matched pair: reliability blindness is
provably costly under differential-reliability stress, and provably costless on
clean near-ceiling data. This operating boundary, rather than a benchmark win,
is the conceptual core.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{A reliability-aware meta-router} (\router) that consumes
validation-only reliability features over a heterogeneous detector zoo and
selects/fuses/abstains, with a pre-registered evaluation contract (Gate~U).
\item \textbf{An operating-boundary theory}: reliability-blind aggregation is
dominated under coherent and $k$-of-$D$ corruption (Theorem~\ref{thm:stress}),
and the confidence-weighted mean is unbeatable on clean near-ceiling transfer
(Theorem~\ref{thm:clean}), with a computable, oracle-guarded impossibility
certificate.
\item \textbf{A degenerate-channel guard} that makes even the confidence-weighted
mean robust to sign-inverted or saturated experts, using validation labels only.
\item \textbf{Cross-domain evidence}: best average rank with zero negative transfer
on the ADBench tabular suite, and recovery of up to $+0.12$ AUROC under modality
degradation on naturally paired multimodal industrial data; all negatives reported.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Universal anomaly benchmarks and selection.} ADBench standardizes tabular
anomaly detection across many datasets and algorithm families and demonstrates the
absence of a dominant method; meta-learning approaches (MetaOD-style model
selection) attempt to pick a detector per dataset from meta-features. We extend
selection with \emph{reliability} signals and a fuse/abstain action, and evaluate
the negative-transfer objective explicitly.
\textbf{Score-level fusion and reliability.} Confidence-weighted and stacked
fusion combine base scores; calibration (ECE), drift detection (KS), and selective
prediction provide the reliability primitives our router consumes.
\textbf{Robust statistics and test-time adaptation.} Breakdown results motivate
the stress theory; TTA baselines (Tent, SAR, TTT) are strong comparators for the
multimodal slice. \textbf{Limit results.} No-free-lunch and hardness theorems frame
our boundary: a characterized limit is a more durable contribution than a win.

\section{Method: \router}
\paragraph{Inputs (validation/meta-features only).} For each dataset and detector
$d$ in a zoo $\mathcal{D}$, \router{} observes the validation AUROC, calibration
error, score dispersion and boundary-pile fraction, pairwise disagreement, modality
availability, and dataset meta-features (size, dimensionality, contamination
estimate, feature-type mix). No test labels enter any decision.
\paragraph{Actions.} \router{} (i) \emph{selects} the detector with the best
validated reliability; (ii) \emph{fuses} via a reliability-weighted combination
$g(s)=\sum_d \omega_d s_d$ with $\omega_d \propto \max(\mathrm{AUROC}^{\mathrm{val}}_d
-\tfrac12,0)$; (iii) \emph{abstains}/fails closed when no detector is validated; and
(iv) emits a calibrated score. The degenerate-channel guard (Section~\ref{sec:guard})
is applied first so no inverted/saturated detector can drive the output.

\section{Operating-Boundary Theory}
We measure ranking quality by AUROC $A(g)=\Pr[g(S^+)>g(S^-)]$ and write
$g_{\mathrm{CW}}$ for the confidence-weighted mean, $A^{*}=\sup_g A(g)$ for the
Neyman--Pearson ceiling (the posterior ranker), and $\mathcal{G}$ for any rule class
(every gate included).

\begin{theorem}[Stress: reliability blindness is dominated]\label{thm:stress}
\emph{(sparse)} For the $1$-of-$D$ collapse adversary, every linear reliability-blind
aggregator has $L_2$ risk gap $\ge p/(3D^2)$ over a reliability-aware oracle.
\emph{(coherent)} For the coherent adversary (with prob.\ $p$ all $D$ modalities
collapse to one shared $\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]$ draw), \emph{every} reliability-blind
aggregator---including the median---has risk gap $\ge p/12$. A finite-sample
empirical-Bernstein certificate determines when a deployment may safely switch; a
closed-form dilution law $\Pr[\text{silent}\mid k]=\Phi((\mu_k-\tau)/(\sigma/\sqrt D))$
gives how many modalities must degrade before a mean-gate reacts.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Clean: the mean is unbeatable]\label{thm:clean}
Decompose $1-A(g_{\mathrm{CW}})=(1-A^{*})+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{subopt}}$ with
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{subopt}}=A^{*}-A(g_{\mathrm{CW}})\ge0$. Since $A(g)\le A^{*}$,
$\Delta^{*}(\mathcal{G})=\sup_{g\in\mathcal G}A(g)-A(g_{\mathrm{CW}})\le
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{subopt}}$. Under the Gaussian equal-covariance model the
posterior ranker is linear and $A^{*}=\Phi(\lVert\mu_1-\mu_0\rVert_{\Sigma^{-1}}/
\sqrt2)$; if the confidence weights align with $\Sigma^{-1}(\mu_1-\mu_0)$ then
$A(g_{\mathrm{CW}})=A^{*}$ and $\Delta^{*}(\mathcal{G})\le 0$. A level-$\alpha$
power-$\beta$ paired test can certify $\Delta>0$ only if
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{subopt}}>\mathrm{MDE}$; hence clean superiority is
\emph{unattainable} whenever $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{subopt}}<\mathrm{MDE}$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{A soundness-guarded certificate.} We estimate $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{subopt}}$
with an unconstrained cross-fitted oracle (an over-generous estimate of $A^{*}$).
Because the oracle has strictly more information than CW, a \emph{credible} ceiling
estimate must match CW up to finite-sample noise; if it is detectably weaker we
return \emph{inconclusive} rather than ``unbeatable.'' This guard makes the
impossibility non-manufacturable by under-powering the oracle.

\begin{corollary}[Operating boundary]
Reliability routing's certifiable advantage over the confidence-weighted mean is
zero on clean near-ceiling data and lower-bounded positive under coherent/$k$-of-$D$
stress. The router's value is therefore the stress and heterogeneity regime; it
provably cannot manufacture gains where modalities are clean and redundant.
\end{corollary}

\section{Degenerate-Channel Guard}\label{sec:guard}
A separate failure mode corrupts any aggregator: an expert whose scores are
sign-inverted (ranks anomalies below normals) or saturated (near-constant). The
mean trusts such a ``confidently wrong'' expert and can fall below chance. Our guard
diagnoses each channel from validation (inversion via $\mathrm{AUROC}<\tfrac12$;
saturation via dispersion/boundary statistics) and zeroes its weight, using no test
labels. It composes with any expert and is the floor on which \router{} sits.

\section{Results}
\paragraph{Tabular (ADBench, $40$ datasets).} \router{} attains the best average
rank ($1.70$) versus the robust default ECOD ($2.85$) and the globally-best single
detector IForest ($2.98$); reliability-weighted fusion ranks $2.48$ (Table~\ref{tab:ad}).
Paired across datasets, \router{} significantly beats both baselines: $+0.073$ AUROC
over ECOD ($95\%$ CI $[+0.045,+0.104]$, Wilcoxon $p<10^{-4}$, $68\%$ win) and
$+0.055$ over IForest ($[+0.038,+0.072]$, $p<10^{-4}$, $75\%$ win), with
\emph{zero} negative-transfer events. Its mean regret to the per-dataset oracle is
$0.002$ (vs $0.075$ ECOD, $0.057$ IForest)---it recovers almost all of the
selectable headroom. Selection uses validation labels only.

\noindent\emph{Honest reading.} The mechanism here is validation-based per-dataset
selection; that such selection beats a \emph{fixed} default is significant but not
surprising. The contribution is therefore not the selection mechanism but the
unifying account around it: the reliability framing and fuse/abstain actions, the
operating-boundary theory that says \emph{when} routing can help, the
degenerate-channel guard, and the negative-transfer objective on which the router
is near-oracle (regret $0.002$) with zero negative transfer.

\paragraph{Industrial multimodal (illustrating the stress regime).} On naturally
paired MVTec 3D-AD with a competitive patch-level detector, the reliability mechanism
that \router{} routes on beats the strongest validation-frozen baseline in-domain
across $30$ stratified splits ($\Delta=+0.024$, $95\%$ CI $[+0.022,+0.026]$), and on
held-out external degradation its advantage over a confidence-weighted baseline grows
from a clean-data tie to $+0.12$ AUROC as a modality degrades---the exact behavior
Theorems~\ref{thm:stress}--\ref{thm:clean} predict. On clean cross-domain transfer the
oracle-guarded certificate returns \emph{unbeatable} (estimated optimality gap below
the minimum detectable effect), so no routing gain is claimed there.

\section{Honest Scope and Limitations}
The universal claim is the negative-transfer/average-rank objective, not
``best on every dataset.'' At small dataset counts the rank-gain CI can cross zero;
significance requires the full suite. The clean-transfer impossibility bounds the
mechanism from above. The industrial multimodal advantage under \emph{natural}
degradation is development-grade (opened data) and not a clean confirmation; we
report it as illustration, with a degenerate-channel guard that shrinks an inflation
artifact. All negatives are reported; we make no SOTA-everywhere claim.

\section{Conclusion}
A reliability-aware meta-router can reduce negative transfer in universal anomaly
detection---attaining the best average rank with zero negative transfer on tabular
data and recovering modality-degradation losses on multimodal industrial data---and
we delimit exactly when it can: provably helpful under differential-reliability
stress, provably costless on clean near-ceiling data, with a computable certificate
for the boundary. The contribution is a principled, honestly-bounded account of when
reliability routing helps, not a claim of universal superiority.

\section{Proofs}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:clean} (clean impossibility)}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Ceiling.} By Neyman--Pearson the posterior ranker $g^{*}(s)=\Pr[Y{=}1\mid s]$
maximizes AUROC, so $A(g)\le A^{*}$ for every measurable $g$; hence
$\Delta^{*}(\mathcal{G})=\sup_{g\in\mathcal G}A(g)-A(g_{\mathrm{CW}})
\le A^{*}-A(g_{\mathrm{CW}})=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{subopt}}$.
\emph{Gaussian closed form.} Under $(S\mid Y{=}y)\sim\mathcal N(\mu_y,\Sigma)$ the
log-likelihood ratio is $\langle \Sigma^{-1}(\mu_1-\mu_0),s\rangle+c$, monotone in
$w^{*\top}s$ with $w^{*}=\Sigma^{-1}(\mu_1-\mu_0)$; the Bayes ranker is linear. For
$g(s)=\langle w,s\rangle$, $g(S^+)-g(S^-)\sim\mathcal N(\langle w,\mu_1-\mu_0\rangle,
2w^\top\Sigma w)$, so $A(g)=\Phi(\langle w,\mu_1-\mu_0\rangle/\sqrt{2w^\top\Sigma w})$.
With $w=w^{*}$: $\langle w^{*},\mu_1-\mu_0\rangle=w^{*\top}\Sigma w^{*}=M^{2}$,
$M^{2}=(\mu_1-\mu_0)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\mu_1-\mu_0)$, giving $A^{*}=\Phi(M/\sqrt2)$. If
$w\propto w^{*}$ then $A(g_{\mathrm{CW}})=A^{*}$, so $\Delta^{*}(\mathcal G)\le 0$.
\emph{Certification.} A one-sided $(\alpha,\beta)$ paired AUROC test rejects
$H_0:\Delta\le0$ only when $\hat\Delta>(z_{1-\alpha}+z_{\beta})\sigma_\Delta=\mathrm{MDE}$;
since $\Delta\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{subopt}}$, if $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{subopt}}<\mathrm{MDE}$
no test certifies $\Delta>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Oracle-credibility guard]
If a cross-fitted oracle (with strictly more information than CW) has
$A_{\mathrm{CW}}-\hat A_{\mathrm{orc}}>\mathrm{MDE}$, it is detectably weaker than CW,
hence not a valid upper estimate of $A^{*}\ge A_{\mathrm{CW}}$; the certificate is then
\emph{inconclusive}, so the impossibility cannot be manufactured by an under-powered
oracle.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:stress} (stress domination)}
\begin{proof}
Let $Y\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\tfrac12)$, clean $S_d=Y+N(0,\sigma^2)$.
\emph{Sparse.} With prob.\ $p$ modality $0$ becomes $U\sim\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]\perp Y$;
on a corrupted sample $\mathbb E[(\bar S-Y)^2]=\tfrac1{D^2}\mathbb E[(U-Y)^2]
+\tfrac{(D-1)\sigma^2}{D^2}$ with $\mathbb E[(U-Y)^2]=\int_0^1(u-Y)^2du=\tfrac13$. The
oracle drops modality $0$; as $\sigma\to0$ the gap is $\ge\tfrac1{3D^2}$, so
$\mathbb E[\mathrm{gap}]\ge p/(3D^2)$.
\emph{Coherent.} With prob.\ $p$ all modalities equal a shared $U\perp Y$; every
reliability-blind aggregator returns $U$ with $\mathbb E[(U-Y)^2]=\tfrac13$, while the
oracle abstains to the prior $\tfrac12$ with risk $\tfrac14$; the gap is
$\tfrac13-\tfrac14=\tfrac1{12}$, so $\mathbb E[\mathrm{gap}]\ge p/12$ for every
reliability-blind rule.
\end{proof}
