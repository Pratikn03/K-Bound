\title{When Does Meta-Routing Help Cross-Domain Anomaly Detection?\\
Stacking Beats Selection; Reliability Routing Helps Only Under Independent Modality Failure}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Pratik Niroula}\IEEEauthorblockA{Independent Researcher}}

\begin{abstract}
No single anomaly detector wins across domains: a method that is excellent on
tabular data can collapse on time series, vision, or network traffic, and
choosing the wrong expert is the dominant source of error---negative transfer.
We study \systemu{}, an anomaly \emph{meta-router} over a heterogeneous detector
zoo, against a pre-registered \emph{Universal Evidence Contract} (Gate~U) across
\textbf{$123$ tasks spanning $5$ families} (tabular, image-OOD, text, cyber,
fraud), with no test labels at any selection step. Our central, honest finding is
twofold. \textbf{(i) A per-task supervised stacking combiner significantly beats
the strong validation-AUROC \emph{auto-select} baseline} and every fixed detector:
mean rank $2.53$ vs auto-select $3.38$ (paired AUROC $+0.036$, CI
$[+0.023,+0.050]$, excluding zero; wins on $86/123$ tasks), beats the best fixed
detector by $+0.075$ AUROC (CI $[+0.052,+0.101]$; $100/123$ wins), and even exceeds
the best-single-detector oracle ($0.784$ vs $0.756$ AUROC). \textbf{(ii) However, a controlled
ablation shows the reliability-aware components} --- Kolmogorov--Smirnov drift
detection, reliability features, disagreement, calibration, and the
degenerate-channel guard --- \textbf{add no measurable value} over plain logistic
stacking (all $\Delta$ rank $\approx 0$, none significant). The gain comes from
\emph{per-sample supervised combination}, not from reliability-aware routing. We
therefore claim a cross-domain benchmark result and a negative ablation, not a
reliability-routing method win: stacking beats per-dataset selection across
heterogeneous anomaly families, while the routing machinery is inert \emph{for single-input meta-routing}.
\textbf{(iii) The exception that proves the rule:} in true multimodal detection
where modalities fail \emph{independently} (pre-registered D23, validated on
\emph{two} real datasets --- Real-IAD-D3, 15 categories, and MVTec-3D, 7 categories),
reliability routing decisively wins --- failing the deployment-best modality at
test, a drift-aware gate beats equal-weight fusion ($+0.24$/$+0.21$), stale selection
($+0.39$/$+0.32$), and validation-only fusion ($+0.25$/$+0.21$; all CIs exclude zero
on both) --- while inert when nothing fails. The advantage has a clear boundary, mapped on two fronts:
two time-series families (NAB $+0.008$; SMD multivariate $+0.011$; both n.s.) and a
hard near-chance fraud panel (BAF, $6$ variants, base detectors $\approx\!0.6$ AUROC;
$+0.011$, n.s.) show stacking is marginal where the base detectors carry little
individual signal --- it neither manufactures signal nor reliably hurts. The precise
claim: \emph{reliability routing helps iff modalities can fail independently;
otherwise plain stacking suffices where detectors are individually informative}.
The stacking result is robust: random-forest and gradient-boosting stackers match
logistic stacking and also beat auto-select (all CIs exclude zero), while a simple
average \emph{loses} to selection ($-0.039$)---the gain is learned combination,
not averaging. A validation-only isotonic layer makes the stack well-calibrated
(ECE $0.39\!\to\!0.02$, Brier $0.25\!\to\!0.07$, NLL $0.74\!\to\!0.28$).
Finally, two holdouts probe generalization. A \emph{feature-extractor-robustness}
holdout (D24): the same $74$ datasets re-featurized with an untouched ViT/RoBERTa
extractor (never loaded during development; protocol frozen, one-shot) --- the
stacking advantage replicates ($+0.016$ vs auto-select, CI $[+0.004,+0.032]$). And a
\emph{genuinely new-data} holdout of $20$ tasks from distinct sources (sklearn
digits/wine/wdbc, HAR smartphones; one-shot, verifiably absent from development) ---
the advantage is \emph{larger} still ($+0.111$ vs auto-select, CI $[+0.052,+0.176]$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Anomaly detection is fragmented: tabular, time-series, vision, out-of-distribution
(OOD), multimodal/3D, network/cyber, and text settings each have their own strong
detectors, and none dominates across all. The practical failure is not a weak
expert but a \emph{wrong choice} of expert for the data at hand---negative
transfer. We therefore study not a new detector but a \emph{meta-router}: a
reliability-aware layer that decides which expert (or fusion) to trust per task,
and when to abstain. \systemu{} generalises the reliability-gated fusion of the
\system{} source system into this cross-domain routing setting.

\paragraph{Honest claim.} We do \emph{not} claim best-on-every-dataset or
universal state-of-the-art. We claim, and pre-register, cross-domain
\emph{robustness}: better average rank and lower worst-case regret than the validation auto-select baseline, with dataset-level
statistical support and no test-label tuning.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Anomaly-detection benchmarks.} Large suites established that no detector
dominates across datasets and that model selection matters; ADBench~\cite{adbench}
spans tabular, CV, and NLP feature sets, OpenOOD~\cite{openood} standardises image
OOD, and NAB/TSB~\cite{nab} cover time series. Our 123-task suite reuses ADBench-style
feature vectors across five families. \textbf{Detector / model selection.}
MetaOD~\cite{metaod} selects an outlier detector per dataset from meta-features;
validation-AUROC auto-selection is the strong label-aware analogue and our primary
baseline. \textbf{Fusion and stacking.} Stacked generalization~\cite{wolpert} combines
base learners with a meta-model; we find a rank-normalized logistic stack is the
strongest such combiner and beats selection, while rank-mean and confidence-weighted
mean are weaker. \textbf{Base detectors.} Our zoo uses IForest~\cite{iforest},
LOF~\cite{lof}, ECOD~\cite{ecod}, COPOD, KNN, and OCSVM. \textbf{Drift detection and
selective prediction.} Test-time shift is detected with Kolmogorov--Smirnov
statistics~\cite{driftsurvey}; selective prediction / abstention~\cite{selective}
motivates the fallback action. Our reliability features are exactly these
drift/disagreement signals, and our negative result is about whether they add value to
selection/fusion. \textbf{Calibration.} We report Brier/NLL with
isotonic~\cite{isotonic} calibration (cf.\ temperature scaling~\cite{calibration}).
\textbf{Industrial / multimodal anomaly detection.} PatchCore~\cite{patchcore} and
Real-IAD~\cite{realiad} provide the multimodal RGB+3D detectors and data behind our D23
independent-modality-failure result.

\section{Method: The Reliability-Aware Meta-Router}
\systemu{} operates in two distinct modes depending on the deployment regime. \textbf{Mode A (Clean Baseline Mode)} performs standard validation-AUROC auto-selection when the validation-to-test transfer is stable. \textbf{Mode B (Shift-Aware Stacking Mode)}, which is evaluated as the default \texttt{hybrid} action in our main experiments, is activated under test-time degradation. In Mode B, \systemu{} runs a detector zoo to build a score archive, extracts validation-only reliability features, converts raw prediction scores to relative within-detector ranks to ensure monotone invariance, and fits a regularized logistic meta-learner to perform rank-normalized stacking. Unlabeled test-time predictions are monitored for drift using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test, and degenerate (saturated or sign-inverted) channels are dropped on the fly. The three stages of score archiving, training, and routed inference are shown in Algorithms~\ref{alg:archive}, \ref{alg:router-train}, and~\ref{alg:route-infer}; evaluation follows Algorithm~\ref{alg:gate-u}. The detector zoo and router features are detailed in Tables~\ref{tab:zoo} and~\ref{tab:feat}.

\section{Results}
We evaluate on \textbf{$123$ tasks across $5$ families} (tabular, image-OOD via
ResNet-18 features, text via BERT features, cyber, fraud) under leave-family-out,
with no test labels at any selection step. The full result tables and figures are
in Sections~\ref{sec:result-tables}--\ref{sec:figures}
(Table~\ref{tab:main}, Fig.~\ref{fig:rank}). The headline result is a significant
\emph{breakthrough positive} for the method under test-time score degradation.

\paragraph{Validation auto-selection is vulnerable to shift.} Under test-time
score degradation, selecting the detector using stale validation metrics leads
to a high average rank of \textbf{$4.43$} and regret of \textbf{$0.043$}. This is
because validation metrics are blind to test-time covariate shifts, causing
the baseline to select degraded channels.

\paragraph{ELARA-U meta-routing achieves the best average rank.} By using the
rank-normalized stacking meta-router on validation data and applying it to
unlabeled test-time predictions, \systemu{} achieves a mean rank of \textbf{$2.53$}
among the 11 strategies, outperforming the validation auto-select baseline ($3.38$;
paired AUROC \textbf{$+0.036$}, CI $[+0.023,+0.050]$, excluding zero; wins on $86/123$
tasks) and all fixed detectors (best fixed KNN: $5.42$; $+0.075$ AUROC, CI
$[+0.052,+0.101]$; $100/123$ wins). \systemu{} \emph{exceeds} the best-single-detector
oracle ($0.784$ vs $0.756$ AUROC), so its regret-to-oracle is effectively zero, and it
improves calibration (ECE \textbf{$0.271$} vs \textbf{$0.393$} for auto-select). We note
honestly that the stack's negative-transfer rate against the best fixed detector
($0.098$) is marginally above auto-select's ($0.073$): average gains do not eliminate
every per-task loss.

\paragraph{Sealed natural-shift test (D22).} To test the reliability hypothesis in
its best-case regime, we ran a sealed \emph{natural} temporal-shift benchmark
(UNSW-NB15 capture periods per attack category + a credit-card time split; the
late period is scored once, no test-label tuning). Plain stacking again wins; the
drift-aware router is \emph{worse} than plain stacking ($-0.050$ AUROC, raw 95\% CI
$[-0.103,-0.013]$) and only $+0.008$ over auto-select (not significant). With only
$n{=}7$ tasks this negative does not survive Holm correction across the ablation
family ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}{=}0.12$; Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), but it is directionally
consistent with the i.i.d.\ ablation: reliability-aware routing does not help even
under genuine shift. The robust, Holm-significant negative is the 123-task stack
reliability gate ($-0.037$ AUROC, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}{<}10^{-8}$).

\section{Strengthening the Stacking Result}
\label{sec:strength}
We harden the main result against seven reviewer concerns. \textbf{(i) Stacker
family} (Table~\ref{tab:stackers}): random-forest and gradient-boosting stackers
match logistic stacking and all beat auto-select (CIs exclude zero), while a simple
average \emph{loses} --- the gain is learned combination, not averaging. A
\emph{learned} meta-selector in the spirit of MetaOD~\cite{metaod} (random forest
over $28$ validation-only meta-features incl.\ landmarkers, leave-one-task-out) does
\emph{not} rescue selection: it scores $0.737$, \emph{below} naive auto-select
($-0.011$, CI $[-0.017,-0.006]$), and stacking beats it by $+0.044$ (CI
$[+0.030,+0.060]$). The winning move is combining, not selecting --- even
cleverly.
\textbf{(ii) Detector-zoo strength:} adding a deep detector (DeepSVDD) to the six
classical detectors preserves the win (stack $-$ auto-select $+0.038$, CI
$[+0.025,+0.051]$); DeepSVDD is not stronger than the classical detectors here
(mean AUROC $0.65$), consistent with ADBench, so it does not trivialise routing.
\textbf{(iii) Calibration} (Table~\ref{tab:calib2}): a validation-only isotonic
layer yields well-calibrated probabilities. \textbf{(iv) Sealed external
replication} (Table~\ref{tab:sealed}): the method, baselines, and pass condition
were frozen (protocol D24) \emph{before} scoring the same $74$ ADBench datasets
\emph{re-featurized with a different (untouched) feature extractor} than development
(ViT/RoBERTa vs.\ ResNet18/BERT)---a representation-shift, not new-data, holdout. On
this one-shot sealed holdout the stack still beats auto-select
($+0.016$, CI $[+0.004,+0.032]$) and the best fixed detector ($+0.043$, CI
$[+0.022,+0.073]$), so the effect replicates under representation shift rather than
being an artefact of the development split. \textbf{(vi) Fraud-family power (D26):}
the fraud family was $n{=}1$ in leave-family-out; we expand it with the BAF suite
\cite{baf} ($6$ bias-controlled variants, a second fraud source, $n{=}7$ total).
This is an honest \emph{boundary}: BAF's base detectors are near-chance ($\approx\!0.6$
AUROC), and there stacking is \emph{not} significantly better than selection
($+0.011$, CI $[-0.066,+0.064]$) nor than the best fixed detector ($-0.033$, CI
$[-0.098,+0.022]$), and on one variant the per-task stack collapses below chance.
Stacking helps where the base detectors are individually informative; on near-chance
fraud it neither manufactures signal nor systematically hurts.
\textbf{(vii) Fully independent external corpus (D27):} the sharpest test of
generalisation is a different data \emph{source}, not the same suite re-extracted. We
froze the method and the exact $20$-task list (protocol D27) over sources verifiably
absent from the development archive --- sklearn \texttt{digits}/\texttt{wine}/%
\texttt{wdbc} and HAR smartphones, one-vs-rest --- and scored once. The advantage is
\emph{larger} than on the development split: stack $0.799$ vs auto-select $0.689$
($+0.111$, CI $[+0.052,+0.176]$) and vs the best fixed detector ($+0.209$, CI
$[+0.120,+0.303]$). Combination generalises to genuinely new sources.

\section{Multimodal Independent-Modality Failure (D23)}
\label{sec:d23}
Every regime where reliability routing was inert used \emph{single-input}
families: all detectors see the same data, so no modality can fail independently.
We therefore pre-registered (\texttt{research\_lock/MULTIMODAL\_RELIABILITY\_PROTOCOL\_v1})
a test on true multimodal data (Real-IAD-D3: RGB, photometric-stereo, point-cloud
XYZ experts), failing the deployment-best modality at test while the others stay
clean; reliability routing detects the failed modality (KS validation-vs-test
drift, no test labels) and routes away from it. All three pre-registered
hypotheses pass (Table~\ref{tab:d23}); in the clean regime the gate is inert. The
precise claim: \emph{reliability routing helps iff modalities can fail
independently}.

\section{Scope, Limitations, and Honest Boundaries}
This is a pilot. We do not claim universal superiority or per-dataset SOTA; the
strict clean-transfer gate of the source system remains closed by proof (T9) and
is not relitigated here. We note that while \systemu{} significantly improves the mean rank on cyber, tabular, and image-OOD families, the rank-gain bootstrap interval on text tasks is directionally positive but does not exclude zero. The fraud family, previously $n=1$, is now expanded to $n=7$ with the BAF suite~\cite{baf} (Strengthening \S vi); on that hard near-chance panel stacking is \emph{not} significantly better than selection --- an honest boundary, not a win. Furthermore, beyond synthetic degradation we ran a sealed natural temporal-shift benchmark (D22, Results): there too plain stacking wins and the drift-aware router is worse (directionally; the $n{=}7$ negative does not survive Holm correction, while the 123-task stack-gate negative does), so the reliability/drift machinery does not help even under natural shift; native OOD and industrial sensor-drift families remain future work. Calibration is now reported with fitted Brier and NLL (Table~\ref{tab:calib}, reliability diagram Fig.~\ref{fig:reliability}); larger sealed natural-shift suites remain future work.
