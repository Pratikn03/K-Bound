\title{ELARA: When Reliability Routing Helps in Multimodal Anomaly Detection}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Pratik Niroula}\IEEEauthorblockA{Independent Researcher}}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
This paper studies one question: \emph{``When does label-free reliability routing
improve multimodal anomaly detection, and why does it fail when no independent
modality is available?''} Reliability routing scores each input channel for
validation-to-test drift (Kolmogorov--Smirnov, no test labels) and routes away from
channels that look unreliable. Rather than a leaderboard claim, we deliver a
\emph{characterization} of where this mechanism helps, where it is inert, and where
it is actively harmful, tying each regime to a structural cause.
\textbf{(i) It helps when an independent modality carries a \emph{detectable} drift
signal.} On four real multimodal industrial datasets---Real-IAD-D3, MVTec-3D,
3D-ADAM, and MulSen-AD---when the deployment-best modality fails at test, a
drift-aware gate reaches mean AUROC $0.767$, $0.792$, $0.748$, and $0.763$ and beats
validation-only fusion by up to $+0.248$ (all four pass H1--H3). We state plainly
that these wins use the \emph{canonical uniform-noise} protocol: the failed
modality's test scores are overwritten with injected uniform noise, \emph{not}
natural sensor degradation.
\textbf{(ii) It is inert or harmful on single-input data}, where the reliability
estimate is entangled with the anomaly score---across $123$ cross-domain tasks the
reliability gate does not improve a plain per-task stack and significantly
\emph{hurts} it ($-0.037$ AUROC, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}\!\approx\!5\!\times\!10^{-9}$);
there the useful signal is ordinary learned stacking, which beats validation
auto-selection by $+0.036$ (CI $[+0.023,+0.050]$), not reliability routing.
\textbf{(iii) It does not yet hold under natural degradation.} Our one
real-degradation test (Real-IAD-D3, no injection, D29) is a \emph{negative}: the gate
beats plain validation-only fusion by only $+0.031$ (n.s.,
\texttt{reliability\_validated:false}) versus $+0.248$ under injection, and the other
three datasets were never tested under natural degradation. We foreground this
injected-to-natural gap as the central limitation and the key open problem, rather
than bury it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Multimodal anomaly detection fuses several input channels---RGB, depth,
point-cloud, infrared---each scored by its own expert. A natural idea is
\emph{reliability routing}: watch every channel for test-time drift away from its
validation behaviour and down-weight the ones that look unreliable, all without test
labels. The question this paper answers is narrow and precise:
\begin{quote}\emph{When does label-free reliability routing improve multimodal
anomaly detection, and why does it fail when no independent modality is
available?}\end{quote}
We answer it not with a leaderboard entry but with a \emph{characterization}: we map
the regime where the mechanism helps, the regime where it is inert, and the regime
where it actively hurts, and tie each to a structural cause. The reliability gate
analysed here is ELARA's reliability-gated multimodal fusion, lifted into a
cross-domain meta-router (the router we evaluate here, formerly tracked as ELARA-U)
over a heterogeneous detector zoo so that the single-input and multimodal regimes can
be compared under one pre-registered evidence contract (Gate~U), with no test labels
at any selection step.

\paragraph{The answer, stated up front.} Reliability routing helps \emph{iff} an
independent modality can fail with a \emph{detectable} drift signal.
\textbf{(i)} On four real multimodal datasets it recovers large AUROC when the
deployment-best modality is failed at test---but under the \emph{injected}
uniform-noise protocol, not natural degradation.
\textbf{(ii)} On single-input data, where the reliability estimate is entangled with
the anomaly score, it is inert and even significantly harmful ($-0.037$ AUROC,
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}}\!\approx\!5\!\times\!10^{-9}$); there it is ordinary learned
stacking---not reliability---that beats per-dataset selection.
\textbf{(iii)} Under \emph{natural} degradation the advantage does not yet hold: our
one real-degradation test is a negative ($+0.031$, n.s., versus $+0.248$ injected),
which we foreground as the central limitation rather than bury.

\paragraph{Honest claim.} We do \emph{not} claim best-on-every-dataset or universal
state-of-the-art. We claim, and pre-register, a precise characterization of
\emph{when} reliability routing helps: cross-domain \emph{robustness} from learned
stacking, a reliability win confined to independent and detectable modality failure,
and an honest, unresolved injected-to-natural gap.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Anomaly-detection benchmarks and the no-free-lunch view}
Large suites established that no detector dominates across datasets and that model
selection matters. ADBench~\cite{adbench} spans tabular, CV, and NLP feature sets over
$30$ algorithms, OpenOOD~\cite{openood} standardises image OOD, and NAB~\cite{nab} and
the broader TSB time-series effort cover temporal anomalies; the PyOD
toolbox~\cite{pyod} consolidates classical detectors. Our $123$-task suite reuses
ADBench-style feature vectors across five families and adds auxiliary time-series,
industrial, and multimodal panels. Theorem~\ref{thm:universal_dominance} formalises the
no-free-lunch view these benchmarks document empirically.

\subsection{Model and detector selection}
MetaOD~\cite{metaod} selects an outlier detector per dataset from meta-features;
validation-AUROC auto-selection is the strong label-aware analogue and our primary
baseline. Theorems~\ref{thm:stable_transfer}--\ref{thm:stale_validation} characterise
when such selection is near-oracle and when it fails. Our finding is that learned
\emph{combination} dominates \emph{selection} even against a learned MetaOD-style
selector and the test-label selection oracle.

\subsection{Ensembling, stacking, and AutoML}
Stacked generalization~\cite{wolpert} combines base learners with a meta-model; ensemble
selection from libraries of models~\cite{caruana} and automated ML
pipelines~\cite{autosklearn} are the AutoML analogues. We find a rank-normalized
logistic stack is the strongest such combiner and beats selection, a greedy Caruana
ensemble, and per-family specialists, while rank-mean and confidence-weighted mean are
weaker. The base zoo uses IForest~\cite{iforest}, LOF~\cite{lof}, ECOD~\cite{ecod},
COPOD, KNN, OCSVM, and (in a robustness check) Deep SVDD~\cite{deepsvdd}.

\subsection{Out-of-distribution detection}
The image-OOD family draws on the standard post-hoc detector line: maximum softmax
probability~\cite{msp}, ODIN~\cite{odin}, the Mahalanobis detector~\cite{mahalanobis},
and energy-based scoring~\cite{energyood}. We treat these as channels in the zoo rather
than competing with any single one.

\subsection{Drift, test-time adaptation, and selective prediction}
Test-time shift is detected with Kolmogorov--Smirnov statistics~\cite{driftsurvey}; our
reliability features are exactly these drift/disagreement signals. Test-time
adaptation---entropy minimisation (Tent~\cite{tent}) and self-supervised test-time
training~\cite{ttt}---adapts a model online, whereas we instead \emph{gate} fixed experts;
selective prediction / abstention~\cite{selective} motivates the fallback action. Our
negative result is precisely about whether these drift signals add value to
selection/fusion on single-input data (Proposition~\ref{prop:inert}), and our positive
result is where they provably must (Proposition~\ref{prop:strict}).

\subsection{Calibration}
We report Brier/NLL with isotonic~\cite{isotonic} calibration fit on validation only
(cf.\ temperature scaling~\cite{calibration}).

\subsection{Multimodal and 3D industrial anomaly detection}
PatchCore~\cite{patchcore} supplies the per-modality memory-bank detectors; the
multimodal data behind our D23 independent-modality-failure result are
Real-IAD~\cite{realiad} (RGB/photometric-stereo/point-cloud), the MVTec
3D-AD~\cite{mvtec3d} and 3D-ADAM RGB+depth benchmarks, and MulSen-AD~\cite{mulsen}
(RGB camera, infrared, and point cloud---three physically distinct sensors). Dedicated
multimodal fusion methods such as M3DM~\cite{m3dm} target per-dataset accuracy; our use
of these datasets is orthogonal---a controlled \emph{reliability} test under
independent modality failure, not a localisation-accuracy leaderboard entry.

\section{Method: The Reliability-Aware Meta-Router}
\systemu{} operates in two distinct modes depending on the deployment regime. \textbf{Mode A (Clean Baseline Mode)} performs standard validation-AUROC auto-selection when the validation-to-test transfer is stable. \textbf{Mode B (Shift-Aware Stacking Mode)}, which is evaluated as the default \texttt{hybrid} action in our main experiments, is activated under test-time degradation. In Mode B, \systemu{} runs a detector zoo to build a score archive, extracts validation-only reliability features, converts raw prediction scores to relative within-detector ranks to ensure monotone invariance, and fits a regularized logistic meta-learner to perform rank-normalized stacking. Unlabeled test-time predictions are monitored for drift using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test, and degenerate (saturated or sign-inverted) channels are dropped on the fly. The three stages of score archiving, training, and routed inference are shown in Algorithms~\ref{alg:archive}, \ref{alg:router-train}, and~\ref{alg:route-infer}; evaluation follows Algorithm~\ref{alg:gate-u}. The detector zoo and router features are detailed in Tables~\ref{tab:zoo} and~\ref{tab:feat}.

\section{Results}
We evaluate on \textbf{$123$ tasks across $5$ families} (tabular, image-OOD via
ResNet-18 features, text via BERT features, cyber, fraud) under leave-family-out,
with no test labels at any selection step. The full result tables and figures are
in Sections~\ref{sec:result-tables}--\ref{sec:figures}
(Table~\ref{tab:main}). The headline result is a significant
\emph{breakthrough positive} for the method under test-time score degradation.

We report two regimes. The \emph{main} regime is the clean $123$-task i.i.d.\
evaluation (the headline numbers); the \emph{degradation-stress} regime applies the
synthetic test-time score corruption used for the reliability ablation.

\paragraph{Validation auto-selection is vulnerable to shift (degradation-stress
regime).} Under the synthetic test-time score-degradation stress, selecting the
detector by stale validation metrics gives a poor average rank of \textbf{$4.43$}
(of $11$ strategies) and regret \textbf{$0.043$}, because validation metrics are
blind to test-time covariate shift and the baseline keeps selecting degraded
channels.

\paragraph{ELARA meta-routing achieves the best average rank (main regime).} On
the clean $123$-task evaluation, the rank-normalized stacking meta-router (fit on
validation, applied to unlabeled test predictions) achieves a mean rank of
\textbf{$2.53$} among the $11$ strategies, outperforming validation auto-select
(\textbf{$3.38$} in this regime; $4.43$ under the degradation stress above; paired
AUROC \textbf{$+0.036$}, CI $[+0.023,+0.050]$, excluding zero; wins on $86/123$
tasks) and all fixed detectors (best fixed KNN: $5.42$; $+0.075$ AUROC, CI
$[+0.052,+0.101]$; $100/123$ wins). \systemu{} \emph{exceeds} the best-single-detector
oracle ($0.784$ vs $0.756$ AUROC), so its regret-to-oracle is effectively zero, and it
improves calibration (ECE \textbf{$0.271$} vs \textbf{$0.393$} for auto-select). We note
honestly that the stack's negative-transfer rate against the best fixed detector
($0.098$) is marginally above auto-select's ($0.073$): average gains do not eliminate
every per-task loss.

\paragraph{Sealed natural-shift test (D22).} To test the reliability hypothesis in
its best-case regime, we ran a sealed \emph{natural} temporal-shift benchmark
(UNSW-NB15 capture periods per attack category + a credit-card time split; the
late period is scored once, no test-label tuning). Plain stacking again wins; the
drift-aware router is \emph{worse} than plain stacking ($-0.050$ AUROC, raw 95\% CI
$[-0.103,-0.013]$) and only $+0.008$ over auto-select (not significant). With only
$n{=}7$ tasks this negative does not survive Holm correction across the ablation
family ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}{=}0.12$; Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), but it is directionally
consistent with the i.i.d.\ ablation: reliability-aware routing does not help even
under genuine shift. The robust, Holm-significant negative is the 123-task stack
reliability gate ($-0.037$ AUROC, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}{<}10^{-8}$).

\section{Strengthening the Stacking Result}
\label{sec:strength}
We harden the main result against seven reviewer concerns.
\textbf{(i) Comprehensive baselines} (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}): the headline
logistic stack beats \emph{every} non-oracle baseline with $95\%$ CIs excluding zero
--- selection (best fixed $+0.073$, validation auto-select $+0.033$, a learned
MetaOD-style~\cite{metaod} selector $+0.045$), averaging (raw / rank /
confidence-weighted $\approx+0.073$; calibrated average $+0.048$), an AutoML-style
greedy ensemble (Caruana, $+0.032$), and a per-family specialist stacker ($+0.049$).
Decisively, it also beats two \emph{test-label oracle} ceilings: the
best-single-detector selection ceiling ($+0.025$, CI $[+0.012,+0.039]$) and the best
equal-weight pair-fusion ceiling ($+0.032$, CI $[+0.018,+0.048]$). A weak-baseline win
cannot exceed the oracle selection ceiling; learned $6$-way per-sample combination can.
\textbf{(ii) Stacker family:} random-forest, gradient-boosting, and hist-GBM stackers
match logistic (differences n.s.), and a rank-normalized logistic variant is
statistically identical ($0.783$); the gain is learned combination, not the choice of
learner.
\textbf{(iii) Detector-zoo strength:} adding a deep detector (DeepSVDD) to the six
classical detectors preserves the win (stack $-$ auto-select $+0.038$, CI
$[+0.025,+0.051]$); DeepSVDD is not stronger than the classical detectors here
(mean AUROC $0.65$), consistent with ADBench, so it does not trivialise routing.
\textbf{(iv) Calibration} (Table~\ref{tab:calib}): a validation-only isotonic
layer yields well-calibrated probabilities. \textbf{(v) Sealed external
replication} (Table~\ref{tab:sealed}): the method, baselines, and pass condition
were frozen (protocol D24) \emph{before} scoring the same $74$ ADBench datasets
\emph{re-featurized with a different (untouched) feature extractor} than development
(ViT/RoBERTa vs.\ ResNet18/BERT)---a representation-shift, not new-data, holdout. On
this one-shot sealed holdout the stack still beats auto-select
($+0.016$, CI $[+0.004,+0.032]$) and the best fixed detector ($+0.043$, CI
$[+0.022,+0.073]$), so the effect replicates under representation shift rather than
being an artefact of the development split. \textbf{(vi) Fraud-family power (D26):}
the fraud family was $n{=}1$ in leave-family-out; we expand it with the BAF suite
\cite{baf} ($6$ bias-controlled variants, a second fraud source, $n{=}7$ total).
This is an honest \emph{boundary}: BAF's base detectors are near-chance ($\approx\!0.6$
AUROC), and there stacking is \emph{not} significantly better than selection
($+0.011$, CI $[-0.066,+0.064]$) nor than the best fixed detector ($-0.033$, CI
$[-0.098,+0.022]$), and on one variant the per-task stack collapses below chance.
Stacking helps where the base detectors are individually informative; on near-chance
fraud it neither manufactures signal nor systematically hurts.
\textbf{(vii) Fully independent external corpus (D27):} the sharpest test of
generalisation is a different data \emph{source}, not the same suite re-extracted. We
froze the method and the exact $20$-task list (protocol D27) over sources verifiably
absent from the development archive --- sklearn \texttt{digits}/\texttt{wine}/%
\texttt{wdbc} and HAR smartphones, one-vs-rest --- and scored once. The advantage is
\emph{larger} than on the development split: stack $0.799$ vs auto-select $0.689$
($+0.111$, CI $[+0.052,+0.176]$) and vs the best fixed detector ($+0.209$, CI
$[+0.120,+0.303]$). Combination generalises to genuinely new sources.

\section{Multimodal Independent-Modality Failure (D23)}
\label{sec:d23}
Every regime where reliability routing was inert used \emph{single-input}
families: all detectors see the same data, so no modality can fail independently.
We therefore pre-registered (\texttt{research\_lock/MULTIMODAL\_RELIABILITY\_PROTOCOL\_v1})
a test on true multimodal data, failing the deployment-best modality at test while the
others stay clean; reliability routing detects the failed modality (KS validation-vs-test
drift, no test labels) and routes away from it. We run it on \emph{four} real datasets
that together span both the two-sensor and three-sensor regimes: Real-IAD-D3 (RGB,
photometric-stereo, point-cloud XYZ), MVTec-3D (RGB, depth), 3D-ADAM (RGB, depth), and
MulSen-AD (RGB camera, infrared, point cloud)~\cite{mulsen}. MulSen-AD is the cleanest
case --- three \emph{physically distinct sensors}, so the independent-failure premise is
intrinsic rather than induced. All three pre-registered hypotheses pass on \emph{every}
dataset (Table~\ref{tab:d23}, Fig.~\ref{fig:d23}); in the clean regime the gate is inert
on all four. The two-sensor datasets (MVTec-3D, 3D-ADAM) confirm the effect does not
require three modalities. The precise claim: \emph{reliability routing helps iff
modalities can fail independently}. Theory Propositions~\ref{prop:inert}
and~\ref{prop:strict} explain why this boundary --- not three datasets' coincidence ---
is where the gate must help.

\paragraph{Failure-model dependence: the headline win is on \emph{injected} failure.}
The four-dataset wins above are obtained under the canonical \emph{uniform-noise}
failure protocol: the deployment-best modality's test scores are \emph{overwritten
with injected uniform noise} (\texttt{St[:,best]=rng.uniform(0,1)} in the harness), a
controlled drift-inducing fault, not natural sensor degradation. This distinction is
decisive. On the one dataset where we instead use \emph{real} degradation with no
injection, the effect does not survive: the gate-vs-validation-only-fusion gain
collapses from $+0.248$ (injected; CI excludes $0$;
\texttt{reliability\_validated:true}) to $+0.031$ (natural; n.s.;
\texttt{reliability\_validated:false}). Two checks locate this boundary.
\textbf{(i) Real sensor dropout:} on MulSen-AD we replace noise with a stuck/constant
sensor reading (a realistic outage): the gate still beats stale single-modality
selection ($+0.28$) but \emph{not} plain fusion (vs equal-weight $-0.03$, n.s.; vs
validation-only fusion $-0.05$). \textbf{(ii) Real natural degradation (D29):} on
Real-IAD-D3 with per-modality deep-PatchCore scores taken from the raw data ($16$
categories, \emph{no injection}), the gate again beats stale selection ($+0.13$) but
not plain fusion ($+0.02$/$+0.03$, n.s.). The honest refinement: \emph{fusion robustly
beats selection, but the gate's extra value over fusion is specific to drift-inducing
faults}; under a stuck sensor or mild natural degradation there is no test-time drift to
exploit and plain fusion is as good --- exactly Proposition~\ref{prop:inert} (no
label-relevant test signal $\Rightarrow$ gate inert).

\section{Scope, Limitations, and Honest Boundaries}
\paragraph{The headline multimodal wins rest on injected failure.} Our central
positive result---reliability routing recovering AUROC on Real-IAD-D3, MVTec-3D,
3D-ADAM, and MulSen-AD---is obtained under the canonical \emph{uniform-noise} failure
protocol, in which the deployment-best modality's test scores are replaced by
injected uniform noise. This is a controlled, drift-\emph{inducing} fault, not real
sensor degradation. \textbf{The one test we ran on genuine natural degradation is a
negative:} on Real-IAD-D3 with no injection (D29), the gate beats plain
validation-only fusion by only $+0.031$ (n.s.) versus $+0.248$ under injection, and
does not validate (\texttt{reliability\_validated:false}); the other three datasets
were \emph{never} tested under natural degradation. Whether the injected-noise win
transfers to real-world degradation is therefore \emph{open}, and is the most
important limitation of this work: the mechanism provably needs a detectable
test-time drift signal (Proposition~\ref{prop:inert}), and a stuck sensor or mild
natural drift may not supply one. This is a characterization with a clearly marked
boundary, not a deployable multimodal method.

This is a focused cross-domain study, not a universal-SOTA claim. The positive
(stacking) is now supported by a comprehensive baseline sweep including oracle
ceilings (D28), calibration, a representation-shift holdout (D24), and a fully
independent external corpus (D27); the negative (reliability routing) is Holm-robust
on single-input data and reversed only under independent multimodal failure (D23).
We do not claim universal superiority or per-dataset SOTA; the strict clean-transfer
gate of the source system remains closed by proof (T9) and is not relitigated here. We note that while \systemu{} significantly improves the mean rank on cyber, tabular, and image-OOD families, the rank-gain bootstrap interval on text tasks is directionally positive but does not exclude zero. The fraud family, previously $n=1$, is now expanded to $n=7$ with the BAF suite~\cite{baf} (Strengthening \S vi); on that hard near-chance panel stacking is \emph{not} significantly better than selection --- an honest boundary, not a win. Furthermore, beyond synthetic degradation we ran a sealed natural temporal-shift benchmark (D22, Results): there too plain stacking wins and the drift-aware router is worse (directionally; the $n{=}7$ negative does not survive Holm correction, while the 123-task stack-gate negative does), so the reliability/drift machinery does not help even under natural shift; native OOD and industrial sensor-drift families remain future work. Calibration is now reported with fitted Brier and NLL (Table~\ref{tab:calib}, reliability diagram Fig.~\ref{fig:reliability}); larger sealed natural-shift suites remain future work.

\section{Conclusion}
We asked \emph{when label-free reliability routing improves multimodal anomaly
detection, and why it fails when no independent modality is available}, and answered
it with a pre-registered evidence contract rather than a leaderboard claim. The
answer is a characterization with three parts. \textbf{First, stacking beats selection.} A per-task logistic stack beats
validation auto-selection ($+0.036$) across $123$ tasks and, in a comprehensive
sweep (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}), beats every standard combiner, a learned
MetaOD-style selector, an AutoML greedy ensemble, and even the test-label
oracle best-single and best-pair ceilings --- so the gain is genuine learned
combination, not a weak-baseline artefact. It replicates under representation shift
(D24, $+0.016$) and on a fully independent external corpus (D27, $+0.111$), and is
well-calibrated after a validation-only isotonic layer. \textbf{Second, reliability
routing does not help single-input meta-routing}: KS drift, disagreement, and a
degenerate-channel guard add no value over plain stacking, and a reliability gate is
Holm-significantly worse --- a clean negative. \textbf{Third, and most important for honest scope: the
multimodal win is real but rests on injected failure}. When a modality fails
\emph{independently and detectably} (D23, four real datasets), a drift-aware gate
recovers large AUROC ($0.767$/$0.792$/$0.748$/$0.763$) and beats validation-only
fusion by up to $+0.248$---but under the canonical \emph{uniform-noise} injection,
not natural degradation. Our single test on genuine natural degradation
(Real-IAD-D3, no injection) is a \emph{negative}: $+0.031$ (n.s.,
\texttt{reliability\_validated:false}) over plain fusion, and the other three datasets
were never tested this way. The gate provably needs a detectable test-time drift
signal (Proposition~\ref{prop:inert}); supplying one from \emph{natural} degradation
is the central open problem we leave. The advantage is
also honestly bounded --- marginal on time-series (NAB, SMD) and on the near-chance BAF
fraud panel, where the base detectors carry little individual signal. The practical
takeaway: \emph{combine detectors by default; add reliability gating only where
inputs can fail independently}.

\paragraph{A tested new direction (per-sample routing) --- and an honest negative.}
We include this refuted method deliberately: it shows the pre-registered evidence
contract catching a false positive in real time --- the same machinery that licenses
the positive claims above. Proposition~\ref{prop:inert}'s inertness is specific to
\emph{per-channel} reweighting (constant across samples), suggesting an escape: a
\emph{per-sample} reliability-gated stacker (PS-RGS) whose weights vary with the input
lies \emph{outside} the linear class the proposition bounds. On the $123$-task classical zoo PS-RGS gives only a directional,
non-significant gain ($+0.0068$, CI $[-0.0002,+0.015]$), with a positive-but-n.s.\
correlation to detector heterogeneity (Spearman $r\!\approx\!0.14$) --- consistent with
a conjecture (T10) that per-sample routing should help in proportion to reliability
heterogeneity. We \emph{tested} that conjecture on a heterogeneous deep$+$classical zoo
(adding DeepSVDD, an autoencoder, and a VAE). On tabular development data PS-RGS
significantly beat plain stacking ($+0.016$, CI excl.\ $0$); but in a \textbf{pre-registered
one-shot held-out confirmation} (protocol D32) the effect \emph{did not replicate} ---
ADBench-CV $+0.002$ (n.s.) and ADBench-NLP $-0.020$ (significantly \emph{worse}). We
therefore report PS-RGS as a theoretically-motivated method whose advantage is
\textbf{not robust}: a development-only signal refuted by held-out confirmation --- a
cautionary result, and a concrete demonstration of why pre-registration matters. The
one effect that \emph{does} replicate is minor: a deep zoo modestly improves plain
stacking on feature-vector families (tabular $+0.010$, CV $+0.004$; both CIs exclude
$0$; n.s.\ on NLP). Future work includes a fully independent multimodal industrial
holdout and native OOD/sensor-drift families.
