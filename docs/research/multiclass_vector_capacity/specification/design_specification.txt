Formally Verified Multiclass Vector-Capacity Research Design

Status

This document specifies an independent, breakthrough-seeking theory program for
K-Bound. It does not change the current K-Bound manuscript, claim ledger, result
authority, or submission-closure protocol. Results may be proposed for inclusion
in K-Bound only after the promotion gates in this specification pass.

The program is high risk. It does not assume that its headline conjectures are
true or novel. A valid negative result, a counterexample, or a literature collision
ends or narrows the affected claim. No engineering milestone or quantity of
experiments can substitute for a new and correct theorem.

Research objective

Characterize the exact information required to decide whether test-time
adaptation is safer than freezing in a multiclass, cost-sensitive deployment,
then derive matching passive and adaptive target-label complexity for decisions
that are not identifiable from unlabeled evidence alone.

The strongest intended result is a linked theorem family containing:

a necessary-and-sufficient multiclass benefit-identification criterion;

a minimal supplementary-information characterization;

a sharp label-free impossibility result;

matching passive-label upper and lower bounds;

matching adaptive-label upper and lower bounds;

a non-vacuous regime with a strict active-over-passive label advantage;

a multi-adapter, cost-sensitive ADAPT/FREEZE/ABSTAIN controller with
finite-sample false-commit control;

complete Lean 4 formalization of every mathematical theorem claimed by the
program.

Relationship to existing K-Bound and ORIUS

The work lives in an isolated multiclass_vector_capacity theory track. The
existing K-Bound paper remains the stable baseline and continues through its own
reconciliation and submission-closure workflow.

If the new program produces only a correct partial characterization, it remains a
separate theory note or follow-up paper. If it produces the complete matching
theorem family and survives novelty review, it may justify restructuring K-Bound
around the result. Its eventual ORIUS role would be a safety-decision layer that
selects among a frozen controller and adaptive candidates under asymmetric
physical costs and sparse delayed feedback. ORIUS integration is outside this
program and cannot be used as evidence for a theorem.

Literature boundary

The novelty claim must be made against, at minimum, these neighboring areas:

robust decisions from partially calibrated high-dimensional forecasts;

decision calibration, multicalibration, omniprediction, and downstream regret;

partial identification and minimax-regret decisions;

multiclass risk estimation under label, covariate, and joint shift;

structured credal learning and distributionally robust classification;

active testing, active risk estimation, optimal experimental design, and
best-arm identification;

class-conditional conformal validity and target-label complexity;

safety and risk monitoring for test-time adaptation.

Two particularly close references known at design time are:

Kiyani, Hassani, Pappas, and Roth, Robust Decision Making with Partially
Calibrated Forecasts, arXiv:2510.23471 (2025), which derives efficiently
computable minimax policies under partial calibration;

Venkatesh, Hüllermeier, Bischl, and Rezaei, Structured Credal Learning, UAI
2026, which studies tractable robust learning under separated covariate and
label uncertainty.

The program is not novel merely because it uses a robust ambiguity set, a
multiclass cost matrix, abstention, or a minimax policy. Its target distinction is
the adaptation-benefit functional, its observable-equivalence class, the exact
supplement needed to identify the decision, and matching passive/adaptive label
complexity.

Mathematical setting

Finite deployment model

The authoritative initial setting is finite:

outcome classes Y = {1, ..., K}, with K >= 3;

observable deployment strata S = {1, ..., m};

a frozen policy indexed by 0 and candidate adapters indexed by
J = {1, ..., p};

known target stratum probabilities q_s, obtained from the unlimited unlabeled
population, with q_s >= 0 and sum_s q_s = 1;

unknown target conditional label vectors eta_s in the K-simplex;

known bounded safety costs C_s(y, a) in [0, 1] for each stratum, outcome, and
policy output/action.

Costs outside [0, 1] are handled by an explicitly recorded positive scale
factor. Scaling must be reversed when reporting application-level quantities.
Context-dependent costs are allowed because the stratum index is observable.

For adapter j, define the known cost-contrast vector

g_{s,j}(y) = C_s(y, f_0(s)) - C_s(y, f_j(s))

and the target benefit

Delta_j(eta) = sum_s q_s * inner(g_{s,j}, eta_s).

Positive benefit means adapter j has lower target safety cost than freezing.
All orientation conventions are fixed by this definition and tested by mutation.

Observable restrictions and ambiguity fiber

Unlimited unlabeled evidence and declared structural knowledge induce a finite
linear restriction

A * eta = b(O),

together with the product-simplex constraints on eta. The observable O
contains only permitted quantities: target stratum frequencies, inputs, model
outputs, logits or representations reduced to declared strata, model
disagreement, and source-derived or structurally justified calibration moments.
It contains no target label, target loss, target confusion entry, target benefit,
or proxy assumption equivalent to one of those quantities.

The ambiguity fiber is

F(O) = {eta in product_s Simplex(K) : A * eta = b(O)}.

Every structural restriction must have a named scientific justification and an
ablation that removes it. An empty fiber is a protocol failure, not a certificate.

Sign and action identification

The identified benefit set is

I(O) = {Delta(eta) : eta in F(O)}.

For one adapter, ADAPT is identified when inf I(O) > 0, FREEZE is identified
when sup I(O) < 0, and the controller abstains otherwise. For multiple adapters,
a strict adapter selection requires its worst-case cost to be lower than freezing
and every competing committed candidate under the declared comparison rule.

Point identification, uniform identification over an observable model class, and
sign identification at one realized fiber are separate statements. A proof of one
must not be described as a proof of another.

Theorem program

Every item below begins as a conjecture. It becomes a theorem only when its Lean
declaration compiles under the formal-verification policy.

T1: Multiclass cost-benefit identity

Prove the exact vector identity expressing every frozen-versus-adapter safety-cost
difference as the linear functional Delta_j(eta) above. Include arbitrary
finite K, strata, candidates, and bounded stratum-dependent costs.

T2: Uniform identification criterion

Test and, if correct, prove that the complete benefit vector is uniformly
identified over the affine observable model exactly when the relevant rows of the
cost-contrast operator vanish on the observable null space, equivalently when
they lie in the row space generated by the observable restrictions after the
simplex affine hull is incorporated.

The simplex affine constraints must be part of the operator before any rank or
row-space calculation. Omitting them is a required mutation test.

T3: Minimal linear supplement

Test and, if correct, prove that the minimum number of independent additional
linear moments needed for uniform point identification equals the rank increment
of the contrast operator over the observable-plus-simplex operator. State and
prove the precise field, finite-dimensionality, independence, and admissible
supplement class. Do not translate the dimension automatically into “bits.”

The result is not allowed to define an admissible supplement by directly exposing
Delta, its sign, or an invertible encoding of the answer. Scientifically useful
supplements must also be instantiated using observable structural knowledge or
target labels.

T4: Realized-fiber sign capacity

Characterize sign identification through the extrema of I(O) and prove the
soundness of the ternary frontier. Determine which claims are uniform and which
are fiber-specific. Establish non-vacuous examples where point identification
fails but sign identification succeeds.

T5: Label-free impossibility

When a relevant contrast direction survives in the observable null space,
construct admissible target worlds with identical unlabeled observable laws and
opposite optimal strict decisions. Prove the strongest correct decision-error
lower bound. A 1/2 statement is promoted only under explicit symmetric or
two-point conditions sufficient to make that constant valid; otherwise the
correct total-variation or minimax bound is used.

T6: Passive delayed-label complexity

In the passive model, each labeled observation has stratum drawn independently
from q, and its label is revealed after a declared delay. Label complexity counts
revealed labels; wall-clock/sample-arrival complexity is reported separately.

Derive a finite-sample confidence region and a matching minimax or
instance-dependent lower bound for the requested commitment task. Bounds must
show their dependence on safety margin, contrast range or variance, stratum mass,
candidate count, confidence, and any effective contrast dimension. Upper and
lower bounds called “matching” must agree up to explicitly stated universal or
logarithmic factors.

T7: Adaptive delayed-label complexity

In the active pool model, unlabeled strata arrive from q; the controller may
choose whether to request each label using only information available before that
label. Query probabilities and estimators must preserve validity under adaptive
selection and delayed returns.

Derive the optimal or provably near-optimal allocation for the commitment task,
an upper bound for the resulting controller, and a matching information-theoretic
lower bound. Label count and unlabeled waiting time are distinct resources.

T8: Strict active-over-passive separation

Construct a scientifically meaningful family, such as rare informative strata
under asymmetric safety costs, in which active querying uses asymptotically fewer
labels than passive feedback at the same false-commit level. The separation must
hold under the same target family, margin convention, and cost normalization.

T9: Multi-adapter safe controller

Give simultaneous confidence control for all frozen-versus-adapter and required
adapter-versus-adapter contrasts. Prove that the controller's probability of a
wrong strict commitment is at most the declared delta, including adaptive query
selection and the multiplicity method. Define ties and abstention explicitly.

Formal verification policy

Authority

Lean 4 is authoritative for mathematical theorem status. A LaTeX proof, Python
validator, LP certificate, simulation, or expert review cannot promote a claim
whose Lean declaration does not compile.

Every mathematical theorem claimed by this program must be fully formalized,
including deterministic, optimization, probabilistic, passive-label,
adaptive-label, lower-bound, and controller-validity results. Corollaries described
as mathematical guarantees are subject to the same rule.

Lean module structure

The intended module boundary is:

Basic.lean: finite types, strata, candidates, simplexes, probabilities, costs;

Benefit.lean: cost contrasts and the benefit identity;

ObservableFiber.lean: restrictions, fibers, and observable equivalence;

Identification.lean: null-space and row-space equivalences;

MinimalSupplement.lean: rank increment and admissible supplements;

SignCapacity.lean: identified extrema and ternary frontier;

Impossibility.lean: indistinguishable worlds and decision lower bounds;

PassiveLabels.lean: passive estimators, confidence, and lower bounds;

ActiveLabels.lean: predictable querying, estimators, bounds, and separation;

MultiAdapter.lean: simultaneous comparisons and multiplicity;

SafeController.lean: commitment rule and false-commit theorem;

Audit.lean: exported headline theorem inventory and axiom inspection.

Modules may be split when necessary for clarity, but no module may combine theorem
discovery scripts, empirical data processing, and formal proofs.

Prohibited proof gaps

The formal tree must contain no sorry, admit, by_contra! misuse hidden behind
an inconsistent assumption, custom unproved axiom, or placeholder declaration.
Standard Lean and mathlib logical foundations may appear in #print axioms and
must be reported. Every locally introduced axiom is a release-blocking failure.

An automated audit scans source and compiled declarations, runs lake build, and
captures #print axioms for every headline declaration. The allowlist covers only
documented standard Lean/mathlib foundations, never a program-specific claim.

Statement parity

Each paper-facing theorem receives a stable theorem ID. A machine-readable ledger
maps that ID to:

the exact Lean declaration;

the human-readable statement;

assumptions and scope;

dependency declarations;

compiled axiom report;

source and build hashes;

exact validators and witnesses;

promotion status.

A statement-parity test fails if the manuscript changes a quantifier, inequality,
constant, action orientation, scope, or assumption relative to the ledger and Lean
declaration.

Discovery and adversarial verification

Exact oracle

A separate discovery implementation uses exact rational arithmetic for finite
simplexes, ranks, fibers, and linear-program certificates. Floating-point LP output
may guide search but cannot certify a theorem or witness.

For each claimed extremum, store primal and dual certificates when the selected
formulation admits them. The exact checker independently validates feasibility,
objective equality, and sign orientation.

Counterexample search

Before proof effort, enumerate bounded rational systems and run property-based
mutations covering at least:

K = 3, 4, 5;

one and multiple strata;

one and multiple adapters;

symmetric, asymmetric, zero, and catastrophic cost patterns;

full-rank, deficient-rank, redundant, and inconsistent observable operators;

disconnected and overlapping conflict structures;

zero-margin and near-boundary cases;

zero-mass strata;

simplex vertices, faces, and interior points.

Bounded enumeration does not prove a theorem. Its role is to kill false statements
early and ensure validators fail loudly when a known condition is removed.

Required mutations

The verification suite must detect deliberate corruption of:

benefit orientation;

strict versus non-strict inequalities;

simplex normalization or nonnegativity;

incorporation of the simplex affine hull into rank calculations;

observable-equivalence constraints;

label-query predictability;

importance weights;

multiplicity correction;

confidence level;

passive/active resource accounting.

Statistical and empirical validation

Experiments demonstrate relevance and non-vacuity; they do not prove theorems.

Synthetic exact suite

Generate sealed finite problems spanning identification rank, safety margins,
stratum rarity, candidate count, cost asymmetry, and active-query informativeness.
Include positive, negative, boundary, and assumption-violation families. Compare
empirical commitment error and label use with the formal bounds without changing
the protocol after observing results.

Natural multiclass suite

Use existing K-Bound-compatible multiclass shifts only after the theorem survives
the synthetic and Lean gates. Candidate datasets may include existing CIFAR,
ImageNet, PACS, OfficeHome, and WILDS tracks, subject to source integrity and
licensing. The experiment protocol must predeclare:

cost matrices and their domain justification;

frozen and candidate adapters;

strata and query policy;

passive and active label budgets;

delay model;

safety margin and confidence level;

baselines and statistical unit;

primary and negative-control endpoints;

all promotion and failure rules.

No cost matrix may be tuned to make K-Bound win. At least one independent
domain-informed matrix and one sensitivity family must be used.

Baselines

Required conceptual baselines are label-free commitment, passive uniform labels,
stratified passive labels, uncertainty sampling, cost-weighted heuristics,
oracle allocation, always freeze, always adapt, and the best valid ambiguity-set
or partial-calibration decision rule identified by the novelty audit.

Work products

The independent track will contain:

a living research specification and theorem ledger;

a dated novelty matrix with claim-level comparisons;

Lean source, pinned toolchain, build manifest, and axiom reports;

exact rational oracle and counterexample corpus;

passive and active discovery simulations;

sealed synthetic and natural experiment protocols;

generated tables and figures sourced from authoritative artifacts;

a standalone theory manuscript only after at least T1--T5 are formalized and
the novelty gate remains open.

The current K-Bound manuscript is not an output target until the promotion decision.

Promotion and kill gates

Claim-level kill conditions

A claim is withdrawn or narrowed if any of the following occurs:

a published result directly subsumes it under equivalent assumptions;

an exact or Lean-checked counterexample refutes it;

its proof requires observing target risk, target benefit, or an equivalent oracle;

its assumptions make the ambiguity fiber singleton by definition without a
scientifically available information source;

a stated matching bound has a substantive unclosed rate gap;

active querying has no strict label advantage in any non-degenerate common model;

the result holds only by choosing costs or strata after inspecting outcomes.

Program success levels

Verified foundation: T1--T5 formalized, novel, and non-vacuous. This supports
a strong theory contribution but is not called a breakthrough.

Major result: verified foundation plus a correct passive or active label
complexity theory with a meaningful application.

Breakthrough-potential result: T1--T9 formalized, passive and active bounds
match under declared criteria, strict active separation is proved, the novelty
audit finds no subsuming result, and prospective experiments support relevance.

No document may state “breakthrough” as an established fact. Venue reviewers and
subsequent scientific use determine impact; the program may state only that the
complete package has breakthrough potential.

Promotion into K-Bound

Promotion requires all of:

the relevant theorem set is fully Lean-verified;

exact-oracle, mutation, parity, and build gates pass from a clean checkout;

the claim-level novelty matrix remains favorable;

a separate adversarial review finds no fatal assumption or overclaim;

the current K-Bound submission closure remains valid;

manuscript integration does not alter or reinterpret frozen empirical outcomes;

a written decision explains why integration is stronger than a separate paper.

Until all seven conditions hold, the track remains independent.
