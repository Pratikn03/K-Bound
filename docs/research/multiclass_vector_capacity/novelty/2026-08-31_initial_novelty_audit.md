## 1. Leading collisions

The closest task-level antecedent is **paired active risk comparison**, not only general calibration or distributionally robust learning. Sawade, Landwehr, and Scheffer already studied a baseline and challenger on a target test distribution with costly labels in 2012. Hara et al. later developed unbiased adaptive loss-difference estimation, and Karimi et al. studied online selective querying among pretrained multiclass classifiers. The phrase “query labels to determine whether adaptation improves risk” therefore cannot carry the novelty claim. See the primary-source comparisons in Section 4.

Two requested publication checks are resolved:

- Kiyani, Hassani, Pappas, and Roth have an **ICLR 2026** paper, titled *Robust Decision-Making with Partially Calibrated Forecasters* in the [official conference record](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10007595). The linked arXiv version has the slightly different title *Robust Decision Making with Partially Calibrated Forecasts*, [arXiv:2510.23471v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23471v1), dated 2025-10-27. It should not be described as merely an unreviewed 2025 proposal.
- Venkatesh, Hüllermeier, Bischl, and Rezaei's *Structured Credal Learning* is **published in UAI 2026, PMLR 337:6917–6952**, not merely “claimed UAI” or forthcoming. The [official proceedings record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v337/venkatesh26a.html) specifies 17–21 August 2026. A stale institutional “to be published” notice does not override that record.

## 2. T1–T9 disposition

“Known form” means that the stated mathematical operation or problem framing is already standard; it does not assert that a cited paper contains the exact proposed Lean statement. “Unresolved” means that neither novelty nor full subsumption has been demonstrated.

Consequently, the design's requirement that T1–T5 constitute a *novel* verified foundation is **not met by their current formulations**. Proving standard lemmas in Lean would not change that novelty assessment. It may support a separately scoped formal-methods contribution, which would require its own prior-art audit.

## 3. Immediate claim-level gates and scope corrections

### 3.1 T5: an exact sign-identified, non-point-identified fiber

This is a mathematical counterexample derived from the supplied design, not a literature assertion or a claim of compiled formal verification.

Take one stratum of mass one and three outcomes. Let the observable restriction be eta_1 = 1/5, with nonnegativity and sum eta = 1. Set the frozen and candidate cost columns to

    C_0 = (0, 1/2, 1),   C_1 = (1, 0, 0),   g = C_0 - C_1 = (-1, 1/2, 1).

All costs lie in [0,1]. On this fiber,

    Delta = -1/5 + eta_2/2 + eta_3 = 3/5 - eta_2/2,
    0 <= eta_2 <= 4/5,   so Delta ranges over [1/5, 3/5].

The feasible direction v = (0,1,-1) is annihilated by both the observable and simplex equalities, but g dot v = -1/2. Thus the contrast is not point-identified, while ADAPT is strictly correct throughout the fiber. No pair of opposite strict decisions exists there.

The correct additional sign condition for a single contrast is that feasible extrema satisfy L < 0 < U. For multiple candidates, require feasible worlds with the relevant disjoint correct-answer sets. The familiar 1/2 randomized two-world error floor concerns a forced incompatible decision, or a success definition that counts abstention as failure. It is not a 1/2 lower bound on false commits when abstention is free.

### 3.2 T2 and T3: do not promote the unrestricted algebra

Write H for the stacked observable and simplex equality operators. On the unrestricted affine model, a target contrast is constant on every H-fiber precisely when it annihilates ker H. On a particular feasible simplex fiber F, the exact object is span(F-F). For example, eta_1 + eta_2 = 0 and simplex constraints force eta = (0,0,1), although the equality kernel alone contains (1,-1,0). Boundary-induced identification must not be excluded by an inappropriate full-kernel test.

Similarly, rank([H;G]) - rank(H) is the unrestricted supplement count. If permitted measurements come from a fixed dictionary, a single missing contrast direction may require several dictionary measurements or may be unavailable. A dimension lower bound can remain valid while its unrestricted attainability proof becomes inadmissible. Neither an invertible re-encoding of the answer nor observing a noisy label is automatically an allowed noiseless scalar moment.

### 3.3 Safety is not a positive sample-complexity objective

A controller that always returns ABSTAIN has zero false-commit probability and needs zero target labels. Positive T6/T7 lower bounds therefore require an explicit additional condition, such as correct commitment with probability at least 1-delta on specified separated instances, or finite stopping with a correct non-abstaining answer. Ties and arbitrarily small gaps require separate treatment. This is the condition that permits a best-answer lower bound to be transferred; a safety-only guarantee does not suffice.

### 3.4 Normalize the active/passive comparison

Since each cost is in [0,1], each contrast lies in [-1,1]. If all nonzero contributions occur in strata of total mass r, then |Delta| <= r. A rare-stratum construction cannot keep a larger fixed absolute benefit margin while taking r to zero.

If every queried stratum has positive arrival probability, unlimited unlabeled arrivals are free, and delay only postpones eventual returns without informative censoring, a label-only active procedure can wait for its desired stratum. Arrival mass then affects waiting cost, not the set of feasible long-run label-allocation proportions. A delay extension that merely serializes an existing procedure need not create new label complexity. Returned-label selection under label-dependent delays, on the other hand, needs its own likelihood or validity assumptions.

## 4. Closest primary literature: exact overlap and limits

### 4.1 Paired active evaluation and online model selection

**Christoph Sawade, Niels Landwehr, Tobias Scheffer. *Active Comparison of Prediction Models*. NeurIPS 25, 2012.** [Proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/hash/92fb0c6d1758261f10d052e6e2c1123c-Abstract.html); [paper, Section 3 and Theorem 1](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/92fb0c6d1758261f10d052e6e2c1123c-Paper.pdf).

This is the strongest direct framing collision. It compares two models' target risks using actively acquired labels; the target is their paired risk difference. Theorem 1 gives an oracle proposal proportional to target density times the conditional root-mean-square of the centered loss difference, optimizing an asymptotic test-power approximation. Section 3 also discusses multiple-model comparisons. The oracle proposal depends on unknown conditional labels and risk difference and is approximated in the implementation. This is not a finite-sample, uniformly valid, moment-constrained categorical identification theorem. It nevertheless rules out presenting paired-benefit targeting or active baseline/challenger comparison as new.

**Satoshi Hara, Mitsuru Matsuura, Junya Honda, Shinji Ito. *Active model selection: A variance minimization approach*. Machine Learning 113:8327–8345, 2024; published 21 November.** [Publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-024-06603-1).

Section 3 estimates paired test-loss differences with LURE under adaptive finite-pool sampling. Theorem 1 characterizes a one-step conditional-variance oracle for two models; Section 3.3 extends the proposal to multiple models. This further precludes a novelty claim based on unbiased active risk differences or merely adding candidates. Its finite-population target and surrogate-dependent proposals differ from the proposed unknown categorical kernels. Its variance objective is not, without another proof, the same as minimax fixed-confidence identification complexity. The multi-model formula should be independently checked before importing it as an optimal-allocation theorem.

**Mohammad Reza Karimi, Nezihe Merve Gürel, Bojan Karlaš, Johannes Rausch, Ce Zhang, Andreas Krause. *Online Active Model Selection for Pre-trained Classifiers*. AISTATS 2021, PMLR 130:307–315.** [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/reza-karimi21a.html); [paper, Sections 3–4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/reza-karimi21a/reza-karimi21a.pdf).

MODEL PICKER queries an unlabeled stream selectively and recommends among pretrained multiclass classifiers. In the IID setting, Theorems 5 and 6 give expected query and identification-probability bounds with a unique-best-model gap and disagreement-dependent difficulty. Thus streaming selective querying, multiple committed models, and high-probability identification are already studied together. Those results concern classification loss and their own round/query model; they are not a matching characterization for general context-dependent costs, affine side information, and delayed categorical observations. Care is needed because rounds with complete model agreement are excluded from parts of their analysis.

**Jannik Kossen, Sebastian Farquhar, Yarin Gal, Tom Rainforth. *Active Testing: Sample-Efficient Model Evaluation*. ICML 2021, PMLR 139:5753–5763.** [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/kossen21a.html). Active test-point acquisition and sampling-bias correction are established. This is an additional evaluation comparator, not a substitute for the more directly paired Sawade and Hara comparisons.

### 4.2 Partial calibration and structured credal ambiguity

For **Kiyani et al.**, the theorem locators below refer specifically to the [arXiv v1 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23471v1), not an asserted line-for-line verification of the conference PDF. Equations (4)–(5) define label-conditional ambiguity through calibration moments and optimize worst-case utility. Theorem 3.1 supplies a dual characterization of robust decision-making; Theorems 4.1–4.2 and Corollary 4.3 connect decision calibration and simultaneous decision problems. Multiclass linear-in-outcome utility is included. These are direct antecedents to calibrated ambiguity and robust decisions. They do not establish the proposed admissible-supplement or active-label lower bounds.

The finite-support reduction is explicit: if strata are forecast values v_s, a calibration test h imposes sum_s q_s h(v_s)(eta_s-v_s) = 0. Such operators are instances of A eta = b. An arbitrary A need not have this specific calibration structure; claiming total subsumption requires a reduction, not analogy. Conversely, renaming these calibration constraints as vector capacity does not create novelty. Nor does a source-trained calibration condition automatically hold on the target distribution.

For **Structured Credal Learning**, the detailed locators refer to [arXiv:2603.14070v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14070v1), dated 2026-03-14; the UAI publication metadata is independently verified above. Definition 3.3 constructs convex joint-distribution ambiguity from candidate covariate marginals and label kernels. Section 5 analyzes fixed-covariate disagreement/diameter estimation, including finite-sample concentration, and Proposition 6.1 reduces robust optimization to extreme distributions. Its observed candidate label kernels or annotator information must not be equated with merely seeing two models' predictions. Neither that distinction nor the present fixed-q specialization proves a new label-complexity theorem.

An additional mathematical comparison: mapping eta to P(s,y)=q_s eta_s sends the proposed finite fiber to a joint-distribution polytope. It has a finite vertex representation. Thus “robust optimization over this polytope reduces to extrema” is already general polyhedral mathematics. Half-space versus supplied-vertex representations can differ computationally, but an actual complexity separation would be needed to make that distinction substantive.

Uniform strict superiority is not identical to minimax selection: risks R_0=(0.9,0.1) and R_1=(0.8,0.4) across two feasible worlds favor policy 1 under worst-case risk, while the paired benefit takes both signs. This audit example explains a genuine difference of decision criteria; it does not by itself establish novelty.

### 4.3 Decision calibration and omniprediction

**Shengjia Zhao, Michael Kim, Roshni Sahoo, Tengyu Ma, Stefano Ermon. *Calibrating Predictions to Decisions: A Novel Approach to Multi-Class Calibration*. NeurIPS 34, 2021.** [Proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/bbc92a647199b832ec90d7cf57074e9e-Abstract.html); [paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/bbc92a647199b832ec90d7cf57074e9e-Paper.pdf).

Decision calibration already asks for outcome distributions to be indistinguishable to downstream decision-makers. Theorem 1 relates this to established calibration notions; Theorem 2 and Proposition 2 give multiclass, action-sensitive calibration guarantees and moment characterizations. The target is calibrated prediction/recalibration under labeled data, not strict sign identification on an unobserved target fiber. Still, decision-relevant moments instead of full probability identification are not a new principle.

**Parikshit Gopalan, Adam Tauman Kalai, Omer Reingold, Vatsal Sharan, Udi Wieder. *Omnipredictors*. ITCS 2022, LIPIcs 215, 79:1–79:21.** [Publisher](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2022.79). Multicalibration supports post-processing for families of losses and comparators; this is not a target-label-free identification guarantee. The foundational multicalibration paper is **Úrsula Hébert-Johnson, Michael Kim, Omer Reingold, Guy Rothblum**, ICML 2018, [PMLR 80:1939–1948](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/hebert-johnson18a.html).

**Lunjia Hu, Kevin Tian, Chutong Yang. *Simultaneous Blackwell Approachability and Applications to Multiclass Omniprediction*. COLT 2026, PMLR 336:3593–3634.** [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/hu26c.html). This published multiclass extension includes statistical sample-complexity and online guarantees against a possibly infinite comparator family. “Multiclass rather than binary omniprediction” is not an unoccupied novelty claim. A full rate-level comparison is still required if omniprediction becomes part of the proposed result.

### 4.4 Partial identification and indistinguishability

**Timothy Christensen, Hyungsik Roger Moon, Frank Schorfheide. *Optimal Decision Rules When Payoffs are Partially Identified*. Review of Economic Studies, rdag017, online 2 March 2026.** [Publisher](https://academic.oup.com/restud/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/restud/rdag017/8502914); [journal-hosted manuscript, 23 January 2026](https://www.restud.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/MS33570manuscript.pdf).

The paper studies finite decisions whose payoffs depend on a partially identified parameter, with observed data informing a point-identified parameter that restricts the payoff set. It combines conditional minimax ambiguity treatment with statistical learning of that identified parameter. This directly overlaps the high-level “observables restrict payoffs, then decide” framing. Its asymptotic decision-risk objective is not the same as uniform strict dominance or active categorical sign identification. Its existence prevents treating generic robust decision-making from a payoff identified set as a contribution.

**Alexander Balke, Judea Pearl. *Bounds on Treatment Effects from Studies with Imperfect Compliance*. JASA 92(439):1171–1176, 1997.** [Publisher](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01621459.1997.10474074); [author-hosted paper](https://ftp.cs.ucla.edu/pub/stat_ser/r199-jasa.pdf). Sharp bounds from observable restrictions on a latent probability model using linear programming are long established. The causal assumptions differ; the relevance is the identification/bounding method, not direct equivalence of application models.

**Shai Ben-David, Tyler Lu, Teresa Luu, Dávid Pál. *Impossibility Theorems for Domain Adaptation*. AISTATS 2010, PMLR 9:129–136.** [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v9/david10a.html). The paper already constructs observationally indistinguishable adaptation tasks where reweighting helps one and hurts another. The proposed T5 still needs its own feasible sign-crossing hypotheses; the existence of classical indistinguishability arguments cannot make its presently false implication true.

### 4.5 Information bounds and allocation

**Emilie Kaufmann, Olivier Cappé, Aurélien Garivier. *On the Complexity of Best-Arm Identification in Multi-Armed Bandit Models*. JMLR 17(1):1–42, 2016.** [Journal](https://jmlr.org/papers/v17/kaufman16a.html); [paper, Lemma 1](https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/papers/volume17/kaufman16a/kaufman16a.pdf). Its general change-of-measure inequality relates expected query counts times armwise KL divergence to the binary KL of a terminal event, for mutually absolutely continuous arm laws and suitable stopping times. This is the direct lower-bound tool for categorical stratum observations; candidate policies need not themselves be arms.

**Aurélien Garivier, Emilie Kaufmann. *Optimal Best Arm Identification with Fixed Confidence*. COLT 2016, PMLR 49:998–1027.** [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v49/garivier16a.html); [paper, Theorems 1 and 14](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v49/garivier16a.pdf). The characteristic-time game optimizes sampling proportions against alternative parameters, and Track-and-Stop asymptotically matches it for the stated one-parameter bandit models. A matching constant or rate cannot be imported wholesale into a different observation family.

**Tanner Fiez, Lalit Jain, Kevin G. Jamieson, Lillian Ratliff. *Sequential Experimental Design for Transductive Linear Bandits*. NeurIPS 32, 2019.** [Proceedings](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/8ba6c657b03fc7c8dd4dff8e45defcd2-Abstract.html); [paper, Theorems 1–2](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/file/8ba6c657b03fc7c8dd4dff8e45defcd2-Paper.pdf). Measurement directions and decision directions are explicitly different. There is a Gaussian lower bound and a near-optimal, up-to-logarithms procedure under the stated sub-Gaussian setting. Targeted contrast geometry rather than full parameter estimation is already central. Here a label reveals a categorical block, not the scalar linear observation assumed there; this needs an exact reduction or a genuinely different theorem.

**Rémy Degenne, Wouter M. Koolen. *Pure Exploration with Multiple Correct Answers*. NeurIPS 32, 2019.** [Primary paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/file/60cb558c40e4f18479664069d9642d5a-Paper.pdf); [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03475). The formalism permits structured parameter subsets and set-valued correct answers. It is highly relevant to distinguishing “any safe candidate” from “the unique best candidate.” Its stated observation model uses one-parameter, one-dimensional exponential families; general categorical kernels are not automatically a verbatim special case.

The resulting audit obligation can be stated without claiming a new theorem. For two admissible kernels eta and eta' with different correct decisions and identical free observables, the standard prospective comparison is

    sum_s E_eta[N_s] KL(eta_s || eta'_s) >= kl(1-delta, delta).

One label at stratum s reveals its class and hence the costs of every committed policy on that example. It must not be charged as a fresh independent sample per candidate. Restricting alternatives by A eta' = b, optimizing the allocation, and writing a supremum-infimum expression are standard steps; novelty would need to reside in its evaluated geometry, achievable finite-sample rate, or another demonstrated nontrivial result. Support boundaries and the delayed observation filtration remain proof obligations.

## 5. Other required boundaries already visible in primary sources

- **Classical allocation:** Jerzy Neyman, *On the Two Different Aspects of the Representative Method: The Method of Stratified Sampling and the Method of Purposive Selection*, JRSS 97(4):558–606, 1934. [Publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.2397-2335.1934.tb04184.x). In the simplest fixed-allocation calculation here, variance is sum_s q_s^2 sigma_s^2/n_s; minimizing it gives n_s proportional to q_s sigma_s. That allocation calculation is not a new capacity result. See also Katariya, Iyer, and Sarawagi's *Active Evaluation of Classifiers on Large Datasets*, ICDM 2012, [author paper](https://www.cse.iitb.ac.in/~sunita/papers/icdm2012.pdf), for active stratified evaluation.
- **Unsupervised risk / shift assumptions:** Steinhardt and Liang, *Unsupervised Risk Estimation Using Only Conditional Independence Structure*, NeurIPS 2016, [proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2016/hash/f2d887e01a80e813d9080038decbbabb-Abstract.html), already target risk without full conditional-distribution estimation using structural moments. Lipton, Wang, and Smola, *Detecting and Correcting for Label Shift with Black Box Predictors*, ICML 2018, [PMLR 80:3122–3130](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/lipton18a.html), rely on invariant class conditionals and an invertible source confusion matrix. Such assumptions need scientific justification; unlabeled target observables do not establish them by themselves.
- **Conditional coverage:** Vladimir Vovk, *Conditional Validity of Inductive Conformal Predictors*, ACML 2012, [PMLR 25:475–490](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v25/vovk12.html), Section 4 / Proposition 3, distinguishes label-conditional validity and its calibration counts. Gibbs, Cherian, and Candès, *Conformal prediction with conditional guarantees*, JRSS B 87(4):1100–1126, 2025, [publisher record](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/87/4/1100/8058684), study guarantees over classes of covariate shifts. Neither prediction-set coverage nor its dimensionality alone is a lower bound for paired-benefit sign identification. Nontrivial set efficiency, conditioning target, and resource accounting must be matched before a comparison is meaningful.
- **Sequential safety:** Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe, and Sekhon, *Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences*, Annals of Statistics 49(2):1055–1080, 2021, [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08240), provide a standard foundation for time-uniform bounds. Simultaneous coverage followed by deterministic safe gating is an application, not automatically a new theorem.
- **TTA monitoring:** Schirmer, Jazbec, Naesseth, and Nalisnick, *Monitoring Risks in Test-Time Adaptation*, NeurIPS 38, 2025, [proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/746960ad49ddb47248970a0e1404230c-Abstract-Conference.html), explicitly combines adapted models and sequential risk monitoring. It is not interchangeable with a label-assisted, paired-benefit certificate; its surrogate assumptions and monitored criterion need comparison if T9 is pursued.
- **Delayed-label action gating:** Lamaakal, Yahyati, El Makkaoui, Ouahbi, and Maleh, *Drift-to-Action Controllers: Budgeted Interventions with Online Risk Certificates*, [arXiv:2603.08578v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08578v1), 9 March 2026. Its Appendix D uses uniformly randomized auditing within a window, time-uniform risk bounds, and a gating implication; the target is absolute windowed risk. The arXiv record reports CAO Workshop at ICLR 2026, and a [primary OpenReview PDF](https://openreview.net/pdf/0e57e8d292a65cd1dedcbd11bde75dbbebb484b1.pdf) carries that workshop header. This audit has not independently reconciled the workshop's acceptance record. This is prior delayed-label risk-to-action framing, not evidence of matching T6–T8 rates.

## 6. Remaining work before any favorable novelty decision

1. **Freeze a valid target.** Narrow T5, separate affine and realized-fiber identification, and specify the admissible supplement dictionary. Record whether the answer is any improving candidate, the unique best candidate, or minimax selection. Define non-abstaining success and behavior on ties before stating a label lower bound.
2. **Write the exact experiment.** State whether O and A are known exactly, how they were obtained without unavailable labels, which categorical laws are admissible, what each query reveals, how delayed returns enter the filtration, and which clocks are charged. Include noisy/estimated-moment misspecification separately if claimed; it is not covered by an exact A eta = b identity.
3. **Prove or refute reductions to the closest results.** Compare T6–T9 with Sawade, Hara, Karimi, the categorical change-of-measure lemma, transductive design, and multiple-answer pure exploration under identical parameter families and success criteria. A Gaussian or one-parameter theorem is neither automatically equivalent nor automatically irrelevant.
4. **Evaluate rather than rename the complexity.** A rank deficit, an LP, or an unevaluated KL optimization does not yet establish novel capacity. Any dimension-to-label result must retain the relevant margins, covariance/contrast geometry, admissible observations, and attainability conditions. Compare active and passive results on the same normalized family, with optimal baselines rather than only a weak empirical sampler.
5. **Reconcile final versions.** Kiyani's conference publication and SCL's UAI publication are verified, but the detailed theorem locators used here came from their identified arXiv v1 texts. The linked final PDFs were not successfully retrieved in this audit. Check final-version theorem/assumption parity before writing a publication-facing subsumption claim. Independently check Hara's multi-model optimality formula before using it as a baseline theorem.
6. **Close targeted residual literature, not an unrestricted search.** If multiclass calibration or delay becomes the differentiator, inspect Farina and Perdomo's *An Efficient Black-Box Reduction from Online Learning to Multicalibration, and a New Route to Phi-Regret Minimization*, [arXiv:2604.19592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19592), whose primary abstract explicitly discusses delayed/censored feedback and multiclass omniprediction. For stratified evaluation, inspect Kumar and Raj's *Classifier Risk Estimation Under Limited Labeling Resources*, PAKDD 2018, [publisher chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-93034-3_1) and [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.02665). These sources are flagged for further theorem-level comparison, not claimed to subsume the program.

A potentially substantive remaining question is the exact, attainable cost of identifying a strict policy decision from shared categorical stratum labels under independently justified side information, including a fair passive/active comparison. This is a question to investigate, **not a finding that the answer is novel**. The present audit supports retaining UNRESOLVED / PARTIAL_COLLISION and withholding both a “novel verified foundation” claim and a whole-program kill.
