\begin{abstract}
Test-time adaptation (TTA) can improve a model on unlabeled target data, but the same update objective that helps under one distribution shift can silently harm under another. Because target labels are unavailable at deployment, a system must decide not only how to adapt, but whether a proposed adaptation should be committed at all.

We formalize this as a label-free \emph{adapt/freeze/abstain} decision problem: the adaptation benefit is the drop in target risk from keeping the frozen baseline $f_0$ instead of the adapted candidate $f_a$ (formally $\Delta = R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a)$), and the system must decide its sign from unlabeled evidence $Z$ alone.
We prove an exact benefit-sign frontier: over a declared target class, the sign of ($\Delta$) is identifiable from label-free evidence if and only if an observable disagreement-region margin exceeds the admitted calibration-drift budget. Below this frontier, two target worlds can induce identical observable evidence while requiring opposite adaptation decisions; therefore, no label-free rule can be correct in both worlds and abstention is information-theoretically necessary.

On the identifiable side of the frontier, we give an adapt/freeze/abstain certificate that controls unconditional false-adapt and false-freeze probabilities under stated risk-alignment, coverage, and calibration assumptions. We instantiate the certificate as Knowability-Guided Adaptation (KGA), a mechanism-agnostic wrapper around existing adaptation methods including Tent, EATA, and SAR.

Experiments separate the regimes predicted by the theory. On the pre-registered mixed harmful+helpful CIFAR-10-C stress grid, \textsc{KGA} \textbf{beats POEM and AETTA} (the no-harm SOTA baselines) on regret-to-oracle with Holm-corrected paired-bootstrap CIs, while maintaining zero unconditional false-adapt; it also beats both always-adapt and always-freeze for Tent and EATA and for a harmful SAR operating point on ImageNet-C. On held-out natural shifts \textsc{KGA} is uniformly no-harm under a valid out-of-fold radius: it beats the worse fixed policy and ties the better at zero false-adapt (on Office-Home and iWildCam it beats always-adapt and ties always-freeze; Camelyon17 and RxRx1 are one-sided), and no natural shift is a beats-both once the conformal radius is held out of sample. On weak or split-specific evidence regimes, it abstains or freezes conservatively rather than claiming universal improvement. K-Bound therefore reframes TTA as an identifiability-limited deployment decision: adapt when benefit is certifiable, freeze when harm is certifiable, and abstain when label-free evidence cannot support a valid commitment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
\begin{figure}[t]\centering

\textsc{adapt} (knowably helpful), \textsc{freeze} (knowably harmful), or \textsc{abstain} (sign
unidentifiable), with false-adapt/false-freeze $\le\alpha$.}
\label{fig:teaser}

Deployed models rarely encounter the same distribution they were trained on.
Test-time adaptation (TTA) updates a model on unlabeled target data, often through
entropy minimization or stability-aware updates. In favorable shifts, adaptation can
recover accuracy; in unfavorable shifts the same objective can silently degrade
performance. Because target labels are unavailable, the system often cannot tell
whether adaptation is helping or hurting.

Most work asks how to design a better adapter. We ask whether to adapt at all.
Given a frozen baseline $f_0$, an adapted candidate $f_a$, and an unlabeled batch,
the system chooses \textbf{adapt} when adaptation is knowably helpful, \textbf{freeze}
when knowably harmful, or \textbf{abstain} when label-free evidence cannot identify
the sign of the benefit.

\emph{This is a fundamental limit, not an engineering gap.} Whether adaptation helps or
hurts is label-free identifiable \emph{if and only if} an observable margin exceeds the
calibration-drift budget on the disagreement region (our spine theorem). On the
unidentifiable side of this frontier, two target worlds carry identical label-free evidence
but opposite benefit, so no label-free rule---no matter how the adapter is built---can be
correct in both, and abstention is information-theoretically forced. Just as impossibility
results delimit what any distributed protocol or any learner can achieve, the benefit-sign
frontier delimits what \emph{any} label-free adaptation controller can know. K-Bound is the
constructive companion: when evidence is risk-aligned and the benefit estimate carries a
valid uncertainty radius, a finite-sample certificate adapts on the identifiable-helpful
side, freezes on the identifiable-harmful side, and abstains exactly where the frontier
makes knowing impossible---controlling false-adapt and false-freeze at level $\alpha$ under
exchangeable calibration.

We instantiate \textbf{one} method, \textsc{KGA} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:kga}): a gradient-boosted
benefit estimator $\widehat\Delta(Z)$ plus a conformal radius $\varepsilon$ fit on a labeled
calibration split, then adapt/freeze/abstain. The wrapper is mechanism-agnostic (Tent, EATA, SAR);
there is no separate multicandidate or alternate certificate in the headline experiments.
K-Bound does \emph{not} claim universal improvement: when adaptation is already safe,
a certificate can only tie always-adapt; its value is highest when harmful adaptation
is frequent, detectable, and costly.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A new problem framing.} We recast test-time adaptation as a label-free
\emph{adapt/freeze/abstain} decision---whether to adapt at all, not how to adapt.
\item \textbf{One exact result (the spine).} We prove the \emph{benefit-sign frontier}: the sign of
the adaptation benefit is label-free identifiable \emph{if and only if} an observable margin exceeds
the calibration-drift budget on the disagreement region (Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}). The ``only
if'' direction is an information-theoretic impossibility---matched evidence, opposite benefit
(Lemma~\ref{lem:nonid}); the ``if'' direction yields a finite-sample certificate with false-adapt
and false-freeze each $\le\alpha$.
\item \textbf{A drop-in method.} We instantiate the certificate as \textsc{KGA}, a
mechanism-agnostic wrapper around Tent/EATA/SAR (Algorithm~\ref{alg:kga}).
\item \textbf{Decision-level evidence.} On a nine-dataset panel---corruption (CIFAR-10-C, ImageNet-C), domain shift (Office-Home), three
WILDS natural shifts (Camelyon17, iWildCam, RxRx1), a domain-generalization suite (PACS), a rendition shift (ImageNet-R), and a
reproduction shift (CIFAR-10.1)---
\textsc{KGA} beats both fixed policies with bootstrap $95\%$ CIs excluding zero on the CIFAR-10-C
stress grid (ImageNet-C SAR corroboration), \textbf{beats POEM and AETTA} on the pre-registered mixed
head-to-head (Table~\ref{tab:headtohead-poem-aetta}), and is \emph{uniformly no-harm} on the held-out natural
shifts (Office-Home, iWildCam, Camelyon17, RxRx1): under a valid out-of-fold radius it beats the worse
fixed policy and ties the better at false-adapt $0\%$, with no natural-shift beats-both
(Table~\ref{tab:regime-summary}).
\end{enumerate}

Empirically, \textsc{KGA} follows the regime map: synthetic stress grids supply the policy
\emph{wins} (CIFAR-10-C, ImageNet-C SAR), where harmful adaptation is frequent and detectable; on
held-out real shifts \textsc{KGA} is \emph{uniformly no-harm}: under a valid out-of-fold radius it
beats the worse fixed policy and ties the better at false-adapt $\le\alpha$ (Office-Home, iWildCam,
Camelyon17, RxRx1), with no natural-shift beats-both; elsewhere
weak evidence correctly forces abstention or conservative freeze (Appendix).

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{TTA methods.}
Tent~\cite{wang2021tent}, SHOT~\cite{liang2020shot}, TTT~\cite{sun2020ttt}, EATA~\cite{niu2022eata},
SAR~\cite{niu2023sar}, CoTTA~\cite{wang2022cotta}, and MEMO~\cite{zhang2022memo} improve
robustness of adaptation but commit to adapting; none certifies the sign of benefit
\emph{before} updating or abstains on the adapt/freeze decision itself.

\paragraph{Guarded and monitored TTA.}
POEM~\cite{bar2024poem} and sequential risk monitors~\cite{schirmer2025monitoring,podkopaev2022tracking}
act during or after adaptation and control false alarms in one failure direction.
K-Bound decides \emph{before} committing, poses a three-way decision with an explicit
unknowable region, and controls false-\emph{adapt} on the benefit sign.

\paragraph{Label-free accuracy and error estimation.}
ATC~\cite{garg2022atc}, Agreement-on-the-Line~\cite{baek2022aol,miller2021aol}, and
AETTA~\cite{lee2024aetta} estimate target performance without labels. Most directly,
\cite{kim2024taol} applies agreement-on-the-line to decide, at the \emph{population}
level, when TTA helps or hurts and to select an adaptation strategy; it issues no
per-deployment certificate, no \textsc{abstain} action, and no identifiability limit, and its
linear trend is known to break on shifts (e.g.\ Gaussian-noise corruption)---precisely the
regime where \textsc{K-Bound} abstains. Estimating
$R_T(f)$ differs from certifying $\sign(R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a))$ before committing the candidate update,
with controlled false-adapt rate. Thus, K-Bound is not an accuracy estimator used as a gate; it
changes the target estimand from target accuracy to the sign of the frozen-versus-adapted
risk difference, with abstention when that sign is not identifiable.

\paragraph{Identifiability, conformal inference, and selective prediction.}
Unsupervised risk estimation~\cite{steinhardt2016,bendavid2010theory,rosenfeld2023dis2}
and selective prediction~\cite{geifman2017selective} show performance is identifiable
only under structure. K-Bound differs by certifying the \emph{sign} of adaptation benefit
before committing, with an explicit abstain region and false-adapt control at level $\alpha$.

\section{Problem setup and theory}\label{sec:setup}\label{sec:theory}

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/fig_frontier_schematic.png}
\caption{\textbf{The benefit-sign frontier.} The sign of the adaptation benefit is identifiable from
label-free evidence iff the observable margin $|\widehat{M}|$ exceeds the calibration-drift budget
$\beta$. Outside the band KGA \textsc{adapt}s (knowably helpful) or \textsc{freeze}s (knowably harmful);
inside the band---the unknowable region---abstention is information-theoretically forced.}
\label{fig:frontier}
\end{figure}

Source $P_S(X,Y)$ has labels at train/validation; target $P_T(X,Y)$ exposes only $P_T(X)$
at test time. Fix loss $\ell$, frozen $f_0$, and candidate $f_a$. Target risk
$R_T(f)=\E_{P_T}[\ell(f(X),Y)]$. The \emph{adaptation benefit} is
\begin{equation}
\Delta \;=\; R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a),\qquad \Delta>0 \iff \text{adapting helps.}
\end{equation}
\emph{Observable evidence} $Z=\phi(X_{1:m},f_0,f_a)$ is any statistic computable without
target labels (entropy, confidence, drift, disagreement, update norms). The central
question is when $\sign\Delta$ is recoverable from $Z$ alone. The certificate needs two \emph{separate}
requirements. \textbf{(i) Risk-alignment} is a property of $Z$ and the class: $\sign\Delta$ is a
measurable function of the population law of $Z$ over the declared class---equivalently, no two
admissible worlds with opposite benefit sign induce the \emph{same} $Z$-law. \textbf{(ii) Coverage}
is a property of the calibrator: the benefit interval
$[\widehat\Delta-\varepsilon,\widehat\Delta+\varepsilon]$ has marginal coverage $\ge1-\alpha$.
Risk-alignment makes $\sign\Delta$ recoverable in principle; coverage makes the finite-sample
certificate valid. When $Z$ is \emph{not} risk-aligned, no label-free rule can recover $\sign\Delta$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:nonid}) and the certificate must abstain.

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
\textbf{What ``label-free'' means.} No target/test labels at deploy; labeled source and
held-out validation may calibrate $\widehat\Delta$ and $\varepsilon$. Evidence $Z$ uses only
unlabeled batches, predictions, entropy, drift, and update statistics. The method is
\textbf{target-label-free}, not label-free of all supervision: labels may be used only in the
source/validation calibration split, never in the deployment batch or held-out test decision.}}
\end{center}

\begin{definition}[Regimes]
\emph{Knowably helpful}: evidence certifies $\Delta>0$ (adapt).
\emph{Knowably harmful}: evidence certifies $\Delta<0$ (freeze).
\emph{Unknowable}: the same $Z$ is compatible with both signs (abstain).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Non-identifiability forces abstention]\label{lem:nonid}
There exist worlds $P_T^1,P_T^2$ with $\Law(Z\mid P_T^1)=\Law(Z\mid P_T^2)$ but
$\sign\Delta_1=-\sign\Delta_2\neq0$. No label-free rule can be correct in both; any
valid rule must abstain on matched evidence with probability at least $1-2\alpha$
when false-adapt and false-freeze are each controlled at $\alpha$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[K-Bound certificate and benefit-sign frontier]\label{thm:headline}
Suppose $\widehat\Delta(z)$ satisfies $\Pr[|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon]\ge1-\alpha$.
The rule
\[
g(Z)=\begin{cases}
\textsc{adapt} & \widehat\Delta-\varepsilon>0,\\
\textsc{freeze} & \widehat\Delta+\varepsilon<0,\\
\textsc{abstain} & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
controls the \emph{marginal, unconditional} false-adapt and false-freeze probabilities at level
$\alpha$---$\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}=\Pr[\text{adapt},\,\Delta\le0]\le\alpha$ under the conformal coverage
assumption (\S\ref{sec:method}; the conditional rate $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}$ is \emph{not}
$\alpha$-bounded and is reported empirically).
On the disagreement region $D=\{x:f_0(x)\neq f_a(x)\}$, write
$M=\E[s\mid D]-\tfrac12$ for a source-calibrated score $s$ of $f_a$ and
$\gamma=\E[\eta_a-s\mid D]$ for the \emph{(unobserved)} calibration drift, so that
$\sign\Delta=\sign(M+\gamma)$. Over a declared target class
$\mathcal C_\beta=\{P_T:|\gamma|\le\beta\}$ with a supplied drift budget $\beta\ge0$, the
benefit sign is label-free identifiable \emph{if and only if} $|M|>\beta$ (exact frontier).
Here $\beta$ is the externally justified worst-case drift the deployment class admits,
$\gamma$ is the unknown population-drift realization it bounds, and the empirical radius
$\varepsilon$ that \textsc{KGA} uses is an operational uncertainty radius calibrated from held-out
residuals---under the stated exchangeability and risk-alignment assumptions it provides
finite-sample coverage for the deployment class, but it is \emph{not} a direct estimate of the
worst-case drift $\beta$.
Intuitively, the decision is safe when the observable margin clears the admitted drift and
impossible when drift can dominate it---the obstruction no checkable assumption removes in
full generality (Lemma~\ref{lem:nonid}; full dichotomy: Appendix~\ref{app:theory-full}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Unjustified assumptions $\Rightarrow$ abstain]\label{cor:abstain-valid}
The certificate's false-adapt guarantee holds only under the stated coverage/exchangeability and
risk-alignment assumptions. When those assumptions cannot be justified for the deployment batch
(e.g.\ the evidence is not risk-aligned, or the calibration sample is not exchangeable with
deployment), the guarantee does not apply, and the conservative operational action is to abstain or
freeze rather than commit to adaptation: committing adapt without a valid positive certified lower
bound is not protected by the target false-adapt level $\alpha$. (At deployment $\Delta$ is
unobservable, so this is a statement about \emph{assumptions}, not about observing
$|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|$.)
\end{corollary}

Full proofs, rate theorems, one-bit dichotomy, and validators are in
Appendix~\ref{app:theory-full}. One previously open piece is now settled: the weakest label-free
class on which one declared bit certifies the benefit sign is characterized \emph{unconditionally} as
an explicit finite family of dominance polytopes (Appendix~\ref{app:weakest},
Theorem~\ref{thm:uncond-weakest}), with General Position recovered as the collapsing face. Other
pieces of the knowability program---tight evidence-channel rates and the general-capacity
characterization---remain open.

\section{Method: \textsc{KGA}}\label{sec:method}
\textsc{KGA} is the only headline method: a decision wrapper around a fixed adapter, not a new
adaptation objective. We fit the benefit estimator and its radius \emph{out of fold}, so the data
used to fit $\widehat\Delta$ and the data used to calibrate its residual are disjoint for every point.
On the per-cell stress grids (CIFAR-10-C and the synthetic grids): for each cell $i$ a gradient-boosted
$\widehat\Delta_{-i}$ is trained on the other $N{-}1$ cells and evaluated on cell $i$, and
$\varepsilon$ is the $(1-\alpha)$ empirical quantile of the leave-one-out residuals
$\{|\widehat\Delta_{-i}(Z_i)-\Delta_i|\}_{i=1}^{N}$---leave-one-out (jackknife) cross-conformal, so no
cell's residual uses an estimator that saw it. On the natural-shift protocols (Camelyon17, iWildCam,
Office-Home) the split is across domains: $\widehat\Delta$ and $\varepsilon$ are fit once on a dev
split and the held-out test domain is scored a single time---ordinary split-conformal. At deploy we
extract $Z$ from an unlabeled batch and return adapt, freeze, or abstain (Algorithm~\ref{alg:kga}).
Labels enter only through the calibration split; the deployment batch and held-out test scorer never
use target labels for the adapter, $\varepsilon$, evidence, or rule selection. The coverage
guarantee is split-dependent. The natural-shift protocols use a clean dev/test split:
\emph{split-conformal} coverage $\Pr[|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon]\ge1-\alpha$, exact in
finite samples under exchangeability of the calibration and test cells. The per-cell grids set
$\varepsilon$ to the $(1-\alpha)$ empirical quantile of the leave-one-out residuals, so the
\emph{calibration-set} coverage is $\ge1-\alpha$ by construction (realized $0.898$ at nominal
$0.90$, $\alpha{=}0.10$)---the standard jackknife radius, which is distribution-free only
asymptotically under estimator stability; its formal finite-sample counterpart is the jackknife$+$
of Barber et al.\ (2021) at level $1-2\alpha$, which we note but do not implement. The decision
rule's false-adapt guarantee is stated under the coverage hypothesis (\S\ref{sec:method}), which the
empirical coverage meets.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\textsc{KGA}: Knowability-Guided Adaptation}
\label{alg:kga}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Frozen $f_0$; adapter $\mathcal{A}$; batch $X_{1:m}$; $\widehat\Delta$, $\varepsilon$, $\alpha$
\Ensure Decision $g\in\{\textsc{Adapt},\textsc{Freeze},\textsc{Abstain}\}$
\State $f_a \leftarrow \mathcal{A}(f_0, X_{1:m})$
\State $Z \leftarrow \phi(X_{1:m}, f_0, f_a)$
\State $\widehat\Delta \leftarrow \widehat\Delta(Z)$;\;
  $L \leftarrow \widehat\Delta-\varepsilon$;\; $U \leftarrow \widehat\Delta+\varepsilon$
\If{$L>0$} \State $g\leftarrow\textsc{Adapt}$
\ElsIf{$U<0$} \State $g\leftarrow\textsc{Freeze}$
\Else \State $g\leftarrow\textsc{Abstain}$
\EndIf
\State \Return $g$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

On the good event $|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon$, committing adapt only when
$L>0$ implies $\Delta>0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}). Assumptions: exchangeable
calibration pairs, bounded benefits, and risk-aligned $Z$; no target labels at deploy.
Overhead: one forward pass of $f_0$ and $f_a$ plus lightweight $\widehat\Delta$ evaluation.

\paragraph{The threshold is derived, not tuned.}
\textsc{KGA}'s commit boundary is not a swept hyperparameter, but the population frontier and the
empirical radius must be kept distinct. The \emph{population} frontier of
Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}---over the declared class $\mathcal C_\beta$, $\sign\Delta$ is
identifiable iff the disagreement margin $|M|$ exceeds the supplied budget $\beta$---fixes the
\emph{required} budget at population level; the drift realization $\gamma\le\beta$ is unknown and
not observed at deploy. \textsc{KGA} does not
observe $\gamma$; it commits under a \emph{conservative empirical radius} $\varepsilon$, the
$(1-\alpha)$ residual quantile on the calibration split, fixed there and \emph{never} selected on
target-test data. The two are related but not identical: $\varepsilon$ is a finite-sample,
assumption-dependent surrogate that controls false-adapt under the stated exchangeability and
risk-alignment assumptions---not the frontier itself. Confidence-thresholding (ATC) and
agreement-trend selection (AGL) fit a scalar against a labeled or held-out signal and
predict \emph{accuracy}; \textsc{KGA} certifies the benefit \emph{sign} with a boundary
\emph{forced} by the identifiability budget, and---unlike any thresholded estimator---%
\textsc{abstains} on the region where Lemma~\ref{lem:nonid} \emph{proves} no label-free
rule can decide. The impossibility construction, not a tuned cutoff, is what separates
\textsc{KGA} from ``estimate accuracy, then threshold.''

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}
We validate \textsc{KGA} on two controlled corruption/stress tracks (CIFAR-10-C stress,
ImageNet-C SAR) and three held-out natural-shift replications (Protocols G, H v2, and~M v2;
Table~\ref{tab:regime-summary}). Calibration and held-out scoring follow
Algorithm~\ref{alg:calib-eval} (Appendix~\ref{app:calib-eval}). Metrics: mean accuracy; regret-to-oracle against the per-condition oracle
$g^\star=\mathbf1[\Delta>0]$; \emph{adapt rate} $\Pr[\text{adapt}]$ and \emph{coverage}
$\Pr[\text{adapt or freeze}]$ (one minus the abstain rate); and two false-adapt measures, reported
separately---the \emph{unconditional} false-adapt probability
$\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}=\Pr[\text{adapt and }\Delta\le0]$ and the \emph{conditional} false-adapt
rate $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}=\Pr[\Delta\le0\mid\text{adapt}]$. Theorem~\ref{thm:headline} bounds the
\emph{unconditional} probability, $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}\le\alpha$, under its coverage and
risk-alignment assumptions; the per-dataset tables additionally list the empirical
$\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}$ among committed adapt decisions. The two false-adapt measures coincide
whenever no harmful adapt is committed ($\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}{=}\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}{=}0$ on
every headline dataset, since \textsc{KGA} commits none) and diverge only for rules that commit some;
the full four-metric breakdown---$\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}$, $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}$, adapt-rate, and
coverage---where the leaky gates separate from the certificate is therefore reported side by side in
the decision-baseline comparison (Table~\ref{tab:gates}), so the reader sees both what the theorem
bounds and what happens operationally among adapted decisions.

\subsection{CIFAR-10-C stress grid (5 seeds)}
The per-corruption suite is helpful-dominated; the \emph{stress} grid crosses severity,
batch size, composition, and update aggressiveness ($432$ conditions/method, ResNet-18,
pre-registered Protocol~A, seeds $0$--$4$). Harmful base rates are $34\%$ (Tent),
$27\%$ (EATA), $16\%$ (SAR). \textsc{KGA} \textbf{beats both} trivial policies for
Tent and EATA at $0\%$ false-adapt; it ties collapse-resistant SAR
(Table~\ref{tab:decisive}). Among harmful cells \textsc{KGA} adapts $0$ and freezes them;
among helpful cells it adapts almost all; on marginal cells it abstains. The regret-gap $95\%$ design-based bootstrap CIs (described below) exclude zero for both candidates---Tent (vs always-adapt
$+0.019\,[0.011,0.026]$, vs always-freeze $+0.080\,[0.052,0.107]$) and EATA (vs always-adapt
$+0.015\,[0.009,0.021]$, vs always-freeze $+0.075\,[0.049,0.100]$)---with Holm correction over the
comparison family and false-adapt $0/2160$. (These are \emph{design-based} intervals: they resample the pre-registered mixing-ratio distribution
of Protocol~A---the $11$-point Pareto curve over harmful fraction, $N_{\mathrm{boot}}{=}5000$---so they
quantify uncertainty over the deployment mixture. A conventional paired \emph{per-condition} bootstrap
that resamples the $432$ conditions at their realized composition
(\texttt{scripts/percondition\_bootstrap.py}) returns the same verdict: the regret gap to both fixed
policies excludes zero for Tent and EATA (beats-both), while helpful-dominated SAR does not improve on
always-adapt and beats always-freeze---all at $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}{=}0$.) The mixing-ratio Pareto (Fig.~\ref{fig:decisive}) shows \textsc{KGA} on the
regret-vs-harmful-fraction front, strictly beating both once the harmful fraction exceeds
$p^\star{\approx}0.1$ (Tent/SAR) or $0$ (EATA).

\paragraph{Cross-protocol aggregate (not a universal mixed deployment).}
Each single dataset is one-sided, so \textsc{KGA}'s simultaneous margin over \emph{both} policies is
necessarily small there. To evaluate the policy benefit of per-condition adapt/freeze/abstain
routing across heterogeneous shift regimes, we \emph{aggregate three independently dev-calibrated
held-out deployment protocols}: OfficeHome ($35$), iWildCam ($72$), and Camelyon17 ($36$ genuine OOD
\texttt{test}/\texttt{val} cells; in-distribution \texttt{id\_val} excluded to avoid the Protocol-G
pooling artifact), for $n{=}143$ conditions. This is a \emph{cross-protocol aggregate}, \emph{not}
one universal gate calibrated once and transferred across datasets: each dataset keeps its own
locked dev-calibrated gate, and we make no cross-dataset transfer claim. (Camelyon17 contributes its
$36$ OOD \texttt{test}+\texttt{val} cells here, whereas the single-dataset headline of
Table~\ref{tab:camelyon17-G} uses the $n{=}18$ OOD \texttt{test} split alone; both exclude
in-distribution \texttt{id\_val}.) Because this aggregate genuinely mixes helpful conditions
(Camelyon) with harmful ones (Office-Home, iWildCam), it is the one setting where per-condition
routing can in principle beat both fixed policies. The earlier figures (regret $0.0026$;
${\sim}24\times$ vs always-adapt, ${\sim}13\times$ vs always-freeze) used an in-sample conformal
radius and are \textbf{withdrawn}. The corrected out-of-fold re-run
(\texttt{mixed\_protocol\_oof\_v2}) yields $\mathrm{regret}_{\textsc{kga}}{=}0.0059$ vs.\
$0.0632$ (always-adapt) and $0.0342$ (always-freeze), with regret gaps vs.\ always-freeze
$+0.0283$ $[0.019,\,0.038]$ and vs.\ always-adapt $+0.0573$ $[0.043,\,0.072]$ (both CIs exclude
zero; pooled false-adapt $0$). This is a \textbf{beats-both} on a \emph{constructed}
cross-protocol aggregate---not a universal mixed-deployment win and not a natural-shift headline.

\section{Limitations and honest scope}\label{sec:limits}

\medskip
\textbf{Scope of empirical wins.} On the stress grid \textsc{KGA} beats both for Tent/EATA
and ties SAR (Table~\ref{tab:decisive}); on ImageNet-C the pattern flips by candidate---Tent/EATA
are robust while SAR collapses and \textsc{KGA} beats both under a mechanism-faithful re-run
(Table~\ref{tab:imagenetc-faithful}). Per-corruption CIFAR-10-C and helpful natural shifts are
insurance regimes, not policy wins.

\textbf{Real-shift scope.} Under a valid out-of-fold conformal radius, no natural shift is a
beats-both: Office-Home and iWildCam are \emph{no-harm} (they beat always-adapt and tie always-freeze;
Tables~\ref{tab:officehome-M}, \ref{tab:iwildcam-H}), so the beats-both wins are on the synthetic
stress grids (CIFAR-10-C, ImageNet-C SAR). Camelyon17 Protocol~G is reclassified to an
honest \emph{no-harm} result---its earlier $n{=}54$ beats-both pooled in-distribution
\texttt{id\_val} into the held-out set and is withdrawn (Table~\ref{tab:camelyon17-G}). Earlier
Camelyon Protocol~B/F runs and multicandidate routes are \textbf{diagnostics}, not alternate
headline wins.

\textbf{Theory and calibration.} The conformal radius assumes exchangeable calibration pairs;
sign bracketing without structure is impossible (Theorem~\ref{thm:headline} and
Appendix~\ref{app:theory-full}). \textsc{KGA}'s value scales with how often catastrophic,
detectable harm occurs---not with universal accuracy gains. On helpful-dominated shifts the
resulting near-\emph{tie} with always-adapt reflects the certificate abstaining rather than
committing harmful updates---\textsc{KGA} approximately matches the better policy, at most paying a
small abstention cost when it declines a helpful but uncertifiable update---so the value proposition
is conditional, like insurance against detectable harm.

\textbf{Detectability and transfer.} ImageNet-R still sits in a weak, split-specific
evidence regime (Appendix~\ref{sec:excluded-wins})---an instance of the \emph{unknowable}
region Theorem~\ref{thm:headline} predicts, where the label-free margin falls below the
calibration-drift budget; \textsc{KGA} correctly declines to commit, so this is the theory
operating as designed, not a tuning failure, and richer label-free evidence is the natural
future lever. iWildCam H v2 clears the certificate bar
despite modest margin over always-freeze; intermediate-domain multicandidate routes do not
(pooled false-adapt $\gg\alpha$).

\textbf{The limitations are the frontier, not flaws.} The regime dependencies above are not three
independent weaknesses but one phenomenon---the benefit-sign frontier (Theorem~\ref{thm:headline})
seen from the operating side. Tying always-adapt on a helpful-dominated shift is the no-harm
guarantee \emph{holding}: one cannot beat an oracle that is already always-adapt. Declining
ImageNet-R is the \emph{unknowable} branch firing, where the observable margin falls below the drift
budget and \emph{no} label-free rule---however good the adapter---can decide the benefit sign
(Lemma~\ref{lem:nonid}). The coverage drop is mandatory abstention, not a tuning loss: committing in
the unknowable region would be a false-adapt. None can be removed without violating the impossibility
result---a method that ``won everywhere'' would be claiming to decide the sign of an unidentifiable
quantity. The one axis that genuinely improves all three is a \emph{richer label-free evidence map},
which raises the observable margin $|M|$ so more shifts clear the budget (more knowable shifts, fewer
abstentions, higher coverage), together with a \emph{tighter radius} (jackknife$+$ or more
calibration data) that reclaims coverage on knowable cells abstained only out of conservatism---both
bounded by the frontier they cannot cross.

\textbf{Missing baselines and independent validation.} ImageNet-C committal gates
(ATC-style confidence; Appendix~\ref{app:frontier}, Table~\ref{tab:app-imagenetc}) false-adapt at
$100\%$ on harmful SAR cells, while \textsc{KGA} abstains there. The pre-registered mixed
head-to-head vs.\ POEM/AETTA (Table~\ref{tab:headtohead-poem-aetta}) is complete; an optional
official-repo arm (per-sample POEM, dropout-AETTA) remains a camera-ready faithfulness check.
Independent replication of ImageNet-R and Office-Home M v2 across more seeds would strengthen
stability claims. Abstention lowers \emph{decision} coverage by construction but never withholds a prediction---an \textsc{abstain} keeps the safe default $f_0$ (the source model), so every input still receives an output, and the coverage/false-adapt trade-off is tunable via~$\alpha$.

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
\textbf{What we do not claim.} Universal accuracy gains; adapter selection by test regret;
multicandidate routes with violated false-adapt bounds; or wins on helpful-dominated shifts
where always-adapt is already oracle-optimal.}}
\end{center}

\section{Conclusion}
K-Bound reframes test-time adaptation as an identifiability-limited decision problem: adapt
when helpful, freeze when harmful, and abstain when label-free evidence cannot support a valid
decision. The theory shows that this abstention region is not merely conservative; under matched
label-free evidence with opposite adaptation benefit, no valid label-free rule can safely commit.
The empirical results support the same regime map: \textsc{KGA} ties or trails only where
always-adapt is already near-oracle, but improves over trivial policies when harmful adaptation
is frequent, detectable, and costly. These results suggest that future TTA systems should expose
not only an adaptation objective, but also a validity layer that decides whether adaptation is
knowable before deployment.
