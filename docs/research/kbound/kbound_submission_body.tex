\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:compact-intro}

Test-time adaptation changes a model using unlabeled deployment data. Entropy minimization,
filtering, regularization, and related methods can recover accuracy under shift. The same update can
also make predictions worse~\cite{wang2021tent,niu2022eata,niu2023sar,wang2022cotta}. The central
deployment question is therefore directional action correctness: an adapter supplies a candidate,
not a reason to deploy it.

Our primary research question is:
\emph{When can evidence available without new target labels justify replacing a frozen predictor
with a fixed adapted candidate?}
Let $f_0$ be the frozen predictor and $f_a$ the candidate. Their population benefit is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta=R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a).
  \label{eq:compact-benefit}
\end{equation}
A positive value favors the update; a negative value favors the frozen model. We use three
decisions. \adapt asserts positive benefit, \freeze asserts negative benefit, and \abstain asserts
neither. Both non-adapt decisions serve $f_0$, but only \freeze makes a directional claim.

K-Bound treats this as partial identification. In a binary model under $0/1$ loss, we fix the input
law, the predictors, and every label-free observable. We then vary the target label kernel within a
declared class. The compatible benefits give an exact frontier for uniform strict decisions. The
class is indexed by a bound on a hidden target calibration residual. That bound is an assumption,
not a quantity supplied by an unlabeled batch. A second theorem shows that an audit using the same
evidence must cover the residual range that the evidence leaves unresolved.

Knowability-Guided Adaptation (KGA) is the empirical companion. It predicts a model pair's measured
benefit on an evaluation cell. It acts only when a residual-calibrated interval excludes zero. KGA
is label-free at the scored decision, not during development and calibration: labeled development
cells train the predictor, and disjoint residual-calibration cells calibrate its errors. No new cell
outcome is read before its decision is fixed.
KGA neither estimates the population residual bound nor evaluates the
population frontier numerically. Section~\ref{sec:compact-bridge} connects the decision logic while
keeping the guarantees separate.

\subsection{Three Scientific Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{An exact benefit frontier on a declared class.}
  We construct target label laws with identical label-free evidence, derive their feasible benefit
  interval, and characterize its strict positive, strict negative, and unresolved regions,
  including the zero-benefit boundary.
  \item \textbf{An evidence-fibre audit limit.}
  We identify the least residual bound that the same observable law can support uniformly,
  explaining why historical labels help only through a stated link to the deployment domain.
  \item \textbf{An empirical paired-benefit gate.}
  We give a cell-outcome-disjoint three-way cross-fit for the controlled grids and evaluate its
  ADAPT/FREEZE/ABSTAIN behavior on constructed shifts and its retention behavior on natural shifts.
  We report action exposure and false-action denominators before secondary accuracy and regret consequences.
  The standard coverage-to-action implication states exactly what follows if the interval covers
  its declared target.
\end{enumerate}

The opened CIFAR-10-C diagnostic first asks whether each committed direction was correct and reports
the corresponding action exposure. Tent makes two false ADAPT actions and one false FREEZE; EATA
makes neither; SAR makes no FREEZE decisions. The secondary utility result is candidate-specific:
Tent and EATA have lower point regret than either fixed policy, whereas SAR favors always-adapt.
CCT-20 instead preserves frozen-model utility in all 45 cells with no ADAPT exposure. This is
all-frozen fail-closed service, not selective routing on an unseen natural shift. The supplement
retains adverse, incomplete, and withheld studies so that this boundary does not depend on removing
unfavorable evidence.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:compact-related}

\subsection{Candidate Adaptation and Update Selection}

Tent, EATA, SAR, CoTTA, MEMO, SHOT, and test-time training construct candidates through entropy
objectives, filtering, regularization, temporal stabilization, augmentation, or auxiliary tasks
~\cite{wang2021tent,niu2022eata,niu2023sar,wang2022cotta,zhang2022memo,liang2020shot,sun2020ttt}.
Other methods decide where or when to update. TTSA selects modality-specific adapters
~\cite{chen2025ttsa}. GALA selects layers and filters samples by gradient alignment
~\cite{sahoo2025gala}. Informed Adaptation learns topological features associated with useful
updates~\cite{mutlu2026informed}. KGA starts later in this pipeline: it compares one realized
candidate with its frozen baseline. Learned routing and rollback are established ideas, not our
novelty claim.

\subsection{Label-Free Performance and Strategy Estimation}

ATC, agreement-on-the-line, AETTA, and prediction-matrix statistics estimate performance from
unlabeled outputs and suitable development information
~\cite{garg2022atc,baek2022aol,miller2021aol,lee2024aetta,deng2023confidence,xie2024mano}.
The closest empirical comparator is Kim et al.'s NeurIPS 2024 work,
\emph{Test-Time Adaptation Induces Stronger Accuracy and Agreement-on-the-Line}
~\cite{kim2024ttaline}. It uses agreement-based ALine estimators after adaptation, supports
hyperparameter selection, and compares adaptation strategies without labeled OOD data.

KGA fits the paired observed benefit of one frozen/candidate pair and makes a three-way interval
decision. The distinction is the fitted target and decision procedure. It does not imply that
separate accuracy estimates cannot be differenced. A synchronized current-policy comparison with
the published TTA/ALine procedure and AETTA remains missing.

\subsection{Monitoring and Adaptation Controllers}

POEM protects adaptation through entropy matching and online betting~\cite{bar2024poem}.
Schirmer et al. monitor an adapting model's running risk against a source-risk reference under a
proxy-informativeness condition~\cite{schirmer2025monitoring}. The supervised precursor uses a
labeled test stream~\cite{podkopaev2022tracking}. Drift-to-Action Controllers use delayed labels
and online certificates to gate adaptation, abstention, rollback, and retraining
~\cite{lamaakal2026drifttoaction}. These methods differ in target, label access, and sequential
guarantee. KGA evaluates one paired benefit and does not establish an anytime-valid risk guarantee.

\subsection{Partial Identification and Robust Decisions}

An identified set contains the values compatible with the observations and assumptions. Decision
theory under partial identification studies actions when that set leaves payoffs ambiguous.
Christensen et al., for example, combine worst-case payoff uncertainty with statistical learning of
identified parameters~\cite{christensen2026optimal}. K-Bound asks the narrower question of whether
one strict benefit sign is uniform over the set. It neither solves the general optimal-decision
problem nor claims interval thresholding as a new idea.

Indistinguishable problems are already central to domain-adaptation impossibility
~\cite{bendavid2010theory,bendavid2010impossibility}. Our contribution is a
disagreement-conditional label-kernel construction, the resulting clipped benefit set, and its
residual-audit floor. The proof does not introduce label-free impossibility.
Risk-estimation assumptions based on conditional independence~\cite{steinhardt2016}, label shift
~\cite{lipton2018bbse,garg2020labelshift}, or disagreement discrepancy~\cite{rosenfeld2023dis2}
restrict the compatible laws. Likewise, MMD-credal risk intervals impose RKHS and covariate-shift
structure~\cite{ariq2026mmd}. Each restriction can shrink the identified set. A converse theorem
for a richer class does not contradict those results.

\subsection{Marginal and Conditional Coverage}

Selective classification and reject-option learning abstain on individual predictions
~\cite{geifman2017selective,chow1970reject,cortes2016boosting}. KGA instead abstains on an update.
Its coverage-to-action proposition uses standard interval logic
~\cite{tibshirani2019conformal,barber2023beyond,bates2021rcps,angelopoulos2025ltt}. The statistical
burden is to justify coverage for the intended unit, especially after selection or under shift.

Jin and Ren's JOMI procedure targets coverage conditional on selection, assuming exchangeability
and a compatible selection rule~\cite{jin2025selection}. Gibbs et al. obtain conditional guarantees
over specified shift classes through a different calibration construction
~\cite{gibbs2025conditional}. KGA's ordinary residual interval implements neither method. Its
marginal false-adapt implication must therefore remain separate from error conditional on ADAPT and
from coverage within each target environment. Learn Then Test addresses the separate problem of
configuration selection and multiplicity~\cite{angelopoulos2025ltt}.

\subsection{Model Pair, Evidence, and Evaluation Unit}

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P_T)$ support a target draw $(X,Y)$, and let $P_S$ denote the source law.
For loss $\ell$, define
\[
  R_T(f)=\mathbb E_{P_T}[\ell(f(X),Y)].
\]
We condition on a realized pair of measurable predictors. The frozen predictor is $f_0$; an
adapter $\mathcal A$ uses an unlabeled batch $X_{1:m}$ to construct the fixed candidate
$f_a=\mathcal A(f_0,X_{1:m})$. Population risk then integrates a fresh target draw while holding
this pair fixed.

The gate may read label-free evidence
\[
  Z=\phi(X_{1:m},f_0,f_a),
\]
such as predictions, logits, and update statistics. It may not read the outcome of the cell it is
about to score.

An \emph{evaluation cell} $j$ is a predeclared collection of records $\mathcal E_j$ on which both
models are scored. For a fixed higher-is-better score $S$, its measured benefit is
\[
  \Delta_j^{\mathrm{cell}}=S(f_a;\mathcal E_j)-S(f_0;\mathcal E_j).
\]
This is not the population benefit $\Delta$. For accuracy it is an average of paired correctness
differences. For macro-F1 it is a difference between two cell-level scores, not an average of
per-example losses. We therefore report metric scales separately.

\subsection{Actions and Their Operational Meaning}

\begin{definition}[Decision semantics]
The gate $g$ maps its permitted evidence and frozen calibration artifacts to
$\{\adapt,\freeze,\abstain\}$. \adapt commits $f_a$ when an interval supports positive benefit.
\freeze rejects $f_a$ when an interval supports negative benefit. \abstain retains $f_0$ when
neither strict direction is supported or the assessment is unavailable.
\end{definition}

Freeze is a supported rejection; abstention is an unresolved assessment. An operator may attach
monitoring, label acquisition, or review to an abstention. The experiments here evaluate only the
frozen fallback and uncertainty record, not those additional interventions. If evidence is missing,
retaining $f_0$ must be logged as ABSTAIN, not as an interval-supported FREEZE.

The observed-cell oracle chooses the model with the larger score. Regret is the policy's score
shortfall from that oracle, averaged over the named cells with the stated weights. Always-freeze
regret is $\max(\Delta_j^{\mathrm{cell}},0)$; always-adapt regret is
$\max(-\Delta_j^{\mathrm{cell}},0)$. KGA serves the candidate only after ADAPT. Lower regret is
better. A zero-benefit tie has zero regret for either model but supports neither strict sign.

\subsection{Commitment, Interval Coverage, and Error}

Let $J$ be a random evaluation unit drawn from a predeclared deployment-unit distribution $Q$.
The common probability space includes fitting and calibration randomness, candidate construction,
$J$, and the records in $\mathcal E_J$. Write
$\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}=\Delta_J^{\mathrm{cell}}$ and $g=g_J$. The probabilities below are
prospective marginal quantities under that joint law. When we instead summarize a finite recorded
grid, we use the equal-weight empirical measure over its cells and call the result descriptive. It
does not estimate the prospective law without an additional sampling design.

With this law and the gate fixed, we distinguish three measurements:
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{Commit}
    &=\Pr(g\in\{\adapt,\freeze\}), \label{eq:commitment-rate}\\
  \mathrm{Cov}_{\mathrm{cell}}
    &=\Pr(|\widehat\Delta-\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}|\le\varepsilon), \label{eq:interval-coverage}\\
  \mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}
    &=\Pr(g=\adapt,\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}\le0). \label{eq:compact-fau}
\end{align}
Conditional false adaptation is
$\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}=\Pr(\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}\le0\mid g=\adapt)$, defined only when
$\Pr(g=\adapt)>0$. The operational proposition below bounds only the marginal error, and only when
the stated interval coverage premise holds. It does not bound $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}$. A gate that
never adapts has zero false adaptations mechanically, so every error report includes action
exposure. A false FREEZE is defined analogously by $g=\freeze$ and
$\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}\ge0$. Its conditional rate is undefined when no FREEZE action occurs.

The population theorem uses binary labels and $0/1$ loss. The empirical gate can target any
predeclared scalar score difference, including a multiclass metric. That interface does not extend
the binary theorem. Under multiclass $0/1$ loss,
$\Delta=\Pr_T(D)(p_a-p_0)$, with $p_a=\Pr_T(f_a=Y\mid D)$ and
$p_0=\Pr_T(f_0=Y\mid D)$; only the binary setting forces $p_0=1-p_a$.

\paragraph{The six-symbol map.}
Before turning to the formal results, it is useful to keep six quantities in view. $M$ is the
population-side disagreement margin: the visible signal that favors one predictor before target
calibration error is considered. $\gamma$ is the hidden target calibration residual, and $\beta$
is an externally declared bound on its magnitude. Together, $M$, $\gamma$, and $\beta$ determine
which directions can be certified at the population level. Its target is
$\Delta=R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a)$, the expected risk reduction from committing the candidate. Deployment
uses the realized analogue $\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$. Because that cell's labels are unavailable at
decision time, KGA predicts its benefit as $\widehat\Delta$ and attaches the uncertainty radius
$\varepsilon$. Thus the population theory brackets $\Delta$ through $(M,\gamma,\beta)$, whereas the
finite-sample procedure brackets $\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$ through
$(\widehat\Delta,\varepsilon)$.

\noindent\textbf{Population box.}
\[
  \boxed{(M,\gamma,\beta)\longrightarrow\Delta}
\]
\emph{Question:} Across all allowed target worlds, can the sign of benefit change?

\noindent\textbf{Deployment box.}
\[
  \boxed{Z\longrightarrow\widehat\Delta\mathbin{\pm}\varepsilon
  \longrightarrow\{\adapt,\freeze,\abstain\}}
\]
\emph{Question:} For this deployment unit, is estimated benefit sufficiently separated from zero?

The conceptual bridge between the two boxes is benefit, not an equality of estimands:
\[
  \begin{gathered}
  \boxed{\Delta\ \text{(population)}}\\[-2pt]
  \updownarrow\\[-2pt]
  \boxed{\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}\ \text{(deployment unit)}}
  \end{gathered}
\]
Their uncertainty objects are not interchangeable. $\beta$ bounds a population residual by
assumption. $\varepsilon$ quantifies empirical prediction error under a calibration protocol.
Sections~\ref{sec:compact-theory}--\ref{sec:compact-budget} develop the population box.
Section~\ref{sec:compact-kga} develops the deployment box.

\section{Population Partial-Identification Frontier}
\label{sec:compact-theory}

An adaptation algorithm has already proposed the candidate $f_a$. The decision depends on one
unknown sign: the sign of $\Delta$. Target labels hide that sign. This section asks whether the
permitted label-free evidence, together with a declared calibration budget, rules out one
direction. $M$ supplies the visible direction, $\gamma$ records what labels still hide, and
$\beta$ limits how large that hidden term may be. We first connect these objects to $\Delta$.
We then identify exactly where the sign is knowable and where abstention is unavoidable.

\subsection{Disagreement-Region Model}

The two predictors can differ in risk only where they disagree. We therefore isolate that region
before introducing the score and its hidden residual.

\begin{assumption}[Deployment setup]
\label{ass:deploy}
Let $Y\in\{0,1\}$ and use $0/1$ loss. Fix measurable binary predictors $f_0$ and $f_a$.
Their disagreement region is $D=\{x:f_0(x)\ne f_a(x)\}$; assume $\mu_T(D)>0$.

Let $s:\mathcal X\to[0,1]$ be a fixed measurable candidate-correctness score fitted from source
information. The target law has the measurable correctness kernel
$\eta_a(x)=\Pr_T(f_a(X)=Y\mid X=x)$. Conditional on $D$, define
\begin{equation}
 M=\E[s(X)\mid D]-\tfrac12,
 \qquad
 \gamma=\E[\eta_a(X)-s(X)\mid D].
 \label{eq:compact-Mgamma}
\end{equation}
The class radius $\beta\ge0$ is supplied externally. It defines
$\mathcal C_\beta=\{P_T:|\gamma(P_T)|\le\beta\}$. Because $s\in[0,1]$, the feasible margin is
$M\in[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$. The identities below do not assume that $s$ is source-calibrated.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[Residual identity]
\label{rem:gamma-residual}
The definition gives
\begin{align*}
M+\gamma
&=\bigl(\E[s(X)\mid D]-\tfrac12\bigr)
  +\E[\eta_a(X)-s(X)\mid D]\\
&=\E[\eta_a(X)\mid D]-\tfrac12.
\end{align*}
This is an algebraic identity. It is not evidence that calibration transfers. We therefore call
$\gamma$ the target disagreement-conditional calibration residual, not a source-to-target drift
term. Source calibration need not survive conditioning on $D$. Consequently, $\gamma$ may be
nonzero even when $P_T=P_S$. A literal label-kernel-shift interpretation needs an extra reference,
such as $s(x)=\Pr_S(f_a(X)=Y\mid X=x)$. The
\KBSuppRef{reviewer guide in Appendix}{app:compact-reviewer-guide}{supplementary reviewer guide}
gives the no-shift counterexample. Calibration also depends on its conditioning information.
Top-label calibration, for example, conditions on the predicted class~\cite{gupta2021toplabel}.
Neither it nor ordinary score calibration automatically yields calibration conditional on $D$.
\end{remark}

Two notions separate a useful signal from a justified action. Risk alignment asks whether the same
evidence can hide opposite nonzero benefits. Strict directional soundness asks the stronger
question needed for commitment: does every compatible target law support the claimed direction?

\begin{definition}[Risk alignment]
\label{def:risk-align}
Evidence $Z$ is risk-aligned on a class $\mathcal P$ when two laws with opposite nonzero benefit
signs cannot induce the same law of $Z$. This is weaker than uniform strict-sign
identification. For example, a fibre containing zero and positive benefit is risk-aligned, but it
does not support a uniformly positive claim. The frontier fixes $M$ on each evidence fibre. This is
equivalent to using the augmented observable $\widetilde Z=(Z,M)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Strict directional soundness]
\label{def:strict-sound}
At a fixed augmented-evidence law, \adapt is uniformly directionally sound when
$\Delta(P)>0$ for every compatible $P\in\mathcal C_\beta$. Likewise, \freeze is sound when
$\Delta(P)<0$ throughout the fibre. A pointwise maximal three-way rule commits whenever one strict
action is sound. It abstains otherwise. If $\Delta=0$, the two models have equal task risk, but
neither strict claim is sound.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Label-Kernel Freedom on the Disagreement Region}

The central obstacle is now visible. Labels can change on $D$ while the input distribution and all
model outputs stay fixed. The next lemma isolates that freedom.

\begin{lemma}[Label-kernel freedom on disagreement]
\label{lem:fibre}
Fix $(\mu_T,P_S,f_0,f_a,s)$, and therefore fix $D$ and $M$. Also fix the conditional label law
outside $D$. For every measurable $\eta:D\to[0,1]$, there is a target law with input marginal
$\mu_T$ and $\Pr(f_a(X)=Y\mid X=x)=\eta(x)$ on $D$. Across these laws, every label-free observable
formed from the input batch, fixed models, and model outputs has the same distribution. Meanwhile,
\[
 \gamma=\E[\eta(X)\mid D]-\tfrac12-M.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $D$, one binary predictor outputs 0 and the other outputs 1. Assign conditional probability
$\eta(x)$ to $Y=f_a(x)$ and $1-\eta(x)$ to $Y=f_0(x)$. Retain the fixed label kernel outside $D$.
This construction changes neither the input marginal nor any model output. It therefore preserves
every label-free evidence law. Equation~\eqref{eq:compact-Mgamma} gives the displayed expression
for $\gamma$.
\end{proof}

With this construction in hand, the proof proceeds through four questions. First, how does risk
reduce to the disagreement region? Second, can the same evidence support opposite benefit signs?
Third, what happens on the boundary? Finally, where is strict commitment sound?

\subsection{Interpretation and Limits of the Frontier}

The theorem is exact only for the declared class. Choosing $\beta=0$ assumes a zero target
disagreement-conditional residual. It is not a conservative default. Without a credible upper
bound on $|\gamma|$, the frontier cannot support a robust strict action. More unlabeled samples do
not close this gap because the missing information lies in the target label kernel.

Empirical abstention does not prove structural unknowability. A finite-sample interval may be wide
because calibration data are scarce, prediction is poor, or transfer fails. This distinction leads
to the next question: can the required population residual bound be learned from the same
label-free evidence?

\section{What Label-Free Evidence Cannot Learn}
\label{sec:compact-budget}

The population frontier becomes usable only after $\beta$ has been declared. That requirement
creates the next problem. Can the same label-free evidence learn a smaller valid budget? If it
could, the system might replace domain knowledge with an audit. This section shows the exact limit
of that route: an audit must cover every residual value left unresolved by its evidence.

\subsection{Audit Setup and Evidence Fibres}

Let $W$ collect everything a deployment-time audit may read: the unlabeled target batch, fixed
models, score function, and derived label-free statistics. It may also contain a source or
calibration sample whose law does not depend on the target label kernel. A randomized audit returns
$\widehat\beta=g(W,U)\ge0$, where $U$ is independent of $W$.

For a declared class $\mathcal C$ and an observable-law value $z$, define the evidence fibre
\[
 \mathcal C(z)=\{P\in\mathcal C:\Law_P(W)=z\}
\]
and its unresolved residual radius
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_z(\mathcal C)=\sup_{P\in\mathcal C(z)}|\gamma(P)|.
 \label{eq:compact-fibre-radius}
\end{equation}
The fibre contains target laws that the audit cannot distinguish. Its radius is the smallest
uniform bound on $|\gamma|$ that this observable law can support.

\subsection{Exact Fibre-Wise Audit Floor}

The answer is a floor, not an estimator. Any audit that is valid throughout one evidence fibre must
cover the largest residual that remains possible in that fibre.

\begin{theorem}[Exact label-free audit floor]
\label{thm:compact-beta-minimax}
Fix a nonempty fibre $\mathcal C(z)$ and $\delta\in[0,1)$. Let $\Pr_z$ denote the common law of
$(W,U)$ on this fibre. Suppose $\widehat\beta$ satisfies
$\Pr_P(|\gamma(P)|\le\widehat\beta)\ge1-\delta$ for every
$P\in\mathcal C(z)$. Then
\[
 \Pr_z\!\left(\widehat\beta\ge\Gamma_z(\mathcal C)\right)\ge1-\delta.
\]
The constant audit $\widehat\beta\equiv\Gamma_z(\mathcal C)$ is valid with $\delta=0$.
Consequently, on the displayed high-probability event, an audited frontier can commit only where
the frontier with radius $\Gamma_z(\mathcal C)$ already commits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every $P$ in the fibre induces the same law for $W$ and therefore for $\widehat\beta$. Choose
$P_n\in\mathcal C(z)$ so that $|\gamma(P_n)|\uparrow\Gamma_z(\mathcal C)$. Under the common law,
each event $\{\widehat\beta\ge |\gamma(P_n)|\}$ has probability at least $1-\delta$. These events
decrease to $\{\widehat\beta\ge\Gamma_z(\mathcal C)\}$, so continuity from above proves the bound.
The constant audit is valid by the definition of the supremum. Finally,
$\widehat\beta\ge\Gamma_z$ and $|M|>\widehat\beta$ imply $|M|>\Gamma_z$.
\end{proof}

Useful budgets can still exist. The theorem says that one label-free fibre cannot reveal the target
label kernel it leaves unresolved. A valid audit must cover that fibre's radius. Computing more
functions of the same $W$ cannot shrink it.
The constant $\Gamma_z(\mathcal C)$ is the oracle benchmark for the fixed evidence-law fibre.
The theorem does not infer an unknown law $z$, or its radius, from a finite batch. It is therefore
a fibre-wise audit floor, not a general minimax estimator.

\subsection{Declared Budget and Abstention Radius}

For the full declared class
$\mathcal C_\beta=\{P:|\gamma(P)|\le\beta\}$ with
$0\le\beta\le\tfrac12$, the label-kernel construction in
Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre} gives
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_z(\mathcal C_\beta)=\beta.
 \label{eq:compact-beta-radius}
\end{equation}
Thus the least fibre-wide residual bound that a label-free audit can certify is the bound already
declared by the class. The same number is the population abstention radius:
\[
 \boxed{\begin{gathered}
 \text{least valid fibre-wide residual bound}\\
 =\text{population abstention radius}=\beta
 \end{gathered}}
\]
This equality connects the audit floor to the strict-commitment frontier. It does not turn the
declared $\beta$ into an estimate.

\subsection{Why Labels at the Wrong Domain Buy Nothing}

Suppose the audit observes a fully labeled calibration domain but the declared class does not link
its label kernel to the deployment kernel. Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre} can then change deployment labels
on $D$ while leaving the calibration sample unchanged. The augmented evidence law remains the same
across the target fibre, so Theorem~\ref{thm:compact-beta-minimax} cannot yield a smaller radius.
Labels help only when they come from the deployment domain or a stated assumption links the two
domains.

\subsection{What Additional Structure Can Buy}

Extra information can shrink the fibre, but each route changes the problem. Deployment labels can
estimate target correctness directly. Historical labeled deployments can support an
episode-marginal bound under an exchangeability model. A structural coupling can constrain the
change between calibration and target domains. Domain knowledge can set a physically meaningful
limit. Every gain comes from labels or assumptions, not from the current unlabeled batch. Detailed
rates and extended constructions are kept in the long-form theory record. The empirical claims here
do not require them.

\subsection{Implications for K-Bound}

KGA does not estimate $\beta$ or evaluate $|M|>\beta$ on the real-data benchmarks. It places the
empirical radius $\varepsilon$ around the predicted cell benefit $\widehat\Delta$. Population
benefit $\Delta$ and measured cell benefit $\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$ concern the same model pair,
but at different inferential levels. Coverage for the cell outcome transfers to population risk
only with an additional sampling-error argument. The reported intervals do not supply that
argument. The next section therefore changes the question from population identification to
deployment-time prediction.

\section{KGA: An Empirical Benefit-Interval Gate}
\label{sec:compact-kga}

Population theory asks whether every target law in a declared class supports one direction.
Deployment requires a different decision. For the current evaluation unit, can KGA support which
side of zero the benefit lies on? KGA answers by predicting the cell benefit from label-free
evidence and attaching a residual-calibrated radius. The common object is benefit; the guarantee
and uncertainty radius are different.

\subsection{What Transfers from the Population Analysis}
\label{sec:compact-bridge}

The two boxes above share a decision principle: commit only when the available uncertainty set
supports one strict direction. KGA is an empirical companion to K-Bound, not an implementation of
the population frontier. The binary population analysis gives
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=2\mu_T(D)(M+\gamma).
\label{eq:compact-bridge}
\end{equation}
For a recorded evaluation cell, the empirical target is
\[
 \Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}=S(f_a;\mathcal E)-S(f_0;\mathcal E),
\]
and its fitted prediction is
\[
 \widehat\Delta=h_\theta(Z).
\]
KGA does not estimate $M$, $\gamma$, or $\beta$. It never applies $|M|>\beta$ numerically. Its
$\varepsilon$ describes prediction error for $\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$ under an empirical
calibration protocol. It is not the range of population benefits compatible with a label-free
evidence law.

KGA is label-free only at the scored decision. Labeled development cells fit $h_\theta$, and
disjoint residual-calibration cells supply calibration residuals. A new decision reads $Z$ and
frozen fitted artifacts, but not that cell's outcome. The empirical coverage premise is
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(|\widehat\Delta-\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}|\le\varepsilon)\ge1-\alpha.
 \label{eq:compact-coverage}
\end{equation}
The next proposition concerns exactly this cell-level target, and only when the premise holds.

\subsection{The Coverage-to-Action Proposition}

The following standard implication makes the decision semantics explicit. It is not a calibration
theorem. Coverage is the premise, and establishing it for the intended units is the statistical
burden.

A population application would instead require coverage of $\Delta$. For accuracy, one possible
bridge adds a bound $b$ on $|\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}-\Delta|$. The triangle inequality and a union bound
then give radius $\varepsilon+b$ for the population benefit.
\KBSuppRef{Appendix}{app:population-transfer}{The supplementary population-transfer result}

\subsection{Evidence, Calibration, and the Action Rule}

The controlled implementation stores 11 label-free features. Two are deterministic differences, so
the effective rank is 9. The features summarize confidence, class balance, model disagreement, and
update size. Each recorded candidate--seed grid receives a separate
\texttt{GradientBoostingRegressor}~\cite{pedregosa2011sklearn}. It uses squared-error loss, 250
trees, depth 2, learning rate $0.05$, subsample $0.8$, and seed 0.
Natural protocols retain their own fitted model and feature schema; for example, CCT-20 uses a
ridge model. The
\KBSuppRef{evidence inventory in Appendix}{app:compact-evidence}{supplementary evidence inventory}
gives the schemas. A common interface does not make
an estimator or radius transferable across adapters.

Given residuals $r_i=|\widehat\Delta_i-\Delta_i^{\mathrm{cell}}|$, ordered as
$r_{(1)}\le\cdots\le r_{(n)}$, the order-statistic rule is
\begin{equation}
 k=\left\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\right\rceil,\qquad
 \varepsilon=
 \begin{cases}
 r_{(k)},&k\le n,\\
 +\infty,&k>n.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:compact-rank}
\end{equation}
Split-conformal coverage requires joint exchangeability of the calibration and next-unit scores
under the fitting and candidate-construction protocol
~\cite{tibshirani2019conformal,barber2023beyond}. One sufficient route uses an independently fitted
predictor and exchangeable calibration/deployment units. Here $n$ counts evaluation cells, not the
images within them.

Each recorded candidate--seed grid uses a deterministic five-fold, three-way cross-fit keyed by
stable cell IDs. For each score fold, its complement is split into an estimator-fit subset and a disjoint
residual-calibration subset. The predictor is fitted only on the first subset. The exact-rank
radius uses only residuals from the second. Prediction, radius, and action for a scored cell
therefore depend on no outcome in its score fold. A whole-pipeline invariance test changes one
scored $\Delta_i^{\mathrm{cell}}$, reruns the procedure, and verifies that the same cell's
$\widehat\Delta_i$, $\varepsilon_i$, and action do not change.

This construction removes scored-cell outcome flow; it does not create independent environments.
Cells still share one source checkpoint, an underlying corpus, corruption families, and overlapping
fit/calibration pools across folds. We claim neither exchangeability nor unseen-environment coverage
from the cross-fit. Natural protocols use separate development, calibration, and target partitions
only where their archived designs record that separation.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{KGA decision for one proposed update}
\label{alg:compact-kga}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Frozen $f_0$, adapter $\mathcal A$, unlabeled $X$, frozen $h_\theta$ and calibration artifacts
\State Create $f_a\leftarrow\mathcal A(f_0,X)$ in isolated shadow state
\State Extract $Z=\phi(X,f_0,f_a)$ and validate the assessment inputs
\If{the assessment is unavailable}
  \State retain $f_0$, log the reason, and return \abstain
\EndIf
\State $\widehat\Delta\leftarrow h_\theta(Z)$; obtain $\varepsilon$ from the frozen calibration artifact
\State $L\leftarrow\widehat\Delta-\varepsilon$; $U\leftarrow\widehat\Delta+\varepsilon$
\If{$L>0$} \State commit $f_a$ and return \adapt
\ElsIf{$U<0$} \State discard $f_a$ and return \freeze
\Else \State retain $f_0$, log uncertainty, and return \abstain
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

For radius $0.01$, predictions $0.04$, $-0.04$, and $0.005$ produce \adapt, \freeze, and
\abstain, respectively. These values illustrate the rule; they are not experimental results.

The CIFAR-10-C diagnostic~\cite{hendrycks2019cifar10c} is fully opened. It is also dependent and
constructed. Each candidate uses six corruption families: Gaussian noise, defocus blur, fog,
contrast, pixelate, and JPEG compression. Each appears at severities 1 and 5. The grid crosses those 12 shifts
with three batch sizes (200, 16, and 8) and three compositions (IID, imbalanced, and single-class).
It also crosses two recorded update schedules (10 steps at $10^{-3}$ and 50 steps at
$2.5\times10^{-3}$) and two repeats. This gives
$6\times2\times3\times3\times2\times2=432$ cells per RNG seed and 2,160 cells per candidate over
seeds 0--4.

Tent, EATA, and SAR are local adapter ports applied to one ResNet-18 source checkpoint. The five
seeds repeat stream construction and adapter randomness; they are not independently trained
checkpoints. Primary point estimates weight all cells equally. The six-family sensitivity analysis
first averages seeds and condition cells within each corruption family, then weights the six family
means equally.

The recorded candidate is a composite operating point. Adaptation runs in training mode with target
batch statistics replacing BatchNorm running statistics. Gradient updates modify the selected
BatchNorm and, where present, LayerNorm affine parameters. Candidate scoring retains that
train-mode BatchNorm behavior in
evaluation chunks of 512 images. Thus each measured $\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$ bundles batch-statistic
replacement and parameter updates; it does not isolate either component. A BatchNorm-only control
exists in code but was not executed on this grid. The local ports and operating points are protocol
diagnostics, not official default reproductions of the published adapters.

The current replay uses the three-way cross-fit from Section~\ref{sec:compact-kga}. It removes the
scored fold's outcomes from both estimator fitting and residual calibration. It does not make the
shared checkpoint, source corpus, or corruption cells independent. We therefore describe CIFAR as
an opened, dependent, constructed six-family diagnostic, not as prospective confirmation or an
untouched deployment trial.

\subsection{CCT-20 Design and Safe Utility}
\label{sec:cct20-design}

The CCT-20 camera-location study~\cite{beery2018recognition} crosses
\CCTCheckpointCount{} independent source checkpoints with \CCTLocationCount{} target locations,
giving \CCTCellCount{} checkpoint--location cells. It uses
\CCTProbeImageCount{} probe images and \CCTEvaluationImageCount{} evaluation images
(\CCTTargetImageCount{} total). The primary metric is set-membership top-1 accuracy: a prediction
is correct if it matches any distinct archived category for that image.
It is not an official single-label leaderboard reproduction. The candidate uses the official Tent
implementation and one non-episodic Adam update per incoming batch at learning rate $10^{-3}$.
Pre-update logits score each batch; adaptation state is carried across batches within a
checkpoint--location cell and reset between cells.

This was a cell-outcome-unopened, internally sealed execution: individual cell outcomes remained
unopened until one-shot scoring. Aggregate target-label metadata had previously been inspected, so
the study was not globally label-unopened or publicly preregistered. The source ridge fit used 10
rows from two camera locations.
Calibration used 45 rows aggregated to nine cis-test locations. Transfer to the trans-test
locations remains an assumption.

For fixed policy $b$, let $G_b$ be mean baseline regret minus mean KGA regret. Positive $G_b$
favors KGA. The \emph{safe-utility} endpoint is a predeclared, limited harm-avoidance check. Using
nominal pointwise 95\% bootstrap lower endpoints, it passes when
\begin{equation}
 L_{\mathrm{adapt}}>0,\qquad
 L_{\mathrm{freeze}}>\CCTSafeUtilityMargin.
 \label{eq:cct-safe-utility}
\end{equation}
The second threshold is a frozen-policy noninferiority margin on the accuracy-proportion scale.
This endpoint can pass when KGA always serves $f_0$. The word \emph{safe} names the endpoint; it is
not a population guarantee.

The locked \emph{strong-success} rule is deliberately stricter. It requires complete data for all
45 cells and improvement over both fixed policies. Both baseline-minus-KGA contrasts must have
nominal 97.5\% Bonferroni intervals strictly above zero. Both exact one-sided tests over the nine
location means must survive Holm adjustment at 0.05. Action exposure must also satisfy ADAPT rate
$\ge0.10$, FREEZE rate $\ge0.10$, and total
commitment $\ge0.30$. Failure of any component means no strong-success claim.

The nominal pointwise 95\% intervals in the CCT safe-utility
\KBSuppRef{table}{tab:cct-safe-utility}{table} belong to the locked safe-utility endpoint. The
nominal 97.5\% Bonferroni intervals in the
\KBSuppRef{strong-success audit}{tab:cct-strong-audit}{supplementary strong-success audit} belong to
a separate, stricter two-comparison criterion; they are not alternate versions of the same interval.

\subsection{Scoring, Dependence, and Statistical Inference}
\label{sec:compact-inference-units}

All policies are scored on the same evaluation records. Outcomes are joined only after actions are
fixed. In plain terms, candidate TTA is transductive: an episodic candidate may update on the
unlabeled evaluation inputs. An online candidate may use evaluation-batch BatchNorm statistics.
Separating development and calibration data therefore does not imply fresh-sample independence.

We report oracle regret, action counts, commitment, and false-commit frequencies. Interval inclusion
and width are separate diagnostics. Every paired regret contrast is baseline minus KGA, so positive
values favor KGA. Accuracy and macro-F1 remain on separate scales.

Bootstrap levels are nominal throughout. CIFAR sensitivity resamples six corruption families
conditional on the one archived checkpoint. CCT-20 uses a paired two-way product bootstrap over
checkpoint rows and location columns. Its sign-flip calculation instead uses nine location means.
For $G=(G_1,\ldots,G_m)$, a vector of $m$ cluster gaps, the sign-flip reference model is
\begin{equation}
 G\overset{d}{=}s\odot G
 \quad\text{for every }s\in\{-1,+1\}^{m}.
 \label{eq:compact-signflip-null}
\end{equation}
Independent zero-symmetric cluster gaps are sufficient. Exchangeability, marginal symmetry, or a
single global sign symmetry alone is not sufficient~\cite{hemerik2018exact}. Enumeration removes
Monte Carlo error but does not verify this null model. Holm adjustment requires valid input
$p$-values. Nominal family intervals also require valid marginal coverage of their constituent
intervals. The
\KBSuppRef{inference audit in Appendix}{app:compact-inference}{supplementary inference audit}
records the full specification and retrospective
status.

\section{Limitations and Broader Impact}
\label{sec:compact-sensitivity}

The measured action record does not demonstrate universal safety. Directional errors are observed
only under the recorded exposure and denominators, while utility preservation is limited to the
fixed candidates, metrics, and protocols evaluated here. Accuracy and regret summarize consequences
after outcomes are opened; they do not upgrade the gate's evidentiary scope.

\subsection{Assumptions, Not an Unconditional Safety Guarantee}

The population frontier is exact within its declared rich binary class. A credible $\beta$ must
come from domain knowledge, target labels, or a stated transfer assumption. A narrower class may
permit stronger decisions. An unsupported bound cannot justify a strict population claim. The
substantive object is the class-dependent feasible set, not the inequality by itself.

\paragraph{The development-based surrogate failed.}
We tested estimating $\beta$ from source-like development cells rather than declaring it. The
estimate is $1.4$--$50\times$ too small on real evaluation cells (24--73\% of cells fall outside the
declared class $\mathcal C_\beta$; commit error 0.4--16.6\%), and returns zero commitments on all 405
ImageNet-C cells in 5 of 10 configurations. We therefore withdraw the estimation route and declare
$\beta$ from domain knowledge instead.

This test used an operational development proxy; it could not observe the theorem's target
disagreement-conditional residual directly. The repository retains the reported result text but not
the named raw beta-sweep artifact. The negative result is therefore historically documented but not
presently regenerable from the canonical bundle. KGA neither estimates nor numerically uses
$\beta$.

For KGA, empirical interval coverage needs separate support at the intended decision unit. A larger
$\varepsilon$ reduces commitment but may increase regret when $f_0$ is worse. A smaller
$\varepsilon$ increases exposure and the cost of calibration failure. Changing the adapter,
checkpoint, feature schema, or update settings changes the prediction problem. Each change requires
recalibration or a justified transfer argument.

\paragraph{Repeated deployment needs a separate guarantee.}
A predeclared policy does not by itself create exchangeability or time-uniform safety. Reusing
calibration data, replacing the frozen baseline after an accepted update, or selecting later candidates from
earlier outcomes changes the joint process. Adaptive conformal inference studies online coverage
frequency with feedback~\cite{gibbs2021adaptive}; it does not automatically give time-uniform
control of KGA's false commitments. Selection-conditional and group-conditional guarantees require
additional methods, as Section~\ref{sec:compact-related} explains.

\subsection{What Evidence Is Still Missing}

The controlled results reuse one checkpoint, one corpus, and related corruption cells. Their
cell-outcome-disjoint split fixes a leakage defect; it does not remove dependence. The interval
inclusion table is retrospective, not an independent calibration test. The natural studies mainly
evaluate retention: CCT-20 has no ADAPT exposure, and So2Sat stops during development. Empirical
abstention may reflect weak prediction, limited data, a wide $\varepsilon$, or transfer failure. It
does not establish population non-identifiability.

The search history is substantial. One superseded census reported 1,387 determinations and 326
positives (23.5\%). A broader archived scan reported 1,427 determinations and 345 positives (24.2\%).
Neither census froze a reproducible family definition, file list, and repository state. They are
overlapping historical search summaries, not two predeclared families and not the multiplicity
family for the current panel. The controlled results remain explicitly post hoc.

Four tests would strengthen the empirical case. First, synchronize the current policy with official
TTA/ALine, AETTA, POEM, and simple benefit-estimation baselines. Second, run a new natural-shift
study containing helpful and harmful updates with nonzero ADAPT and FREEZE exposure. Third,
evaluate interval inclusion and directional error on untouched groups. Fourth, repeat inference
across independently trained checkpoints and held-out environments. These tests are missing; they
cannot be supplied by reclassifying the existing panel. The
\KBSuppRef{confirmation protocol in Appendix}{app:compact-confirmation}{supplementary prospective-confirmation protocol}
specifies a future protocol.

\subsection{Operational Scope and Potential Harm}

Rejecting an update can prevent adaptation harm. It does not prove that the frozen model is accurate
or appropriate for a high-stakes setting. Treating abstention as a certificate would be especially
risky. A deployment should retain the assessment reason and route unresolved cases to
domain-appropriate review or evidence acquisition. Our experiments do not validate clinical,
safety-critical, or autonomous operation. We claim neither a held-out physical camera deployment
nor a common end-to-end latency result.

The maintained software distinguishes an unavailable assessment from a supported negative interval.
Its tests and proof artifacts document a scoped validity, commitment-control, and rollback layer.
They do not certify every data pipeline or production system. The supplement records reproduction
instructions, formal scope, excluded historical extensions, and release checks.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:compact-conclusion}

K-Bound separates missing information from estimable uncertainty. For a fixed binary model pair
under $0/1$ loss, the declared class induces a feasible set of population benefits. That set supports
a strict direction exactly outside the closed band $|M|\le\beta$. The evidence-fibre audit theorem
shows why the same unlabeled observations cannot remove the residual range they leave unresolved.

KGA addresses a different uncertainty object. It predicts $\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$ and uses
$\varepsilon$ to accept, reject, or explicitly defer an update. Its empirical headline is
directional action correctness under recorded exposure: the CIFAR diagnostic reports
ADAPT/FREEZE/ABSTAIN counts and false-action denominators, while CCT-20 demonstrates only frozen-model
utility preservation with zero ADAPT exposure. Candidate-specific accuracy and point regret are
secondary measured consequences. These findings motivate evidence-aware commitment without turning
retrospective diagnostics into population guarantees. Selective routing on unseen natural shifts
and independent interval validation remain open tests.
