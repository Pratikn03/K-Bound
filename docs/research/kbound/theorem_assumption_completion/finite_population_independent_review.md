# Independent review of the finite-frame audit

The reviewed finite-frame construction is sound under its stated design: a fixed finite frame, fixed predictions/bin definitions, correct labels, and iid uniform draws with replacement. It does not require source/target exchangeability. It does require the declared random-sampling law; a checksum or a software random-number call does not independently prove random generation or truthful labels.

For general single-class or set-membership correctness, paired benefit is the helpful-event probability minus the harmful-event probability. Bonferroni bounds on the two binomial probabilities therefore bound mean finite-frame benefit. Sampling only prediction disagreements multiplies the conditional mean by the exactly known disagreement fraction. Agreement contributes exactly zero. For binary single-class labels, precisely one prediction is correct on disagreement, giving the one-binomial transformation `d(2p-1)` and the residual bound against the known disagreement-score mean. That identity is deliberately not used for set-membership labels: both predictions can be correct even when they differ.

The transport-budget audit bounds source and target class-conditional bin probabilities given their class counts, bounds target priors, and uses the coordinatewise worst-case TV differences followed by exact maximization over the boxed prior simplex. A union bound applies even if the two sampled frames are dependent. A class truly absent from a frame has no identified conditional law: the function now explicitly permits any declared normalized extension and retains its vacuous TV bound. Combining this audit-derived rho with a subsequent certificate requires adding the audit failure budget.

The initial implementation checked record hashes but could accept a recomputed hash on malformed data. The revision now reconstructs a canonical plan and validates schemas, exact rational strings, prediction/class alignment, label semantics and optional arrays. Draw validation checks schemas, plan identity, exact integer eligible indices and the declared sampling mode even after a checksum is recomputed. This protects representation and computation; it does not authenticate the history of an otherwise structurally valid record. Adversarial resealing tests, a mixed-label binary residual coverage enumeration, and membership/transport rejection tests were added. **33 finite-frame tests passed** within the combined 68-test targeted pass.

## CCT-20 pre-draw review

The public manifest contains 90 prediction-role objects: 45 small descriptors and 45 full prediction payloads. The runner's role and byte-size filter selects those 45 full payloads, checks unique checkpoint/location identities, and uses only original `evaluation` rows. The payload rows contain predictions/logits and roles, without ground-truth labels. The hashed label contract maps the archived categories exactly to output indices 0 through 15. The scorer uses set-membership top-1 correctness and reconstructs the original per-cell primary benefit before accepting a new result.

The declared family allocation is valid: 45 cells times `1/900` equals `1/20`. The two-event split occurs within each cell's budget. Shared image labels/checkpoints do not invalidate this family union bound, which does not require independent cells. These are intervals for each recorded finite frame; they are not uncertainty intervals for a population of camera locations or newly trained models.

The pre-draw review requested source/snapshot and protocol checks for replay. The runner added those checks before execution. The complete subsequent replay used the saved inventories, without any new random draw, and reproduced every cell result and the summary. All three executed-source bindings matched at review time.

## Observed result and interpretation

The retrospective label-budget simulation produced **45/45 finite-frame containment, 35 FREEZE, 10 ABSTAIN and no ADAPT decisions**. The fixed frames contain one helpful and 44 harmful candidate effects; the helpful frame was not selected. No accepted harmful update occurred because no update was accepted. Conditional accepted error is therefore undefined, and the result does not demonstrate useful selective adaptation.

There were 23,040 random draws, 16,428 unique label uses summed within cells, and **8,954 distinct sampled image IDs across the entire study**. All labels were previously opened. These are simulated lookup counts, not newly acquired labels, measured annotation labor or prospective labeling cost. The already known full-frame outcomes support a retrospective interval-containment check; they do not make the study prospective.

The numerical replay and original CCT scientific authorities were not edited by this review. Exact protocol/summary bindings and replay checks are recorded in `cct20_finite_frame_independent_review.json`.

## Manuscript derivation review and additional transport check

The new `audit_assisted_bounds.md` and `audit_assisted_appendix.tex` were compared against the executed implementation and saved CCT records. Their benefit, residual and transport formulas agree with the procedure. The finite-frame randomization argument, lack of future-deployment inference, set-membership qualification, union-bound allocation and additional failure budget are stated correctly. No substantive mathematical error was found in this review; it is an internal independent review, not external peer review or a formal verification of every implementation line.

Three clarifications were recommended to the manuscript owner: specify sampling uniformly within the disagreement stratum for the paired-accuracy construction; describe error-budget allocation as exact rational division rather than merely conservative rounding; and say the numerical certificates preserve the binomial construction's finite-sample coverage rather than implying deterministic statistical containment. The historical 4,860 trials retain their executed numerical scope. The 81-case numerical replay is a separate implementation check, not a new real-world experiment. Label counts should distinguish 16,428 per-cell uses from 8,954 globally distinct IDs and describe the exercise as a simulated lookup budget over previously opened labels.

An additional transport test enumerates all multinomial count states for two three-record frames with six independent draws per frame. Exact integer multiplicities represent all `3^12 = 531441` ordered draw pairs. The true prior-weighted conditional TV is `2/3`, with both classes present and unequal class-conditional shifts. The exact probability of containing that truth meets the declared error budget; some draw states produce bounds below one, so the check is not satisfied solely by vacuous full-unit bounds. This supplements the absent-class, residual-coverage and malformed-record tests without modifying executed sources or study outputs.
