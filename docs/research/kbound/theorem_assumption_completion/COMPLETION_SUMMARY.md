# Scoped theorem and statistical-assumption completion

This completion phase strengthens the maintained K-Bound scope without claiming that every deployment assumption is true. The frozen September 23 release is preserved as a historical artifact; this directory records the new scoped work.

## Completed and checked

- **Formal core:** 238 registered Lean declarations build successfully in 3,534 jobs. These are formal declarations supporting the scoped development, not 238 distinct manuscript theorems. The kernel audit reports only the permitted foundational axioms (`Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`, and `propext`). The added declarations cover matched-evidence abstention, audit floors, compound coverage, cell-versus-population counterexamples, paired transport and compactness, sampled disagreement/frontier arguments, calibration order statistics, and paired evaluation.
- **Manuscript correspondence:** the active TeX theorem inventory contains 13 named result environments and 20 statistical assumptions. `theorem_assumption_correspondence_receipt.json` is a source-bound PASS; it checks active statement/proof bytes, source bindings and assumption references. It does not establish mathematical truth or deployment validity.
- **Numerical certification:** the bounded replay contains 81 synthetic cases. All 81 saved rational primal witnesses were checked exactly; directed probability boxes and dual bounds were independently regenerated. The historical 4,860-trial record keeps its earlier qualification and is not relabeled as a result of this implementation.
- **Retrospective finite-frame audit:** the CCT-20 public bundle was replayed over 45 fixed evaluation cells with 512 uniform-with-replacement draws per cell. All 45 full-frame benefits were contained in their simultaneous finite-frame intervals. The resulting actions were 35 `FREEZE`, 10 `ABSTAIN`, and 0 `ADAPT`; the one helpful frame was not selected. These audit-assisted actions are distinct from the original KGA actions in the main paper (0 `ADAPT` / 44 `FREEZE` / 1 `ABSTAIN`). The audit used 23,040 draws and 8,954 globally distinct sampled image IDs. The labels had already been opened, so this is retrospective evidence and the label-use total is a simulated draw/lookup count, not new annotation labor.
- **Software checks:** the focused Python suite passes 213 tests. The independent numerical/statistical review records 69 targeted coverage and adversarial checks; the CCT replay review and transport review both report PASS. Existing warnings are syntax/linter warnings only.
- **Document:** the rebuilt `kbound_submission.pdf` is 70 pages and includes the audit-assisted appendix. The LaTeX log has no unresolved references or citations.

## What this establishes

The new work establishes a more complete, explicitly scoped mechanization and a reproducible finite-frame audit route. It also makes the numerical LP certificate auditable without treating floating-point solver success as a proof. The retrospective CCT result supports conservative retention on the inspected fixed frames.

## What remains conditional or unestablished

- Exchangeability, independent sampling, fixed predictors, faithful labels and the declared frame are premises of the finite-frame and probabilistic statements; a source hash cannot prove them for a future deployment.
- External residual (`beta`) and transport (`rho`) bounds remain scientific inputs unless justified by independent domain knowledge or a valid target-label design. The completion phase does not manufacture those bounds.
- The CCT result is not prospective natural-shift validation, an environment-population guarantee, a macro-F1 guarantee, or evidence of useful adaptation: it issued no `ADAPT` decisions.
- The formal receipt covers the registered scoped declarations. It is not a proof of every informal manuscript sentence, every historical extension, or the empirical validity of every statistical assumption.
- Historical experiments whose original execution authority is incomplete remain qualified. The unrestricted orbit-selection extension remains excluded where its counterexample applies.

The appropriate scientific claim is therefore a conditional decision framework with a stronger mechanized and audit-assisted evidence chain, together with explicit empirical limits—not universal calibration validity or production effectiveness.
