\section{Formal Proof and Reproducibility Atlas}
\label{fr:formal-atlas}

This chapter explains what is proved, what is checked mechanically, what remains a scientific
assumption, and how a reader can reproduce the maintained publication surfaces without confusing
those four categories.  The distinction is central to K-Bound.  A theorem may be correct under its
encoded assumptions while a benchmark fails those assumptions.  A data validator may establish
that a table agrees with a sealed artifact while saying nothing about the theorem.  A successful
PDF build establishes neither.  Treating all three as a single notion of ``verification'' would
erase the boundary that the paper is designed to expose.

The current formal receipt registers 142 selected Lean declarations: 65 retained core
declarations and 77 newer foundational or counterexample capstones.  This is a
registry count, not the total number of authored lemmas, and it is not a score of novelty or
scientific importance.  Supporting declarations compile as dependencies, while some declarations
that are displayed by the theorem map are intentionally not registry capstones.  The precise
claim is therefore narrow: the dated strict-core build and transitive-axiom audit report success
for the 142 registered names under their encoded assumptions.  The machine-readable receipt is
\path{audits/formal_foundations_2026_08_31.json}.  It does not report that every sentence in every
earlier theory draft has been formalized.

\subsection{Four independent verification layers}
\label{fr:four-verification-layers}

The repository separates four layers that are often collapsed in machine-learning papers.

\paragraph{Mathematical statement.}
A paper theorem specifies objects, assumptions, and a conclusion.  The decisive questions are
whether the assumptions define a nonempty class, whether every symbol has one type and one unit,
and whether the conclusion follows for every member of that class.  For the population frontier,
the key objects are the target law, the frozen and adapted predictors, the positive-mass
disagreement region, and the externally declared residual budget.  For the empirical
coverage-to-action implication, the target is instead an observed cell-level benefit and the
conclusion is an event containment conditional on interval coverage.

\paragraph{Proof implementation.}
Lean encodes selected statements and checks proof terms against a pinned kernel and library.  A
compiled proof establishes the encoded statement, not a nearby prose statement.  Small differences
matter.  A fixed finite-real radius is not an arbitrary measurable extended-real random radius.
A full measurable correctness-field class is not an arbitrary restricted deployment class.
Arithmetic showing that two opposite worlds force abstention is not, by itself, the measure-law
construction showing that the worlds have identical observable evidence.

\paragraph{Empirical authority.}
Generated tables are checked against current result artifacts and the claim ledger.  These checks
can detect stale counts, a mismatched hash, or an unsupported table cell.  They do not transform a
retrospective diagnostic into prospective confirmation, and they do not prove exchangeability or
population transfer.  The result validators guard factual consistency; the paper's interpretation
must still state the design and denominator.

\paragraph{Publication rendering.}
The TeX build resolves references, lays out tables, and emits the PDFs.  Rendering every page can
detect clipping, blank pages, malformed glyphs, or an unreadable table.  That is a necessary
publication check, but it has no authority over the science.  In particular, a clean log cannot
rehabilitate a stale numerical claim or an invalid theorem module.

does \emph{not} authorize.

\subsection{Clause-level proof coverage}
\label{fr:clause-coverage}

The maintained theorem map is \path{formal/KBound/TheoremMap.lean}.  It is an executable index: its
\texttt{\#check} commands ensure that named declarations remain available with types accepted by
Lean.  The map also documents the intended correspondence between paper clauses and formal
objects.  That correspondence is more informative than reporting one aggregate proof count.

\paragraph{Benefit reduction.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} has an algebraic finite core and a measurable target-law realization.
The finite declarations check the binary sign reduction and margin split.  The measurable files
construct label kernels, a joint target law, and the population zero--one loss integral.  On the
disagreement region, the constructed class is supported on the two predictor outputs.  This gives
an exact formal counterpart for the maintained binary reduction under the encoded class.  It does
not silently extend the same scalar residual geometry to unrestricted multiclass kernels or to a
different loss.

\paragraph{Matched-evidence nonidentifiability.}
The finite arithmetic says that opposite fixed benefits cannot both receive a sound strict
direction from the same evidence.  Separate measurable constructions show how target label kernels
can vary while the target input law and all label-free evidence derived from it remain fixed.  The
combination supports the maintained full-class necessity argument.  Two limitations remain
explicit.  First, the construction does not imply that an arbitrary restricted deployment class
contains both witnesses.  Second, the probabilistic interpretation that composes matched evidence
with the abstract abstention-probability inequality is presented in the paper; the finite
\path{Impossibility.lean} file checks the arithmetic abstraction rather than that complete
composition as one declaration.

\paragraph{Closed band and strict frontier.}
The formal core checks the positive and negative sufficient branches, the deterministic action
rule, the zero witness in the closed band, opposite-sign witnesses in the open band, and the two
boundary configurations in which zero competes with a strict sign.  A finite target-law file gives
a distributional lift under an explicit richness premise.  The measurable frontier file gives a
separate construction over the full measurable correctness-field class and clips the feasible
interval to the probability range.  These are strong but scoped statements.  They do not prove
richness for a deployment class selected by a practitioner.

\paragraph{Coverage-to-action certificate.}
The deterministic certificate checks pointwise containment: a false ADAPT or false FREEZE action
can occur only if the named interval misses the named scalar target.  A measure-level file proves
the corresponding probability inequality for a fixed finite-real radius.  A conformal file proves
a specialized random-threshold result under exchangeable measurable residual scores, including
ties.  The paper states a more general event-containment proposition with a measurable random
extended-real radius.  At that generality, the containment proof is pen-and-paper; it should not be
described as wholly covered by the fixed-radius file.  Conversely, the specialized conformal
formalization proves its own score-law result but does not prove that the heterogeneous benchmark
cells used in the empirical study are exchangeable.

\paragraph{Probability foundations outside the headline.}
The strict-core also includes general testing, concentration, optional-stopping, and measurable
swap results.  They strengthen the library available for future work, but they do not enlarge the
headline theorem by proximity.  The general Le Cam layer treats arbitrary probability measures
and randomized measurable tests.  The concentration layer treats bounded independent sums and
adapted martingale differences under explicit assumptions.  The filtered Ville layer treats
nonnegative supermartingales and predictable betting products.  The measurable swap layer proves
invariance and opposite-benefit constructions for a channel, and the channel counterexample shows
why selecting one representative per swap orbit does not orient an entire evidence fibre.  None of
these facts proves the unfinished historical one-bit, ratio, or rate characterization.

\subsection{The strict-core gate and the disclosed incomplete layer}
\label{fr:strict-core-gate}

The recommended command invokes the pinned Lean build and then the strict-core audit.  The audit
checks that every registered name exists, scans the maintained formal source for forbidden proof
holes, and asks Lean to print transitive axiom dependencies.  The allowed kernel dependencies are
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.  A missing declaration,
failed build, proof hole, or extra axiom fails the gate.  Running the audit without the build flag
is only a static inventory check and is not reported as kernel verification.

The command \texttt{-{}-full-foundations} deliberately asks a stronger question.  It remains a
failure because the sixth, one-bit/channel characterization is incomplete: a verified
counterexample refutes the earlier orbit-selection sufficiency idea, and the associated H/ratio
and rate claims have not been repaired.  This failure is informative rather than cosmetic.  It
prevents a local success on the five maintained probability layers from being advertised as a
proof of every proposed extension.  The full report uses the strict-core receipt and preserves the
stronger failure as a boundary.

Three counts should therefore never be conflated:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item the number of registered capstones audited transitively;
\item the number of declarations displayed by the theorem map; and
\item the total number of theorem and lemma declarations compiled as source or dependency.
\end{enumerate}

Only the first count is 142 in the current receipt.  The second is a deliberately broader index,
and the third is not claimed by that number.  Reporting this distinction avoids the common but
incorrect phrase ``142 total proofs.''

\subsection{Authority graph for numerical claims}
\label{fr:authority-graph}

The numerical publication path is a directed graph, not a collection of manually copied table
cells.  Raw or reconciled result artifacts feed bounded generator scripts.  Generated TeX macros
feed shared manuscript sources.  The claim ledger records allowed wording, supporting artifacts,
and prohibited interpretations.  A storage manifest binds mutable current authorities by byte
count and SHA-256, while sealed evidence entries retain their own immutable checksums.  The
manuscript validator then compares these surfaces.  Finally, the PDF builder validates before it
regenerates presentation files.

The order matters.  If a current claim-ledger byte count changes, refreshing only the PDF cannot
repair the broken storage binding.  If a table macro changes without its current source artifact,
the validator should fail rather than accept the visually plausible number.  If a historical seal
does not match, the bounded mutable-authority refresh must not rewrite that seal.  Each repair must
occur at the layer that owns the discrepancy.

This graph also explains why the canonical panel hash appears in the primary result presentation.
It is an identity for the scored panel, not a decorative reproducibility token.  A reviewer can

folded into that panel identity.

\subsection{Reproduction modes and mutation boundaries}
\label{fr:reproduction-modes}

Reproduction is safer when read-only verification is separated from regeneration.  The current
workflow has three practical modes.

\paragraph{Inspection.}
A reader may inspect the claim ledger, current manifests, generated macros, and theorem map without
changing any artifact.  This is the right starting point for peer review.  It can answer questions
such as: Which artifact supports this count?  Is a result current evidence or non-evidentiary
provenance?  Which formal file covers this theorem clause?  Inspection cannot establish that the
local checkout is complete or that every external dataset is mounted.

\paragraph{Bounded manuscript verification.}
The manuscript-claim validator checks the maintained TeX closure, canonical panel, release
consistency surfaces, direct storage hashes, and sealed evidence hashes.  The PDF build runs this
check before table and figure regeneration.  New output is compiled in a fresh temporary directory
so an old auxiliary file cannot supply a missing label or citation.  Only allowlisted PDF and log
names can be published back to the manuscript directory.  These controls make it harder for an old
successful build to mask a broken current source.

\paragraph{Full release.}
A publication release is stronger than a manuscript build.  It requires a reviewed clean source
freeze, pinned Python and external-tool content, the complete declared test inventory, the Lean
strict-core build, archive and privacy verification, source sealing, rendered-page inspection, and
a final outer checksum inventory.  The repository's older end-to-end release wrapper, page-render
helper, and checksum verifier have not been generalized to this new four-artifact family.  This
handoff therefore uses the bounded build flags together with a separate manual all-page rendering
check and a newly emitted outer SHA-256 inventory.  That is a handoff receipt, not a clean source
seal.  External publication actions---pushing a tag, uploading an archive, creating a DOI, or
submitting to a venue---remain author actions and are not implied by a local pass.

The separation also defines what not to do.  A presentation build must not rerun training.  A
manifest refresh must not reseal historical evidence.  A checksum file must be emitted only after
the final PDFs are fixed.  A dirty-tree checksum is useful for local handoff but is not a clean
source seal.  A missing cloud-backed Git object cannot be repaired by describing a component test
as a release pass.

\subsection{A tested implementation invariant}
\label{fr:software-invariance}

The empirical action for cell $i$ is intended to be outcome-disjoint: its own scored outcome
$B_i$ cannot influence its prediction, radius, or action.  The recorded regression test checks
that property for index 7 in two synthetic controlled-grid instances.  Each instance holds the
fold assignment, features, and hyperparameters fixed, perturbs only $B_7$, and reruns the recorded
cross-fit procedure.  For the focal cell it requires equality of the prediction, radius, and
action.  Changes for other cells are allowed because their fitted models may legitimately use the
perturbed cell.

This is a tested implementation invariant, not a software theorem or a population theorem.  Its
scope is the two fixed synthetic instances; it does not exercise every possible upstream feature,
preprocessing, or hyperparameter-selection path.  It also does not establish coverage,
exchangeability, or generalization.  Conversely, a mathematical coverage proposition cannot
compensate for an implementation that leaks the scored outcome.  The formal and software claims
are complementary because they guard different arrows in the authority graph.

The most useful failure diagnostics localize the broken arrow:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item a changed prediction implicates fit, selection, or preprocessing leakage;
\item an unchanged prediction but changed radius implicates residual or calibration leakage;
\item unchanged prediction and radius but changed action implicates action-rule state or a hidden
tie-breaking path;
\item a stable focal cell with changes elsewhere can be correct under cross-fitting and should not
be rejected automatically.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reviewer trace procedure}
\label{fr:reviewer-trace}

A compact way to audit any important sentence is to trace it backwards through six questions.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Claim type.} Is the sentence a definition, theorem, finite-panel observation,
diagnostic interpretation, or future protocol?
\item \textbf{Target.} Does it concern population benefit $\Delta$, observed cell benefit
$\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$, a fitted prediction $\widehat\Delta$, or an action consequence?
\item \textbf{Scope.} Which candidate, dataset family, action exposure, loss, and deployment class
are named?
\item \textbf{Authority.} Which current artifact or proof clause supports it, and is that identity
bound by a current hash or theorem map?
\item \textbf{Negative boundary.} Which nearby stronger reading is expressly not supported?
\item \textbf{Reproduction.} Which read-only validator or build command would fail if the supporting
surface changed?
\end{enumerate}

For example, a sentence about a false ADAPT count is incomplete until it names the ADAPT exposure
and the all-cell denominator.  A sentence about an interval is incomplete until it names the
scalar target and whether the interval is nominal, retrospective, or prospectively covered.  A
sentence about the population frontier is incomplete until it names the declared class and the
external residual budget.  A sentence about formalization is incomplete until it distinguishes
the paper clause from the encoded declaration.

\subsection{Reproducibility checklist for this report}
\label{fr:report-checklist}

The following checklist is intentionally shorter than the repository release runbook.  It states
the evidence needed to reproduce the publication family itself.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Verify the storage manifest and immutable evidence bindings before generating tables.
\item Run the manuscript-claim validator and the focused claim-consistency tests.
\item Run the scored-cell outcome-invariance regression and publication-driver boundary tests.
\item Build the strict Lean core with the pinned toolchain; retain the stronger
full-foundations failure rather than relabeling it as a pass.
\item Build each PDF from a fresh auxiliary directory with its explicit artifact flag.
\item Reject undefined references or citations, multiply defined labels, missing files, and serious
overfull boxes.
\item Confirm the short main-paper boundary, the standalone supplement boundary, and the minimum
full-report page count from the physical PDFs rather than source estimates.
\item Extract PDF text and scan for stale counts, prohibited current-superiority wording, and a
missing population-versus-empirical distinction.
\item Render every page of every latest PDF.  Inspect contact sheets and full-resolution pages for
tables, equations, hash wrapping, and appendix transitions.
\item Only after all artifacts are final, emit and independently verify their SHA-256 inventory.
\end{enumerate}

The checklist cannot guarantee a favorable review.  Its purpose is narrower and more useful: it
makes contradictions, stale evidence, invalid source boundaries, and rendering defects observable
before submission.  The scientific conclusion remains the one supported by the theory and current
diagnostics: strict action can be justified only inside an explicit scope; abstention is the
correct output when that scope does not orient the compatible outcomes.
