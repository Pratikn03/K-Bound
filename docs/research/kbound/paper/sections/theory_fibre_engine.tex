\begin{definition}[Audit data]\label{def:audit-data}
The \emph{audit data} $W$ is the totality of what a deployment-time procedure may read:
the unlabeled deployment batch $X_{1:m}\sim\mu_T$, the fixed maps $(f_0,f_a,s)$ and every
label-free statistic $Z=\phi(X_{1:m},f_0,f_a)$ derived from them, together with any sample
$S$ --- labeled or not --- drawn from a source or calibration law that is specified
independently of the target label kernel. An \emph{audit} is a measurable
$\widehat\beta=g(W,U)\ge0$ where $U$ is a randomisation seed independent of everything else.
$W$ contains \emph{no target labels}. This is exactly the ``target-label-free'' convention
boxed in Assumption~\ref{ass:deploy}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Kernel freedom on the disagreement region]\label{lem:fibre}
Fix the observables $(\mu_T,P_S,f_0,f_a,s)$, hence $D$ and $M$, and fix any off-$D$
conditional label law $K^\circ$. For every measurable $\eta:D\to[0,1]$ there is a target law
$P_T^{\eta}$ on $\mathcal X\times\{0,1\}$ with input marginal $\mu_T$, off-$D$ kernel
$K^\circ$, and
\[
\Pr_{P_T^{\eta}}\bigl(f_a(X)=Y\mid X=x\bigr)=\eta(x)\quad\text{for }x\in D .
\]
Moreover $\Law(W\mid P_T^{\eta})$ does not depend on $\eta$, and
\[
\bar a(P_T^\eta)=\E_{\mu_T}[\eta\mid D],\qquad
\gamma(P_T^\eta)=\E_{\mu_T}[\eta\mid D]-\tfrac12-M ,
\]
so that as $\eta$ ranges over all of $[0,1]^D$, $\gamma$ ranges over exactly
$\bigl[-\tfrac12-M,\ \tfrac12-M\bigr]$, an interval of length $1$ containing $0$ in its
interior whenever $|M|<\tfrac12$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Existence.} On $D$ we have $f_0(x)\neq f_a(x)$ and $Y$ is binary, so
$\{f_0(x),f_a(x)\}=\{0,1\}$; setting $K^\eta(x,f_a(x))=\eta(x)$ and
$K^\eta(x,f_0(x))=1-\eta(x)$ therefore defines a valid Bernoulli conditional law on
$\{0,1\}$ for each $x\in D$, measurable in $x$ because $\eta,f_0,f_a$ are. Off $D$ take
$K^\circ$. Then $P_T^\eta(dx,dy):=\mu_T(dx)K(x,dy)$ is a probability law with the stated
marginal and pointwise correctness.

\emph{Invariance of the audit data.} By Definition~\ref{def:audit-data}, $W$ is a function of
$X_{1:m}$, of the fixed maps $(f_0,f_a,s)$, of $S$, and of $U$. The construction changes
neither $\mu_T$ (so $\Law(X_{1:m})$ is fixed), nor the maps, nor the law generating $S$, nor
$U$. Hence $\Law(W\mid P_T^\eta)=\Law(W)$ for every $\eta$.

\emph{Range.} $\bar a=\E_{\mu_T}[\eta\mid D]$ by definition of $\bar a$, and
$\gamma=\bar a-\tfrac12-M$ by Remark~\ref{rem:gamma-residual}. Taking $\eta\equiv c$ for
$c\in[0,1]$ gives $\bar a=c$, so $\bar a$ attains every value in $[0,1]$ and $\gamma$ every
value in $[-\tfrac12-M,\tfrac12-M]$; no larger range is possible since $\bar a\in[0,1]$
always. Since $\mu_T(D)>0$ the conditional expectation is well defined.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The three uses are one construction]\label{rem:one-construction}
Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre} restricted to the two-point subfamily $\eta\equiv\tfrac12\pm\delta$ with
$\delta<\min\{\beta-|M|,\tfrac12\}$ \emph{is} the proof of the matched-evidence impossibility
(Theorem~\ref{lem:nonid}). The same lemma at the two \emph{extreme} points $\eta\equiv1$ and
$\eta\equiv0$ \emph{is} the proof of audit vacuity (Corollary~\ref{cor:audA}). Everything in
this section is the same one-parameter family $\{\eta\equiv c\}_{c\in[0,1]}$ read at a
different radius. In particular the construction that forces abstention and the construction
that forbids auditing the budget which decides abstention are literally the same object;
Corollary~\ref{cor:beta-is-beta} makes this an equality of numbers, not an analogy.
No total-variation trade-off is available to sharpen or weaken it: within a fibre the
observable laws coincide \emph{exactly}, which is the degenerate --- and strongest --- case
of a two-point argument, so Le Cam's method has nothing to add here. It reappears, genuinely,
only in Theorem~\ref{thm:lecam}, where labels make the two laws distinguishable.
\end{remark}
