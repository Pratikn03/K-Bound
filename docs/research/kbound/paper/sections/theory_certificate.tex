\begin{theorem}[Finite-sample adapt/freeze/abstain certificate]\label{thm:certificate}\label{thm:cert}
Suppose $\Delta$, $\widehat\Delta$, and the nonnegative random radius $\varepsilon$ are measurable
on a common probability space containing all calibration, benefit-model fitting, and
deployment-unit randomness, and that the benefit interval satisfies marginal coverage
\[
\Pr\!\bigl[\,|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon\,\bigr]\ \ge\ 1-\alpha .
\]
Define
\[
g(Z)=\begin{cases}
\textsc{adapt} & \widehat\Delta-\varepsilon>0,\\
\textsc{freeze} & \widehat\Delta+\varepsilon<0,\\
\textsc{abstain} & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Then, marginally over that common probability space,
\begin{align*}
\Pr\bigl[g(Z)=\textsc{adapt},\Delta\le0\bigr]&\le\alpha,\\
\Pr\bigl[g(Z)=\textsc{freeze},\Delta\ge0\bigr]&\le\alpha.
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the event $\{|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon\}$, the condition
$\widehat\Delta-\varepsilon>0$ implies $\Delta>0$, so a false adapt requires the complement of
this event, which has probability at most $\alpha$. The freeze case is symmetric.
\end{proof}
Exchangeability, or an explicit shift correction, is one route to this coverage assumption. Risk
alignment is not required for the conditional-on-coverage implication.

\begin{remark}[Marginal vs.\ conditional error]\label{rem:fa-marginal}
Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate} bounds the \emph{marginal, unconditional} false-adapt and
false-freeze probabilities ($\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}$). It does \emph{not} bound the conditional
rate $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}=\Pr[\Delta\le0\mid\textsc{adapt}]$, which we report empirically.
Risk alignment (Definition~\ref{def:risk-align}) is required for the \emph{frontier}
identifiability claim; coverage alone certifies the \emph{interval} rule above.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Fallback when assumptions are unsupported]\label{cor:abstain-valid}
If interval coverage---or the calibration assumptions supporting it (exchangeability, or an explicit
shift correction)---cannot be justified on the deployment batch, Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate}
supplies no level-$\alpha$ protection for either strict action. A deployment must then use a
separately declared operational fallback, such as abstention or serving the frozen model; this is a
policy recommendation, not a conclusion that either fallback is optimal. If instead \emph{risk alignment}
(Definition~\ref{def:risk-align}) cannot be justified, the population-frontier interpretation and the
transferability of the benefit estimator are unsupported, even though the interval implication of
Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate} remains valid \emph{conditional on coverage} (cf.\ Theorem~\ref{lem:nonid}).
\end{remark}
