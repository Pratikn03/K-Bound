\subsection{The assumption gate}\label{sec:gate}

The failure-safe defaults above abstain when the batch is too small, the evidence lies
outside calibration support, a configuration hash differs, or no valid radius exists.
Those are the right reflexes, and the gate below is what they look like once they are
stated as a precondition on \emph{certification} rather than as guards inside the
decision path. Because (A1)--(A6) cannot be verified label-free
(Remark~\ref{rem:contract-not}), a deployment is permitted to issue strict
\textsc{adapt}/\textsc{freeze} decisions only after passing it. Thresholds are fixed
before deployment and versioned with the protocol: a gate tuned after seeing the outcome
violates (A6) and voids the certificate.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Inputs.} Frozen candidate $f_a$; frozen benefit estimator
$\widehat\Delta$; calibration evidence and residuals; unlabelled deployment evidence $Z$;
the declared inference unit; thresholds fixed before deployment.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{Verify protocol separation.} If target labels affected candidate,
    feature, threshold or stopping choice: \emph{certification disabled} --- (A4).
  \item \textbf{Verify inference-unit adequacy.} If the effective number of independent
    calibration units is below the declared minimum: \emph{diagnostic only} --- (A5).
  \item \textbf{Test evidence-support overlap.} If deployment evidence lies outside the
    calibrated support: \textsc{abstain} or \textsc{freeze} --- (A1)/(A2) suspect.
  \item \textbf{Test calibration stability.} Recompute $\varepsilon$ across folds, seeds,
    domains or time blocks. If the radius or the action proportions are unstable:
    \textsc{abstain} or \textsc{freeze}.
  \item \textbf{Check sensitivity.} Vary the declared calibration construction and the
    clustering unit. If the promoted conclusion changes: \emph{diagnostic only}.
  \item \textbf{Apply the certified rule.} \textsc{adapt} if $L>0$; \textsc{freeze} if
    $U<0$; otherwise \textsc{abstain}.
  \item \textbf{Monitor sequentially.} If support, evidence distribution or action rates
    drift beyond predeclared thresholds, suspend strict decisions and revert to
    \textsc{freeze}/\textsc{abstain}.
\end{enumerate}

\smallskip
\noindent Steps 1--2 are protocol checks and are fatal; steps 3--5 are statistical
diagnostics and degrade the permitted behaviour along Table~\ref{tab:fallback-ladder}
rather than aborting; step 7 runs for the lifetime of the deployment. The gate fails
closed: an \emph{unevaluated} check counts as a failed check, so a pipeline that does not
yet supply deployment evidence or unit labels returns a restricted decision rather than a
clean one.

\subsection{Diagnostics, and what each one cannot do}\label{sec:diagnostics}

\small

\label{tab:diagnostics}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.24\columnwidth}p{0.34\columnwidth}p{0.34\columnwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Diagnostic & Detects & Cannot show \\
\midrule
Evidence-support overlap
  & deployment evidence outside the calibrated envelope; high
    calibration-vs-deployment separability
  & that low separability implies transfer \\[2pt]
Radius stability
  & $\varepsilon$ or the action mix depending on an arbitrary choice of fold, seed,
    domain or block
  & that a stable radius is a correct radius \\[2pt]
Risk-alignment audit
  & benefit-sign errors, rank decorrelation, coverage varying by evidence region
  & anything about a condition with no labels; it is retrospective by construction \\[2pt]
Leakage audit
  & target labels or test-set influence entering candidate, threshold or
    hyperparameter choice
  & analyst decisions outside the recorded protocol \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Evidence-support overlap.} We report the distance to the nearest calibration
unit, the fraction of deployment evidence outside the calibration envelope, a
cross-fitted domain-classifier AUROC, and the maximum standardised feature deviation.
High separability is evidence \emph{against} direct transfer; low separability is not
evidence for it, since two conditions can be indistinguishable in $Z$ and still have
different $\Delta$ --- which is exactly the configuration \S\ref{sec:theory}
constructs, not a corner case. This is the same one-sidedness already noted for the
separability screen in \S\ref{sec:limits}.

\paragraph{Radius stability.} We recompute $\varepsilon$ leaving out one unit at a time
and report the mean radius, its range and coefficient of variation, and the
decision-disagreement rate against the pooled radius. The number that matters is the
fraction of promoted conclusions that change under an alternative \emph{equally valid}
split: a conclusion that survives only one admissible clustering is a conclusion about
the clustering. The check is not an outlier detector --- a contaminated fraction well
below $\alpha$ does not move the quantile and should not.

\paragraph{Risk-alignment audit.} Where evaluation labels exist we report
benefit-estimation error, rank correlation between predicted and realised benefit,
coverage stratified by evidence region, false-adapt by domain or cluster, and whether
benefit \emph{sign} prediction transfers across domains. This is a retrospective audit of
(A2) on the tracks we evaluated. It is not available at deployment time, which is the
entire difficulty.

\paragraph{Leakage audit.} For each promoted claim we record when the candidate was
fixed, when the calibration design was fixed, whether target labels were accessed,
whether the test set influenced hyperparameters, the protocol-lock identifier, and
whether failed runs were retained. Chronology is the evidence for (A6); an assertion of
pre-registration without a timestamped lock is not, and the audit fails closed on a
missing record rather than assuming the benign case.

results, so the assumption state travels with the number instead of living in prose.
Every value is computed from raw units by \path{kga.assumptions}; none is transcribed by
hand. The records are released with the artifacts.

\begin{remark}[Reading a report]\label{rem:report-reading}
\texttt{theoretical\_coverage\_claimed} is \texttt{false} on every track in this paper.
That is not a formatting artefact: there is no track on which we are willing to assert
\eqref{eq:cov-theoretical} for an unseen deployment condition, because (A1)--(A3) are not
checkable from the evidence we hold. \texttt{observed\_coverage} is the statistic of
Definition~\ref{def:cov-observed}, reported with \texttt{n\_units} and an interval
computed at the declared unit, so that a coverage figure computed on correlated cells
cannot be read as though it came from that many independent draws.
\end{remark}
