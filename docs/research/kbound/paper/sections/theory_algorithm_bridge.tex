\subsection{From the Population Frontier to KGA}
\label{sec:theory-algorithm-bridge}

The population frontier and the empirical certificate concern the same target
functional, but they certify it in different ways. Under
Assumption~\ref{ass:deploy} and binary $0/1$ loss,
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} gives the exact bridge
\begin{equation}
  \Delta
  = 2\mu_T(D)(M+\gamma),
  \qquad
  \widehat\Delta=h_\theta(Z),
  \qquad
  \widehat\Delta-\Delta
  =h_\theta(Z)-2\mu_T(D)(M+\gamma).
  \label{eq:population-empirical-bridge}
\end{equation}
Thus $M+\gamma$ determines the population benefit direction, while
$\widehat\Delta$ estimates the resulting benefit $\Delta$ directly.  The
calibrated radius $\varepsilon$ covers the total benefit-estimation residual in
the last expression; it neither estimates $\beta$ nor decomposes that residual
into observable margin and latent drift.  In particular, the reported KGA rule
does not compute $M$, $\gamma$, or $\beta$ at deployment.

\begin{proposition}[Population-to-KGA implication chain]
\label{prop:theory-algorithm-bridge}
Fix the outer population class
$\mathcal C_\beta=\{P_T:|\gamma(P_T)|\le\beta\}$ and an operational
calibration/deployment class $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{KGA}}\subseteq\mathcal C_\beta$.
Let the realized target law belong to $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{KGA}}$.  Suppose the
following chain is declared before the deployment unit is evaluated.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Population specification.} Assumption~\ref{ass:deploy} holds,
    $M$ is determined on each augmented-evidence fibre, and the class has the
    richness required by Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}.
  \item \textbf{Operational risk alignment.} The evidence $Z$ is risk-aligned
    on $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{KGA}}$ in the sense of
    Definition~\ref{def:risk-align}.  This is an additional restriction used
    for estimator transfer, not a consequence of fitting $h_\theta$ and not a
    claim that the full class $\mathcal C_\beta$ is risk-aligned.
  \item \textbf{Estimable benefit.} A measurable predictor
    $\widehat\Delta=h_\theta(Z)$ is fixed using permitted development data and
    without labels from the deployment unit.
  \item \textbf{Calibration validity.} A nonnegative radius $\varepsilon$ is
    fixed so that, over the declared calibration and deployment randomness,
    \begin{equation}
      \Pr\!\left(|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon\right)
      \ge 1-\alpha.
      \label{eq:bridge-coverage}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Then two distinct conclusions follow.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] If $|M|>\beta$, the population frontier supports the unique
    strict action with direction $\sign M$; if $|M|\le\beta$, no strict action
    is uniformly directionally sound over the full declared class and the
    pointwise maximal population rule abstains.
  \item[(b)] The empirical rule
    \[
      \widehat\Delta-\varepsilon>0\Rightarrow\textsc{adapt},
      \qquad
      \widehat\Delta+\varepsilon<0\Rightarrow\textsc{freeze},
    \]
    and \textsc{abstain} otherwise, controls the marginal false-adapt and
    false-freeze events at level $\alpha$ as in
    Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate}.
  \item[(c)] On the coverage event in~\eqref{eq:bridge-coverage}, whenever
    $|M|>\beta$ and KGA commits, its action agrees with the population-frontier
    direction.  KGA may nevertheless abstain because its finite-sample interval
    is too wide.  Conversely, a KGA commitment at $|M|\le\beta$ is a marginal
    statement about the narrower operational sampling law under
    \eqref{eq:bridge-coverage}; it does not establish uniform directional
    soundness over $\mathcal C_\beta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Part~(a) is Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}.  Part~(b) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate}.  On the coverage event, an empirical
\textsc{adapt} decision implies $\Delta>0$ and an empirical \textsc{freeze}
decision implies $\Delta<0$.  When $|M|>\beta$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} and $|\gamma|\le\beta$ give
$\sign\Delta=\sign M$, proving the agreement statement in~(c).  The remaining
claims distinguish the pointwise interval event for the realized law from the
uniform quantification over all laws in $\mathcal C_\beta$.
\end{proof}

The logical chain is therefore
\[
  \boxed{\mathcal C_\beta\ \text{declared}}
  \longrightarrow
  \boxed{\mathcal P_{\mathrm{KGA}}\subseteq\mathcal C_\beta\
         \ \text{risk-aligned for }Z}
  \longrightarrow
  \boxed{\widehat\Delta=h_\theta(Z)}
  \longrightarrow
  \boxed{\Pr(|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon)\ge1-\alpha}
  \longrightarrow
  \boxed{\textsc{adapt}/\textsc{freeze}/\textsc{abstain}}.
\]
The arrows are not all learned by KGA.  Declaring a credible outer class and
drift budget, specifying the narrower operational class, defending risk
alignment there, and justifying calibration transfer at the correct inference
unit remain external validity obligations.  KGA implements the
evidence-to-benefit predictor, residual-radius construction, and interval
decision rule.  Once coverage is assumed, risk alignment is not an additional
logical premise of Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate}; it instead supports the
transfer interpretation and the possibility of obtaining a useful, finite
interval.  The two classes are essential: the matched worlds used by
Theorem~\ref{lem:nonid} make the rich $\mathcal C_\beta$ non-risk-aligned inside
the population band.  If an operational class retains those worlds, a valid
interval must absorb their ambiguity through width and abstention rather than
silently assuming it away.

\paragraph{Plain-language interpretation.}
K-Bound says what a whole declared family of target worlds permits: if every
allowed drift preserves one strict direction, that direction is population
sound; otherwise the maximal uniform rule abstains.  KGA addresses the realized
deployment unit under a separately declared operational transfer model: it
predicts the actual risk difference and commits only when a calibrated interval
excludes zero.  The common object is $\Delta$; the
population theorem brackets it through $(M,\gamma,\beta)$, whereas KGA brackets
it through $(\widehat\Delta,\varepsilon)$.  The algorithm therefore realizes
the framework's adapt/freeze/abstain semantics, but it does not turn the
external population assumptions into data-derived facts.

\subsection{Robust Conformal Extensions for Real-World Deployment}
\label{sec:robust-conformal-extensions}

When deployed across heterogeneous real-world environments, two practical calibration failure modes can arise:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Domain non-exchangeability (Unit mismatch).} If calibration cells originate from development environments $\{g_1, \dots, g_G\}$ but a novel target deployment exhibits higher drift variance, standard pooled conformal calibration can under-cover. KGA resolves this via \emph{Worst-Group Domain Conformal Calibration}:
  \begin{equation}
    \varepsilon_{\mathrm{robust}} = \max_{g \in \{1, \dots, G\}} \mathrm{conformal\_radius}(R_g, \alpha),
    \label{eq:worst-group-radius}
  \end{equation}
  which guarantees $1-\alpha$ coverage over the worst-case development cluster and restores target coverage under domain shift.
  \item \textbf{Estimator overfitting.} If the benefit predictor $\widehat\Delta = h_\theta(Z)$ overfits development data, in-fold residuals underestimate prediction error. KGA enforces \emph{Out-of-Fold (OOF) Jackknife Calibration}: residuals $R_j^{\mathrm{OOF}} = |\widehat\Delta_{-k}(Z_j) - \Delta_j|$ are computed strictly on held-out folds, ensuring $\varepsilon$ absorbs estimator prediction error and forces safe \textsc{abstention} whenever $\widehat\Delta$ mispredicts benefit direction.
\end{enumerate}
