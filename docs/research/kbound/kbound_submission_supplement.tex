\section{A Rigorous Bridge from Cell Coverage to Population Coverage}
\label{app:population-transfer}

The formal theory of K-Bound (\KBMainRef{sec:compact-theory}{Section}{the main theory section} through \KBMainRef{sec:compact-budget}{Section}{the main residual-budget section}) characterizes the
exact population partial-identification frontier under a declared bound $\beta$ on the hidden
disagreement-conditional residual $\gamma$. In contrast, the empirical gate KGA (\KBMainRef{sec:compact-kga}{Section}{the main KGA specification})
predicts measured evaluation-cell benefit $\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$ and constructs a prediction
interval $[\widehat\Delta-\varepsilon, \widehat\Delta+\varepsilon]$. This appendix provides the
rigorous mathematical bridge between these two formulations, corrects a common dimensional
misconception, provides a formal counterexample to naive transfer, and establishes sufficient
conditions under which an enlarged conformal interval provably covers population benefit $\Delta$.

\subsection{Scale and Structural Differences Between Deductive and Inductive Bounds}
\label{app:unit-scaling}

It is tempting to conjecture that an empirical gate could directly enforce the population frontier
by setting its empirical conformal radius equal to the population residual bound, $\varepsilon = \beta$.
This conjecture is fundamentally false: $\varepsilon$ and $\beta$ represent different mathematical objects
living on different scales.

Recall the exact disagreement-region reduction under binary $0/1$ loss (\KBMainRef{lem:reduction}{Lemma}{the main disagreement-reduction lemma}):
\begin{equation}
 \Delta = 2d(M + \gamma), \qquad d = P_T(D) > 0,
 \label{eq:app-reduction}
\end{equation}
where $D = \{x : f_0(x) \neq f_a(x)\}$ is the disagreement region,
$M = \mathbb E[s(X) \mid D] - \frac{1}{2} \in [-\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}]$ is the observable disagreement margin,
and $\gamma = \mathbb E[\eta_a(X) - s(X) \mid D]$ is the hidden calibration residual. Under the externally declared
residual class $\mathcal C_\beta = \{P_T : |\gamma(P_T)| \le \beta\}$, the feasible population benefit set satisfies:
\begin{equation}
 |\Delta - 2dM| = 2d|\gamma| \le 2d\beta.
\end{equation}
Consequently, the unclipped population-benefit envelope centered at $2dM$ has radius
\begin{equation}
 r_{\mathrm{pop}} = 2d\beta,
\end{equation}
not $\beta$; the exact population benefit set $\Delta(\mathcal C_\beta)$ in \KBMainRef{thm:frontier}{Theorem}{the main frontier theorem}
includes clipping to $[-d, d]$: $\Delta(\mathcal C_\beta) = 2d[\max(-\tfrac12, M-\beta), \min(\tfrac12, M+\beta)]$.
Here $d = P_T(D) \in (0, 1]$ is the disagreement probability, so the scaling factor $2d \in (0, 2]$ is twice that probability.
For example, with typical values $d = 0.20$, $M = 0.15$, and $\beta = 0.05$, the population-benefit interval is:
\begin{align*}
 2(0.20)[0.15 - 0.05, \; 0.15 + 0.05] &= 0.40[0.10, \; 0.20] \\
 &= [0.04, \; 0.08].
\end{align*}
The interval center is $0.06$ and its half-width on the risk-reduction scale is $0.02$. The declared residual
budget is $\beta = 0.05$, whereas the benefit-scale radius is $0.02$. Equating $\varepsilon$ and $\beta$
conflates two fundamentally different inferential paradigms: $\beta$ bounds the absolute target disagreement-conditional calibration residual $|\gamma|$, whereas $\varepsilon$ is a calibrated prediction-error radius for the declared empirical cell-benefit target. Because \KBMainRef{thm:compact-beta-minimax}{Theorem}{3} proves $\beta$ cannot be audited
from label-free data alone, \KGA purposefully departs from the deductive parameter $\beta$ in favor of the inductive
empirical radius $\varepsilon$.

\subsection{Counterexample: Perfect Cell Prediction Does Not Imply Population Coverage}
\label{app:cell-counterexample}

We now prove that even an ideal empirical predictor with zero cell-prediction error ($\varepsilon = 0$) can
fail to cover population benefit and can commit to false adaptation with substantial probability.

\begin{proposition}[Failure of automatic cell-to-population transfer]\label{prop:cell-fail}
There exists a data-generating process, a fixed model pair $(f_0, f_a)$, and a benefit predictor $\widehat\Delta$
such that $\Pr(|\widehat\Delta - \Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}| = 0) = 1$ (perfect cell coverage with $\varepsilon = 0$),
yet the cell gate selects $\adapt$ with probability $0.45$ despite the true population benefit being strictly
negative ($\Delta = -0.10$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix binary predictors $f_0(x) \equiv 0$ and $f_a(x) \equiv 1$. Let the target input space consist of two points,
$\mathcal X = \{a, b\}$, with input distribution
\[
 \Pr(X = a) = 0.45, \qquad \Pr(X = b) = 0.55,
\]
and deterministic target labels $Y(a) = 1$ and $Y(b) = 0$.
The zero-one population risks are:
\[
 R_T(f_0) = \Pr(f_0(X) \neq Y) = \Pr(X = a) = 0.45,
\]
\[
 R_T(f_a) = \Pr(f_a(X) \neq Y) = \Pr(X = b) = 0.55.
\]
The true population benefit of candidate adaptation is strictly negative:
\[
 \Delta = R_T(f_0) - R_T(f_a) = 0.45 - 0.55 = -0.10 < 0.
\]
Now consider an evaluation cell consisting of a single independent target sample ($m=1$).
The measured cell benefit is
\begin{align*}
 \Delta^{\mathrm{cell}} &= \mathbf 1\{f_a(X) = Y\} - \mathbf 1\{f_0(X) = Y\} \\
 &=
 \begin{cases}
 +1, & X = a, \\
 -1, & X = b.
 \end{cases}
\end{align*}
Define the fixed label-free cell predictor $h(a) = +1$ and $h(b) = -1$. This predictor observes only the
unlabeled input $X$ and achieves perfect prediction of the cell outcome:
\[
 |\widehat\Delta - \Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}| = |h(X) - \Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}| = 0 \quad \text{almost surely.}
\]
A conformal interval with radius $\varepsilon = 0$ achieves $100\%$ marginal coverage of $\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$.
However, whenever $X = a$ (which occurs with probability $0.45$), $h(a) - \varepsilon = +1 > 0$, triggering
an $\adapt$ decision. The probability of false adaptation is therefore:
\[
 \Pr(g = \adapt, \; \Delta \le 0) = \Pr(X = a) = 0.45.
\]
Specializing this to residual notation with constant score $s(x) \equiv 1/2$, the disagreement region is $D = \{a, b\}$,
$d = 1$, $M = 0$, and $\gamma = \mathbb E[\eta_a(X) - s(X) \mid D] = 0.45 - 0.50 = -0.05$.
The tightest valid residual bound is $\beta = 0.05$, whereas the empirical cell-prediction radius is $\varepsilon = 0$.
Thus $\varepsilon = 0 \neq 0.05 = \beta$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:cell-fail} demonstrates why empirical cell-level validity cannot be equated with
population-level safety: a gate that accurately predicts small-batch or single-cell advantages can repeatedly
commit to globally harmful adaptations.

\subsection{Formal Protocol for Cell-to-Population Transfer}
\label{app:formal-transfer-protocol}

To rigorously bridge cell prediction to population benefit, we specify a formal statistical protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(P\arabic*)}]
 \item \textbf{Independent Estimator Fitting:} The benefit predictor $h$ and feature extractor $\phi$ are fitted
 on historical development episodes $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dev}}$ and frozen prior to calibration and deployment.
 \item \textbf{Representative Episode Exchangeability:} Conditional on the frozen development
 data $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dev}}$ (which determines the fitted predictor $h$ and feature representation $\phi$),
 the $N$ calibration episodes $(Z_1, B_1), \dots, (Z_N, B_N)$ and the deployment episode $(Z_*, B_*)$
 are exchangeable draws from a deployment meta-distribution $\mathcal Q$. Each episode $e$ defines an environment $P_e$.
 \item \textbf{Pre-Evaluation Candidate Isolation:} For each episode $e$, candidate $f_{a,e} = \mathcal A(f_0, X_{e, \mathrm{adapt}})$
 is constructed from its unlabeled adaptation window, and its parameter and normalization states are strictly
 frozen prior to scoring.
 \item \textbf{Fresh Evaluation Sample:} Evaluation cell $e$ comprises $m$ fresh, conditionally i.i.d.\ draws
 $(X_{e,j}, Y_{e,j})_{j=1}^m \sim P_e$ that are independent of the adaptation data.
 \item \textbf{Matched Risk Metric:} The cell outcome is the sample paired accuracy difference
 $B_e = \Delta_e^{\mathrm{cell}} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^m W_{e,j}$, where
 $W_{e,j} = \mathbf 1\{f_{a,e}(X_{e,j}) = Y_{e,j}\} - \mathbf 1\{f_0(X_{e,j}) = Y_{e,j}\} \in [-1, 1]$,
 matching the population risk reduction $\Delta_e = R_{P_e}(f_0) - R_{P_e}(f_{a,e})$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Theorem and 4-Step Proof of Population Risk Control}
\label{app:transfer-proof}

Under protocol \textbf{(P1)}--\textbf{(P5)}, we prove that an enlarged decision interval achieves finite-sample
population-benefit coverage and strict directional error control.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-sample population benefit certificate]\label{thm:app-pop-cert}
Under protocol \textbf{(P1)}--\textbf{(P5)}, let $\alpha_{\mathrm{cell}} \in (0, 1)$ and $\delta \in (0, 1)$ be
predeclared error budgets such that $\alpha_{\mathrm{cell}} + \delta < 1$. Let $\varepsilon$ be the order-statistic
split-conformal radius over $N$ calibration residuals:
\begin{equation}
 k = \lceil(N+1)(1-\alpha_{\mathrm{cell}})\rceil, \qquad
 \varepsilon = \begin{cases} r_{(k)}, & k \le N, \\ +\infty, & k > N. \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Define the finite-sample concentration radius
\begin{equation}
 b(m, \delta) = \min\left\{2, \; \sqrt{\frac{2\log(2/\delta)}{m}}\right\}.
 \label{eq:app-hoeffding-radius}
\end{equation}
Then for a new deployment episode $* \sim \mathcal Q$, the compound interval
$I_{\mathrm{population}} = [\widehat\Delta_* - (\varepsilon + b), \; \widehat\Delta_* + (\varepsilon + b)]$
satisfies:
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\bigl(\Delta_* \in I_{\mathrm{population}}\bigr) \ge 1 - \alpha_{\mathrm{cell}} - \delta.
\end{equation}
Furthermore, the three-way decision rule
\begin{equation}
 g_* = \begin{cases}
 \adapt, & \widehat\Delta_* - (\varepsilon + b) > 0, \\
 \freeze, & \widehat\Delta_* + (\varepsilon + b) < 0, \\
 \abstain, & \text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
controls the marginal probability of committing to an incorrect strict direction:
\begin{align}
 \Pr\bigl(\{g_* = \adapt, \Delta_* \le 0\} &\cup \{g_* = \freeze, \Delta_* \ge 0\}\bigr) \notag \\
 &\le \alpha_{\mathrm{cell}} + \delta.
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof proceeds in four discrete steps:

\paragraph{Step 1: Conformal coverage of measured cell benefit.}
By \textbf{(P1)} and \textbf{(P2)}, the residuals $r_e = |h(Z_e) - B_e|$ for $e \in \{1, \dots, N, *\}$ are
exchangeable random variables conditional on the frozen development data $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dev}}$
(and hence conditional on $h$ and $\phi$). By standard split-conformal quantile
properties~\cite{tibshirani2019conformal}, the $k$-th order statistic guarantees marginal coverage:
\begin{equation*}
 \Pr\bigl(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conf}}\bigr) \ge 1 - \alpha_{\mathrm{cell}},
\end{equation*}
where $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conf}} = \{|h(Z_*) - B_*| \le \varepsilon\}$.

\paragraph{Step 2: Hoeffding concentration of sample benefit to population benefit.}
Conditioned on environment $P_*$ and the frozen model pair $(f_0, f_{a,*})$, the variables
$W_{*,j} = \mathbf 1\{f_{a,*}(X_{*,j}) = Y_{*,j}\} - \mathbf 1\{f_0(X_{*,j}) = Y_{*,j}\}$ are independent and bounded
in $[-1, 1]$ with range $c = 1 - (-1) = 2$. Their conditional expectation is the true population benefit:
\[
 \mathbb E[W_{*,j} \mid P_*, f_0, f_{a,*}] = R_{P_*}(f_0) - R_{P_*}(f_{a,*}) = \Delta_*.
\]
By Hoeffding's inequality for the sample average $B_* = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^m W_{*,j}$:
\begin{align*}
 \Pr\left(|B_* - \Delta_*| > t \;\middle|\; P_*, f_0, f_{a,*}\right)
 &\le 2\exp\left(-\frac{2m^2 t^2}{m(2)^2}\right) \\
 &= 2\exp\left(-\frac{m t^2}{2}\right).
\end{align*}
Setting the right-hand side equal to $\delta$ yields $t = \sqrt{2\log(2/\delta)/m}$. Since $B_*$ and $\Delta_*$ both
lie in $[-1, 1]$, their difference cannot exceed $2$. Hence, defining $b(m, \delta) = \min\{2, \sqrt{2\log(2/\delta)/m}\}$,
we have unconditionally:
\begin{equation*}
 \Pr\bigl(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conc}}\bigr) \ge 1 - \delta,
\end{equation*}
where $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conc}} = \{|B_* - \Delta_*| \le b(m, \delta)\}$.

\paragraph{Step 3: Compound interval coverage.}
On the intersection event $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conf}} \cap \mathcal E_{\mathrm{conc}}$, the triangle inequality gives:
\[
 |h(Z_*) - \Delta_*| \le |h(Z_*) - B_*| + |B_* - \Delta_*| \le \varepsilon + b(m, \delta).
\]
Applying the union bound to the complements:
\begin{align*}
 \Pr\bigl(\Delta_* \in I_{\mathrm{population}}\bigr)
 &\ge \Pr\bigl(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conf}} \cap \mathcal E_{\mathrm{conc}}\bigr) \\
 &\ge 1 - \Pr(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conf}}^c) - \Pr(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conc}}^c) \\
 &\ge 1 - \alpha_{\mathrm{cell}} - \delta.
\end{align*}
Crucially, this step requires no independence assumption between $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conf}}$ and $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conc}}$.

\paragraph{Step 4: Strict directional error bound.}
Suppose $g_* = \adapt$, which requires $h(Z_*) - (\varepsilon + b) > 0$. On the coverage event
$\{\Delta_* \in I_{\mathrm{population}}\}$, this implies $\Delta_* \ge h(Z_*) - (\varepsilon + b) > 0$.
Therefore, a false adaptation ($g_* = \adapt$ and $\Delta_* \le 0$) can occur only on the complement event
$\Delta_* \notin I_{\mathrm{population}}$. Symmetrically, a false freeze ($g_* = \freeze$ and $\Delta_* \ge 0$)
also requires $\Delta_* \notin I_{\mathrm{population}}$. Since the actions $\adapt$ and $\freeze$ are mutually
exclusive, the union of false commitments satisfies:
\begin{align*}
 &\{g_* = \adapt, \Delta_* \le 0\} \cup \{g_* = \freeze, \Delta_* \ge 0\} \\
 &\qquad\subseteq \{\Delta_* \notin I_{\mathrm{population}}\}.
\end{align*}
Taking probabilities yields the claimed bound $\le \alpha_{\mathrm{cell}} + \delta$.
\end{proof}

As an illustrative scenario, consider an evaluation cell with $m = 512$ samples (a common benchmark batch size),
a forecast benefit of $\widehat\Delta = +0.04$ (a $4\%$ point gain), conformal prediction error $\varepsilon = 0.015$,
and concentration risk budget $\delta = 0.05$. Here the Hoeffding radius is $b = 0.1200$, yielding a total half-width
$\varepsilon + b = 0.1350$. Even with a positive point prediction, the population lower bound is:
\[
 \widehat\Delta - (\varepsilon + b) = 0.04 - 0.1350 = -0.0950 < 0.
\]
The interval spans zero, forcing the population decision rule to retain the frozen predictor and record \textbf{ABSTAIN}.
Holding the prediction $\widehat\Delta = 0.04$ and cell radius $\varepsilon = 0.015$ fixed, certifying a positive
population benefit requires $\widehat\Delta - \varepsilon - b(m, \delta) > 0$, or:
\begin{align*}
 0.04 - 0.015 - \sqrt{\frac{2\log(2/\delta)}{m}} > 0 &\iff m > \frac{2\log(40)}{(0.025)^2} \\
 &\approx 11{,}804.41.
\end{align*}
Thus $m = 11{,}805$ is the smallest integer satisfying this specific condition. We emphasize that this is an
illustrative calculation from this particular Hoeffding bound holding the prediction and cell radius fixed, not
an experimental finding or a universal sample requirement for certifying a $4\%$ gain under other procedures or assumptions.

This calculation illustrates the potential commitment cost of the proposed population interval. It does not
identify the cause of abstention or freezing in the reported benchmarks, whose gates did not use this sampling
correction. Establishing a necessary sample-size requirement would require a separate lower-bound argument.
Furthermore, the empirical outcome on CCT-20 was not universal abstention, but conservative retention:
KGA issued $44$ FREEZE decisions, $1$ ABSTAIN, and $0$ ADAPT. When evidence of positive benefit is insufficient,
a prudent gate must retain the frozen baseline and record ABSTAIN; FREEZE specifically requires a supported
negative interval, and uncertainty alone does not justify a directional freeze claim.

\subsection{Alternative Route: Direct Empirical Sampling of the Population Frontier}
\label{app:direct-sampled-frontier}

Finally, for completeness, we formalize an alternative operationalization that directly samples the theoretical
population frontier $|M| > \beta$ rather than training a learned benefit regressor $\widehat\Delta = h(Z)$.

\begin{proposition}[Sampled population frontier]\label{prop:sampled-frontier}
Let $(f_0, f_a)$ be fixed binary predictors and let $s : \mathcal X \to [0, 1]$ be a source-fitted score.
Suppose target inputs $X_1, \dots, X_m \sim \mu_T$ are drawn independently, and let
$D_m = \{i \in \{1, \dots, m\} : f_0(X_i) \neq f_a(X_i)\}$ be the observed disagreement indices with count $n_D = |D_m|$.
If $n_D > 0$, define the empirical disagreement margin:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat M = \frac{1}{n_D} \sum_{i \in D_m} s(X_i) - \frac{1}{2}.
\end{equation}
For confidence level $1 - \alpha \in (0, 1)$, define the margin concentration radius:
\begin{equation}
 r_M(n_D, \alpha) = \sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\alpha)}{2n_D}}.
\end{equation}
Under the declared residual class $\mathcal C_\beta = \{P_T : |\gamma(P_T)| \le \beta\}$, the decision rule
\begin{equation}
 g_{\mathrm{margin}} = \begin{cases}
 \adapt, & \widehat M - r_M > \beta, \\
 \freeze, & \widehat M + r_M < -\beta, \\
 \abstain, & \text{otherwise (or if } n_D = 0\text{)},
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
satisfies $\Pr(g_{\mathrm{margin}} = \adapt, \Delta \le 0) \le \alpha$ and $\Pr(g_{\mathrm{margin}} = \freeze, \Delta \ge 0) \le \alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditional on $n_D > 0$, the disagreement samples $\{X_i : i \in D_m\}$ are i.i.d.\ draws from the conditional
marginal $\mu_T \mid D$. Since $s(X) \in [0, 1]$, Hoeffding's inequality for variables of range $1$ gives:
\begin{align*}
 \Pr\left(|\widehat M - M| > r_M \;\middle|\; n_D\right)
 &\le 2\exp\left(-\frac{2n_D^2 r_M^2}{n_D(1)^2}\right) \\
 &= 2\exp\left(-2n_D r_M^2\right) = \alpha.
\end{align*}
On the coverage event $|\widehat M - M| \le r_M$, the condition $\widehat M - r_M > \beta$ implies:
\[
 M \ge \widehat M - r_M > \beta \ge |\gamma| \ge -\gamma \implies M + \gamma > 0.
\]
By the disagreement reduction (\KBMainRef{lem:reduction}{Lemma}{the main disagreement-reduction lemma}), $\Delta = 2d(M + \gamma) > 0$.
Thus, a false adaptation requires $|\widehat M - M| > r_M$, which has probability at most $\alpha$.
The freeze case is identical by symmetry.
\end{proof}

This direct route demonstrates the additive relationship between uncertainties:
\begin{align*}
 \text{decision threshold} ={} &\underbrace{r_M(n_D, \alpha)}_{\text{finite-sample margin uncertainty}} \\
 &+ \;\underbrace{\beta}_{\text{structural calibration uncertainty}}.
\end{align*}
While this rule directly embodies \KBMainRef{thm:frontier}{Theorem}{the main frontier theorem}, it still requires an externally declared
budget $\beta$, proving once again that label-free data alone cannot resolve structural non-identifiability.

\subsection{Explicit Implementation Boundaries and Operational Scope}
\label{app:implementation-boundaries}

To maintain absolute transparency regarding theoretical constructs versus empirical evaluations,
we explicitly delineate three distinct objects formulated in this paper:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Empirical KGA ($\widehat\Delta \pm \varepsilon$):}
 The cell-benefit prediction interval uses the residual construction specified for each study
 (\KBMainRef{sec:compact-kga}{Section}{the main KGA specification}). Current primary CIFAR results,
 the 108-cell ImageNet-C replay, and the corrected 27-cell SAR audit use Protocol B's
 separate fitting, calibration, and scoring roles. Historical pooled leave-one-out
 analyses retain Protocol A's outcome-dependence limitation; natural studies retain
 their separately documented data partitions. These are cell-benefit decisions, not population certificates.
 \item \textbf{Population Extension ($\widehat\Delta \pm (\varepsilon + b)$):}
 The compound decision interval incorporating finite-sample Hoeffding concentration $b(m, \delta)$
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:app-pop-cert}). This procedure is exercised under controlled synthetic diagnostic
 simulations with exact ground truth (an observed $100\%$ empirical population-inclusion rate and zero
 wrong-direction commitments in the finite trials); it is
 not a population guarantee for the historical empirical benchmark tables, which evaluate transductive
 evaluation-cell benefit rather than independent population draws. The separate 108-cell benchmark
 replay below exercises the enlarged interval as a sensitivity analysis, not as fresh-sample validation.
 \item \textbf{Sampled Frontier Gate ($\widehat M \pm r_M$ under external $\beta$):}
 The direct finite-sample operationalization of the knowability frontier (Proposition~\ref{prop:sampled-frontier}).
 This provides a formal alternative requiring an externally supplied residual bound $\beta$; it has no
 real-data benchmark instantiation in this paper.
\end{enumerate}
Neither the population transfer theorem nor the sampled frontier retroactively converts the empirical
benchmark results into population-safety claims.

\paragraph{Synthetic population simulation protocol and evidence.}
To empirically evaluate the compound interval $I_{\mathrm{population}} = [\widehat\Delta - (\varepsilon + b), \; \widehat\Delta + (\varepsilon + b)]$
under exact ground truth, we conducted a diagnostic simulation.
In each episode, an environment covariate state $Z \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$ is drawn with equal probability ($1/3$ each) and held fixed while $m$ test examples are sampled with conditional label distributions
$\Pr(Y=1 \mid Z=-1)=0.20$ ($\Delta = -0.60$, harmful), $\Pr(Y=1 \mid Z=0)=0.50$ ($\Delta = 0.00$, tie/null), and $\Pr(Y=1 \mid Z=1)=0.80$ ($\Delta = +0.60$, helpful).
The benefit estimator $\widehat\Delta(Z) = 0.6Z$ is the exact Bayes-optimal predictor.
Calibration draws $N_{\mathrm{cal}}$ independent episodes to form residual pool $\{r_c = |\widehat\Delta(Z_c) - b_c|\}$.
Risk parameters are $\alpha_{\mathrm{cell}} = 0.05$ and $\delta_{\mathrm{sampling}} = 0.05$ (total population error budget $\alpha_{\mathrm{pop}} = 0.10$).
Across calibration sizes $N_{\mathrm{cal}} \in \{18, 19, 39, 99\}$ and evaluation batch sizes $m \in \{32, 128, 512, 2048\}$ over 300 independent trials per configuration
(Table~\ref{tab:synthetic-pop-diagnostic}), empirical coverage and action distributions confirm:
(1)~at $N_{\mathrm{cal}}=18$, exact rank $k = \lceil 19 \times 0.95 \rceil = 19 > 18$ is infeasible, setting $\varepsilon = \infty$ and $100\%$ abstention (fail-closed rank protection);
(2)~at $m=32$, the sampling radius $b(32, 0.05) = 0.4802$ combined with calibration radius $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cell}} \approx 0.28$ yields compound width $\varepsilon + b \approx 0.76 > 0.60$, bracketing zero across all three states and enforcing $100\%$ abstention (the Price of Rigor);
(3)~at $m \ge 128$, the gate safely commits on non-zero states while abstaining on ties ($\approx 33.3\%$, matching the null frequency);
(4)~wrong-direction error is $0.0000$ across all 16 configurations ($\le \alpha_{\mathrm{pop}} = 0.10$).
An observed $100\%$ population coverage rate on finite simulation trials reflects consistency under the simulated model, not a mathematical proof of universal coverage.
Full summary records are archived in the supplementary repository.

\paragraph{Non-oracle population diagnostic.}
To evaluate the setting where the benefit predictor $\widehat\Delta$ is not an oracle Bayes rule but an imperfect empirical model fitted on finite development episodes, we conducted an extended non-oracle diagnostic (1{,}000 independent trials per configuration; archived in the supplementary repository).
In each trial, an imperfect linear predictor is fitted on 15 independent development episodes ($m=32$) subject to estimation noise. We then evaluate three decision mechanisms across the $(N_{\mathrm{cal}}, m)$ grid (Table~\ref{tab:nonoracle-pop-diagnostic}):
(1)~\textbf{Point Rule ($\operatorname{sign}(\widehat\Delta)$):} Blindly committing to adaptation whenever $\widehat\Delta > 0$ achieves $0\%$ abstention, but incurs a $31\%$--$36\%$ wrong-direction commitment error rate; crucially, $100\%$ of these errors occur on null/tie states ($Z=0$, $\Delta=0$) due to random estimation noise around zero, with strictly zero errors on harmful states ($\Delta = -0.6$).
(2)~\textbf{Cell Interval ($\widehat\Delta \pm \varepsilon_{\mathrm{cell}}$):} Conformal residual calibration over the imperfect predictions guarantees valid empirical cell coverage ($93.9\%$--$96.5\%$, tightly tracking $1-\alpha_{\mathrm{cell}} = 0.95$) and eliminates wrong-direction error ($0.0000$). Furthermore, in this simulated model, the cell interval already achieves $\approx 100\%$ empirical population coverage of $\Delta$ across all configurations.
(3)~\textbf{Population Interval ($\widehat\Delta \pm (\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cell}} + b)$):} By incorporating the Hoeffding sampling concentration $b(m, \delta)$, the compound interval achieved an observed $100\%$ empirical population-inclusion rate and zero wrong-direction error in these finite trials ($0.0000 \le \alpha_{\mathrm{pop}}$). At $m=32$, it enforces the Price of Rigor through $99.9\%$--$100\%$ abstention as a consequence of the universal Hoeffding penalty ($b(32) = 0.4802$), while at $m \ge 128$ it safely commits on non-zero states while withholding action on ties ($\approx 32\%$--$34\%$ abstention, matching the $33.3\%$ null state frequency).

\subsubsection{Published Baseline Reproducibility Audit (Task 3 Closeout)}
\label{app:baseline-audit}

We surveyed third-party published baseline implementations. Upstream Kim et al.~(TTA / ALine) was unavailable for native execution due to upstream repository deprecation.

To provide a synchronized head-to-head comparison against a closely related published comparator on the exact same conditions, we evaluated a deployment recovery decision rule built from the authenticated native AETTA accuracy estimator~\cite{lee2024aetta} (official AETTA implementation~\cite{lee2024aetta}) across all 36 conditions of the mixed-sign panel across all three independent ResNet-18 source checkpoints (108 evaluations total; Table~\ref{tab:task1_independent_models}, Panel~C). The comparison equalizes source model, adapted candidate, target conditions, evaluation records, and test-time label access, but not the development-information budget: KGA uses historical labeled shift outcomes for benefit-model fitting and calibration, whereas AETTA uses its published label-free accuracy-estimation mechanism. The AETTA estimator was evaluated using its official 10-draw MC-dropout accuracy estimator ($p=0.4$), and the deployment recovery decision rule retains updates (\adapt) only if post-adaptation accuracy is estimated not to degrade relative to the frozen model ($\widehat{a}_{\mathrm{adapt}} \ge \widehat{a}_{\mathrm{frozen}}$) and estimated accuracy remains above the absolute hard-reset threshold ($100 \times \widehat{a}_{\mathrm{adapt}} \ge 20.0$); otherwise the model recovers to the frozen state (\freeze).

(1)~Under leave-one-out calibration (within-model leave-one-condition-out calibration on the evaluated checkpoint, which pools peer cells from the same model), KGA achieves lower point oracle regret than the AETTA-based rule across every evaluated model: $0.08\%$ vs.\ $0.36\%$ on Model 0, $0.09\%$ vs.\ $0.18\%$ on Model 101, and $0.23\%$ vs.\ $0.36\%$ on Model 102 (a regret advantage of $+0.09$ to $+0.28\text{ pp}$), translating to higher realized test accuracy. The point ordering replicated across all three independently trained checkpoints; with three model instances, we treat this as replication evidence rather than population-level inference over model training.
(2)~Because this recovery rule operates as an unhedged binary choice without conformal uncertainty calibration, its accuracy estimator misjudges several severe collapse conditions, committing 4--5 observed false adaptations per model. Consequently, under the same descriptive 0.10 benchmark threshold used to summarize KGA, the AETTA-based rule yielded observed $\mathrm{FA}_u = 0.1111$--$0.1389$ across the three checkpoints ($0.1389$ on Model 0, $0.1111$ on Model 101, and $0.1389$ on Model 102).
(3)~In contrast, under LOO calibration, KGA leverages empirical residual calibration to abstain on borderline conditions (issuing 9--13 \abstain decisions), with zero observed false adaptations on Models 0 and 102 ($\mathrm{FA}_u = 0.0000$) and one on Model 101 ($\mathrm{FA}_u = 0.0278$, overall observed $\mathrm{FA}_u \le 0.0278$), while achieving lower point oracle regret than the AETTA-based rule and both fixed baselines. Complete per-condition traces and execution receipts are archived in the supplementary repository.

\subsection{Withdrawn Development Proxy for the Population Budget}
\label{app:withdrawn-beta-proxy}

K-Bound characterizes population benefit over a declared residual class $\mathcal C_\beta$.
KGA instead predicts measured evaluation-cell benefit $\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$ and uses a separately
calibrated residual interval. Its direct mathematical action implication is
\KBMainRef{thm:certificate}{Proposition}{the main marginal-error certificate}, conditional on coverage for that declared target.
It neither estimates the population residual budget $\beta$ nor numerically evaluates the population
frontier. Appendix~\ref{app:population-transfer} gives a separate population-transfer implication
requiring an additional sampling-error bound $b(m,\delta)$ and an enlarged radius $\varepsilon+b$.
The currently reported transductive benchmark panels do not establish these additional premises.

For the historical $\beta$ proxy, \KBMainRef{sec:compact-sensitivity}{Section}{the main sensitivity discussion} discloses the negative result.
The historical summary ($1.4$--$50\times$ undersizing, $24$--$73\%$ proxy interval violations,
and $0.4$--$16.6\%$ decision errors on CIFAR-10-C) represents the operational failure of a development-based
proxy heuristic, not a verified numerical measurement of latent theoretical-class violations on evaluation cells.
The raw sweep artifact is unavailable, and this estimation route is completely withdrawn. A credible
budget $\beta$ must come from domain knowledge, a declared coupling, or historical labeled deployments
under an explicit transfer assumption. Setting $\beta=0$ assumes zero target disagreement-conditional
residual; it is not a safe default.

\section{Calibration and Evaluation Contracts}
\label{app:compact-contracts}

\paragraph{Controlled-grid contract.}
The fitting unit is the condition cell. The current primary CIFAR and synchronized
internal-baseline tables use Protocol B: each score fold is excluded from both
estimator fitting and residual calibration, and those two subsets are disjoint.
Historical Protocol-A tables instead use leave-one-cell-out predictions and
peer-dependent residual calibration; excluding a cell's own residual does not
remove its indirect influence through other fitted predictions. The execution
mapping in \ref{sec:compact-repro} identifies the construction for each table.
Neither construction alone establishes exchangeability across corruption
families, checkpoints, or future environments. Saved-feature outcome-exclusion
tests verify their stated computational boundary, not full upstream independence.

\paragraph{Natural-shift contract.}
Development conditions fit the benefit model; a disjoint calibration partition supplies residuals;
choices are frozen; and the target partition is scored once where the archived protocol records all
three stages. When the saved evidence does not support that complete chain, the row is marked
descriptive or diagnostic. Replaying a table does not strengthen its original design.

The candidate TTA step itself is transductive. Episodic candidates update on each unlabeled
evaluation batch before predicting that batch. Online candidates update on a separate auxiliary
stream, but prediction still uses evaluation-batch BatchNorm statistics. Thus ``disjoint'' above
refers to development, residual-calibration, and target partitions (and to the recorded auxiliary
stream versus evaluation sample identities); it does not mean that candidate inference is
inductive. No target labels enter adaptation, prediction, evidence, or the action.

\paragraph{Action contract.}
Every action is computed from $Z$, $\widehat\Delta$, and $\varepsilon$ without target labels.
Offline labels determine $\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$, the measured oracle action, empirical regret,
and false-commit indicators only after the action is fixed. Existing artifact fields named
\texttt{delta} store this cell outcome; their schema and numerical values are unchanged.
Abstention resolves to the frozen prediction for measured task-performance accounting, while its log
retains the distinction from a certified freeze.

\paragraph{Configuration contract.}
The checkpoint, candidate adapter, optimizer, adapted parameter subset, update count, evidence
schema, feature normalization, benefit estimator, calibration split, $\alpha$, and software version
are part of one calibration identity. Changing any of them requires either a declared transfer
argument or recalibration. The fail-closed default on identity mismatch is non-commitment.

G_{bj}=\operatorname{regret}_{b,j}-\operatorname{regret}_{\mathrm{KGA},j}.
\]
A positive mean gap means lower regret for KGA. All paired routing-gap intervals reported in this
paper use this baseline-minus-KGA sign convention. The point-estimate two-comparator criterion requires both
means to be positive. Interval support additionally requires the lower confidence limit for both
paired means to exceed zero at the declared resampling unit. These definitions are intentionally separate:
Table~\ref{tab:compact-canonical} can establish a point ordering, but not an inference claim without
the associated unit, interval artifact, and justified coverage assumptions. A positive computed
lower endpoint is numerical interval support under the resampling model, not verified coverage.
Multiplicity adjustments do not repair invalid marginal intervals or tests.

Condition-level resampling treats cells as exchangeable and can understate uncertainty when
severities and compositions share a corruption family. Corruption-family resampling preserves
observed within-family dependence, but few clusters limit reliability; conservativeness is not
guaranteed. Seed resampling measures variation across repeated training or test streams only when
the seeds represent the intended deployment randomness. The compact paper therefore does not use
one generic ``bootstrap CI'' label. An inference claim must state whether the unit is cell,
corruption--severity pair, corruption family, seed, backbone, domain, or physical session.

\subsection{False-Adapt Accounting}

The event in \KBMainEqRef{eq:compact-fau}{the main paper's marginal false-ADAPT event} is unconditional and concerns measured cell benefit.
Its reported frequency divides ADAPT decisions with $\Delta_j^{\mathrm{cell}}\le0$ by all
evaluated units, including freezes and abstentions. This need not estimate the population-harm
event $\{g=\adapt,\Delta\le0\}$. Conditional false-adapt
divides by adapt decisions and is undefined when no adaptation occurs. A zero observed count should
be accompanied by the number of adapt decisions; otherwise a controller that never adapts appears
perfect. Table~\ref{tab:compact-actions} exposes that denominator.

If the scored residual is included in the same finite residual vector whose order statistic decides
the action, the number of residuals above the order statistic is algebraically constrained. Such
coverage is not an independent empirical validation. Excluding the scored residual removes direct
self-inclusion, but pooled LOO inclusion remains rank-constrained. It is not independent validation
and does not supply the exchangeability or stability needed for a finite-sample theorem. Clean
split calibration and LOO empirical calibration therefore remain different protocol labels.

\section{Extended KGA Implementation Contract}
\label{app:compact-implementation}

\subsection{State Isolation}

The frozen and candidate predictors must not share mutable normalization buffers, optimizer state,
or parameter storage during preview. The candidate is created from a checkpoint, adapted on the
unlabeled window, and evaluated without overwriting the active state. Only an adapt decision may
replace the active checkpoint. Freeze and abstain discard the temporary state. A unit test should
hash the active parameters before and after each non-adapt decision and require equality.

\subsection{Evidence Determinism}

Evidence extraction must define preprocessing, batch order, numerical precision, class ordering,
normalization constants, and missing-value behavior. Paired features require frozen and candidate
outputs on the same samples in the same order. Source statistics and ATC thresholds are immutable
calibration artifacts. Evidence schema versions should be explicit rather than inferred from vector
length, because two schemas can share a dimension while changing semantics.

\subsection{Calibration Identity}

The calibration identity should hash the source checkpoint, candidate adapter class, adapted
parameter names, optimizer and learning rate, update steps, evidence schema, benefit-model
hyperparameters, feature scaler, residual construction, $\alpha$, and inference-unit definition.
The radius belongs to that identity. Reusing it after a candidate or evidence change is not a benign
software optimization; it changes the random variable whose residual is being covered.

\subsection{Runtime Accounting}

A deployment report should time capture/preprocessing, frozen inference, candidate adaptation,
candidate inference, evidence extraction, benefit prediction, interval decision, copy creation, and
rollback separately. Mean and tail latency should be reported at the decision-window level. Peak
memory should include both model states and optimizer buffers. The compact manuscript leaves these
cells unfilled because the canonical cross-dataset manifest does not provide one comparable runtime
measurement. Omitting an unsupported number is preferable to quoting controller-only latency as
end-to-end cost.

\section{Prospective Natural-Shift Confirmation Protocol}
\label{app:compact-confirmation}

The sealed CCT-20 study supplies a completed natural-shift target evaluation. Its
\CCTCellCount{} checkpoint--location cells contain \CCTHelpfulCount{} helpful and
\CCTHarmfulCount{} harmful candidate updates, but KGA makes \CCTFreezeCount{} FREEZE,
\CCTAbstainCount{} ABSTAIN, and \CCTAdaptCount{} ADAPT decisions. It therefore matches

avoidance under natural shift. The predeclared stronger routing criterion was not met because the
policy never selected ADAPT and did not select the single helpful cell. A second
locked attempt on So2Sat-LCZ42 stopped at its development-feasibility gate with no eligible Tent or
SAR candidate. No held-out gate-calibration rows or target inputs were accessed. The following
protocol therefore describes a new versioned confirmation, not a continuation or reinterpretation
of v1.

All So2Sat v1 gate-fit outcomes are open and cannot be used to tune a fresh confirmatory arm. Its
target protocol also defines one action per city, whereas the current runner creates one action per
city--checkpoint cell. That mismatch did not affect the development stop, but the target runner
must remain disabled until a versioned lock resolves the action unit. The design below is future
evidence and is not counted as a completed target experiment in this paper.

\subsection{Lock Before Target Labels}

The protocol should name one source checkpoint, one candidate adapter, one evidence schema, one
benefit-model class, one calibration rule, one $\alpha$, and one target-domain family. Adapter
hyperparameters must be selected on development domains and then frozen. The lock should hash the
checkpoint, code revision, configuration, calibration records, and planned scoring script. It must
also name the test unit and the multiplicity family. A later change creates a new protocol version;
it does not overwrite an earlier development-stopped or incomplete arm.

Natural heterogeneity should be built through independently defined environments rather than by
pooling whichever benchmark conditions produce opposite signs. Examples include hospitals, camera
locations, laboratory batches, acquisition days, physical devices, or visual domains. Environment
membership may be observed at deployment, but the measured cell-benefit label remains hidden. The target
test environments should be absent from estimator fitting, residual calibration, threshold tuning,
and candidate selection.

\subsection{Three-Way Data Separation}

Development data fit $h_\theta$ and select the evidence pipeline. A separate calibration set
produces residuals for \KBMainEqRef{eq:compact-rank}{the main paper's calibration-rank rule}. The held-out target set is opened exactly once
after all software and configuration hashes match the lock. Splitting adjacent frames, patches, or
samples from the same session across these stages is leakage even when filenames differ. The split
must occur at the environment or session level that defines deployment exchangeability.

The target stream is replayed identically for always-freeze, always-adapt, simple gates, KGA without
a radius, and the full certificate. Model randomness should either be frozen for paired replay or
repeated through independently trained checkpoints declared in advance. Every policy receives the
same ordered windows. Labels are stored behind a separate audit interface and are unavailable to
the live decision process.

\subsection{Endpoints and Decision Rule}

The primary endpoint is paired regret against each fixed policy. Success requires both baseline
minus KGA regret gaps to be positive and both predeclared intervals to exclude zero, with coverage
justified for the declared estimand and sampling model. If the intended claim concerns population
risk rather than measured cells, the protocol must also justify that transfer. The false-adapt
endpoint is $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}$ with the adapt count
reported beside it. Secondary endpoints are $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}$, adapt rate, freeze rate,
abstain rate, commitment rate, task accuracy, and latency. Conditional false-adapt is descriptive
unless a separate selective-risk result is proved.

The primary certificate rule remains unchanged: adapt if
$\widehat\Delta-\varepsilon>0$, freeze if
$\widehat\Delta+\varepsilon<0$, and abstain otherwise. The experiment must not lower
$\varepsilon$, change $\alpha$, or select a different benefit regressor after observing target
labels. Exploratory sensitivity analyses may be reported, but they remain outside the primary bar.

Four outcomes are scientifically useful. An interval-supported reduction in regret relative to each

\section{Theorem Dependencies and Delimited Scope}
\label{app:theorem-dependencies}

The compact paper uses three related logical statements. First, the disagreement reduction is an
identity under the binary setup. Second, the population frontier is a uniform statement over a rich,
declared class. Third, the empirical certificate is a marginal implication from interval coverage.
The population statements concern $\Delta$; the reported empirical calibration concerns
$\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$. Connecting these targets requires an additional validity argument.

\paragraph{Why the boundary is separate.}
For $|M|<\beta$, the construction chooses two latent residuals that yield opposite nonzero benefits.
At $|M|=\beta>0$, one admissible residual can make the benefit zero while another retains the sign of
$M$; opposite nonzero signs need not exist. Both cases block a uniform strict commitment, but for
different reasons. At $M=\beta=0$, every law in the zero-residual class has zero benefit under the
model. Abstention remains the maximal action only under the paper's strict-direction convention,
not because either task-risk choice is worse.

\paragraph{Why a calibration residual need not be drift.}
Let $P_T=P_S$, with equiprobable inputs $a,b$, deterministic labels $Y(a)=1$, $Y(b)=0$,
and candidate $f_a\equiv1$. Set $f_0(a)=0$, $f_0(b)=1$, and $s\equiv\tfrac12$.
The candidate is correct on half the source inputs, so this constant score is source-calibrated
in the ordinary sense. Yet disagreement occurs only at $a$, where the candidate is always
correct. Thus $M=0$, $\gamma=\tfrac12$, and $\Delta=\tfrac12$, although the source and target
laws are identical. Conditioning on disagreement, not a distribution change, explains the
nonzero residual. The frontier requires a bound on this residual regardless of its cause.

\paragraph{Why coverage is the premise.}
The certificate proof is elementary once \KBMainEqRef{eq:compact-coverage}{the main paper's marginal-coverage condition} holds: on the coverage
event, an interval wholly above zero cannot contain a nonpositive $\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$. The
scientific burden is therefore to justify coverage for the declared outcome and
calibration/deployment pair. Joint exchangeability of the residual scores is one route;
known shift correction or another valid construction may be another. Risk alignment can explain why
a useful predictor transfers, but it is not an additional logical premise after coverage is given.

\paragraph{Why cell coverage is not population coverage.}
Consider the fixed binary predictors $f_0\equiv0$ and $f_a\equiv1$. Let the target input be $a$
with probability $0.45$ and $b$ with probability $0.55$, with deterministic labels $Y(a)=1$ and
$Y(b)=0$. Population benefit is $\Delta=-0.10$. In a one-item evaluation cell, a fixed predictor
that maps $a$ to $+1$ and $b$ to $-1$ predicts $\Delta^{\mathrm{cell}}$ perfectly without reading
the new label. With radius zero, cell coverage is perfect, yet the gate selects ADAPT with
probability $0.45$ despite negative benefit under the fixed population law. This constructed example
does not contradict \KBMainRef{thm:certificate}{Proposition}{the main marginal-error certificate}; it shows why its covered target cannot be changed.

\paragraph{Why exchangeability alone does not validate sign flips.}
As a separate null example, let all nine cluster gaps equal the same fair random sign $S$.
This vector is exchangeable, each gap is symmetric about zero, and a global sign change preserves
its law. Nevertheless, when $S=+1$, independent-coordinate sign-flip enumeration for the mean gives
a reference value of $1/512$. That occurs with probability $1/2$, not $1/512$. The vector fails
\KBMainEqRef{eq:compact-signflip-null}{the main paper's coordinatewise sign-flip invariance condition}. This example explains the required assumption; it is not a
model fitted to the CCT-20 observations.

\paragraph{Why a null is not an impossibility result.}
A null benchmark row can arise because the true effects are small, the candidate is one-sided, the
evidence map is weak, the benefit model underfits, the radius is wide, or calibration does not
transfer. The population impossibility requires evidence-identical target laws inside the declared
class with incompatible strict directions. The canonical benchmark table does not estimate that
full fibre. It therefore reports weak or adverse empirical regimes without calling them proven
unknowable worlds.

\paragraph{What would falsify the practical claim.}
The practical claim is conditional and testable. A clean target replay with the locked pipeline can
show excessive $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}$, poor coverage, or regret above both fixed policies. A
checkpoint or adapter change can show that calibration does not transfer. A family-held-out split
can show that cell-level cross-fitting was optimistic. Such failures do not refute the population
identity; they refute the operational assumptions used to obtain a useful empirical interval.

\section{Formalization and Artifact Scope}
\label{app:compact-formal}

The Lean project under \path{docs/research/kbound/formal/} provides a kernel-checked inventory for
the theorem names it exports. The compact paper relies on the LaTeX statements above; it does not
claim that every sentence has a one-to-one Lean theorem.

In particular, deployment-class richness,
risk alignment of a learned evidence map, exchangeability of a benchmark split, and correctness of
data preprocessing remain outside the proof assistant unless separately encoded.

The current strict-core audit kernel-checks 150 named declarations: 70 finite, algebraic,
and deterministic population-transfer results, and 80 probability, construction,
counterexample, and corrected conditional-extension results. The added modules derive one-shot residual
coverage from exchangeable measurable scores, including ties; filtered Ville and bounded
optional-stopping bounds; general KL/TV testing bounds for finite product experiments; and
independent bounded and martingale-difference concentration inequalities. Their assumptions are
explicit. In particular, these results do not establish exchangeability or conditional nulls for
the benchmark data, nor do they turn batch-level coverage into population-risk coverage.

The measurable target-law construction also goes beyond the original two-atom example. It fixes
the input law and off-disagreement label kernel, varies a measurable correctness field on
disagreement, and integrates the actual zero-one loss difference. With labels supported on the
two predictions on disagreement, feasible margins, and positive disagreement mass, it proves the
clipped identified interval and exact strict ADAPT/FREEZE frontiers without assuming
\texttt{RichAt}. It does not prove richness for an arbitrary restricted deployment subclass.

The finite-sample population-transfer procedure is also separated into a deterministic
machine-checked layer. \texttt{KBound/Population.lean} defines the compound radius
$\varepsilon+b$, its interval, and the strict action rule, and proves that a committed
action has the correct benefit sign whenever the interval-containment premise holds.
The outcome-blind replay script \path{scripts/run_population_panel.py} computes the
same interval from fitted predictions, cell radii, and fresh-sample sizes while refusing
target or outcome fields. This closes the procedure-to-action interface, not the
statistical premises: the benchmark tables still lack a sealed per-cell sampling and
exchangeability record, so they are not relabeled as population-certified results.

The separate synthetic episode runner \path{scripts/run_population_locked_panel.py}
checks disjoint episode identities across fitting, calibration, and scoring. Its evidence
writer reproduces the supplied decisions from the stated inputs and risk budgets before
saving them, refuses an occupied output directory, and reruns the scored-outcome
perturbation check with those same budgets. The perturbation stays within the valid
benefit range $[-1,1]$. With one calibration episode at $\alpha_{\mathrm{cell}}=0.05$,
the requested rank is unavailable; the implemented interval is unbounded and the
procedure abstains. Saved JSON represents these endpoints explicitly as strings
\texttt{-inf} and \texttt{+inf}, rather than nonstandard JSON numbers. These boundary
and evidence-integrity checks concern the specified synthetic procedure, not an
additional benchmark or evidence of real-population exchangeability.

One historical extension remains outside the verified scope. A measurable label swap preserves
label-free evidence and reverses population benefit, but selecting one representative from each
swap orbit need not identify the sign: distinct orbits can share the same evidence law and have
opposite selected signs. Lean verifies this counterexample and the corresponding set-theoretic
fibre-consistency criterion, not a general measurable decoder. A corrected conditional
H/ratio-rate contract also proves that a source miss budget at most $\alpha$ transfers to
a target miss budget at most $H\alpha$, assuming $H\geq0$ and the supplied domination
premise $\mathrm{targetMiss}\leq H\,\mathrm{sourceMiss}$. It does not derive that premise,
estimate $H$, or establish benchmark exchangeability. The unrestricted historical
claims are excluded from this compact submission. The
\texttt{--full-foundations} gate therefore remains failing for the sixth layer; the scoped
kernel audit checks the registered results and rejects nonstandard proof axioms.

The formal build and empirical build answer different questions. Lean checks that a proposition
follows from formal assumptions. The reconciliation scripts check that displayed aggregates match
saved records under implemented definitions. Neither checks the truth of an unencoded deployment
assumption, and neither substitutes for independent experimental replication.

The authoritative compact artifacts are layered: (1)~this source and its compiled PDF; (2)~shared theorem inputs for exact proofs; (3)~canonical JSON and generated LaTeX for numbers; (4)~the source manifest for record-level provenance; and (5)~package tests and Lean logs for implementation and formal checks.

Historical drafts, dashboards, and exploratory search folders can remain useful development
records, but they are not evidence sources for this compact submission unless referenced by the
canonical manifest.
