Test-time adaptation updates a deployed model without target labels, but an update can help or
harm. K-Bound asks the decision that comes first: when does label-free evidence justify replacing a
frozen model with an adapted candidate? It allows three actions---\adapt, \freeze, and \abstain---for
the benefit $\Delta=R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a)$. In a binary disagreement model,
$\Delta=2\mu_T(D)(M+\gamma)$ separates an observable margin $M$ from hidden calibration drift
$\gamma$. If the declared class satisfies $|\gamma|\le\beta$, a strict population action is
uniformly supportable exactly when $|M|>\beta$. Throughout the closed band $|M|\le\beta$, the same
label-free evidence does not uniformly support either strict action. Without added labels or a stated
coupling, the same label-free evidence cannot shrink the unresolved drift budget. The
practical method, Knowability-Guided Adaptation (\KGA), is separate: it predicts $\Delta$ and acts
only when $\widehat\Delta\pm\varepsilon$ excludes zero. Here $\varepsilon$ is an empirical
uncertainty radius, not an estimate of $\beta$.
On controlled CIFAR-10-C, reconciled results give point gains for Tent and EATA. Tent also has
positive ordinary intervals over six corruption families, but its preregistered six-comparison Holm
values are $0.09375$, so we make no confirmatory win claim. On the locked CCT-20 camera-location
shift, \CCTCheckpointCount{} checkpoints across \CCTLocationCount{} locations produced
\CCTCellCount{} cells. KGA made \CCTFreezeCount{} FREEZE, \CCTAbstainCount{} ABSTAIN, and
\CCTAdaptCount{} ADAPT decisions. It matched always freeze, and its mean accuracy was
\CCTVsAdaptPoint{} higher than always adapt. It did not support the predeclared stronger claim.
This is a safe-utility result, not evidence of selective natural-shift routing. K-Bound is a
validity and rollback layer, not a universal accuracy booster.
