Test-time adaptation can improve or degrade a model, leaving a separate decision: when does
label-free evidence justify replacing the frozen predictor? K-Bound characterizes this decision
in a binary population model under an externally declared calibration-residual bound $\beta$.
Holding label-free observations fixed, we derive the exact feasible interval for paired benefit:
a strict decision is justified precisely when $|M|>\beta$ on disagreement. An evidence-fibre audit
shows why the same observations cannot justify a smaller uniform residual bound without additional
structure. A separate finite-sample multiclass construction bounds paired benefit under an explicit
conditional-transport budget; synthetic examples illustrate its value and failure under assumption
violations. Knowability-Guided Adaptation (\KGA) is an empirical companion, not an implementation
of the population frontier. Using labeled historical outcomes, it predicts observed evaluation-cell
benefit and chooses \adapt, \freeze, or \abstain through residual intervals. Controlled studies
show candidate-dependent routing gains, while SAR favors always-adapt. A shared-predictor comparison
finds lower observed loss for CIFAR Tent at a prespecified utility--harm trade-off, but simpler
rules often win. Nominal 90\% constant-cell inclusion is only $50.51$--$60.69\%$ on held-out CIFAR
corruptions. CCT-20 has no ADAPT decisions, and So2Sat stops before target scoring. These findings
do not establish interval coverage or population-risk protection on unseen natural shifts; useful
prospective routing remains open.
