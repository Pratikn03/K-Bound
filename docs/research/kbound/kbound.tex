\title{When Is Label-Free Adaptation Knowable? \\
Helpful, Harmful, and Unknowable Regimes Under Distribution Shift}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}\\
\IEEEauthorblockA{Paper under double-blind review}}

\begin{abstract}
Test-time adaptation (TTA) updates a model on unlabeled data to counter distribution
shift, but the same update that recovers accuracy under one shift can quietly destroy it
under another---and with no target labels, the system cannot tell which case it faces. We
study the decision that comes \emph{before} adaptation---should the model adapt at all?---and
cast it as a three-way choice: \emph{adapt} when adaptation is knowably helpful,
\emph{freeze} when it is knowably harmful, and \emph{abstain} when the unlabeled evidence
cannot decide. That some such decisions are \emph{provably unanswerable} without extra
assumptions is not new: identifying out-of-distribution accuracy is already known to be as
hard as identifying the optimal predictor~\cite{garg2022atc}, and the optimal predict-versus-abstain
trade-off under shift has a known decision-theoretic form~\cite{kalai2021abstain}. Our
contribution \emph{specializes and sharpens} this landscape for the adaptation-benefit sign.
We prove an \emph{exact} success condition---the sign of the benefit is recoverable over the
declared drift-budget class \emph{iff} an observable confidence margin exceeds how far
calibration can drift (the ``calibration-drift budget''), a clean computable geometric
criterion in observable quantities---together with a finite-sample certificate that realizes
the unique maximal sound adapt/freeze/abstain rule while holding the false-adapt and
false-freeze rates at a chosen level $\alpha$. We further pin the minimal untestable
supplement at exactly \emph{one bit} of orientation in this test-time-adaptation setting, and
show that the standard label-free accuracy estimators (ATC, DoC, GDE, COT, AETTA,
agreement-on-the-line) are the zero-budget face of this frontier---sound precisely when
calibration does not drift. We package this as \textsc{KGA}, a drop-in
wrapper around existing adapters (Tent, EATA, SAR) that decides \emph{whether} to trust them
rather than how to adapt. \textbf{Our headline empirical result} is uniform \emph{no-harm} on every
held-out natural shift in a pre-registered, un-cherry-picked panel (Office-Home, iWildCam,
Camelyon17, RxRx1, PACS): under a valid out-of-fold radius, \textsc{KGA} matches the better fixed
policy and beats the worse at $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}{=}0$---conditional safety when adaptation is
risky, not universal accuracy gains. In \emph{identifiable} mixed harmful+helpful regimes (CIFAR-10-C
stress grid, ImageNet-C SAR), it further beats both trivial policies and \textbf{beats POEM and
AETTA} with Holm-corrected regret gaps, exactly where the frontier predicts knowability.
\textsc{KGA} is a safety layer, not a universal accuracy booster: its wins are regime-specific, and we
report---rather than hide---the regimes and honest nulls where it only ties. Full per-dataset tables
and the complete benchmark battery appear in the body and appendix.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.86\columnwidth]{figures/fig_decision_flow.png}
\caption{\textbf{K-Bound previews the decision.} From an unlabeled batch under shift, the certificate
estimates the adaptation benefit $\widehat\Delta$ with a finite-sample radius $\varepsilon$ and returns
\textsc{adapt} (knowably helpful), \textsc{freeze} (knowably harmful), or \textsc{abstain} (sign
unidentifiable), with false-adapt/false-freeze $\le\alpha$.}
\label{fig:teaser}
\end{figure}
Should a model adapt to unlabeled target data, or would adapting make it worse?
Test-time adaptation (TTA) updates a model on the test stream---through entropy
minimization, sample filtering, or stability-aware updates---and in favorable
shifts recovers lost accuracy. In unfavorable shifts the same unlabeled objective
silently degrades it: the model sharpens wrong predictions, adapts to label
imbalance, or collapses under non-stationary streams. Because target labels are
unavailable at deployment, the system usually cannot tell whether adaptation is
helping or hurting.

Most work on TTA asks how to design a better adaptation mechanism. This paper asks
the prior question: should the system adapt at all? We study label-free adaptation
as a decision problem. Given a frozen baseline, an adapted candidate, and an
unlabeled target batch, the system must choose one of three actions---adapt when
adaptation is knowably helpful, freeze when knowably harmful, and abstain when the
available evidence cannot identify the sign of the benefit. Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}
previews this decision certificate.

This decision is constrained by identifiability. That label-free deployment quantities
cannot in general pin down a target risk---and hence cannot in general decide adaptation
without some assumption on the shift---is established: Garg et al.~\cite{garg2022atc} show
identifying out-of-distribution accuracy is as hard as identifying the optimal predictor,
and Kalai and Kanade~\cite{kalai2021abstain} characterize the optimal predict-versus-abstain
trade-off under shift against a shift budget. We do not re-derive this general impossibility;
we \emph{specialize} it to the adaptation-benefit sign and make its boundary exact. Concretely,
two target worlds in the declared drift-budget class can carry identical label-free evidence
yet opposite adaptation benefit, so no label-free rule is correct in both and abstention is
information-theoretically necessary, not merely conservative. Conversely, when the label-free
evidence is risk-aligned and the benefit estimate admits a valid uncertainty radius, a
finite-sample certificate can safely route between adapt, freeze, and abstain while
controlling false-adapt and false-freeze errors.

Our main theoretical contribution is an \emph{exact} benefit-sign frontier that sharpens this
known landscape into a computable criterion. The sign of the adaptation benefit is identifiable
from label-free observables precisely when an observable margin on the disagreement region
exceeds a calibration-drift budget ($|M|>\beta$). Intuitively, the margin is how confidently the
label-free evidence points toward ``adapt'' or ``freeze,'' and the budget is how far that signal
can be thrown off by calibration drift; the decision is trustworthy exactly when confidence
outweighs drift. This makes precise why common label-free heuristics can work in benign regimes and
fail under drift: they implicitly assume that this drift is zero or negligible---i.e., they live on
the $\beta{=}0$ face of the frontier. In the test-time-adaptation setting studied here, we further
quantify \emph{how much} untestable information the decision needs: the minimal supplement that no
label-free-checkable assumption can remove is exactly \emph{one bit} of orientation, which can only
be supplied through structural assumptions that are falsifiable but not verifiable. (In full
generality the irreducible supplement is problem-dependent---an interval under affine
ambiguity---so we state the one-bit count for the TTA/panel setting under our stated hypothesis,
not for arbitrary functionals.)

We instantiate the framework as \textsc{KGA}, a mechanism-agnostic wrapper around
existing adaptation candidates such as Tent, EATA, and SAR. \textsc{KGA} does not
introduce a new adaptation objective. Instead, it estimates the label-free benefit
of a candidate, attaches a calibrated uncertainty radius, and returns adapt,
freeze, or abstain. This makes \textsc{KGA} a safety layer for TTA rather than a
replacement for TTA methods.

Empirically, \textsc{KGA} behaves according to the theory. \textbf{Headline (natural shifts):}
under a valid out-of-fold conformal radius \textsc{KGA} is \emph{uniformly no-harm} on every held-out
natural benchmark: it matches the better fixed policy and beats the worse one at false-adapt
$\le\alpha$, with \emph{no} natural-shift beats-both. Office-Home (Protocol~M v2) and iWildCam
(Protocol~H v2) beat always-adapt (CIs exclude zero) but \emph{tie} always-freeze
(Tables~\ref{tab:iwildcam-H}, \ref{tab:officehome-M}); their earlier in-sample-radius
``beats-both'' was a calibration bug, corrected here to no-harm. On the one-sided WILDS shifts
Camelyon17 (Protocol~G) and RxRx1, \textsc{KGA} likewise matches the better fixed policy at
false-adapt $\le\alpha$ without beating both; the earlier Camelyon17 Protocol-G beats-both was a
separate pooling artifact (in-distribution \texttt{id\_val} cells mixed into the held-out set,
\S\ref{sec:natural-suite}). PACS is no-harm on $3/4$ leave-one-domain-out splits
(Table~\ref{tab:pacs}). \textbf{Confirmatory (identifiable synthetic/mixed):} on the pre-registered
CIFAR-10-C stress grid, where harmful adaptation is frequent and detectable, \textsc{KGA} beats both
always-adapt and always-freeze for Tent and EATA while making zero false-adapt decisions, and it
ties the collapse-resistant SAR; on the same mixed stream it \textbf{beats POEM and AETTA}
(Table~\ref{tab:headtohead-poem-aetta}) with Holm-corrected paired-bootstrap CIs. In online
non-stationary CIFAR-10 streams, always-adapt collapses in the harm-dominated regime and
always-freeze forgoes gains in the helpful regime, while \textsc{KGA} remains near-best across
both. At ImageNet scale, \textsc{KGA} also improves over both trivial policies on a harmful SAR
stream. ImageNet-R remains a split-specific lead, not a stable win.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item We formalize label-free TTA as an adapt/freeze/abstain decision problem governed by the sign of adaptation benefit. The underlying impossibility---that deciding without labels needs an assumption on the shift---is inherited from prior work~\cite{garg2022atc,kalai2021abstain}, which we \emph{specialize} to the benefit sign rather than re-derive.
\item \textbf{Exact benefit-sign frontier.} We characterize the sign as identifiable over the declared drift-budget class \emph{iff} $|M|>\beta$---a clean, computable geometric criterion in observable quantities---with the three-way adapt/freeze/abstain rule as the unique maximal sound certificate.
\item \textbf{One-bit quantification.} In the TTA/panel setting under our stated hypothesis, the minimal untestable supplement is exactly one bit of orientation; in general the irreducible supplement is problem-dependent (an interval under affine ambiguity).
\item \textbf{Unification.} We show ATC, DoC, GDE, COT, AETTA, and agreement-on-the-line are the $\beta{=}0$ face of the frontier (sound iff the drift budget vanishes), organizing these label-free estimators as a single limiting case.
\item \textbf{Split-observability (honest generality).} We isolate the structural property our proofs actually use---a benefit functional is \emph{split-observable} if $\sign(\Delta)=\sign(M+\gamma)$ with $M$ observable and $|\gamma|\le\beta$---and show the impossibility, finite-$n$ floor, certificate, and minimax/necessity results hold for \emph{any} split-observable functional, with TTA (via the disagreement-region reduction) as the demonstrated instance. The $|M|>\beta$ criterion and the one-bit count are stated for split-observable functionals (TTA is the demonstrated case), not for arbitrary functionals.
\item We derive a finite-sample certificate that controls false adaptation and false freezing under risk-aligned evidence.
\item We validate the framework on a seven-dataset core panel with bootstrap confidence intervals.
\textbf{Headline:} uniform no-harm on every held-out natural shift (Office-Home, iWildCam,
Camelyon17, RxRx1, PACS) under a valid out-of-fold radius. \textbf{Confirmatory:} beats-both on
synthetic stress grids (CIFAR-10-C, ImageNet-C SAR) and a pre-registered mixed head-to-head win over
POEM and AETTA (Table~\ref{tab:headtohead-poem-aetta}). Controlled synthetic validations and an
additional breadth dataset (PACS) are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:supp}, with CIFAR-10.1 retained
in-body as a supporting low-margin check (\S\ref{sec:external}). A constructed cross-protocol
aggregate ($n{=}143$) beats both fixed policies under out-of-fold calibration, demonstrating
per-condition routing under heterogeneity without claiming a universal mixed-deployment win
(\S\ref{sec:mixedstream}).
\end{itemize}

\noindent\emph{Two bars, stated once.} Throughout, the \textbf{pre-registered headline bar} is a
point-estimate beats-both at false-adapt $\le\alpha$; the \textbf{CI-robust} criterion
(both bootstrap regret-gap CIs exclude zero) is a strictly stronger layer we report as a separate

K-Bound does not claim universal improvement. \textbf{The headline deployment claim is uniform
no-harm on natural shifts}: when adapting is already near-oracle, a certificate can only tie or
slightly trail always-adapt; when freezing is already optimal, it should match freeze rather than
force an unnecessary update. Its distinctive value---and the confirmatory wins on synthetic
grids---appear when harmful adaptation is frequent, detectable, and costly, the regime where blind
adaptation is most dangerous.

\section{Related work and positioning}
We position K-Bound as a precise delta over five adjacent lines of work. In each case the
distinction is the one the theory formalizes: between \emph{improving} an unlabeled objective
and \emph{certifying the sign} of its benefit before acting.

\subsection{Test-time adaptation methods}
Entropy-based TTA updates a frozen model on unlabeled target data. \textbf{Tent}~\cite{wang2021tent} minimizes
prediction entropy over the batch-norm affine parameters; \textbf{SHOT}~\cite{liang2020shot} combines information
maximization with pseudo-labeling for source-free adaptation; \textbf{TTT}~\cite{sun2020ttt} trains a
self-supervised auxiliary task and updates it at test time; \textbf{EATA}~\cite{niu2022eata} adds entropy
filtering of unreliable samples plus a Fisher penalty against forgetting; \textbf{SAR}~\cite{niu2023sar}
adds sharpness-aware updates with a reset that aborts incipient collapse; \textbf{CoTTA}~\cite{wang2022cotta}
stabilizes continual adaptation with teacher-student ensembling; and \textbf{MEMO}~\cite{zhang2022memo}
adapts via test-time augmentation and entropy minimization. These methods make
adaptation \emph{more robust}, and several detect or suppress instability, but all commit to
adapting: none emits a \emph{pre-adaptation} certificate separating helpful, harmful, and
unknowable evidence, and none can \emph{abstain} on the adapt/freeze decision itself. K-Bound
is mechanism-agnostic---it \emph{wraps} any such candidate (we evaluate Tent/EATA/SAR).
Tempora~\cite{tempora2026} further shows that unconstrained offline rankings can invert under
latency and compute pressure, with no fixed policy robust across temporal scenarios. K-Bound is
complementary: rather than selecting one always-on method, it certifies per-condition
\textsc{adapt}/\textsc{freeze}/\textsc{abstain} decisions.

\subsection{Protected and guarded adaptation}
A second line guards adaptation \emph{during} the update. \textbf{POEM}~\cite{bar2024poem}
protects TTA by online entropy matching---a betting-style monitor that halts or dampens a
single failure mode---and \textbf{Schirmer et al.}~\cite{schirmer2025monitoring} run anytime-valid risk monitors over the
TTA stream, raising a sequential alarm when adaptation degrades a proxy risk; both descend
from sequential harmful-shift tracking for deployed models (\textbf{Podkopaev \& Ramdas}~\cite{podkopaev2022tracking}).
These are the nearest neighbours of our certificate, and the delta is precise: they act
\emph{reactively, during or after} the update and control a false-\emph{alarm} rate in one
failure direction, whereas K-Bound decides \emph{before} committing, poses the
\emph{three-way} decision with an explicit \emph{unknowable} region (where abstention is
mandatory, Cor.~\ref{cor:forced-abstain}), controls the false-\emph{adapt} rate on the
benefit sign itself, and proves the matching impossibility floor
(Prop.~\ref{thm:imp-quant}). The two are complementary: a pre-adaptation certificate can
gate what a runtime monitor then tracks.

\subsection{Label-free accuracy and error estimation}
A third line predicts target \emph{performance} without labels: \textbf{ATC}~\cite{garg2022atc} thresholds
softmax confidence calibrated on source; \textbf{Agreement-on-the-Line}~\cite{baek2022aol,miller2021aol} exploits the linear
correlation between in- and out-of-distribution model \emph{agreement} to extrapolate
accuracy; \textbf{AETTA}~\cite{lee2024aetta} estimates TTA accuracy from dropout-prediction disagreement.
Crucially, Garg et al.~\cite{garg2022atc} also establish the governing limit for this entire
line: identifying OOD accuracy is \emph{as hard as} identifying the optimal predictor, so any
label-free accuracy estimate is sound only under an assumption on the shift. Our frontier
\emph{organizes} these estimators rather than competing with them: ATC, DoC, GDE, COT, AETTA,
and agreement-on-the-line are all the zero-budget ($\beta{=}0$) face of the benefit-sign frontier
(Thm.~\ref{thm:frontier}(iv))---each is sound precisely when calibration does not drift
($\gamma{=}0$), and each silently fails exactly where our budget is positive.
Closest in spirit, \textbf{Kim et al.}~\cite{kim2024taol}\ use agreement-on-the-line to decide \emph{when} TTA
helps and to select TTA hyperparameters without labels---a genuine decision use of an
accuracy estimate, but a plug-in one on that same $\beta{=}0$ face, sound only when calibration
transfers, with no finite-sample false-adapt control and no abstain region.
Estimating $R_T(f)$---or detecting that TTA has failed \emph{post hoc}---is strictly different
from certifying $\sign\big(R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a)\big)$ \emph{before} adapting with a controlled
false-adapt rate, the object K-Bound bounds.

\subsection{Impossibility, abstention under shift, and identifiability}
The general impossibility our work specializes is \emph{owned by prior work}, and we cite it as
such. \textbf{Garg et al.}~\cite{garg2022atc} prove that identifying out-of-distribution accuracy
from unlabeled target data is as hard as identifying the optimal predictor---so point
identification provably requires an assumption on the shift---and \textbf{Kalai \&
Kanade}~\cite{kalai2021abstain} give the matching decision theory, characterizing the optimal
abstain-versus-predict rule under shift via an observable-quantity-versus-shift-budget threshold
with a minimax certificate. These are the sources of both the impossibility and the
abstention-under-budget decision we build on; K-Bound does not re-derive them but
\emph{specializes} them to the sign of the adaptation benefit and makes the boundary exact.
This hardness is also visible across unsupervised risk estimation.
\textbf{Steinhardt \& Liang}~\cite{steinhardt2016} estimate risk without labels only under a conditional-independence
structure; \textbf{Ben-David et al.}~\cite{bendavid2010theory,bendavid2010impossibility} bound target risk by source risk plus an
$\mathcal{H}$-divergence; \textbf{Lipton et al.}~\cite{lipton2018bbse} (BBSE) correct label shift under an invertible
confusion matrix; \textbf{Rosenfeld \& Garg}~\cite{rosenfeld2023dis2} bound target error under an explicit,
unverifiable assumption (their Remark~3.9 states informally the same obstruction our
Theorem~\ref{thm:conj1-dichotomy} makes exact for the benefit sign); the rank-one agreement algebra underlying our
Theorem~\ref{thm:conj1-dichotomy} is classical~\cite{parisi2014,jaffe2015,platanios2014,ibrahim2019},
its dependent-classifier diagnostics appear in~\cite{jaffe2016}, and label-free
algebraic evaluation with consistency alarms is developed in the NTQR
program~\cite{corradaemmanuel2024}. Each shows risk is identifiable only under structural assumptions and
impossible in general---the landscape our Theorem~\ref{thm:imp}(ii) specializes and makes
quantitative for the benefit sign. We weaken the target from a \emph{risk} to the \emph{sign of a difference} of
risks, which is identifiable on a strictly larger set (the observable disagreement region,
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}).

\subsection{Selective prediction, conformal, and anytime-valid inference}
\textbf{Selective prediction}~\cite{geifman2017selective} abstains on individual \emph{predictions};
we instead abstain on the \emph{adaptation decision}. Our finite-sample certificate reuses
split-conformal prediction (\textbf{Vovk et al.}~\cite{vovk2005}; \textbf{Angelopoulos \& Bates}~\cite{angelopoulos2023}) to build the
radius $\varepsilon$, and its streaming form (Appendix~\ref{app:anytime}) is a
testing-by-betting e-process in the safe-anytime-valid-inference tradition (\textbf{Shafer}~\cite{shafer2021};
\textbf{Ramdas et al.}~\cite{ramdas2023}); confidence-sequence and concentration tools
(\textbf{Howard et al.}~\cite{howard2021timeuniform}, \textbf{Maurer \& Pontil}~\cite{maurer2009empirical},
\textbf{Hoeffding}~\cite{hoeffding1963probability}); and Vovk's conformal test martingales~\cite{vovk2021martingale}
embody, for exchangeability, the falsify-not-verify stance our budget audit
(Thm.~\ref{thm:conj1-dichotomy}(iv)) takes for the drift budget.

certificate with an \emph{explicit, proven} unknowable region and a false-adapt guarantee.

\subsection{Benchmarks, code, and reproducibility}
TTA progress is benchmarked on standardized corruption suites---CIFAR-10/100-C and
ImageNet-C~\cite{hendrycks2019cifar10c}, plus ImageNet-R~\cite{hendrycks2021manyfaces}---and on
cross-domain shift suites including WILDS/Camelyon17/RxRx1/Office-Home~\cite{koh2021wilds,bandi2019camelyon,taylor2019rxrx1,venkateswara2017officehome}.
The field increasingly emphasizes standardized, executable evaluation
(\textbf{RobustBench}~\cite{croce2021robustbench}; \textbf{DomainBed}~\cite{gulrajani2021domainbed}) and
reproducibility checks on fresh test draws such as CIFAR-10.1~\cite{recht2019cifar10}. Most TTA
methods ship official code, but evaluations vary in batch regime, stream composition, and update
budget---\emph{precisely} the axis along which adaptation flips between helpful and harmful. We
therefore release K-Bound as a \emph{pre-registered, executable} artifact: a public repository
with pinned environments, a one-command reproduction harness, auto-downloaded standard corruption
data, fixed seeds, machine-checked theorem validators, and the full stratified result manifests
behind every table and figure (\S\ref{sec:repro}). Our certificate builds on split-conformal
prediction (Vovk et al.; Angelopoulos \& Bates) and, for the streaming variant, testing-by-betting
e-processes (Shafer; Ramdas et al.). The CAN benchmark formalizes a practical ``adapt-or-skip''
decision in chronological streams~\cite{jeon2025can}; K-Bound complements that benchmark view with
a theorem-level frontier and impossibility account for when such decisions are certifiable from
label-free evidence.

\section{Problem setup}\label{sec:setup}
Source $P_S(X,Y)$ has labels at train/validation; target $P_T(X,Y)$ exposes only
$P_T(X)$ at test time. Fix a loss $\ell$, a frozen model $f_0$, and an
adapted/gated candidate $f_a$. Target risk $R_T(f)=\E_{P_T}[\ell(f(X),Y)]$.
The \emph{adaptation benefit} is
\begin{equation}
\Delta \;=\; R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a),\qquad \Delta>0 \iff \text{adapting helps.}
\end{equation}
\emph{Observable evidence} $Z=\phi(X_{1:m},f_0,f_a)$ is any statistic computable
\emph{without} target labels (entropy, confidence, score-distribution drift,
detector disagreement, predicted-class balance, update norm, density-ratio
diagnostics). The conditional benefit is $\Delta(z)=\E[\ell(f_0(X),Y)-\ell(f_a(X),Y)\mid Z=z]$.
The central question is when $\sign\Delta(z)$ is recoverable from $Z$ alone.

\paragraph{Notation.} $f_0$ is the frozen model and $f_a$ the adapted/gated candidate;
$\Delta=R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a)$ is the population adaptation benefit and $\Delta(z)$ its value
conditioned on evidence $Z{=}z$; $\widehat\Delta$ is a label-free estimator of $\Delta$ with
confidence radius $\varepsilon$ at miscoverage level $\alpha$; $g(Z)\in\{\textsc{adapt},
\textsc{freeze},\textsc{abstain}\}$ is the decision rule; and $D=\{x:f_0(x)\neq f_a(x)\}$ is the
observable disagreement region. \emph{Regret-to-oracle} denotes $R(g)-R(g^\star)$ against the
per-condition oracle $g^\star=\mathbf1[\Delta>0]$.

\section{Definitions}\label{sec:def}
\begin{definition}[Observable risk alignment / Identifiability]
$Z$ is \emph{risk-aligned} if the sign of adaptation benefit $\sign\Delta$ is identifiable from its law (identifiability)—specifically, no two admissible worlds with opposite benefit signs induce the same evidence distribution. We treat conformal coverage of the benefit estimator separately as a property of the calibration sample (see Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}).
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Regimes]
At evidence $z$: \emph{knowably helpful} if $Z$ certifies $\Delta(z)>0$ (\textbf{adapt});
\emph{knowably harmful} if $Z$ certifies $\Delta(z)<0$ (\textbf{freeze});
\emph{unknowable} if the same $Z$ is compatible with both signs (\textbf{abstain}).
\end{definition}

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.95\columnwidth}{\small
\textbf{What ``label-free'' means here.}
\emph{Target-label-free} (our setting): no labels from the target/test stream are ever used.
\emph{Validation labels allowed}: labeled source and a held-out labeled validation/probe set may
calibrate the benefit estimator $\widehat\Delta$ and the radius $\varepsilon$.
\emph{Target-unsupervised evidence}: the deployment batch contributes only $X$, predictions,
entropy, confidence, drift and other label-free statistics $Z$. The online-TTA results that use a
clean labelled validation probe (\S\ref{sec:exp}) are target-label-free in this sense---they use no
\emph{target} labels---not unsupervised of all labels.}}\end{center}

\section{Theory: four pillars}\label{sec:theory}\label{sec:bsfrontier}\label{sec:gmeter}
\noindent\emph{What we specialize, and how general it is.} The general fact that deciding
adaptation label-free needs an assumption on the shift is due to Garg et
al.~\cite{garg2022atc} and Kalai \& Kanade~\cite{kalai2021abstain}; we do not re-derive it.
What follows \emph{specializes} that landscape to the benefit sign and makes its boundary
exact. The structural property our proofs actually use is \emph{split-observability}: a benefit
functional is split-observable if $\sign(\Delta)=\sign(M+\gamma)$ with $M$ an observable margin
and an unobservable calibration term $|\gamma|\le\beta$ bounded by the declared drift budget.
The impossibility, finite-$n$ floor, certificate, and minimax/necessity results below are
world-agnostic---they hold for \emph{any} split-observable functional---and test-time adaptation
is the canonical instance via the disagreement-region reduction (Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}). The
sharper statements---the $|M|>\beta$ identifiability criterion and the exactly-one-bit
supplement---are established for split-observable functionals, with TTA as the demonstrated case;
we do not claim them for arbitrary functionals.

\noindent\emph{Graded refinement.} A knowability--capacity sharpening of these pillars---an exact
$\tau{=}1$ threshold for sign-of-benefit in a $1$-D location model, and its multivariate,
MLR/log-concave, and distribution-free generalization (with an explicit open conjecture)---is
developed in Appendices~\ref{sec:knowcap} and~\ref{sec:knowcap-gen}.

\noindent\emph{Certificate extensions.} The batch certificate (Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}) admits two
verified extensions, stated and proved in Appendix~\ref{app:extensions}. (i)~An \emph{anytime-valid}
sequential form (Theorem~\ref{thm:anytime}): under continuous monitoring of a stream of windows the
false-adapt guarantee holds \emph{time-uniformly} for an arbitrary stopping time---the natural
object for a streaming deployment---via a one-sided betting supermartingale and Ville's inequality.
(ii)~A \emph{multicandidate} form (Theorem~\ref{thm:multicand}): routing among $K$ candidate adapters
with a joint, family-wise false-adapt bound that holds for an arbitrary (even adversarial) selector,
closing the multicandidate gap left open by the single-candidate certificate. Both are
machine-validated (\texttt{theory\_v2/val\_sequential\_anytime.py}, \texttt{val\_multicandidate.py}),
with the full written proofs in Appendix~\ref{app:extensions}.

\section{Method: \textsc{KGA} (Knowability-Guided Adaptation)}
\textsc{KGA} is a decision \emph{wrapper}; it does not require a new adaptation
mechanism. Given a frozen $f_0$, a candidate adaptation, and an unlabeled batch:
(1) extract $Z=\phi(\cdot)$; (2) estimate benefit $\widehat\Delta(Z)$; (3) form a
radius $\varepsilon$; (4) return \textsc{adapt} if $\widehat\Delta-\varepsilon>0$,
\textsc{freeze} if $\widehat\Delta+\varepsilon<0$, else \textsc{abstain}. In our
experiments $\widehat\Delta$ is a gradient-boosted regressor and $\varepsilon$ is the $(1-\alpha)$
empirical quantile of \emph{out-of-fold} (leave-one-task-out) residuals, so the estimator and its
residual-calibration data are disjoint ($\alpha=0.1$). The coverage guarantee is split-dependent: a
clean dev/test split (the natural-shift protocols) gives \emph{exact} split-conformal coverage
$\Pr[|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon]\ge1-\alpha$ under exchangeability, while the per-cell
grids use the leave-one-out (jackknife) radius, whose calibration-set coverage is $\ge1-\alpha$ by
construction and distribution-free only asymptotically (the finite-sample jackknife$+$ of
Barber et al.\ gives $1-2\alpha$; not implemented).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\textsc{KGA} (Knowability-Guided Adaptation)}
\label{alg:kga}
\SetKwInOut{Input}{Input}
\SetKwInOut{Guarantee}{Guarantee}
\Input{frozen $f_0$; candidate adaptation $f_a$; unlabeled target batch $X_{1:m}$; calibrated benefit model $\widehat\Delta(\cdot)$; level $\alpha$.}
Extract label-free evidence $Z=\phi(X_{1:m},f_0,f_a)$ (entropy, confidence, predicted-class balance and its drift, marginal-prediction KL, update norm, detector disagreement)\;
Estimate benefit $\widehat\Delta\leftarrow\widehat\Delta(Z)$ and radius $\varepsilon$ with out-of-fold conformal coverage $\Pr[\,|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon\,]\ge1-\alpha$ (exact under a clean split)\;
\eIf{$\widehat\Delta-\varepsilon>0$}{
  \Return \textsc{adapt} (deploy $f_a$)\;
}{
  \eIf{$\widehat\Delta+\varepsilon<0$}{
    \Return \textsc{freeze} (keep $f_0$)\;
  }{
    \Return \textsc{abstain} (keep $f_0$, flag for review)\;
  }
}
\Guarantee{the false-adapt and false-freeze rates are each $\le\alpha$ (Thm~\ref{thm:cert}).}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Assumptions.} \textsc{KGA} inherits exactly the assumptions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}: (i) a labeled validation/calibration sample of paired benefits that is
exchangeable with deployment, from which $\varepsilon$ is built; (ii) bounded per-sample
benefits; and (iii) an evidence map $Z$ that is \emph{risk-aligned} (it brackets $\sign\Delta$;
\S\ref{sec:def}). When (iii) fails---evidence indistinguishable across opposite-benefit
worlds---the certificate cannot fire and \textsc{KGA} \emph{abstains}, which
Corollary~\ref{cor:forced-abstain} shows is mandatory, not merely conservative. No target
labels are used at any step.

\paragraph{Cost.} \textsc{KGA} adds no training to $f_0$ and introduces no new adaptation
mechanism: per decision it costs one forward pass of $f_0$ and $f_a$ on the batch plus an
evaluation of the lightweight benefit model. It is therefore a drop-in safety wrapper around
any already-deployed TTA candidate.

\paragraph{The threshold is derived, not tuned.} \textsc{KGA}'s commit boundary is not a
swept hyperparameter: $\varepsilon$ is the $(1-\alpha)$ out-of-fold residual quantile
fixed on the calibration split and \emph{never} selected on target-test data, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier} identifies it as the \emph{exact} benefit-sign budget---$\sign\Delta$
is label-free identifiable iff the observable margin exceeds the calibration-drift budget. This
is precisely what separates \textsc{KGA} from confidence-thresholding (ATC) and agreement-trend
selection~\cite{kim2024taol}: those fit a scalar against a held-out accuracy signal and
\emph{predict accuracy}; \textsc{KGA} certifies the benefit \emph{sign} with a boundary
\emph{forced} by the identifiability budget, and \textsc{abstains} exactly where
Corollary~\ref{cor:forced-abstain} proves no label-free rule can decide. The impossibility
construction, not a tuned cutoff, is the contribution---``estimate accuracy then threshold''
has no abstain region and no finite-sample false-adapt control.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}
The empirical claim is narrow and we test it in that order. \textsc{KGA} is a safety layer: it
should beat both fixed policies only where harmful adaptation is frequent and detectable, and do no
harm elsewhere. We first establish the mechanism in a controlled label-free setting (this section
and the online streams that follow), then carry the headline to image corruption grids and held-out
natural shifts (\S\ref{sec:external}--\S\ref{sec:natural-suite}). The anomaly-detection and regression
tracks below are breadth evidence that the certificate's behavior is mechanism-agnostic, not a
competing headline.

We begin with a $123$-task anomaly-score archive (ADBench tabular, image-OOD, text, cyber,
fraud); each task has six detector scores on labeled validation and test. Frozen
$f_0=$ best-validation detector. Controlled-synthetic corruptions validate the
\emph{mechanism}, not real sensor failure. All numbers come from
the released reproducibility package.

\paragraph{Safety on the clean suite.} With candidate $f_a=$ rank-normalized logistic
stack, \textsc{KGA}'s abstentions concentrate where the true benefit is near zero
(mean $|\Delta|=0.021$ on abstained vs $0.132$ on acted tasks); adapt precision $0.90$.
This suite is helpful-dominated, so always-adapt (mean AUROC $0.777$) edges the safe
policy ($0.766$); coverage $17\%$. Honest reading: when adaptation almost always helps,
abstention mostly costs coverage.

\paragraph{Harmful regime.} With $f_a=$ reliability-fusion that \emph{hurts on 80\% of
tasks}, \textsc{KGA} refuses it almost everywhere and matches the safe baseline
(AUROC $0.748$) while always-adapt drops to $0.728$; regret-to-oracle falls from
$0.0214$ to $0.0019$ ($\approx11\times$). See Fig.~\ref{fig:harm}.

\paragraph{Controlled mixed regime (369 instances).} Three conditions per task:
\emph{clean} (adapt mildly harmful), \emph{detectable failure} (best detector
corrupted with a distribution shift; KS drift observable), \emph{covert failure} (best
detector permuted; signal destroyed but marginal---hence $Z$---nearly unchanged).
Decisions by true regime are in Table~\ref{tab:mixed}; adapt precision $0.935$,
false-adapt rate $0.065$. Policy AUROC: always-adapt $0.697$, always-freeze $0.586$,
\textsc{KGA} $0.690$, oracle $0.719$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:mix}). \textsc{KGA} beats
always-freeze by $0.10$ and ties always-adapt---because harmful adaptation is mild in
this domain.

\paragraph{Online non-stationary regime: cross-regime robustness (the decisive test).}
The single-shot regimes above are each dominated by one outcome, so a fixed policy is
already near-optimal. The real test is whether \emph{any single fixed policy is robust
across opposite regimes}. We evaluate \emph{online, continual} TTA on non-stationary

a \textbf{helpful-dominated} stream (mostly recoverable corruptions) and a
\textbf{harm-dominated} stream (long ``trap'' runs---single-class label shift, near-pure
noise, small batches---so continual Tent accumulates damage and \emph{collapses}).

collapses, giving the \textbf{highest mean across regimes} ($0.490$ vs always-freeze

better fixed policy (it edges always-adapt by $+0.005$ in the helpful regime and trails
always-freeze by $0.014$ in the harm regime); its advantage is \emph{robustness}---it is
the only policy that is near-oracle in both regimes, which is exactly the knowability
thesis (route per regime rather than commit to one policy).

knowably-harmful region. \emph{Corrected uncertainty quantification (per-condition).} An
earlier draft reported mixing-bootstrap intervals over a synthetic harmful-fraction

\paragraph{Fourth natural-shift track: Office-Home --- a \emph{covariate} shift where detectability and mixedness \emph{anti-correlate}.}
Office-Home ($4$ domains, $65$ classes, $15{,}588$ images) is a pure \emph{covariate} style shift
(Art / Clipart / Product / Real-World), a priori the most label-free-detectable shift family and so
the sharpest test of whether the harmful-\emph{detectable} corner survives away from label shift.
Source $f_0$ is a ResNet-50 (ImageNet-V2) fine-tuned on Real-World ($86.1\%$ source-val); the other
three domains are the targets, each split val/test, with $\tau^\star$, the candidate set, the
deployed adapter, and the conformal certificate calibrated on source$+$target-\emph{val} and the
held-out test evaluated \emph{once}. The $14$-candidate panel augments
Tent/EATA/SAR$\times\{$online,episodic$\}\times\{$mild,aggressive$\}$ with two \emph{inference-only}
priors --- a label-shift MLLS logit correction and a damped ``conservative'' correction (no
parameter update) --- over $2$ seeds $\times\{$iid,imbalanced,single-class$\}\times\{$tiny,small$\}$
($504$ target records/split). The decisive finding is structural: across domains, \emph{detectability
and mixedness anti-correlate}. The two domains whose gradient-TTA harm is cleanly detectable (Art,
Clipart: best-feature in-sample harm-AUC $0.90$/$0.99$ on val, $0.99$/$0.94$ on test) are
\emph{helpful-dominated} ($3$--$9\%$ harm), so there is essentially nothing to freeze; the one
genuinely \emph{mixed} domain (Product, $39\%$/$23\%$ harm) is exactly where the harm is \emph{not}
label-free detectable on transfer (certificate transfer-AUC $0.53$/$0.63$). Pooled, the best single
evidence feature separates harm \emph{in-sample} (AUC $0.81$/$0.79$, $n{\approx}432$ --- no longer a

\subsection{Natural-shift suite: do the harmful regimes occur on real data?}\label{sec:natural-suite}
The synthetic stress grids above manufacture harm by construction. The sharper question is whether
the \emph{harmful, detectable} regime---the wedge where the certificate is meant to earn its
keep---arises on \emph{natural} shift, and whether the operating point we calibrated on synthetic
data fires there. We probe this with two real shifts that bracket the detectability frontier.

\paragraph{Camelyon17 composition-stress grid: real harm that is label-free \emph{detectable}.}
\emph{Two tracks, kept distinct:} the 4-seed Camelyon17 TTA table
(Table~\ref{tab:camelyon}) is \emph{helpful-dominated}---adaptation helps on every
seed, always-adapt is the oracle, and \textsc{KGA} trails it by ${\approx}0.02$
regret---whereas the separate multi-candidate audit below shows harmful,
\emph{detectable} natural-shift cells exist, but is not yet a policy win because the
current $\tau$ threshold abstains at this operating point.
Re-running the hospital-shift candidates over a composition stress grid (iid / class-imbalanced /
single-class $\times$ \{test, val, id-val\} domains $\times$ mild/aggressive updates $\times$ $4$
seeds; $72$ conditions $\times$ $6$ TTA variants ${=}\,432$ cells, debug-scale
$n_{\mathrm{eval}}{=}256$/cell) turns the benign picture of Table~\ref{tab:camelyon} into a
\emph{mixed} one: $28.7\%$ of cells are harmful ($\bar B{=}{+}0.039$), and---crucially---the harm is
label-free \emph{detectable}: the best single evidence feature reaches harm-AUC $0.78$, and the
conformal certificate's own $-\widehat B$ score reaches $0.91$. This is the first natural-shift
confirmation that the harmful-and-detectable regime the theory targets is not a synthetic artifact.
\emph{Yet at our current operating point \textsc{KGA} abstains rather than commits.} The
multi-candidate route's gate passes on only $2.8\%$ of conditions---the real multi-candidate
statistic $\bar\tau{=}0.89$ exceeds the synthetic-calibrated threshold $\tau^\star{=}0.52$, so the
router \textsc{abstain}s on $61/72$ conditions, \textsc{freeze}s $10$, and commits a single adapt
(the six hospital-TTA variants are co-trained on one backbone---the very correlation the gate is
built to catch). The smooth-drift fallback likewise stays conservative ($21$ freeze / $51$ abstain,
bracket coverage $0.44$): its source-concept term is a conservative $f_0$ surrogate, so the
smooth-drift hypothesis is not cleanly satisfied here. Honest reading: Camelyon17 supplies the
missing \emph{harmful-and-detectable} natural-shift data point, but under the \emph{frozen,
Camelyon-free} $\tau^\star{=}0.52$ \textsc{KGA} does \emph{not} beat both---multi-candidate
regret-to-oracle $0.076$ vs always-freeze $0.071$ vs always-adapt $0.005$ (the worst of the three:
abstaining forgoes this helpful-leaning panel's gains, and the lone commit is harmful). The
$\tau^\star$ fit on synthetic grids is mis-matched to this panel's $\tau$ scale ($\bar\tau{=}0.89$);
\emph{recalibrating $\tau^\star$ on Camelyon17 itself would forfeit its role as an
external-validation set}, so we do not. The honest open direction is a \emph{scale-invariant},
source-calibrated $\tau$ statistic (future work), not per-dataset retuning. Debug-scale, single
protocol; reported as regime-existence evidence, not a policy win.

\paragraph{Headline natural-shift results (Protocols H v2, M v2): uniformly no-harm under a valid out-of-fold radius, and the Camelyon17 (Protocol~G) reclassification.}
The headline natural-shift protocols use canonical \textsc{KGA} (GBR $\widehat\Delta(Z)$ + global
\emph{out-of-fold} $\varepsilon$, $\alpha{=}0.10$ fixed): held-out test scored once, false-adapt
$\le\alpha$. Under a valid out-of-fold conformal radius, \emph{none} is a beats-both: each is
\emph{no-harm}---it matches the better fixed policy and beats the worse one. An earlier draft
reported these as beats-both wins, but those used an \textbf{in-sample} conformal radius (the
benefit estimator and its residual were fit on the same small dev set), which made the radius
roughly $10\times$ too small and manufactured false wins. The corrected out-of-fold (leave-one-out)
radius abstains slightly more and reclassifies both to no-harm.

\textbf{Protocol~G} (WILDS Camelyon17, composition-stress grid): fixed online EATA, rich

\paragraph{Diagnostic ladder (Protocols B/F; not headline).}
Earlier Camelyon runs (sparse $Z$, multicandidate $\tau^\star$, synthetic $\varepsilon$ transplant)
explain failed operating points; they motivated Protocol~G. Protocol~F route audits on the same GPU
records show multicandidate routing fails; GBR+global and PPI+Mondrian variants beat both only on
the same domain-pooled set as Protocol~G (so they inherit its \texttt{id\_val} artifact) and are
not the headline method; the held-out headline natural-shift results are Protocols~H v2 / M v2
(no-harm under a valid out-of-fold radius; Tables~\ref{tab:iwildcam-H}, \ref{tab:officehome-M}),
with Protocol~G reclassified to no-harm (Table~\ref{tab:camelyon17-G}).

\paragraph{Operating-point repair: $\varepsilon$ recalibrated, $\tau^\star$ left alone (a calibration \emph{diagnostic}, not external validation).}
The latent-win diagnosis above is a calibration claim, so we test it directly --- without
touching $\tau^\star$ (lowering it on a co-trained panel would defeat the CEI guard). \emph{Because
this step re-estimates $\varepsilon$ on a Camelyon17 split held out by seed, it touches the external
panel and is therefore a calibration diagnostic, not external validation---and, as shown next, it is
a negative in any case.} The
single-candidate radius $\varepsilon$ was fit on \emph{synthetic} grids; transplanted to
Camelyon17 it violates the Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} exchangeability premise. Re-estimating
$\varepsilon$ from a Camelyon17 calibration split held out \emph{by seed} restores that premise
(calibration and test residuals then share one law). We use all $\binom{4}{2}{=}6$ assignments of
$2$ calibration $+\,2$ test seeds; on each, fit the benefit estimator on the calibration seeds,
set $\varepsilon{=}\widehat{Q}_{1-\alpha}(|\widehat\Delta-B|)$ on \emph{their} residuals
($\alpha{=}0.10$ fixed), freeze it, and decide on the test seeds. The repair is mechanically
real: $\varepsilon$ collapses from the synthetic $\approx0.060$ to $0.030$ and is \emph{stable}
(range $0.007$, CV $0.11$), so the certificate now \emph{commits} (coverage $0.65$) instead of
abstaining. But it is not a level-$\alpha$ win: test-split mean regret is \textsc{KGA} $0.014$
$[0.007,0.021]$ versus always-freeze $0.052$ $[0.037,0.067]$ (cleanly beaten) but always-adapt
$0.013$ $[0.010,0.016]$ (CIs \emph{overlap} --- the panel is helpful-dominated), while
false-adapt is $0.185$ $[0.159,0.211]$, $\approx\!1.9{\times}\,\alpha$. Honest verdict: the harm
is label-free \emph{detectable} (AUC $0.91$) but \emph{not certifiable} at $\alpha{=}0.10$ at this
debug-scale $n_{\mathrm{eval}}{=}256$/cell --- a precise negative. Closing it needs more samples
per cell (tighter residuals) or a harm-skewed panel, not a smaller $\alpha$ or a moved $\tau^\star$.

\paragraph{Pre-registered full-scale re-run ($n_{\mathrm{eval}}{=}1024$, $5$ seeds): the gap
persists.} We pre-registered the $1/\sqrt{n}$ test \emph{before} observing any result
in Protocol~B: quadrupling the per-cell eval
pool to $n_{\mathrm{eval}}{=}1024$ should halve the conformal radius (predicted ratio
$\sqrt{256/1024}{=}0.5$) and pull false-adapt to $\le\alpha{=}0.10$. The $\alpha$-fixed, frozen-$\varepsilon$
seed-split rule was held identical. \emph{Outcome: the prediction is not confirmed.} The full-scale
runner serialized only one aggregate $(Z,B)$ point per seed (tent, eata), \emph{not} the $72{\times}6$
composition grid, so the $1/\sqrt{n}$ radius claim could be evaluated only at coarse per-seed
granularity ($5$ points, $\binom{5}{2}{=}10$ calibration splits). At that granularity the radius did
\emph{not} shrink ($\varepsilon{=}0.029$ tent / $0.038$ eata vs the debug $\varepsilon{\approx}0.030$;
ratio $\approx\!1.0$, not $0.5$) and false-adapt did \emph{not} fall below $\alpha$ ($0.33$ on the
committed seeds vs the archived $0.185$). \textsc{KGA} still cleanly beats always-freeze (regret
$0.020$ vs $0.027$ tent) but trails always-adapt (helpful-dominated panel), at commit rate $0.60$.
\textbf{Honest verdict (\textsc{Fails}, by the pre-registered criteria):} the
detectability--certifiability gap is \emph{not} closed by sample size alone---it is a deeper open
problem. A bias--variance decomposition of the radius \emph{explains why} the $1/\sqrt{n}$ prediction
failed. Splitting the eps-recal residual into estimator model error and
binomial measurement noise ($\sigma^2_{\mathrm{meas}}{=}a_0(1{-}a_0)/n{+}a_a(1{-}a_a)/n$,
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}256$) on a leave-one-seed cross-fit gives an observed radius
$\varepsilon_{256}{=}0.112$ against a measurement floor $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{meas}}(256){=}0.044$
(ratio $2.55\times$), with only a fraction $\approx0.16$ of residual variance attributable to
measurement noise. The radius is thus \emph{bias-limited}: dominated by $\widehat{B}(Z)$ model error
(calibration-drift bias), which does not shrink with eval $n$. Hence the variance-limited target
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{meas}}(1024){=}0.022$ is unreachable and a fair re-run \emph{cannot} close the
radius---the $\alpha$-fixed certifiability of this natural shift is bias-limited, not
sample-size-limited. The open problem is therefore reducing estimator bias (tying back to the
$\gamma$-frontier), not collecting more eval samples. (Cross-check: the per-seed $|B|$ spread at $1024$
is $0.71\times$ that at $256$, not the $0.5$ a variance-limited halving requires, though $n{=}10$ is
weak.)

\paragraph{The pair, read together.} Camelyon17 (detectable harm, AUC $0.78$) and ImageNet-R
(\emph{un}detectable harm, AUC $0.66$; Table~\ref{tab:imagenetr}) bracket the frontier of
Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier}: real harm exists on both ($28.7\%$ / $13.9\%$ of cells), but only on
Camelyon17 is it label-free separable. \textsc{KGA} abstains on both---\emph{correctly} on
ImageNet-R, where Corollary~\ref{cor:forced-abstain} makes abstention mandatory for any valid
certificate, and \emph{conservatively} on Camelyon17, where the harm is detectable but the
operating point is not yet tuned to it. Two honest conclusions follow: (i) the harmful regimes the
theory characterises do occur on natural shift, on both sides of the detectability frontier; and
(ii) turning the detectable side into a committed natural-shift win requires a $\tau$ statistic
whose operating point transfers \emph{without} tuning on the target---a \emph{scale-invariant,
source-calibrated} $\tau$ (future work); per-dataset recalibration on Camelyon17 would forfeit its
external-validation role and is not a legitimate path.

\subsection{Cross-protocol aggregate (out-of-fold re-run)}\label{sec:mixedstream}
Each single dataset above is \emph{one-sided}: Camelyon17 is adapt-favorable
(on its held-out OOD cells freezing leaves regret ${\approx}0.11$), whereas OfficeHome and
iWildCam are freeze-favorable (adapting leaves regret $0.047$ and $0.103$). On a one-sided
stream the better trivial policy is already near-oracle, so \textsc{KGA}'s simultaneous margin
over \emph{both} is necessarily small. To evaluate the policy benefit of per-condition
adapt/freeze/abstain routing across heterogeneous regimes, we \emph{aggregate} the held-out test
conditions of the three dev-locked datasets into a single stream ($n{=}143$); to avoid the
Protocol-G pooling artifact, \emph{Camelyon17 enters through its genuine OOD \texttt{test}/\texttt{val}
cells only} ($36$ cells; in-distribution \texttt{id\_val} excluded). This is a \emph{cross-protocol
aggregate}, \emph{not} one universal gate calibrated once and transferred across datasets: each
dataset keeps its own locked dev-calibrated gate, and we make no cross-dataset transfer claim.
Because this aggregate genuinely mixes helpful conditions (Camelyon) with harmful ones (Office-Home,
iWildCam), it is the one setting in our suite where per-condition routing could in principle beat
both fixed policies.

\paragraph{Out-of-fold re-run (\texttt{mixed\_protocol\_oof\_v2}).}
The earlier figures for this aggregate (regret $0.0026$; ${\sim}24\times$ below always-adapt,
${\sim}13\times$ below always-freeze) used an \emph{in-sample} conformal radius---the calibration bug
corrected in \S\ref{sec:natural-suite}---and are \textbf{withdrawn}. Under leave-one-out conformal
calibration per dataset (\texttt{scripts/mixed\_stream\_kbound.py}), the pooled stream yields mean
regret $\mathrm{regret}_{\textsc{kga}}{=}0.0059$ vs.\ $0.0632$ (always-adapt) and $0.0342$
(always-freeze), with pooled false-adapt $0$. Bootstrap regret gaps: vs.\ always-freeze
$+0.0283$ $[0.019,\,0.038]$; vs.\ always-adapt $+0.0573$ $[0.043,\,0.072]$; both $95\%$ CIs exclude
zero (\texttt{research\_lock/KBOUND\_MIXED\_STREAM\_v2.json}). This is a \textbf{beats-both} on a
\emph{constructed} heterogeneous aggregate---not a natural-shift headline and not a universal
cross-dataset gate. Magnitudes are smaller than the withdrawn in-sample-radius multipliers; we do
not report those ratios as findings. The result demonstrates only the \emph{mechanism} of
per-condition routing under heterogeneity.

\section{Excluded wins and regime boundaries}\label{sec:excluded-wins}
Several benchmarks flag beats-both under alternate routes or adapters but do \emph{not} survive the
canonical Protocol~H/M v2 analysis under a valid out-of-fold radius, where the natural-shift results
are uniformly \emph{no-harm}, not wins. \textbf{Superseded protocols:}
iWildCam Protocol~H v1 (fixed \texttt{sar\_online} without dev-lock) and OfficeHome Protocol~M v1
($14$-candidate win-finder) are audit-only; the canonical natural-shift results use H/M v2
(\texttt{research\_lock/IWILDCAM\_PROTOCOL\_H\_v2.yaml},
\texttt{research\_lock/OFFICEHOME\_PROTOCOL\_M\_v2.yaml}).
\textbf{Office-Home (deployed adapter):} mild online EATA is helpful-dominated on target test
($0\%$ harmful); Table~\ref{tab:officehome} is a covariate-shift diagnostic, distinct from
Protocol~M v2. \textbf{ImageNet-R:} weak detectability (harm-AUC $0.66$); mandatory abstention.
\textbf{iWildCam id\_val:} multicandidate routes can flag wins but pooled false-adapt $\approx 78\%\gg\alpha$.
\textbf{Camelyon four-seed slice:} helpful-dominated (Table~\ref{tab:camelyon}); distinct from
Protocol~G mixed-stress grid.
\textbf{Camelyon17 Protocol~G (withdrawn):} its $n{=}54$ beats-both pooled in-distribution
\texttt{id\_val} into the seed-split held-out set; on genuine OOD test online EATA is
helpful-dominated so \textsc{KGA} ties always-adapt (no beats-both). Reclassified as no-harm
(Table~\ref{tab:camelyon17-G}); the audit and re-run are in
\texttt{audits/integrity\_2026-06-20/camelyon\_reconciliation/}. \textbf{CIFAR-10.1:} within-seed safety holds; cross-seed certificate
transfer does not clear the headline bar.
\textbf{PACS (Protocol~P):} DomainBed domain generalization, leave-one-domain-out
($n{=}108$ held-out cells per domain; ResNet-18 ERM source, conformal radius fit on a held-out
\emph{source} domain---no test labels touch the gate). \textsc{KGA} is no-harm on $3/4$ held-out
domains (art\_painting, cartoon, sketch: matches always-freeze, beats always-adapt, false-adapt
$0$), CI-robustly beating always-adapt on cartoon; on the photo domain---where adapting is
oracle-optimal---\textsc{KGA} safely partial-adapts within budget (false-adapt $0.056\le\alpha$),
beating always-freeze but not always-adapt (a null). See Table~\ref{tab:pacs}.


\section{Limitations and honest scope}\label{sec:limits}
\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.95\columnwidth}{\small
\textbf{What we do not claim.} Universal accuracy gains; adapter selection by test regret;
multicandidate routes with violated false-adapt bounds; or wins on helpful-dominated shifts
where always-adapt is already oracle-optimal.}}
\end{center}

\medskip
(1) Scope of the empirical win: on the CIFAR-10-C \emph{stress} grid \textsc{KGA} beats both
trivial policies for Tent and EATA at $0\%$ false-adapt (Table~\ref{tab:decisive}) and ties
the collapse-resistant SAR; on ImageNet-C (Table~\ref{tab:imagenetc}) the pattern \emph{flips}---
Tent and EATA are robust so \textsc{KGA} ties always-adapt, while SAR collapses and \textsc{KGA}
beats both---a win we confirmed survives a \emph{mechanism-faithful} SAR re-implementation
(Table~\ref{tab:imagenetc-faithful}; SAR harmful on $44\%$ of cells, regret-to-oracle $0.023$ vs
$0.112$/$0.027$), so it is not an implementation artifact, modulo SAR's official gentler-LR,
\texttt{layer4}-frozen schedule, which we have not yet run. On \emph{per-corruption} CIFAR-10-C and within a single online stream (both
helpful-dominated) it again \emph{ties} the better fixed policy. The common rule across all of
these: \textsc{KGA} approximately matches the better trivial policy---paying at most a small
abstention cost when it declines a helpful but uncertifiable update---and \emph{strictly} wins only
where catastrophic, detectable harm is actually present---and which method that is depends on the
benchmark. A tighter online (streaming) certificate is future work.
(2) \textbf{Real-shift scope: no natural-shift beats-both.} Under a valid out-of-fold conformal
radius, every natural shift is \emph{no-harm}, not a beats-both, so the beats-both wins are confined
to the synthetic stress grids (CIFAR-10-C, ImageNet-C SAR). Office-Home (Protocol~M v2) and
iWildCam (Protocol~H v2) beat always-adapt with CIs excluding zero but \emph{tie} always-freeze
(Tables~\ref{tab:iwildcam-H}, \ref{tab:officehome-M}); their earlier in-sample-radius beats-both was
a calibration bug, corrected here. Camelyon17 Protocol~G is likewise \emph{not} a win: its pooled
beats-both included in-distribution \texttt{id\_val} cells, and on genuine OOD test \textsc{KGA}
ties always-adapt (Table~\ref{tab:camelyon17-G}, no-harm).
Earlier Camelyon diagnostics (\S\ref{sec:natural-suite}) and multicandidate routes are not
competing headline claims. \textbf{ImageNet-C} harmful SAR win is synthetic corruption.
\textbf{CIFAR-10.1} is a within-seed low-margin probe; cross-seed transfer fails
(\S\ref{sec:excluded-wins}). \textbf{ImageNet-R} sits in the \emph{unknowable} regime the theory predicts: its
label-free evidence margin falls below the calibration-drift budget, so no candidate clears
the bar and \textsc{KGA} correctly declines to commit---a null that \emph{corroborates}
Theorem~\ref{thm:conj1-dichotomy} (richer label-free evidence is the natural future lever),
not a tuning failure. The deployed
\textbf{Office-Home} adapter audit (Table~\ref{tab:officehome}) is an invalid-transfer regime,
distinct from Protocol~M v2.
\textbf{PACS} (Protocol~P) is a domain-generalization breadth check: \textsc{KGA} is no-harm on
$3/4$ leave-one-domain-out splits and safely partial-adapts on the fourth (Table~\ref{tab:pacs}).
We caution that the conformal radius can still fail to transfer under \emph{severe} geographic or
sensor shift, where in-distribution calibration need not hold out-of-distribution; characterizing
that failure mode is future work.
(3) The conformal radius assumes task exchangeability.
(4) The sign-of-difference characterization is proved for binary, multiclass, and
regression (Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}); the unconditional label-free \emph{bracketing}
problem is resolved \emph{negatively} by Theorem~\ref{thm:conj1-dichotomy}: no
evidence-definable assumption class can identify the benefit sign. On the margin-monotone
relative-calibration class the one-bit supplement certifies the sign \emph{unconditionally}, and
that class is \emph{a} weakest such falsifiable class under a general-position assumption
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cmono-weakest}); the \emph{unconditional} weakest classes are characterized as an
explicit finite family of dominance polytopes (Theorem~\ref{thm:uncond-weakest}), with no unique
weakest class and General Position recovered as the collapsing face.
(5) \textsc{KGA}'s value scales with how often catastrophic, detectable harm
occurs---precisely the quantity the theory characterizes. On helpful-dominated shifts
\textsc{KGA} \emph{ties} always-adapt; this is the certificate declining to commit a harmful update,
not a failure---it approximately matches the better policy (at most a small abstention cost), so a
near-tie where always-adapt is already oracle-optimal is the intended outcome, and the value proposition is
conditional (insurance against detectable harm).
(6) Abstention lowers \emph{decision} coverage by construction but never withholds a
prediction: an \textsc{abstain} routes to the safe default (keep $f_0$, the source model), so
every input still receives an output, and the coverage/false-adapt trade-off is explicit and
tunable via the $\alpha$ safety/coverage sweep reported above.
(7) \textbf{These limitations are the frontier, not flaws.} Points (1) and (4)--(6) are not
independent weaknesses but one phenomenon---the benefit-sign frontier (Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier})
seen from the operating side. Tying always-adapt on a helpful-dominated shift is the no-harm
guarantee \emph{holding} (one cannot beat an oracle that is already always-adapt); ImageNet-R in the
\emph{unknowable} regime is the impossibility direction firing where the observable margin falls
below the drift budget and \emph{no} label-free rule can decide the sign
(Theorem~\ref{thm:conj1-dichotomy}); and the coverage reduction is the \emph{mandatory} abstention of
Corollary~\ref{cor:forced-abstain}, not a tuning loss---committing there would be a false-adapt. None
can be removed without violating the impossibility result: a method that ``won everywhere'' would be
claiming to decide the sign of an unidentifiable quantity. The single axis that improves all three is
a \emph{richer label-free evidence map}---raising the observable margin $|M|$ so more shifts clear the
budget (more knowable shifts, fewer abstentions, higher coverage)---together with a \emph{tighter
radius} (jackknife$+$ or more calibration data) that reclaims knowable cells abstained only out of
conservatism; both are bounded by the frontier they cannot cross.

\section{Discussion: when to deploy K-Bound}\label{sec:discussion}
\begin{sloppypar}
The theory and experiments converge on a clear deployment rule: K-Bound's value is governed by how
often \emph{catastrophic, detectable} harm occurs---safety insurance in benign regimes, real wins in
collapse-prone synthetic grids (Table~\ref{tab:decisive}).
The trichotomy reads off accordingly. \textbf{(i)~Benign regimes} where adaptation
almost always helps (per-corruption CIFAR-10-C, clean anomaly suites): always-adapt is already
near-oracle, and K-Bound is \emph{safety insurance}---it matches always-adapt at a controlled
false-adapt rate, costing only coverage. \textbf{(ii)~Collapse-prone regimes}: small or
class-imbalanced batches, single-class/label-shift streams, aggressive updates, long
non-stationary runs: adaptation becomes a per-condition coin flip, and K-Bound delivers a real
win, cutting regret-to-oracle $\sim$$2.5$--$5.4\times$ versus always-adapt at $0\%$ false-adapt
on the CIFAR-10-C stress grid (Table~\ref{tab:decisive}). \textbf{(iii)~Self-protecting
candidates} (e.g.\ SAR's reset): the headroom shrinks, and K-Bound \emph{ties} rather than
beats---approximately matching the better policy, at most a small abstention cost. The practical takeaway: deploy K-Bound as a default guard around
any TTA candidate; its overhead is one forward pass, and its benefit scales with exactly the
risk it is designed to detect.
\end{sloppypar}

\section{Conclusion}
Whether to adapt to unlabeled target data is a decision constrained by identifiability, and we
reframed label-free TTA around it: adapt when benefit is certifiable, freeze when harm is
certifiable, abstain when the unlabeled evidence cannot tell. We proved the unknowable regime is
fundamental (with a witness), gave a false-adapt-controlled certificate under a stated alignment
assumption, and showed on Camelyon17 that real-shift certifiability is often
\emph{bias-limited}---more unlabeled data cannot shrink the radius when $\widehat{B}(Z)$
carries calibration-drift error. On real
anomaly-routing data the certificate abstains where benefit vanishes and recovers large
performance under failure. The CIFAR-10-C Protocol-A re-run supplies the missing
catastrophic-harm audit: with every per-condition array serialized across seeds $0$--$4$,
the \textsc{Tent}/\textsc{EATA} beats-both claim survives paired bootstrap and Holm
correction, while \textsc{SAR} correctly remains a tie when harmful cells are rare.
On natural shifts, under a valid out-of-fold conformal radius \textsc{KGA} is uniformly no-harm:
Protocols~H v2 and~M v2 (iWildCam, Office-Home) beat always-adapt with CIs excluding zero but
\emph{tie} always-freeze, so neither is a beats-both (Tables~\ref{tab:iwildcam-H},
\ref{tab:officehome-M}); their earlier in-sample-radius wins were a calibration bug, corrected
here. The one-sided shifts Camelyon17 and RxRx1 are likewise honest no-harm results---a re-audit
withdrew the earlier Camelyon17 Protocol-G beats-both as a domain-pooling artifact
(Table~\ref{tab:camelyon17-G}). The only beats-both results are the synthetic stress grids
(CIFAR-10-C, ImageNet-C SAR).

\emph{experimental-batch} shift; the official WILDS ERM ResNet-50, in-distribution accuracy
${\approx}0.36$, frozen accuracy $0.283$ on the OOD-test eval, mean over the three seeds) is the
sharpest harmful-regime real shift in our suite. Over the full $9{+}$ protocol
(composition$\times$batch$\times$aggressiveness over $10$ condition seeds and all $3$ official WILDS

defensive, and it delivers: the single-candidate \textsc{KGA} freezes or abstains on \emph{all} $360$

\paragraph{ImageNet-C, mechanism-faithful SAR re-run: the harmful win is not an implementation artifact.}

collapse concentrated atypically at the \emph{larger} batch, raising the worry that the harmful
regime---and thus \textsc{KGA}'s only ImageNet-scale ``beats both''---was an artifact of our first
SAR port. We re-ran the full noise grid with a \emph{mechanism-faithful} SAR (sharpness-aware
minimisation, the entropy/recovery reset, and the EMA-stabilised update re-implemented to the
reference design), at the learning rate \emph{shared} across all candidates (apples-to-apples).
The harmful regime \emph{persisted and broadened}---SAR is now harmful on $44\%$ of cells (vs
$31\%$ in the first port)---and the \textsc{KGA} win \emph{survived}: regret-to-oracle $0.0229$
versus always-adapt $0.1124$ and always-freeze $0.0267$, \emph{beating both} trivial policies
(Pareto crossover $p^\star{=}0.2$; Table~\ref{tab:imagenetc-faithful}). Tent and EATA stay benign
($6\%$/$3\%$ harmful), so \textsc{KGA} ties the near-oracle always-adapt and beats always-freeze,
exactly as in the first port. The implementation-artifact concern is thus removed: under a
reference-faithful SAR the harmful-candidate win is real. \emph{Residual check, stated honestly.}
This holds at the matched/shared learning rate (mechanism-faithful, apples-to-apples); SAR's
\emph{official} gentler schedule (lr $2.5{\times}10^{-4}$ with \texttt{layer4} frozen) was not run,
and is the one configuration that could still soften the collapse---the last box to tick before the
SAR win is treated as fully settled.

\section{Proof and experiment status}\label{app:status}
Theorem~\ref{thm:imp} is proved with a constructed witness and strengthened to a quantitative Le
Cam minimax floor (part (ii)); Lemma~\ref{thm:gate} is a plug-in regret
decomposition (exact identity) with a minimax floor; Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} is proved
\emph{conditional} on a valid label-free interval estimator and complemented by an anytime-valid
e-process (manuscript, App.~B); Corollary~\ref{thm:pos} proves the covariate case and
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} the binary, multiclass, and regression
sign-of-difference results. The multiclass/regression label-free \emph{bracketing} is closed under
a bounded calibration-transfer assumption (manuscript, App.~C); the \emph{unconditional}
bracketing (Conjecture~\ref{conj:gen}) is resolved as a testability dichotomy
(Theorem~\ref{thm:conj1-dichotomy}: no evidence-definable assumption identifies the
sign; the minimal untestable supplement is one bit, which suffices under falsifiable
structure). Every theorem ships a
numerical sanity-check in the release package; all were re-run
from a clean environment and pass: the Le Cam floor tracks the closed form, the regret identity
holds to ${\sim}10^{-17}$, the multiclass identity to $10^{-16}$ with $100\%$ sign agreement over
$4000$ trials, calibration-transfer sign-recovery is $100\%$ when the confidence gap exceeds
$2\eta$, the router generalization gap decays as ${\sim}n^{-0.66}$, and the drift-corrected anytime
false-adapt rate stays $\le\alpha$. Where multi-seed runs are available, experiments report means
$\pm$ std with paired $t$-tests; single-seed/scoping tracks are labeled explicitly in captions and
discussed as regime-classification evidence. We also report evidence/$\alpha$/batch ablations, a
regression covariate-shift track, and a clean
non-identifiability witness.
