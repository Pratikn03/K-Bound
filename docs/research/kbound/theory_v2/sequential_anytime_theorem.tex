\begin{assumption}[Online benefit stream]\label{asm:stream}
Windows arrive sequentially $t=1,2,\dots$ on a filtered probability space
$(\Omega,\mathcal F,(\mathcal F_t)_{t\ge0},\Pr)$. Window $t$ produces a single
bounded, label-free-computable \emph{benefit summary}
$X_t\in[a,b]$ with $a<0<b$, where $X_t$ is the same paired benefit statistic the
batch certificate averages (Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}: on the disagreement
region $f_0$ vs.\ $f_a$, $X_t$ is the per-window plug-in of $\delta=\ell(f_0)-\ell(f_a)$,
estimable without labels under the locked protocol). We write
$\Delta_t:=\E[X_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]$ for the \emph{conditional} benefit of
window $t$ given the past, and assume $X_t$ is $\mathcal F_t$-measurable. No
independence, identical distribution, or stationarity across windows is assumed.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[What $X_t$ must and must not be]\label{rmk:Xt}
Validity below uses only the conditional mean $\Delta_t=\E[X_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]$ and
the bound $X_t\in[a,b]$. As in the batch certificate, this is a guarantee about
the \emph{decision driven by the benefit summary}, not about the fidelity of the
benefit model: if $X_t$ is a biased estimate of the true window benefit (the
$\gamma\neq0$ regime of Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier}), the bound controls
false-adapt with respect to the \emph{sign of $\Delta_t=\E[X_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]$},
the population object the stream actually targets. Identifiability of the true
benefit sign from $X_t$ remains governed by the frontier $|M|>\beta$; the present
result adds time-uniformity on top of that, it does not remove the drift budget.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[One-sided betting supermartingale]\label{lem:smg}
Let $(\lambda_t)_{t\ge1}$ be \emph{predictable} (each $\lambda_t$ is
$\mathcal F_{t-1}$-measurable) with $\lambda_t\in[0,\,c/|a|]$ for a fixed
$c\in(0,1)$. Define
\[
   E_0^{+}=1,\qquad
   E_t^{+}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\big(1+\lambda_i X_i\big).
\]
Then each factor satisfies $1+\lambda_i X_i\ge 1+\lambda_i a\ge 1-c>0$, so
$E_t^{+}>0$. If $\Delta_t\le0$ for every $t$, then $(E_t^{+})_{t\ge0}$ is a
nonnegative $(\mathcal F_t)$-supermartingale with $E_0^{+}=1$. Symmetrically, with
predictable $\lambda_t^{-}\in[0,c/b]$ and
$E_t^{-}=\prod_{i\le t}(1+\lambda_i^{-}(-X_i))$, if $\Delta_t\ge0$ for every $t$
then $(E_t^{-})$ is a nonnegative supermartingale with $E_0^{-}=1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Positivity is immediate from $\lambda_i\in[0,c/|a|]$ and $X_i\ge a$:
$\lambda_i X_i\ge \lambda_i a\ge -c>-1$. For the supermartingale property,
$\lambda_t$ and $E_{t-1}^{+}$ are $\mathcal F_{t-1}$-measurable, so
\[
   \E\!\big[E_t^{+}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}\big]
   =E_{t-1}^{+}\,\big(1+\lambda_t\,\E[X_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\big)
   =E_{t-1}^{+}\,(1+\lambda_t\Delta_t)\ \le\ E_{t-1}^{+},
\]
the inequality because $\lambda_t\ge0$ and $\Delta_t\le0$ give $1+\lambda_t\Delta_t\le1$.
Integrability holds as $0<E_t^{+}\le\prod_i(1+\lambda_i b)\le(1+cb/|a|)^t<\infty$.
The $E^{-}$ claim is the same argument applied to $-X_t$ (mean $-\Delta_t\le0$),
using $-X_t\ge -b$ and $\lambda_t^{-}\in[0,c/b]$. $E_0^{+}=E_0^{-}=1$ by the empty product.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Anytime-valid sequential adaptation certificate]\label{thm:anytime}
Under Assumption~\ref{asm:stream}, fix a level $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and predictable
bets $\lambda_t,\lambda_t^{-}$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:smg}, and run the sequential rule
\[
\textsc{decision}_t=
\begin{cases}
\textsc{adapt}  & \text{if } E_t^{+}\ge 1/\alpha,\\[2pt]
\textsc{freeze} & \text{if } E_t^{-}\ge 1/\alpha,\\[2pt]
\textsc{abstain}& \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\]
committing (absorbing) at the first time a threshold is crossed. Let
$\tau$ be \emph{any} stopping time (an analyst or controller may monitor
continuously and stop for any data-dependent reason). Then:

\textnormal{(i)} \emph{(Time-uniform false-adapt.)} On the global non-benefit
null $H_0:\ \Delta_t\le0$ for all $t$,
\[
   \Pr\!\big(\exists\,t\ge1:\ \textsc{decision}_t=\textsc{adapt}\big)
   \;\le\;\Pr\!\Big(\sup_{t\ge1} E_t^{+}\ge 1/\alpha\Big)\;\le\;\alpha .
\]
In particular, the realized decision $\textsc{decision}_\tau$ at any stopping time
satisfies $\Pr(\textsc{decision}_\tau=\textsc{adapt})\le\alpha$ under $H_0$.

\textnormal{(ii)} \emph{(Time-uniform false-freeze.)} Symmetrically, on
$H_0':\ \Delta_t\ge0$ for all $t$,
$\Pr(\exists t:\ \textsc{decision}_t=\textsc{freeze})\le\alpha$.

\textnormal{(iii)} \emph{(Anytime confidence sequence, common-mean case.)}
If the windows share a common conditional benefit $\Delta_t\equiv\Delta^\star$,
inverting the two one-sided betting supermartingales over a grid of candidate
means yields a $(1-2\alpha)$ confidence sequence $(\mathrm{CI}_t)_{t\ge1}$ for
$\Delta^\star$ with $\Pr(\exists t:\ \Delta^\star\notin\mathrm{CI}_t)\le2\alpha$
(the betting confidence sequence of Waudby-Smith \& Ramdas
\cite{waudbysmith2024betting}); the committal rule above is its
decision-theoretic projection. For genuinely time-varying means, a time-uniform
confidence sequence for the running average
$\overline{\Delta}_t:=\frac1t\sum_{i\le t}\Delta_i$ is obtained instead from the
additive sub-$\psi$ supermartingale of Howard et al.~\cite{howard2021timeuniform}:
the multiplicative betting process certifies the \emph{uniform} one-sided null
$\{\Delta_t\le m\ \forall t\}$, a different functional from $\overline{\Delta}_t$,
and we do not conflate the two. Parts \textnormal{(i)--(ii)} do not depend on this part.

\textnormal{(iv)} \emph{(Generalization of the batch certificate.)} Run
on a single window ($t=1$) with the calibrated bet that matches the conformal
radius, the rule specializes to a level-$\alpha$ single-window decision of the same
adapt/freeze/abstain form as the batch certificate (Theorem~\ref{thm:cert})---the $T{=}1$ slice, of
which the present theorem is the time-uniform extension. (The two level-$\alpha$ budgets are distinct
probabilistic objects---a conformal/exchangeability statement over the calibration draw for
Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}, a martingale statement over the window law here---so this is a structural
specialization, not a claim that the two guarantees coincide identically.) In contrast, applying the batch rule afresh to each of $K$ windows and
committing on the first rejection has worst-case false-adapt
$1-(1-\alpha)^K\to1$, not $\alpha$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{(i)} By Lemma~\ref{lem:smg}, under $H_0$ the process $(E_t^{+})$ is a
nonnegative supermartingale with $E_0^{+}=1$. Ville's maximal inequality for
nonnegative supermartingales \cite{ville1939,howard2021timeuniform,ramdas2023}    
states that for any nonnegative supermartingale $(W_t)$ with $W_0=1$ and any
$\lambda\ge1$, $\Pr(\sup_{t\ge0}W_t\ge\lambda)\le 1/\lambda$. Taking
$W_t=E_t^{+}$ and $\lambda=1/\alpha$,
\[
   \Pr\!\Big(\sup_{t\ge1}E_t^{+}\ge 1/\alpha\Big)\le\alpha .
\]
Because the rule is absorbing at the first crossing of \emph{either} threshold, the event
$\{\exists t:\textsc{decision}_t=\textsc{adapt}\}$ is \emph{contained in}
$\{\sup_t E_t^{+}\ge1/\alpha\}$---a prior \textsc{freeze} crossing can pre-empt an
\textsc{adapt}, and for the one-sided adapt-only process the two events coincide---giving the displayed bound. For the
stopping-time form, $\{\textsc{decision}_\tau=\textsc{adapt}\}\subseteq\{\exists
t\le\tau:E_t^{+}\ge1/\alpha\}\subseteq\{\sup_t E_t^{+}\ge1/\alpha\}$, so the same
$\alpha$ bound holds; this is the optional-stopping content (no per-look
correction and no horizon are needed, which is precisely the failure mode of the
batch rule under continuous monitoring).

\textbf{(ii)} Identical, with $(E_t^{-})$ the supermartingale under $H_0'$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:smg}.

\textbf{(iii)} Under the common-mean hypothesis $\Delta_t\equiv\Delta^\star$, for
each fixed $m$ the process $\prod_{i\le t}(1+\lambda_i(X_i-m))$ has conditional
increment factor $1+\lambda_i(\Delta^\star-m)$, hence is a nonnegative
supermartingale whenever $m\ge\Delta^\star$ (and the mirrored process when
$m\le\Delta^\star$); by Ville each one-sided process crosses $1/\alpha$ with
probability $\le\alpha$, so
$\mathrm{CI}_t=\{m:\ \text{neither one-sided e-process for }X_i-m\text{ has crossed
}1/\alpha\text{ by }t\}$ covers $\Delta^\star$ with
$\Pr(\exists t:\Delta^\star\notin\mathrm{CI}_t)\le2\alpha$ by a union bound over the
two one-sided exceedance events (each $\le\alpha$ by Ville)---the betting
confidence sequence of \cite{waudbysmith2024betting}. The committal rule of the
theorem is the projection ``commit \textsc{adapt} once
$\mathrm{CI}_t\subset(0,\infty)$'', ``\textsc{freeze} once
$\mathrm{CI}_t\subset(-\infty,0)$'', recovering (i)--(ii). For time-varying
$\Delta_t$ the multiplicative process is \emph{not} a supermartingale at
$m=\overline{\Delta}_t$ (a window with $\Delta_i>m$ makes the increment factor
exceed $1$), so the running-average claim does not follow from betting; the
correct object is then the sub-$\psi$ confidence sequence of
\cite{howard2021timeuniform}, which we cite rather than reprove.

\textbf{(iv)} For $T=1$, $E_1^{+}=1+\lambda_1 X_1$ with $\lambda_1$ a fixed
(deterministic, hence predictable) constant, and $E_1^{+}\ge1/\alpha\iff
X_1\ge(1/\alpha-1)/\lambda_1$; choosing $\lambda_1$ so the threshold equals the
batch decision boundary $\hat\Delta-\varepsilon=0$ reproduces the level-$\alpha$
single-batch rule of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} (whose marginal false-adapt is
$\le\alpha$). The multiplicity claim is the elementary observation that $K$
\emph{independent} level-$\alpha$ batch tests, each rejecting with probability
$\alpha$ under the null and combined by ``commit on first rejection'', have
family-wise false-adapt $1-(1-\alpha)^K$, which $\to1$ as $K\to\infty$; for
dependent batches the same conclusion follows from $\Pr(\bigcup_k R_k)\le\alpha$
failing in general (no maximal inequality applies to a sequence of \emph{reset}
batch statistics). Hence repeated batch testing is not anytime-valid, while the
single accumulating e-process is. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Predictable betting and adaptivity]\label{rmk:bet}
Validity (i)--(ii) holds for \emph{any} predictable $\lambda_t\in[0,c/|a|]$; only
\emph{power} depends on the choice. The implementation uses the truncated
predictable plug-in (``aGRAPA'') bet
$\lambda_t=\mathrm{clip}\!\big(\hat\mu_{t-1}/\hat\sigma^2_{t-1},\,0,\,c/|a|\big)$
of Waudby-Smith \& Ramdas \cite{waudbysmith2024betting}, with $\hat\mu_{t-1},
\hat\sigma^2_{t-1}$ the running mean and variance of past benefits; this attains
the empirical-Bernstein detection rate $t\asymp\sigma^2\Delta^{-2}\log(1/\alpha)$
matching the batch sample complexity of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}, while preserving
time-uniform validity.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scope, and the honest boundary]\label{rmk:scope}
The null in (i) is \emph{conditional and global}: $\Delta_t=\E[X_t\mid\mathcal
F_{t-1}]\le0$ for every $t$. This is exactly the event ``the world is never
beneficial'', and a harmful adapt is committing \textsc{adapt} during it; that is
the natural false-adapt event for a controller that acts once on a stream. Two
boundaries are deliberately \emph{not} over-claimed. (a) The guarantee does not
assert per-window conditional coverage of the form ``$\Pr(\textsc{adapt at }t\mid
\Delta_t\le0,\,\text{benefit was positive earlier})\le\alpha$'': once some early
window is genuinely beneficial ($\Delta_s>0$), wealth may legitimately accumulate
and a later \textsc{adapt} then \emph{rejects the global never-beneficial null}
$\{\Delta_t\le0\ \forall t\}$, certifying that some window was genuinely
beneficial---a true positive against that null, not a false adapt. A controller
needing to react to \emph{regime reversals} (benefit turning negative after being
positive) must add a change-detection layer (e.g.\ the CUSUM/Page detector of the
companion $T6$ analysis); that is a detection-delay question with its own
ARL/AED trade-off, orthogonal to the type-I guarantee proved here. (b) As in
Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}, $X_t$ must be a valid (conditionally unbiased up to the
declared drift budget) benefit summary; bias in $X_t$ re-enters through the
frontier $|M|>\beta$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier}, which this result does not
relax.
\end{remark}
