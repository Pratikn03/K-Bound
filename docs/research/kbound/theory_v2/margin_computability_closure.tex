\section{Frontier-margin computability: closed dichotomy}\label{sec:margin-compute-closure}

Conjecture~\ref{conj:dich-compute} asked whether the frontier margin $m(O)$ is always a
computable functional of $Q_X$. Wave~3 attempted an unconditional refutation that was
\textbf{retracted as circular}. This section states the \textbf{repaired} negative construction
and the positive regularity theorem as a single closed dichotomy.

\begin{theorem}[Margin-computability dichotomy]\label{thm:margin-compute-dichotomy}
\textbf{(Negative.)} The literal conjecture ``$m$ is always computable'' is \textbf{false}.
There exists a \emph{computable point} $Q_o$ of the space of Borel probability measures (weak
topology) such that $\Phi$ holds (benefit sign factors through the observable reduct on the
definite region) yet the frontier margin $m(O)$ is \textbf{not} a computable functional of
$Q_X$: a Specker-scheduled atom on $\operatorname{disc}(c_{gf})=\{0\}$ makes
$\int c_{gf}\,\mathrm dQ_o$ left-c.e.\ with a non-located zero crossing.

\textbf{(Positive.)} If $Q_\vartheta$ is atomless on $\operatorname{disc}(c_{gf})$ and the
benefit crossing is transversal ($|\Delta'|$ bounded below), then $\Delta(O)$ is a computable
strictly monotone function of the observable reduct with a computable modulus; $m$ is computable
by bisection.

Hence the dividing line is exact: atom on the discontinuity $\Rightarrow$ $m$ may be
non-computable; atomless + transversal $\Rightarrow$ $m$ computable. Every concrete family in
the paper satisfies the positive hypothesis.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\textbf{Negative (repaired).} Let $Q_o=(1-m_0)\mathrm{Unif}[-1,1]+m_0\delta_{o-s}$ with
$s=\sum_{k\in K}2^{-k}$ a left-c.e.\ Specker real and $c_{gf}=\sgn$. Continuous test integrals
$\int f\,\mathrm dQ_o$ are uniformly computable with modulus $m_0 L\cdot(s-s_{\downarrow}(t))$;
the atom \emph{location} $o-s$ crosses $0$ at $o=s$, so $\int\sgn\,\mathrm dQ_o=m_0\sgn(o-s)$
has a non-computable crossing. The first-draft mass-drift construction is circular because
$\int x\,\mathrm dQ$ becomes non-computable.

\textbf{Positive.} Standard bisection on a computable strictly monotone $\Delta$; see
\texttt{val\_margin\_computability.py} Block P1.
\end{proof}
