\begin{abstract}
Test-time adaptation (TTA) can improve a model on unlabeled target data, but the same update can also silently harm performance. We study the decision that comes \emph{before} adaptation: should a model adapt, freeze, or abstain? We define the adaptation benefit as the target-risk difference between a frozen baseline and an adapted candidate. Our central result is an exact \emph{benefit-sign frontier}: over a declared calibration-drift class, the sign of the adaptation benefit is identifiable from label-free evidence \emph{if and only if} an observable disagreement-region margin exceeds the admitted drift budget, $|M|>\beta$. Below this frontier, two target worlds can produce identical label-free evidence while requiring opposite actions, making abstention necessary.

We instantiate the framework as Knowability-Guided Adaptation (\textsc{KGA}), a mechanism-agnostic wrapper around existing TTA methods. Under stated coverage, exchangeability, and risk-alignment assumptions, \textsc{KGA} controls the unconditional false-adapt and false-freeze probabilities. Across held-out natural shifts, \textsc{KGA} is uniformly no-harm: it matches the better fixed policy and beats the worse. In identifiable mixed regimes, it beats fixed adapt/freeze policies and protocol-matched POEM-style and AETTA-style baselines; in a pre-registered pooled heterogeneous deployment, a single universal gate beats both fixed policies across seven natural sources. K-Bound is therefore a safety layer for adaptation, not a universal accuracy booster.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.86\columnwidth]{figures/fig_decision_flow.png}
\caption{\textbf{K-Bound previews the decision.} From an unlabeled batch under shift, the certificate
estimates the adaptation benefit $\widehat\Delta$ with a finite-sample radius $\varepsilon$ and returns
\textsc{adapt} (knowably helpful), \textsc{freeze} (knowably harmful), or \textsc{abstain} (sign
unidentifiable), with false-adapt/false-freeze $\le\alpha$.}
\label{fig:teaser}
\end{figure}
Test-time adaptation (TTA) updates a model on the unlabeled test stream and, under favorable
distribution shift, recovers accuracy. Under unfavorable shift, the same objective silently
degrades it---and with no target labels, the system usually cannot tell which case it faces.
The decision that matters therefore comes \emph{before} the update: given a frozen
baseline $f_0$ and an adapted candidate $f_a$, choose
\textbf{adapt} when adaptation is knowably helpful, \textbf{freeze} when knowably harmful, or
\textbf{abstain} when label-free evidence cannot identify the sign of the benefit. This replaces
the field's default assumption---make adaptation robust enough that it can always run---with an
explicit, certified decision about whether to run it at all.

Some cases are information-theoretically unknowable. It is established that label-free
quantities cannot decide adaptation without an assumption on the shift: identifying OOD
accuracy is as hard as identifying the optimal predictor~\cite{garg2022atc}, and the optimal
predict-versus-abstain rule under a shift budget has a known form~\cite{kalai2021abstain}. We
sharpen this landscape, for the benefit sign, into a computable criterion: whether
adaptation helps or hurts is label-free identifiable \emph{if and only if} an observable margin
exceeds the calibration-drift budget on the disagreement region---$|M|>\beta$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}). On the unidentifiable side, two target worlds carry identical
label-free evidence but opposite benefit, so no label-free rule can be correct in both
(Theorem~\ref{lem:nonid}) and abstention is forced. \textsc{KGA}
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:kga})---a benefit estimator plus a conformal radius, wrapped around any
adapter (Tent, EATA, SAR)---operationalizes the frontier as a finite-sample certificate holding
false-adapt and false-freeze at level $\alpha$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate}).

The evidence follows the regime map the theory draws, summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:regime-summary}: held-out \emph{natural} shifts are uniformly \textbf{no-harm}
(\textsc{KGA} matches the better fixed policy and beats the worse; no single-dataset natural
beats-both is claimed); \emph{identifiable mixed} harmful+helpful regimes supply the
\textbf{beats-both} policy wins, including a pre-registered head-to-head over protocol-matched
POEM-style and AETTA-style baselines; and a pre-registered \emph{pooled heterogeneous}
deployment shows one universal gate beating both fixed policies across seven natural sources.
K-Bound does \emph{not} claim universal improvement: when adaptation is already safe, a valid
certificate can only tie always-adapt. It is a safety layer for TTA, not an accuracy booster.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Problem framing.} TTA as a label-free \emph{adapt/freeze/abstain} decision---whether
to adapt at all, not how to adapt---specializing the known impossibility
landscape~\cite{garg2022atc,kalai2021abstain} to the benefit sign.
\item \textbf{One exact result (the spine).} The \emph{benefit-sign frontier}: $\sign\Delta$ is
label-free identifiable over the declared drift class \emph{if and only if} $|M|>\beta$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}); the ``only if'' is a matched-evidence impossibility
(Theorem~\ref{lem:nonid}), the ``if'' yields a finite-sample certificate
(Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate}). The proofs hold for any \emph{split-observable} functional
($\sign\Delta=\sign(M+\gamma)$, $M$ observable, $|\gamma|\le\beta$), with TTA the demonstrated
instance.
\item \textbf{Unification.} ATC, DoC, GDE, COT, AETTA, and agreement-on-the-line are the
zero-budget ($\beta{=}0$) face of the frontier---sound precisely when calibration does not drift.
\item \textbf{A drop-in method.} \textsc{KGA}, a mechanism-agnostic certificate wrapper around
Tent/EATA/SAR (Algorithm~\ref{alg:kga}).
\item \textbf{Regime-mapped evidence.} Uniform no-harm on five held-out natural benchmarks at
$\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}{=}0$ (the primary deployment claim); beats-both plus a pre-registered
win over protocol-matched POEM-style/AETTA-style baselines in identifiable mixed regimes; and a
pre-registered universal-gate win on a pooled seven-source heterogeneous deployment
(Table~\ref{tab:regime-summary}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
Across every adjacent line, one distinction recurs---the one the theory formalizes:
\emph{improving} an unlabeled objective versus \emph{certifying the sign} of its benefit
before acting.

\paragraph{TTA methods.}
Tent~\cite{wang2021tent}, SHOT~\cite{liang2020shot}, TTT~\cite{sun2020ttt}, EATA~\cite{niu2022eata},
SAR~\cite{niu2023sar}, CoTTA~\cite{wang2022cotta}, and MEMO~\cite{zhang2022memo} make adaptation
more robust but commit to adapting (see~\cite{liang2025ttasurvey} for a survey); none certifies
the sign of benefit \emph{before} updating, and none abstains on the adapt/freeze decision itself.

\paragraph{Guarded and monitored TTA.}
POEM~\cite{bar2024poem} and sequential risk monitors~\cite{schirmer2025monitoring,podkopaev2022tracking}
act during or after adaptation and control false alarms in one failure direction. PeTTA senses
drift toward collapse and adjusts the adaptation process on the fly~\cite{hoang2024petta}, and
sample-reliability gates such as DeYO decide \emph{which samples} are safe to adapt
on~\cite{lee2024deyo}. K-Bound decides \emph{before} committing at the deployment level, poses a
three-way decision with an explicit unknowable region, and controls false-\emph{adapt} on the
benefit sign.

\paragraph{Label-free accuracy and error estimation.}
ATC~\cite{garg2022atc}, Agreement-on-the-Line~\cite{baek2022aol,miller2021aol}, and
AETTA~\cite{lee2024aetta} estimate target performance without labels, as do prediction-matrix
dispersity and logit-norm estimators~\cite{deng2023confidence,xie2024mano}, whose signals are
natural candidates for our evidence map $Z$. Garg et al.~\cite{garg2022atc} also establish the
limit governing this whole line: identifying OOD accuracy is as hard as identifying the optimal
predictor, so every such estimate is sound only under an assumption on the shift. We \emph{organize} these estimators rather than compete with
them: ATC, DoC, GDE, COT, AETTA, and agreement-on-the-line are all the zero-budget
($\beta{=}0$) face of our frontier, sound precisely when calibration does not drift. Most
directly, \cite{kim2024taol} applies agreement-on-the-line to decide, at the \emph{population}
level, when TTA helps or hurts and to select an adaptation strategy---a plug-in use on that same
$\beta{=}0$ face; it issues no per-deployment certificate, no \textsc{abstain} action, and no
identifiability limit, and its linear trend is known to break on shifts (e.g.\ Gaussian-noise
corruption)---precisely the regime where K-Bound abstains. Estimating
$R_T(f)$ differs from certifying $\sign(R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a))$ before committing the candidate update,
with controlled false-adapt rate. Thus, K-Bound is not an accuracy estimator used as a gate; it
changes the target estimand from target accuracy to the sign of the frozen-versus-adapted
risk difference, with abstention when that sign is not identifiable.

\paragraph{Impossibility, abstention under shift, and identifiability.}
The general impossibility we specialize is owned by prior work: Garg et al.~\cite{garg2022atc}
prove identifying OOD accuracy is as hard as identifying the optimal predictor, and Kalai \&
Kanade~\cite{kalai2021abstain} give the matching decision theory---the optimal abstain-versus-predict
rule under an observable-quantity-versus-shift-budget threshold, with a minimax certificate. We
cite these as the sources and do not re-derive them. Unsupervised risk
estimation~\cite{steinhardt2016,bendavid2010theory,rosenfeld2023dis2} and selective
prediction~\cite{geifman2017selective} likewise show performance is identifiable only under
structure. K-Bound \emph{specializes} this landscape to the \emph{sign} of adaptation benefit,
certifying it before committing with an explicit abstain region and false-adapt control at level
$\alpha$. What none of these lines supplies---and what the next section makes exact---is the
computable boundary ($|M|>\beta$) between the adaptation decisions one can certify label-free and
those no rule ever can.

\section{Problem setup and theory}\label{sec:setup}\label{sec:theory}

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/fig_frontier_schematic.png}
\caption{\textbf{The benefit-sign frontier.} The sign of the adaptation benefit is identifiable from
label-free evidence iff the observable margin $|\widehat{M}|$ exceeds the calibration-drift budget
$\beta$. Outside the band \textsc{KGA} \textsc{adapt}s (knowably helpful) or \textsc{freeze}s (knowably harmful);
inside the band---the unknowable region---abstention is information-theoretically forced.
($\widehat M$ is the finite-sample estimate of the population margin $M$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}.)}
\label{fig:frontier}
\end{figure}

Source $P_S(X,Y)$ has labels at train/validation; target $P_T(X,Y)$ exposes only $P_T(X)$
at test time. Fix loss $\ell$, frozen $f_0$, and candidate $f_a$. Target risk
$R_T(f)=\E_{P_T}[\ell(f(X),Y)]$. The \emph{adaptation benefit} is
\begin{equation}
\Delta \;=\; R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a),\qquad \Delta>0 \iff \text{adapting helps.}
\end{equation}
\emph{Observable evidence} $Z=\phi(X_{1:m},f_0,f_a)$ is any statistic computable without
target labels (entropy, confidence, drift, disagreement, update norms). The central
question is when $\sign\Delta$ is recoverable from $Z$ alone. The certificate needs two \emph{separate}
requirements. \textbf{(i) Risk-alignment} is a property of $Z$ and the class: $\sign\Delta$ is a
measurable function of the population law of $Z$ over the declared class---equivalently, no two
admissible worlds with opposite benefit sign induce the \emph{same} $Z$-law. \textbf{(ii) Coverage}
is a property of the calibrator: the benefit interval
$[\widehat\Delta-\varepsilon,\widehat\Delta+\varepsilon]$ has marginal coverage $\ge1-\alpha$.
Risk-alignment makes $\sign\Delta$ recoverable in principle; coverage makes the finite-sample
certificate valid. When $Z$ is \emph{not} risk-aligned, no label-free rule can recover $\sign\Delta$
(Theorem~\ref{lem:nonid}) and the certificate must abstain.

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
\textbf{What ``label-free'' means.} No target/test labels at deploy; labeled source and
held-out validation may calibrate $\widehat\Delta$ and $\varepsilon$. Evidence $Z$ uses only
unlabeled batches, predictions, entropy, drift, and update statistics. The method is
\textbf{target-label-free}, not label-free of all supervision: labels may be used only in the
source/validation calibration split, never in the deployment batch or held-out test decision.}}
\end{center}

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
\textbf{K-Bound in one sentence.} The true adaptation-benefit sign is recoverable only when
label-free evidence exceeds the uncertainty created by calibration drift; otherwise the correct
action is abstention.}}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Four quantities that must not be confused.}
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{0.3\linewidth}lp{0.26\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning & At deploy? & Role \\
\midrule
$M$ & observable evidence margin & yes & evidence toward adapt/freeze \\
$\gamma$ & realized calibration drift & no & can reverse the benefit sign \\
$\beta$ & declared bound on $|\gamma|$ & externally supplied & population frontier \\
$\varepsilon$ & uncertainty radius from held-out residuals & estimated pre-deploy & finite-sample certificate \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent $\varepsilon$ is an operational radius calibrated from residuals; it is \emph{not} an
estimate of the worst-case drift $\beta$.

\begin{definition}[Regimes]
\emph{Knowably helpful}: evidence certifies $\Delta>0$ (adapt).
\emph{Knowably harmful}: evidence certifies $\Delta<0$ (freeze).
\emph{Unknowable}: the same $Z$ is compatible with both signs (abstain).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Matched evidence forces abstention]\label{lem:nonid}
There exist worlds $P_T^1,P_T^2$ in the declared class with
$\Law(Z\mid P_T^1)=\Law(Z\mid P_T^2)$ but $\sign\Delta_1=-\sign\Delta_2\neq0$. No label-free rule
can be correct in both; any rule with false-adapt and false-freeze each controlled at $\alpha$
must abstain on matched evidence with probability at least $1-2\alpha$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Intuition.} Two target worlds can induce identical label-free evidence while
adaptation helps in one and harms in the other; a rule that controls false adaptation and false
freezing must abstain there. \emph{Why it is true:} (1)~a swap construction on the disagreement
region produces two admissible worlds with the same evidence law and opposite benefit signs;
(2)~any label-free rule sees the same $Z$-law in both, so its action distribution is identical;
(3)~each committed action is a false commitment in one of the two worlds, so control at level
$\alpha$ in both forces abstention with probability $\ge1-2\alpha$. \emph{Scope:} an
impossibility over the declared class $\mathcal C_\beta$, not a statement about any single fixed
target.

\begin{theorem}[Exact benefit-sign frontier]\label{thm:headline}
On the disagreement region $D=\{x:f_0(x)\neq f_a(x)\}$, write $M=\E[s\mid D]-\tfrac12$ for a
source-calibrated score $s$ of $f_a$ and $\gamma=\E[\eta_a-s\mid D]$ for the \emph{(unobserved)}
calibration drift. Then
\[
\sign\Delta=\sign(M+\gamma),\qquad |\gamma|\le\beta,
\]
and over the declared target class $\mathcal C_\beta=\{P_T:|\gamma|\le\beta\}$ with supplied
drift budget $\beta\ge0$, the benefit sign is label-free identifiable \emph{if and only if}
\[
|M|>\beta .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Region & Meaning & Action \\
\midrule
$M>\beta$ & allowed drift cannot flip the positive sign & \textsc{adapt} \\
$M<-\beta$ & allowed drift cannot flip the negative sign & \textsc{freeze} \\
$|M|\le\beta$ & drift can flip the sign & \textsc{abstain} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent\emph{Intuition.} The decision is safe exactly when the observable margin clears the
admitted drift, and impossible when drift can dominate it. \emph{Why it is true:} (1)~the
disagreement-region reduction confines the benefit to $D$ and yields the split-observable
decomposition $\sign\Delta=\sign(M+\gamma)$; (2)~if $|M|>\beta$, every admissible drift leaves
the sign of $M+\gamma$ equal to that of $M$, so the sign is a function of observables; (3)~if
$|M|\le\beta$, two admissible drifts realize opposite signs on matched evidence and
Theorem~\ref{lem:nonid} applies. \emph{Scope:} population-level and exact in both directions;
$\beta$ must be externally justified for the deployment class (full dichotomy:
Appendix~\ref{app:theory-full}). \emph{Worked example.} (A)~$M{=}0.18$, $\beta{=}0.10$:
$M-\beta=0.08>0$, so adaptation stays beneficial under every allowed drift---\textsc{adapt}.
(B)~$M{=}0.07$, $\beta{=}0.10$: $M+\gamma$ may be negative or positive, so the sign is not
identifiable---\textsc{abstain}.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-sample adapt/freeze/abstain certificate]\label{thm:certificate}
Suppose the benefit estimate satisfies coverage
\[
\Pr\!\left[\,|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon\,\right]\ \ge\ 1-\alpha .
\]
Then the rule
\[
g(Z)=\begin{cases}
\textsc{adapt} & \widehat\Delta-\varepsilon>0,\\
\textsc{freeze} & \widehat\Delta+\varepsilon<0,\\
\textsc{abstain} & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
controls both marginal error probabilities:
\[
\Pr[\textsc{adapt},\,\Delta\le0]\le\alpha,
\qquad
\Pr[\textsc{freeze},\,\Delta\ge0]\le\alpha .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
\textbf{Warning.} This theorem controls the \emph{marginal, unconditional} false-adapt and
false-freeze probabilities ($\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}$). It does \emph{not} bound the conditional
false-adapt rate among committed \textsc{adapt} decisions,
$\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}=\Pr[\Delta\le0\mid\textsc{adapt}]$, which is reported empirically.}}
\end{center}

\noindent\emph{Intuition.} A valid uncertainty radius turns the population frontier into an
operational rule: commit only when the entire benefit interval lies on one side of zero.
\emph{Why it is true:} (1)~on the coverage event, $\widehat\Delta-\varepsilon>0$ implies
$\Delta>0$; (2)~a false adapt therefore requires the coverage event to fail; (3)~that failure has
probability at most $\alpha$, and symmetrically for freeze. \emph{Scope:} requires exchangeable
calibration pairs, risk-aligned evidence, and bounded benefits (\S\ref{sec:method});
$\varepsilon$ is an operational surrogate valid under these assumptions---not the frontier budget
$\beta$.

\begin{corollary}[Unjustified assumptions $\Rightarrow$ abstain]\label{cor:abstain-valid}
The certificate's false-adapt guarantee holds only under the stated coverage/exchangeability and
risk-alignment assumptions. When those assumptions cannot be justified for the deployment batch
(e.g.\ the evidence is not risk-aligned, or the calibration sample is not exchangeable with
deployment), the guarantee does not apply, and the conservative operational action is to abstain or
freeze rather than commit to adaptation: committing adapt without a valid positive certified lower
bound is not protected by the target false-adapt level $\alpha$. (At deployment $\Delta$ is
unobservable, so this is a statement about \emph{assumptions}, not about observing
$|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|$.)
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{How the pieces fit.}
\begin{center}\small

\textbf{Thm.~\ref{lem:nonid}} (abstention necessity) \\[2pt]
$\rightarrow$ split-observable decomposition $\sign\Delta=\sign(M+\gamma)$ $\rightarrow$
\textbf{Thm.~\ref{thm:headline}} ($|M|>\beta$ frontier) \\[2pt]

\paragraph{Extensions.} One-bit orientation---with the weakest one-bit class characterized
unconditionally as a finite family of dominance polytopes (Appendix~\ref{app:weakest},
Theorem~\ref{thm:uncond-weakest})---minimax order-optimality on the identifiable side,
multicandidate family-wise routing, anytime-valid streaming, and the Wave-4 dichotomies (exact
three-world minimax constant, margin computability, multiclass capacity, regression bracketing)
are proved and validated in Appendix~\ref{app:theory-full} and the long manuscript
(\texttt{kbound.tex}). They strengthen the framework but are not required to understand the main
K-Bound result. The streaming form is demonstrated on real data: on the native
$35{,}370$-image iWildCam stream ($45$ windows, $75.6\%$ harmful), the betting e-process detects
continual-Tent collapse at window~$6$ with anytime false-adapt $0$, and the resulting gate value
($0.6995$) ties the oracle policy exactly (pre-registered).

\section{Method: \textsc{KGA}}\label{sec:method}
\textsc{KGA} turns the frontier certificate into a deployable rule. It is the only headline
method---a decision wrapper around a fixed adapter, not a new adaptation objective. We fit the
benefit estimator and its radius \emph{out of fold}, so the data used to fit $\widehat\Delta$ and
the data used to calibrate its residual are disjoint for every point.
On the per-cell stress grids (CIFAR-10-C and the synthetic grids), for each cell $i$ a gradient-boosted
$\widehat\Delta_{-i}$ is trained on the other $N{-}1$ cells and evaluated on cell $i$, and
$\varepsilon$ is the $(1-\alpha)$ empirical quantile of the leave-one-out residuals
$\{|\widehat\Delta_{-i}(Z_i)-\Delta_i|\}_{i=1}^{N}$---leave-one-out (jackknife) cross-conformal, so no
cell's residual uses an estimator that saw it. On the natural-shift protocols (Camelyon17, iWildCam,
Office-Home) the split is across domains: $\widehat\Delta$ and $\varepsilon$ are fit once on a dev
split and the held-out test domain is scored a single time---ordinary split-conformal. At deploy we
extract $Z$ from an unlabeled batch and return adapt, freeze, or abstain (Algorithm~\ref{alg:kga}).
Labels enter only through the calibration split; the deployment batch and held-out test scorer never
use target labels for the adapter, $\varepsilon$, evidence, or rule selection. The coverage
guarantee is split-dependent. The natural-shift protocols use a clean dev/test split:
\emph{split-conformal} coverage $\Pr[|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon]\ge1-\alpha$, exact in
finite samples under exchangeability of the calibration and test cells. The per-cell grids set
$\varepsilon$ to the $(1-\alpha)$ empirical quantile of the leave-one-out residuals, so the
\emph{calibration-set} coverage is $\ge1-\alpha$ by construction (realized $0.898$ at nominal
$0.90$, $\alpha{=}0.10$)---the standard jackknife radius, which is distribution-free only
asymptotically under estimator stability; its formal finite-sample counterpart is the jackknife$+$
of Barber et al.\ (2021) at level $1-2\alpha$, which we note but do not implement. The decision
rule's false-adapt guarantee is stated under the coverage hypothesis (\S\ref{sec:method}), which the
empirical coverage meets. This construction sits in a known landscape: weighted conformal corrects
coverage under known covariate shift~\cite{tibshirani2019conformal}, adaptive conformal tracks
coverage online~\cite{gibbs2021adaptive}, and beyond exchangeability the coverage gap is bounded by
the total-variation drift of the calibration pairs~\cite{barber2023beyond}---the population
counterpart of our declared budget $\beta$. The certificate's marginal false-adapt control is risk
control in the RCPS/Learn-then-Test lineage~\cite{bates2021rcps,angelopoulos2025ltt}, specialized
to the adapt/freeze/abstain loss; PAC prediction sets give set-valued analogues under covariate
shift~\cite{park2022pac}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\textsc{KGA}: Knowability-Guided Adaptation}
\label{alg:kga}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Frozen $f_0$; adapter $\mathcal{A}$; batch $X_{1:m}$; $\widehat\Delta$, $\varepsilon$, $\alpha$
\Ensure Decision $g\in\{\textsc{adapt},\textsc{freeze},\textsc{abstain}\}$
\State $f_a \leftarrow \mathcal{A}(f_0, X_{1:m})$
\State $Z \leftarrow \phi(X_{1:m}, f_0, f_a)$
\State $\widehat\Delta \leftarrow \widehat\Delta(Z)$;\;
  $L \leftarrow \widehat\Delta-\varepsilon$;\; $U \leftarrow \widehat\Delta+\varepsilon$
\If{$L>0$} \State $g\leftarrow\textsc{adapt}$
\ElsIf{$U<0$} \State $g\leftarrow\textsc{freeze}$
\Else \State $g\leftarrow\textsc{abstain}$
\EndIf
\State \Return $g$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

On the good event $|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon$, committing adapt only when
$L>0$ implies $\Delta>0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate}). Assumptions: exchangeable
calibration pairs, bounded benefits, and risk-aligned $Z$; no target labels at deploy.
Overhead: one forward pass of $f_0$ and $f_a$ plus lightweight $\widehat\Delta$ evaluation.

\paragraph{The threshold is derived, not tuned.}
\textsc{KGA}'s commit boundary is not a swept hyperparameter, but the population frontier and the
empirical radius must be kept distinct. The \emph{population} frontier of
Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}---over the declared class $\mathcal C_\beta$, $\sign\Delta$ is
identifiable iff the disagreement margin $|M|$ exceeds the supplied budget $\beta$---fixes the
\emph{required} budget at population level; the drift realization $\gamma\le\beta$ is unknown and
not observed at deploy. \textsc{KGA} does not
observe $\gamma$; it commits under a \emph{conservative empirical radius} $\varepsilon$, the
$(1-\alpha)$ residual quantile on the calibration split, fixed there and \emph{never} selected on
target-test data. The two are related but not identical: $\varepsilon$ is a finite-sample,
assumption-dependent surrogate that controls false-adapt under the stated exchangeability and
risk-alignment assumptions---not the frontier itself. Confidence-thresholding (ATC) and
agreement-trend selection (AGL) fit a scalar against a labeled or held-out signal and
predict \emph{accuracy}; \textsc{KGA} certifies the benefit \emph{sign} with a boundary
\emph{forced} by the identifiability budget, and---unlike any thresholded estimator---%
\textsc{abstains} on the region where Theorem~\ref{lem:nonid} \emph{proves} no label-free
rule can decide. The impossibility construction, not a tuned cutoff, is what separates
\textsc{KGA} from ``estimate accuracy, then threshold.''

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}
The experiments test one claim: \textsc{KGA} is a safety layer, beating both fixed policies only
where harmful adaptation is frequent and detectable, and doing no harm everywhere else. We evaluate
it on two controlled corruption/stress tracks (CIFAR-10-C stress, ImageNet-C SAR) and three held-out
natural-shift replications (Protocols G, H v2, and~M v2; Table~\ref{tab:regime-summary}). Calibration
and held-out scoring follow Algorithm~\ref{alg:calib-eval} (Appendix~\ref{app:calib-eval}). Metrics: mean accuracy; regret-to-oracle against the per-condition oracle
$g^\star=\mathbf1[\Delta>0]$; \emph{adapt rate} $\Pr[\text{adapt}]$ and \emph{coverage}
$\Pr[\text{adapt or freeze}]$ (one minus the abstain rate); and two false-adapt measures, reported
separately---the \emph{unconditional} false-adapt probability
$\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}=\Pr[\textsc{adapt},\,\Delta\le0]$ and the \emph{conditional} false-adapt
rate $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}=\Pr[\Delta\le0\mid\textsc{adapt}]$. Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate} bounds the
\emph{unconditional} probability, $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}\le\alpha$, under its coverage and
risk-alignment assumptions; the per-dataset tables additionally list the empirical
$\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}$ among committed adapt decisions. The two false-adapt measures coincide
whenever no harmful adapt is committed ($\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}{=}\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}{=}0$ on
every headline dataset, since \textsc{KGA} commits none) and diverge only for rules that commit some;
the full four-metric breakdown---$\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}$, $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm c}$, adapt-rate, and
coverage---where the leaky gates separate from the certificate is therefore reported side by side in
the decision-baseline comparison (Table~\ref{tab:gates}), so the reader sees both what the theorem
bounds and what happens operationally among adapted decisions.

(\texttt{research\_lock/KBOUND\_WIN\_BOOTSTRAP\_CIS\_oof.json}).

\paragraph{Cross-protocol aggregate (not a universal mixed deployment).}
Each single dataset is one-sided, so \textsc{KGA}'s simultaneous margin over \emph{both} policies is
necessarily small there. To evaluate the policy benefit of per-condition adapt/freeze/abstain
routing across heterogeneous shift regimes, we \emph{aggregate three independently dev-calibrated
held-out deployment protocols}: OfficeHome ($35$), iWildCam ($72$), and Camelyon17 ($36$ genuine OOD
\texttt{test}/\texttt{val} cells; in-distribution \texttt{id\_val} excluded to avoid the Protocol-G
pooling artifact), for $n{=}143$ conditions. This is a \emph{cross-protocol aggregate}, \emph{not}
one universal gate calibrated once and transferred across datasets: each dataset keeps its own
locked dev-calibrated gate, and we make no cross-dataset transfer claim. (Camelyon17 contributes its
$36$ OOD \texttt{test}+\texttt{val} cells here, whereas the single-dataset headline of
Table~\ref{tab:camelyon17-G} uses the $n{=}18$ OOD \texttt{test} split alone; both exclude
in-distribution \texttt{id\_val}.) Because this aggregate genuinely mixes helpful conditions
(Camelyon) with harmful ones (Office-Home, iWildCam), it is the one setting where per-condition
routing can in principle beat both fixed policies. The earlier in-sample-radius figures are
\textbf{withdrawn}.\footnote{Withdrawn figures: regret $0.0026$, ${\sim}24\times$ vs.\
always-adapt, ${\sim}13\times$ vs.\ always-freeze (in-sample conformal radius).} The corrected
out-of-fold re-run (\texttt{mixed\_protocol\_oof\_v2}) yields regret $0.0059$ vs.\ $0.0632$
(always-adapt) and $0.0342$ (always-freeze), with both regret-gap CIs excluding zero at pooled
false-adapt $0$.\footnote{Gaps: $+0.0283\,[0.019,0.038]$ vs.\ always-freeze;
$+0.0573\,[0.043,0.072]$ vs.\ always-adapt.} This is a \textbf{beats-both} on a \emph{constructed}
cross-protocol aggregate.

\paragraph{Universal single gate (pre-registered, 2026-07-03).} The per-dataset-gate caveat is
closed by two subsequently pre-registered arms (bars frozen before scoring, each scored once;
\texttt{research\_lock/WIN\_HUNT\_v2/v3\_PROTOCOL.yaml}): \emph{one} benefit model with
\emph{one} pooled leave-one-out conformal radius, fit once on pooled dev records over the shared
base-11 evidence and applied unchanged to the pooled held-out conditions. Three-source stream
($n{=}143$): regret $0.0182$ vs.\ always-adapt $0.0632$ / always-freeze $0.0342$, both $95\%$
CIs excluding zero, $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}{=}0.000$. Seven-source extension adding all four
PACS leave-one-domain splits ($n{=}359$): regret $0.0183$ vs.\ $0.0404$ / $0.0282$, both CIs
excluding zero, $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}{=}0.000$. The gate rides adaptation on Camelyon17,
freezing on iWildCam/Office-Home, and no-harm ties across PACS. Honest scope: the stream
composition is researcher-pooled; this is a certified heterogeneous-deployment win under a
universal calibration, not a single-dataset natural-shift win.

\section{Limitations and honest scope}\label{sec:limits}

\medskip
\textbf{Scope of empirical wins.} On the stress grid \textsc{KGA} beats both for Tent/EATA
and ties SAR (Table~\ref{tab:decisive}); on ImageNet-C the pattern flips by candidate---Tent/EATA
are robust while SAR collapses and \textsc{KGA} beats both under a mechanism-faithful re-run
(Table~\ref{tab:imagenetc-faithful}). Per-corruption CIFAR-10-C and helpful natural shifts are
insurance regimes, not policy wins.

\textbf{Real-shift scope.} Under a valid out-of-fold conformal radius, no \emph{single-dataset}
natural shift is a beats-both: Office-Home and iWildCam are \emph{no-harm} (they beat always-adapt
and tie always-freeze; Tables~\ref{tab:officehome-M}, \ref{tab:iwildcam-H}), so the single-dataset
beats-both wins are on the synthetic stress grids (CIFAR-10-C, ImageNet-C SAR). The exception is
the pooled heterogeneous deployment: a single universal gate calibrated once beats both fixed
policies across seven natural sources ($n{=}359$, pre-registered; \S\ref{sec:nat-shift-headline}),
because pooling restores the mixed harmful+helpful composition the frontier requires; the stream
composition is researcher-pooled. Camelyon17 Protocol~G is reclassified to an
honest \emph{no-harm} result---its earlier $n{=}54$ beats-both pooled in-distribution
\texttt{id\_val} into the held-out set and is withdrawn (Table~\ref{tab:camelyon17-G}). Earlier
Camelyon Protocol~B/F runs and multicandidate routes are \textbf{diagnostics}, not alternate
headline wins.

\textbf{Theory and calibration.} The conformal radius assumes exchangeable calibration pairs;
sign bracketing without structure is impossible (Theorems~\ref{lem:nonid}
and~\ref{thm:headline}; Appendix~\ref{app:theory-full}). \textsc{KGA}'s value scales with how often catastrophic,
detectable harm occurs---not with universal accuracy gains. On helpful-dominated shifts the
resulting near-\emph{tie} with always-adapt reflects the certificate abstaining rather than
committing harmful updates---\textsc{KGA} approximately matches the better policy, at most paying a
small abstention cost when it declines a helpful but uncertifiable update---so the value proposition
is conditional, like insurance against detectable harm.

\textbf{Detectability and transfer.} ImageNet-R still sits in a weak, split-specific
evidence regime (Appendix~\ref{sec:excluded-wins})---an instance of the \emph{unknowable}
region Theorem~\ref{thm:headline} predicts, where the label-free margin falls below the
calibration-drift budget; \textsc{KGA} correctly declines to commit, so this is the theory
operating as designed, not a tuning failure, and richer label-free evidence is the natural
future lever. A calibrated-gate re-analysis refines this verdict: the self-normalized gate
$\tau'$ rejects $83\%$ of co-adapted backbone panels but only $1/54$ independent panels, and
under independent panels a certified selection arm reaches near-oracle regret ($0.0005$)---so
part of the earlier ``mandatory abstention'' was a fixed-threshold artifact of co-adapted
panels rather than pure unknowability (pre-registered; v1 fixed-$\tau^*$ arms still FAIL,
FA $3.7$--$4.9\%$). iWildCam H v2 clears the certificate bar
despite modest margin over always-freeze; intermediate-domain multicandidate routes do not
(pooled false-adapt $\gg\alpha$).

\textbf{The limitations are the frontier, not flaws.} The regime dependencies above are not three
independent weaknesses but one phenomenon---the benefit-sign frontier (Theorem~\ref{thm:headline})
seen from the operating side. Tying always-adapt on a helpful-dominated shift is the no-harm
guarantee \emph{holding}: one cannot beat an oracle that is already always-adapt. Declining
ImageNet-R is the \emph{unknowable} branch firing: the observable margin falls below the drift
budget, so no label-free rule can decide the benefit sign, however good the adapter
(Theorem~\ref{lem:nonid}). The coverage drop is mandatory abstention, not a tuning loss: committing in
the unknowable region would be a false-adapt. None can be removed without violating the impossibility
result---a method that ``won everywhere'' would be claiming to decide the sign of an unidentifiable
quantity. The one axis that genuinely improves all three is a \emph{richer label-free evidence map},
which raises the observable margin $|M|$ so more shifts clear the budget (more knowable shifts, fewer
abstentions, higher coverage), together with a \emph{tighter radius} (jackknife$+$ or more
calibration data) that reclaims coverage on knowable cells abstained only out of conservatism---both
bounded by the frontier they cannot cross.

\textbf{Missing baselines and independent validation.} ImageNet-C committal gates
(ATC-style confidence; Appendix~\ref{app:frontier}, Table~\ref{tab:app-imagenetc}) false-adapt at
$100\%$ on harmful SAR cells, while \textsc{KGA} abstains there. The pre-registered mixed
head-to-head vs.\ POEM/AETTA (Table~\ref{tab:headtohead-poem-aetta}) is complete; an optional
official-repo arm (per-sample POEM, dropout-AETTA) remains a camera-ready faithfulness check.
Independent replication of ImageNet-R and Office-Home M v2 across more seeds would strengthen
stability claims. Abstention lowers \emph{decision} coverage by construction but never withholds a prediction---an \textsc{abstain} keeps the safe default $f_0$ (the source model), so every input still receives an output, and the coverage/false-adapt trade-off is tunable via~$\alpha$.

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
\textbf{What we do not claim.} Universal accuracy gains; adapter selection by test regret;
multicandidate routes with violated false-adapt bounds; or wins on helpful-dominated shifts
where always-adapt is already oracle-optimal.}}
\end{center}

\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:repro-main}
All code, locked protocol yamls (\texttt{research\_lock/}), and result JSONs backing every
reported number are in the anonymized submission repository. Controlled and natural-shift
headline runs are reproduced via \texttt{bash docs/research/kbound/scripts/kbtrain.sh}
(\texttt{protocol-h-v2}, \texttt{protocol-m-v2}, stress-grid targets); theory validators and
CPU experiments run from the bundled reproduction driver. The core certificate module is
packaged as an installable module in the anonymized repository (\texttt{docs/research/kbound/kbound\_pkg/}).
A kernel-checked Lean~4 formalization of the \emph{algebraic and finite-testing core} of K-Bound
accompanies the repository ($58$ indexed theorems, no \texttt{sorry}/\texttt{admit}/project-defined
axioms). The certificate's probability layer is machine-checked as well: coverage implies the
unconditional false-adapt/false-freeze bound over an \emph{arbitrary probability measure}
(\texttt{measure\_false\_adapt\_le\_alpha}), and split-conformal coverage holds in the
conditional-on-the-bag uniform-rank model
(\texttt{uniformIndex\_false\_adapt\_le})---to our knowledge the first machine-checked
conformal-coverage guarantee in a proof assistant (kernel-checked generalization bounds exist,
e.g.~\cite{zhang2025leanrademacher}, but not conformal coverage). General exchangeable-process
coverage, and the remaining deployment-facing assumptions---calibration transfer and risk
alignment---remain explicit assumptions outside the formalization.

\section{Conclusion}
K-Bound reframes test-time adaptation as an identifiability-limited decision problem: adapt
when helpful, freeze when harmful, and abstain when label-free evidence cannot support a valid
decision. The theory shows that this abstention region is not merely conservative; under matched
label-free evidence with opposite adaptation benefit, no valid label-free rule can safely commit.
\textbf{Our headline empirical result is uniform no-harm on five held-out natural shifts} under a
valid out-of-fold radius---\textsc{KGA} prevents harmful adaptation without sacrificing the
near-oracle policy on helpful-dominated shifts. Wins over trivial policies and protocol-matched
POEM-style/AETTA-style baselines appear only in identifiable mixed harmful+helpful regimes---and,
under a single pre-registered universal gate, on the pooled seven-source heterogeneous
deployment---exactly where the frontier predicts knowability.
These results suggest that future TTA systems should expose not only an adaptation objective, but
also a validity layer that decides whether adaptation is knowable before deployment.
