## 0. One-paragraph verdict

**`beta` is a property of a population of deployments, not of a deployment.** Under two assumptions
— retrospective outcome logging (E1) and exchangeability of the current deployment with `K` logged
ones (E2) — `beta` is identified as a quantile of the episode-level `|gamma|` law, estimable by a
conformal order statistic, feasible at `K >= 1/alpha - 1`, and provably infeasible below
`K >= 1/(e*alpha) - 1`. That is a real positive result and it escapes `thm:short-audA` at a precisely
locatable point: Aud-A quantifies **over the matched-evidence fibre**, the episode budget averages
**over the episode law**, and past labels are what make the average non-vacuous. Neither ingredient
alone suffices, and I prove both halves of that. **But the assumption that does the work, E2, fails
on the real artifacts exactly along the axis a deployment cares about.** On CIFAR-10-C the estimator
attains its nominal 0.900 coverage when the seed, batch size or class-balance protocol changes
(0.862–0.912) and collapses to **0.626** when the corruption family changes and **0.454** when the
severity changes. The weighted-conformal relaxation restores coverage to 1.000 by driving decision
yield to 0.000. So the honest summary is: **episode structure converts an unfalsifiable
per-deployment declaration into a falsifiable population-level one and buys a marginal — not
conditional — guarantee. It does not make `beta` knowable at the deployment that needs it.** This
strengthens the impossibility spine rather than weakening it: it says precisely what would have to be
true for the frontier to operationalize, and then shows it is not.

## 2. The results

All statements, assumptions and full proofs: `theory_beta_estimable.tex`. Status of each is marked in
a comment above it in that file. Summary:

| # | statement | status |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | retrospective outcome logging: `n` labels per past episode, revealed after the fact | assumption |
| E2 | `(V_1,...,V_{K+1})` exchangeable, `V_e = (Z_e, M_e, gamma_e)` | assumption — **the binding one** |
| **B1(a)** | `beta*(alpha) := (1-alpha)`-quantile of `|gamma|` under the episode law `Q` is identified from the episode law of `(W, gamma)`, and is the smallest budget with episode-marginal false-commit rate `<= alpha` | **PROVED** |
| **B1(b)** | `beta*` is **not** identified from the episode law of the observables `W = (Z,M)` alone: for every `b` in `[0,1/2]` there is an episode law with the same `W`-marginal and `beta* = b`. **The binding resource is historical labels, not historical data.** | **PROVED** (Aud-A's kernel construction, applied episodewise) |
| **B2** | episode-conformal budget `beta_hat = S_(j) + t(n,delta)`, `j = ceil((1-alpha+delta)(K+1))`, `S_e = |gamma_hat_e|`; covers `|gamma_{K+1}|` w.p. `>= 1-alpha`; feasible iff `alpha >= 1/(K+1) + delta` | **PROVED** — = Aud-G with `gamma_pred = 0` |
| **B3** | *escape localisation.* (a) any **fibre-blind** budget — including a history-based one — that is valid **uniformly over the fibre** still obeys Aud-A's vacuous bound `beta_hat >= 1/2 + |M|`; (b) B2's guarantee is `Q`-marginal, and replacing the fibre supremum by the `Q`-average is the escape; (c) without labels the `Q`-average is as uninformative as the supremum (B1(b)) | **PROVED** |
| **B4** | *episode floor.* Any budget valid over all i.i.d. episode laws that returns anything below `b` on a drift-free history needs `alpha >= (1/(K+1))(1-1/(K+1))^K >= 1/(e(K+1))`, i.e. `K >= 1/(e*alpha) - 1`. With B2's `K <= 1/alpha - 1`, the requirement is bracketed to a factor of `e`. At `alpha=0.1`: between 3 and 9 historical deployments. | **PROVED** |
| **B5** | *label price.* Bernstein gives `n >= 8 sigma^2 ln(2/delta)/(kappa beta)^2 + 8ln(2/delta)/(3 kappa beta)`. Under pointwise calibration of `s`, `sigma^2 = E[s(1-s)] = Var(C) - Var(s) < Var(C)`: **the score is a control variate and estimating `gamma` is strictly cheaper in labels than estimating `abar - 1/2`, by exactly `Var(s)`.** | **PROVED** |
| **B6** | *probe lower bound + amortization.* Any rule with `k` deploy-time probe labels that commits w.p. `>= 1-eta` with both directional errors `<= alpha` at margin `eps` needs `k >= 3(1-2alpha-eta)^2/(16 eps^2)`; Aud-B achieves `O(eps^-2 log(1/delta))`. Break-even against the episode route at `N* = Kn/k = Theta(K sigma^2 log(1/delta) (eps/beta)^2)`. | **PROVED** |
| **B7** | *graceful degradation.* (a) `dQ'/dQ <= rho` gives coverage `>= 1 - rho*alpha`; retaining `alpha` costs `K >= rho/alpha - 1` episodes. (b) episode covariate shift with stable `gamma|W` is handled exactly by weighted split conformal with **label-free-estimable** weights. | **PROVED** ((b) cites Tibshirani–Barber–Candès–Ramdas 2019, not reproved) |
| **B8** | the conditional-stability hypothesis of B7(b) is itself not auditable label-free (Aud-A at each fixed `w`); it is *falsifiable* given retrospective labels on two episode populations, which a declared `beta` at one deployment is not. **The assumption moves from unfalsifiable to falsifiable, not from unverified to verified.** | **PROVED** (immediate corollary of Aud-A) |
| B9 | open: is there a structure weaker than E2 and falsifiable from the history alone under which `beta*` is identified for a novel episode? | **CONJECTURE**, stated with why the two natural candidates fail |

### The Aud-A escape, stated once more because it is the load-bearing check

`thm:short-audA` has two hypotheses that the episode budget breaks, and **it needs both**:

1. `beta_hat = g(Z)` — a function of the *current* deployment's label-free evidence.
   The episode budget is a function of *past labels*. But this alone is not the escape: B3(a) proves
   that any budget whose law does not vary across the matched-evidence fibre — which includes any
   history-based budget, since the history is independent of the current episode's label kernel —
   still obeys `beta_hat >= 1/2 + |M|` **if validity is demanded uniformly over the fibre**.
2. Validity **uniformly over** `P(mu_T)`. E2 replaces that supremum with an average over `Q`, and the
   adversarial worlds `abar in {0,1}` that force Aud-A's bound carry `Q`-probability at most `alpha`.

Drop (2) and Aud-A still bites. Drop (1) and B1(b) bites: the `Q`-average over unlabeled observables
is consistent with every `beta` in `[0,1/2]`. **The escape is: average instead of supremum, paid for
with historical labels.** The price is that the guarantee is marginal over deployments, not
conditional on the deployment — the same distinction as `rem:fa-marginal`. A deployer told "beta was
estimated" gets a long-run rate, not protection today.

## 3. Is E2 plausible? Honest answer

**Where it holds.** A stable operational process — fixed fleet of sites, recurring seasonal cycle, a
queue drawn from a stable market — with the model, candidate update rule and scoring function held
fixed. This is the same assumption already made whenever a team computes a historical error rate and
uses it as a forecast. It is weakly checkable: past budgets can be back-tested on later episodes.

**Where it fails, and this is the interesting case.** When the current deployment differs *in kind*
rather than in draw: a new corruption mechanism, a new site, a new sensor, a new severity regime.
That is the case that motivates test-time adaptation in the first place. A deployer who could
guarantee E2 could often have retrained instead.

**So the assumption is not one nobody can check — it is one that is checkable in the easy regime and
fails in the hard one.** §4 measures exactly that, and the measurement is the point.

## 4. Numerical validation on the real artifacts

### 4.1 Protocol, and the null

Episodes are the 6480 CIFAR-10-C and 405 ImageNet-C cells (condition × method × seed);
`gamma := Delta - M` in benefit units, exactly as in `beta_sweep.py`. Each split partitions cells
into three disjoint pools:

* `F` — the pool the benefit model `M(.)` is fit on (labeled);
* `H` — the history, labels revealed, scores `S_e = |Delta_e - M(Z_e)|`;
* `D` — deployment, labels used only for evaluation.

`M` is fit **once** on `F` and applied **identically** to `H` and `D`, so the scores on `H` and `D`
are exchangeable exactly when the episode populations are. No deployment label ever touches `M` or a
history score. Five random `F/H` splits per protocol; `alpha = 0.10`.

> **The null is 0.900.** Any protocol whose deployment set is exchangeable with its history *must*
> return coverage `~ 0.90` — that is the theorem, not a finding. The findings are the deviations.
> (This is the same class of trap as the documented `np.quantile` null in
> `adversarial_ablations_results.json`, and it is handled here by evaluating on a disjoint set and by
> reporting the `RAND` positive control.)

**Two bugs I found in my own first implementation and fixed, both recorded in the code:**

1. A nested cross-fit made history residuals systematically larger than deployment residuals,
   manufacturing coverage of 1.000 under the exchangeable control. Fixed by the three-pool design
   above.
2. `episode_weights` used `cross_val_predict(..., cv=5)` without shuffling. `StratifiedKFold` then
   took contiguous blocks, and the deployment rows arrive in the artifact's native
   (method, seed, condition) order, so each fold was a coherent block of conditions. The
   separability AUC came out at **0.24** on splits whose true separability is 0.5. With
   `shuffle=True` the control reads **0.497 ± 0.033**. Anyone reusing `cross_val_predict` on these
   artifacts should shuffle.
   The superseded run is kept as `*_UNSHUFFLED_SUPERSEDED.*` rather than deleted.
3. Seeding used `hash(name)`, which Python salts per process. Replaced by `zlib.crc32`. The script
   is now bit-reproducible.

### 4.3 The seven findings

**F1 — the estimator is correct.** Control coverage 0.905 and 0.912 against a null of 0.900, with
separability AUC 0.497 ± 0.033. The machinery is not broken and is not tuned.

**F2 — the `K` floor is exactly as proved.** `K`-sweep on CIFAR-10-C / `M_gbm`, 200 draws per `K`:

| K | 3 | 5 | **9** | 10 | 20 | 50 | 200 | 1000 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| feasible fraction | 0.00 | 0.00 | **1.00** | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| mean coverage | — | — | **0.907** | 0.909 | 0.912 | 0.905 | 0.897 | 0.903 |
| sd | — | — | 0.088 | 0.084 | 0.059 | 0.043 | 0.031 | 0.021 |

Infeasible at `K = 3, 5`; first feasible and immediately at nominal coverage at
`K = 9 = ceil(1/alpha) - 1`, matching B2/B4's bracket. **Nine logged deployments is the whole
requirement** — the binding constraint is not sample size.

**F3 — nuisance axes are safe, deployment axes are not.** Within 0.04 of nominal for seed, batch
size, class-balance protocol; 0.626 and 0.454 for corruption family and severity. `rho_impl` rises
from 1.0 to 5.5. B7(a) says restoring `alpha = 0.10` under `rho = 5.5` costs `K >= 54` episodes.

The per-split oracle diagnostic is worse than that. For each split I compute the level `alpha'` a
deployer would have had to declare on the history to reach 0.90 coverage on the deployment episodes,
and the implied `K = 1/alpha' - 1`. On the safe axes every split is feasible with `K` between 7 and
13. On the hard axes many splits are **infeasible outright** — the budget needed exceeds *every*
score in the history, so no declared level works at any `K`:

| config | infeasible splits | median K needed (feasible ones) |
|---|---:|---:|
| CIFAR `RAND` / `M_gbm` | 0 / 15 | 9 |
| CIFAR `LOPROT`, `LOBATCH` / `M_gbm` | 0 / 15 | 9–12 |
| CIFAR `LOSO` / `M_gbm` | 5 / 25 — **all five are the `LOSO/0` splits**, see §4.4 | 8 |
| CIFAR `LOMO` / `M_gbm` | 0 / 15 | **46** |
| CIFAR `LOCO` / `M_gbm` | **7 / 30** | **36** |
| CIFAR `LOSEV` / `M_gbm` | **5 / 10** | (11, on the feasible half only) |
| CIFAR `LOSEV` / `M_atc4` | **5 / 10** | (4, on the feasible half only) |
| ImageNet `LOMO` / `M_atc4`, `M_gbm` | **9 / 15**, **8 / 15** | (small, feasible half only) |

Read the infeasible column, not the median: on the severity axis half of all splits admit **no**
episode budget at any level, so quoting a median `K` there would understate the problem.

**F4 — a better benefit model transfers worse.** `M_gbm` beats `M_atc4` on decision yield (0.647 vs
0.446) and is strictly worse on budget transfer on *every* deployment axis: `LOCO` 0.626 vs 0.866,
`LOSEV` 0.454 vs 0.595, `LOSTR` 0.721 vs 0.768, `LOMO` 0.804 vs 0.864. Mechanism: fitting the benefit
surface harder shrinks history residuals without shrinking residuals on a novel episode, so the
quantile it produces is too small. **This is a warning that applies to the shipped `b_hat` too**, and
it is a genuinely uncomfortable finding for any "just use a stronger evidence model" reply.

**F5 — weighted conformal restores validity by destroying yield.** On `LOSEV`, coverage goes
0.454 → **1.000** and yield goes 0.615 → **0.000**; on `LOCO`/`M_atc4`, 0.866 → 0.959 with yield
0.438 → 0.172. The label-free weights concentrate on the handful of history episodes resembling the
deployment, the weighted quantile runs off the end of the score list, and the frontier abstains
everywhere. **This is Aud-A's vacuity reappearing inside the method that was supposed to escape it**
— and it is the cleanest empirical illustration of the impossibility spine in the whole project.

**F6 — the beta-sweep's failure was never a quantile-estimation failure.** Across all 470 splits the
conformal budget and the plug-in `q_{0.90}` budget the beta-sweep declares differ by a **median
relative gap of 0.34%**, with a **median coverage difference of exactly 0.000**. At `K` in the
thousands the conformal correction moves one order statistic. **The beta-sweep's negative result
survives intact and is now explained**: what it measured was E2 failing, not a quantile estimator
failing. Conversely, when E2 holds the frontier does operationalize — on an exchangeable three-way
split of CIFAR-10-C the episode budget delivers yield 0.641, regret 0.0019, `FA_u = 0.0002` against
the shipped conformal-interval rule's 0.662 / 0.0015 / 0.0000 on the same cells. On ImageNet-C it
does not: 0.344 vs 0.508.

**F7 — E2's observable component is falsifiable label-free; its conditional component is not.**
`Delta_sep` = cross-fitted AUC separating history from deployment episodes on `Z` alone (no labels)
correlates with realised coverage at Pearson `r = -0.346` (`p = 1.2e-14`), Spearman `-0.351`,
`n = 470`. As a one-sided screen:

| screen | n splits | mean coverage | frac coverage < 0.85 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `Delta_sep < 0.70` | 142 | **0.905** | **0.077** |
| `Delta_sep >= 0.70` | 328 | 0.782 | 0.433 |

A low reading is informative; a high reading is only a warning. That asymmetry is exactly what B8
predicts — the remaining ~65% of coverage variation lives in the conditional component Aud-A makes
invisible. **Recommendation: report `Delta_sep` alongside any episode budget; do not treat it as a
certificate.**

## 6. What to put in the paper, and what not to claim

**Do claim.**
* `beta` is identified as an episode-level quantile under E1+E2, estimable by a conformal order
  statistic, with `K` bracketed to a factor of `e` and validated at exactly `K = 9` on real data.
* The escape from Aud-A is *average-instead-of-supremum, paid for with historical labels*, and
  neither ingredient alone works (B3 is a three-part statement; the (a) half is the interesting one).
* Historical **labels**, not historical **data**, are the binding resource (B1(b)).
* The score `s` is a control variate: estimating `gamma` costs `Var(s)` less variance than estimating
  `abar - 1/2`. Measured saving factor 0.800 on the calibrated simulation.
* At a single deployment, buying `k` probe labels dominates estimating a budget. The budget is worth
  estimating only because it amortizes, and the break-even is `Theta(K sigma^2 log(1/delta)(eps/beta)^2)`.
* The empirical pattern, all of it, including F4 and F5 which are unflattering.

**Do not claim.**
* Do **not** say `beta` is now estimable, full stop. Say: estimable *under E2*, and E2 fails on the
  axis that matters (0.626 / 0.454).
* Do **not** present weighted conformal as the fix. It is valid and vacuous (F5).
* Do **not** present the guarantee as protecting a deployment. It is marginal over deployments
  (B3(a) + `rem:fa-marginal`).
* Do **not** claim `Delta_sep` certifies E2. It falsifies, one-sidedly, at `r = -0.35`.
* Do **not** claim novelty for B2 over `thm:aud-G`. Credit it in the statement.

**Suggested placement.** `theory_beta_estimable.tex` is written as an appendix section
(`\section{...}\label{app:episode-beta}`) that slots after `auditable_budgets.tex` and uses only
labels that already exist there. One paragraph belongs in the main text, next to the sentence that
says `beta` is declared: *"A budget is estimable from `K >= 1/alpha - 1` logged deployments
exchangeable with the current one (App. E); on CIFAR-10-C that estimator attains its nominal 0.90
coverage when the seed or batch protocol changes and 0.454 when the corruption severity changes, so
the assumption that makes `beta` estimable is exactly the assumption a shifted deployment
violates."*
