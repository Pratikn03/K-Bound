\section{Where a drift budget can come from: episode structure}
\label{app:episode-beta}

Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-A} says a label-free audit of the drift budget is vacuous. The main text
therefore treats $\beta$ as \emph{declared}. This section asks the complementary question --- under
what structure is $\beta$ \emph{estimable}, and at what price --- and answers it in one direction:
$\beta$ is a property of a \emph{population of deployments}, not of a deployment. Under
exchangeability across deployments plus retrospectively revealed outcomes it is identified and
estimable at the conformal rate; without both ingredients it is not identified at all. We then
measure, on the K-Bound artifacts, how far real deployment populations are from that structure. The
answer is: far enough that the estimator is valid along nuisance axes (fitting seed, adaptation
method) and fails along the axis a deployment actually cares about (a corruption family or severity
never seen before).

\subsection{The episode model}

\begin{definition}[Deployment episode]\label{epi:def-episode}
An \emph{episode} $e$ is one deployment: a target law $P_e$, a frozen model $f_0^e$, a candidate
$f_a^e$, a source-calibrated score $s^e$, and the induced disagreement region $D_e$ with
$\mu_{P_e}(D_e)>0$. Write the \emph{observable part}
$W_e=(Z_e,M_e)$ --- the label-free evidence and margin of Assumption~\ref{ass:deploy} --- and the
\emph{latent part} $\gamma_e=\bar a_e-\tfrac12-M_e$. Put $V_e=(W_e,\gamma_e)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[E1: retrospective outcome logging]\label{epi:ass-E1}
For each past episode $e\le K$, a sample of $n$ units drawn i.i.d.\ from $\mu_{P_e}(\cdot\mid D_e)$
has its labels revealed \emph{after} that deployment concluded, yielding
\[
\widehat\gamma_e=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(\mathbf 1\{f_a^e(X_i)=Y_i\}-s^e(X_i)\bigr)\in[-1,1].
\]
No labels are available for the current episode $K+1$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[E2: episode exchangeability]\label{epi:ass-E2}
$(V_1,\dots,V_{K+1})$ is exchangeable. Write $Q$ for the common marginal law of $V$.
\end{assumption}

E1 is an accounting assumption: it says outcomes are eventually observed and logged, which is true
of any deployment with a downstream ground truth (delivered claims, resolved tickets, confirmed
diagnoses) and false of deployments whose outcomes are never revealed. E2 is a much stronger
statement about the deployment pipeline; \S\ref{epi:sec-honesty} argues it is the binding constraint
and \S\ref{epi:sec-numerics} measures its failure.

\begin{definition}[Episode budget]\label{epi:def-beta-star}
For $\alpha\in(0,1)$, $\beta^\star(\alpha):=\inf\{b\ge0:\ Q(|\gamma|\le b)\ge1-\alpha\}$, the
$(1-\alpha)$-quantile of $|\gamma|$ under the episode law.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Identification, and what it costs}

% STATUS: PROVED. (a) is bookkeeping + Aud-D composition; (b) is Aud-A's kernel
% construction applied episodewise.
\begin{theorem}[Episode identification and its price]\label{epi:thm-ident}
Under Assumption~\ref{epi:ass-E2}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textnormal{(a)}] \textbf{(Positive.)} $\beta^\star(\alpha)$ is a functional of the episode law
of $(W,\gamma)$, and it is the smallest budget $b$ for which the declared class holds with episode
probability at least $1-\alpha$, i.e.\ $Q(P\in\mathcal C_b)\ge1-\alpha$. Consequently the frontier
rule of Theorem~\ref{thm:headline} run at $b=\beta^\star(\alpha)$ has episode-marginal
false-strict-commitment probability at most $\alpha$.
\item[\textnormal{(b)}] \textbf{(Negative: the price is historical labels, not historical data.)}
$\beta^\star(\alpha)$ is \emph{not} a functional of the episode law of $W$ alone. For every
$b\in[0,\tfrac12]$ there is an episode law $Q_b$ with the same $W$-marginal and
$\beta^\star_{Q_b}(\alpha)=b$. No quantity of unlabeled historical deployments identifies $\beta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) $\beta^\star(\alpha)$ is by construction the $(1-\alpha)$-quantile of $|\gamma|$ under $Q$, hence
a functional of the $(W,\gamma)$-law; and $\{|\gamma|\le b\}=\{P\in\mathcal C_b\}$ by the definition
of $\mathcal C_b$, so the quantile characterisation is immediate. For the decision consequence, fix
the history and condition on $\{|\gamma_{K+1}|\le b\}$. On that event
Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}(ii) applies, so a strict commitment made at margin $|M_{K+1}|>b$ has the
correct sign; a false strict commitment therefore requires $\{|\gamma_{K+1}|>b\}$, an event of
probability at most $\alpha$. (This is Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-DE}(D) with the audit event supplied by
$Q$ instead of by a concentration bound.)

(b) Fix the $W$-marginal $Q_W$. By the kernel construction in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-A}, for any episode with observables $(\mu,f_0,f_a,s)$ --- hence any value of
$W$ --- and any $\bar a\in[0,1]$ there is a label kernel on $D$ realising $\bar a$ while leaving
$(Z,M)$ unchanged. The achievable drift set at matched evidence is
$I_M=[-(M+\tfrac12),\,\tfrac12-M]$, an interval of length $1$ containing $0$ whose endpoints have
absolute values $\tfrac12+M$ and $\tfrac12-M$, the larger being $\tfrac12+|M|\ge\tfrac12$. Hence for
every $b\in[0,\tfrac12]$ at least one of $+b,-b$ lies in $I_M$; concretely, set
$\theta(W)=+1$ if $b\le\tfrac12-M$ and $\theta(W)=-1$ otherwise, which is admissible because
$b>\tfrac12-M$ forces $M+\tfrac12>1-b\ge b$ for $b\le\tfrac12$. Define $Q_b$ by drawing
$W\sim Q_W$ and realising $\gamma=\theta(W)\,b$ by the corresponding kernel. Then $Q_b$ has
$W$-marginal $Q_W$, and $|\gamma|=b$ $Q_b$-almost surely, so
$Q_b(|\gamma|\le b')=0<1-\alpha$ for every $b'<b$ while $Q_b(|\gamma|\le b)=1$; hence
$\beta^\star_{Q_b}(\alpha)=b$ for every $\alpha\in(0,1)$. Since $b$ was arbitrary in
$[0,\tfrac12]$, the $W$-marginal carries no information about $\beta^\star$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The estimator}

% STATUS: PROVED. This is Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-G} specialised to
% gamma_pred == 0, restated here because that specialisation is the one that
% needs no label-free drift predictor and is the one we validate numerically.
% The conformal backbone is Vovk et al. / Papadopoulos et al.; the hierarchical
% domain-level version is Dunn-Wasserman-Ramdas and Lee-Barber-Willett.
\begin{theorem}[Episode-conformal budget]\label{epi:thm-conformal}
Assume E1 and E2, with a common labeled size $n$ and the same estimator in every episode. Let
$S_e=|\widehat\gamma_e|$ for $e\le K$, let $\delta\in(0,\alpha)$,
$t(n,\delta)=\sqrt{2\ln(2/\delta)/n}$, and
\[
j=\bigl\lceil(1-\alpha+\delta)(K+1)\bigr\rceil,
\qquad
\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{epi}}=S_{(j)}+t(n,\delta),
\]
feasible exactly when $\alpha\ge\tfrac1{K+1}+\delta$. Then
$\Pr\bigl(|\gamma_{K+1}|\le\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{epi}}\bigr)\ge1-\alpha$, with the probability
taken over the joint draw of all $K+1$ episodes and their label samples. Composed with
Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-DE}(D)--(E), the audited frontier makes a false strict commitment with
probability at most $\alpha$ (population $M$) or $\alpha+\delta'$ (empirical $\widehat M$ from $m$
unlabeled deployment points).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Define the counterfactual score $S_{K+1}=|\widehat\gamma_{K+1}|$ that a size-$n$ label sample from
the current episode would produce. Under E1--E2 the vector $(S_1,\dots,S_{K+1})$ is exchangeable:
$V_{1:K+1}$ is exchangeable, the label samples are conditionally i.i.d.\ of common size $n$ given the
episodes, and $S_e$ is the same measurable functional of $(V_e,\text{labels}_e)$ for every $e$. Split
conformal on an exchangeable sequence gives
$\Pr\bigl(S_{K+1}\le S_{(j)}\bigr)\ge j/(K+1)\ge1-\alpha+\delta$. Independently, Hoeffding on the
$n$ i.i.d.\ variables $u_i\in[-1,1]$ gives
$\Pr\bigl(|\widehat\gamma_{K+1}-\gamma_{K+1}|>t(n,\delta)\bigr)\le\delta$. On the intersection,
$|\gamma_{K+1}|\le S_{K+1}+t\le S_{(j)}+t$. A union bound gives failure probability at most
$(\alpha-\delta)+\delta=\alpha$. Feasibility is $j\le K$, i.e.\ $\alpha\ge\tfrac1{K+1}+\delta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation to Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-G}]\label{epi:rem-audG}
Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-conformal} is Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-G} with the label-free drift predictor set
to $\gamma_{\mathrm{pred}}\equiv0$ and ``domain'' read as ``episode''. We restate it because the
specialisation is the object the rest of this section analyses and validates: it needs no auxiliary
predictor, so its coverage failures isolate the exchangeability assumption rather than confounding it
with predictor misspecification. Aud-G's assumption (G3) --- that any predictor be fixed
independently of the $K{+}1$ episodes --- is vacuous here.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Exactly where Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-A} is escaped}

This is the load-bearing check. If Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-conformal} did not violate a hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-A}, one of the two would be false.

\begin{definition}[Fibre-blind budget]\label{epi:def-fibreblind}
Fix observables $(\mu_T,f_0,f_a,s)$ and let $\mathcal P(\mu_T)$ be the matched-evidence fibre of
Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-A}. A random budget $B$ is \emph{fibre-blind} if $\Law(B)$ is the same for every
$P\in\mathcal P(\mu_T)$. Both a deployment-evidence audit $B=g(Z)$ and any budget computed from a
history drawn independently of the current episode are fibre-blind.
\end{definition}

% STATUS: PROVED. (a) is Aud-A's argument verbatim, restated to cover
% history-measurable budgets; (b) and (c) are immediate.
\begin{proposition}[Two ingredients, neither sufficient alone]\label{epi:prop-escape}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textnormal{(a)}] \textbf{Labels alone do not escape Aud-A.} If $B$ is fibre-blind and
$\Pr(|\gamma(P)|\le B)\ge1-\delta$ for \emph{every} $P\in\mathcal P(\mu_T)$, then
$B\ge\tfrac12+|M|$ with probability at least $1-\delta$, and the audited frontier abstains with
probability at least $1-\delta$. In particular $\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{epi}}$, which is fibre-blind,
does \emph{not} satisfy fibre-uniform validity.
\item[\textnormal{(b)}] \textbf{The escape is the average.} Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-conformal}'s
guarantee is $Q$-marginal: the deployment law is itself drawn from $Q$, and the extreme worlds
$\bar a\in\{0,1\}$ that force Aud-A's bound carry $Q$-probability at most $\alpha$. The
hypothesis Aud-A uses and E2 removes is precisely the \emph{supremum over the fibre}, replaced by an
\emph{average over $Q$}.
\item[\textnormal{(c)}] \textbf{The average alone does not escape either.} By
Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-ident}(b), without historical labels the $Q$-average budget is as
uninformative as the fibre supremum: the $W$-marginal is consistent with $\beta^\star(\alpha)=b$ for
every $b\in[0,\tfrac12]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) Take the extreme world $P^\dagger\in\mathcal P(\mu_T)$ with $\bar a\in\{0,1\}$ chosen so that
$|\gamma|=\tfrac12+|M|$, which exists by the kernel construction of Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-A}. Validity
at $P^\dagger$ forces $\Pr(B\ge\tfrac12+|M|)\ge1-\delta$; fibre-blindness transfers this statement to
every other world in the fibre. Since $|M|<\tfrac12+|M|$, neither branch of $|M|>B$ can fire on that
event. (b) and (c) are restatements of Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-conformal} and
Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-ident}(b).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the deployer actually gets]\label{epi:rem-marginal}
Proposition~\ref{epi:prop-escape}(a) is not a technicality. It says the episode budget provides no
protection \emph{at} a deployment: conditional on the episode being one of the adversarial ones, the
frontier commits and is wrong. What is controlled is the long-run rate across deployments. This is
the same marginal-versus-conditional distinction as Remark~\ref{rem:fa-marginal}, and it should be
stated to any deployer who is told $\beta$ was ``estimated''.
\end{remark}

\subsection{How many episodes: a factor-$e$ bracket}

% STATUS: PROVED.  Standard distribution-free two-point argument, stated for
% this object.
\begin{theorem}[Episode floor]\label{epi:thm-floor}
Fix $\alpha\in(0,\tfrac12)$ and $b\in(0,\tfrac12]$. Consider budgets
$\widehat\beta=h(\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_K)$ computed from $K$ historical drifts observed
\emph{without label noise} (the most favourable case), and require validity
$\Pr_{Q}\bigl(|\gamma_{K+1}|\le\widehat\beta\bigr)\ge1-\alpha$ for every i.i.d.\ episode law $Q$.
Then either
\[
h(0,\dots,0)\ \ge\ b
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\alpha\ \ge\ \frac1{K+1}\Bigl(1-\frac1{K+1}\Bigr)^{K}\ \ge\ \frac1{e(K+1)} .
\]
Equivalently: a budget that returns anything strictly below $b$ on a drift-free history requires
$K\ge\tfrac1{e\alpha}-1$ episodes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $p\in(0,1)$ let $Q_p$ be the i.i.d.\ episode law with $|\gamma_e|=b$ with probability $p$ and
$\gamma_e=0$ otherwise (realisable at fixed observables by the kernel construction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-A}, as in Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-ident}(b)). Suppose $h(0,\dots,0)<b$. The event
$A=\{\gamma_1=\dots=\gamma_K=0\}\cap\{|\gamma_{K+1}|=b\}$ has $Q_p$-probability $p(1-p)^K$ and on $A$
the budget misses. Hence $\alpha\ge\sup_{p}p(1-p)^K$. Setting $p=1/(K+1)$ gives
$\alpha\ge\frac1{K+1}(1-\frac1{K+1})^K$. Finally
$K\ln(1-\tfrac1{K+1})=-K\ln(1+\tfrac1K)\ge-1$ since $\ln(1+x)\le x$, so
$(1-\tfrac1{K+1})^K\ge e^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Bracket]\label{epi:cor-bracket}
Let $K^\star(\alpha)$ be the least number of episodes admitting a non-vacuous level-$\alpha$
episode budget. Then, ignoring the label-noise allowance $\delta$,
\[
\frac1{e\alpha}-1\ \le\ K^\star(\alpha)\ \le\ \frac1\alpha-1 .
\]
The upper bound is Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-conformal}'s feasibility condition; the lower bound is
Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-floor}. At $\alpha=0.10$: between $3$ and $9$ historical deployments.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{How many labels: the per-episode price, and a control variate}

% STATUS: PROVED.  Bernstein is standard; the calibration identity is a short
% computation and is the only new part.
\begin{proposition}[Label price per episode]\label{epi:prop-labels}
Write $C=\mathbf 1\{f_a(X)=Y\}$ and $u=C-s(X)\in[-1,1]$ on $D$, $\sigma^2=\Var(u)$. Bernstein's
inequality gives, with probability $1-\delta$,
\[
|\widehat\gamma-\gamma|\ \le\ \sqrt{\tfrac{2\sigma^2\ln(2/\delta)}{n}}+\tfrac{4\ln(2/\delta)}{3n}.
\]
Hence to keep the label-noise inflation in Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-conformal} below $\kappa\beta$ it
suffices to take
$n\ \ge\ \tfrac{8\sigma^2\ln(2/\delta)}{(\kappa\beta)^2}+\tfrac{8\ln(2/\delta)}{3\kappa\beta}$
(bound each of the two terms by $\kappa\beta/2$).
Moreover, if $s$ is pointwise calibrated on $D$, i.e.\ $\E[C\mid X]=s(X)$ $\mu_T$-a.s.\ on $D$, then
\[
\sigma^2=\E[s(1-s)]=\Var(C)-\Var(s)\ <\ \Var(C),
\]
so estimating the drift $\gamma$ is strictly cheaper in labels than estimating $\bar a-\tfrac12$
directly, by exactly the variance of the calibrated score. The score acts as a control variate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Bernstein statement is standard for $u\in[-1,1]$ (range $2$); the sufficient $n$ follows by
bounding each term by $\kappa\beta/2$. For the identity, calibration gives $\E[u]=0$ and
$\E[u^2]=\E\bigl[\E[(C-s)^2\mid X]\bigr]=\E[\Var(C\mid X)]=\E[s(1-s)]$, so $\sigma^2=\E[s(1-s)]$.
Also $\Var(C)=\E[C]-\E[C]^2=\E[s]-\E[s]^2=\E[s(1-s)]+\bigl(\E[s^2]-\E[s]^2\bigr)
=\E[s(1-s)]+\Var(s)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why not just buy labels at deployment? The amortisation argument}

% STATUS: PROVED.  (a) is a Le Cam two-point bound; (b) is arithmetic.
\begin{proposition}[Probe lower bound and break-even]\label{epi:prop-probe}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textnormal{(a)}] Fix $\alpha,\eta\ge0$ with $2\alpha+\eta<1$ and $\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac14]$.
Any rule that observes $k$ i.i.d.\ labeled probes from $\mu_T(\cdot\mid D)$, commits strictly with
probability at least $1-\eta$, and has false-adapt and false-freeze probability at most $\alpha$
whenever $|\bar a-\tfrac12|=\varepsilon$, must use
\[
k\ \ge\ \frac{3(1-2\alpha-\eta)^2}{16\,\varepsilon^{2}} .
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-B} achieves $O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\delta))$, so the probe price is
$\Theta(\varepsilon^{-2})$ up to the confidence log.
\item[\textnormal{(b)}] Over $N$ future deployments the probe route costs $Nk$ labels; the episode
route costs $Kn$ labels once and none thereafter. The routes break even at $N^\star=Kn/k$. Taking
$\alpha=0.10$, $K=9$ (Corollary~\ref{epi:cor-bracket}), $n=8\sigma^2\ln(2/\delta)/\beta^2$
(Proposition~\ref{epi:prop-labels} with $\kappa=1$, dropping the lower-order term) and
$k=3(1-2\alpha-\eta)^2/(16\varepsilon^2)$ from part~(a),
\[
N^\star\ =\ \frac{Kn}{k}\ =\ \frac{384\,K\,\sigma^{2}\ln(2/\delta)}{3(1-2\alpha-\eta)^{2}}
\cdot\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon}{\beta}\Bigr)^{2},
\]
which is $\Theta\bigl(K\sigma^2\log(1/\delta)\,(\varepsilon/\beta)^2\bigr)$: the budget route pays
for itself after a number of deployments proportional to the squared ratio of the typical margin to
the budget. It never pays for itself if $\beta\ll\varepsilon$, i.e.\ if the budget is so tight that
the frontier commits on almost everything anyway.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) Consider the two worlds $\bar a=\tfrac12\pm\varepsilon$ realised at matched observables by the
kernel construction of Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-A}; by Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} they have
$\Delta>0$ and $\Delta<0$ respectively. Each probe is Bernoulli($\bar a$) for the event
$\{f_a(X)=Y\}$. In the $+$ world, $\Pr_+[\textsc{freeze}]\le\alpha$ and
$\Pr_+[\textsc{abstain}]\le\eta$, so $\Pr_+[\textsc{adapt}]\ge1-\alpha-\eta$; in the $-$ world
$\Pr_-[\textsc{adapt}]\le\alpha$. Hence
$\mathrm{TV}\bigl(\mathrm{Ber}(\tfrac12+\varepsilon)^{\otimes k},
\mathrm{Ber}(\tfrac12-\varepsilon)^{\otimes k}\bigr)\ge1-2\alpha-\eta$. Pinsker and tensorisation
give $\mathrm{TV}\le\sqrt{k\,\mathrm{KL}/2}$ with
$\mathrm{KL}=2\varepsilon\ln\frac{1/2+\varepsilon}{1/2-\varepsilon}
=2\varepsilon\cdot2\operatorname{artanh}(2\varepsilon)\le
2\varepsilon\cdot\frac{4\varepsilon}{1-4\varepsilon^2}\le\frac{32\varepsilon^2}{3}$
for $\varepsilon\le\tfrac14$. Thus $1-2\alpha-\eta\le4\varepsilon\sqrt{k/3}$, which rearranges to the
claim. (b) is arithmetic on the two label counts, using
$n\asymp\sigma^2\log(1/\delta)/\beta^2$ and $k\asymp\log(1/\alpha)/\varepsilon^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{epi:prop-probe} is the honest ranking. At a \emph{single} deployment, buying
$k$ probe labels dominates estimating a budget: the probe certifies $\sign(M+\gamma)$ directly,
whereas the budget route certifies the weaker $|M|>|\gamma|$. The budget is worth estimating only
because it is \emph{reused}, and reuse is exactly what E2 licenses. If E2 fails, the budget route
loses both its guarantee and its economic rationale.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Relaxing exchangeability}

% STATUS: PROVED (both parts).  (a) is a one-line change of measure; (b) is
% Tibshirani et al. (2019) weighted conformal, cited not reproved.
\begin{theorem}[Graceful degradation]\label{epi:thm-shift}
Let the history be drawn from $Q$ and the current episode from $Q'$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textnormal{(a)}] \textbf{Bounded episode-law ratio.} If $dQ'/dQ\le\rho$ then the budget of
Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-conformal} run at level $\alpha$ satisfies
$\Pr_{Q'}(|\gamma_{K+1}|\le\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{epi}})\ge1-\rho\alpha$. To retain level $\alpha$
under $Q'$, run the history at level $\alpha/\rho$, which requires $K\ge\rho/\alpha-1$ episodes: a
$\rho$-fold mismatch costs a $\rho$-fold increase in the number of historical deployments.
\item[\textnormal{(b)}] \textbf{Episode covariate shift.} If $Q'(dw,d\gamma)=Q(d\gamma\mid w)\,Q'(dw)$
--- the episode observables shift but the conditional drift law does not --- then weighted split
conformal with weights $w(W)=dQ'_W/dQ_W$ is exactly valid at level $\alpha$, and $w$ is estimable
from \emph{unlabeled} episode observables alone by a two-sample density-ratio fit.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) Write $\mathcal H$ for the history. By Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-conformal},
$\E_{\mathcal H}\bigl[Q(|\gamma|>\widehat\beta(\mathcal H))\bigr]\le\alpha$. Since
$Q'(A)\le\rho\,Q(A)$ for every measurable $A$,
$\Pr_{Q'}(\text{miss})=\E_{\mathcal H}\bigl[Q'(|\gamma|>\widehat\beta)\bigr]
\le\rho\,\E_{\mathcal H}\bigl[Q(|\gamma|>\widehat\beta)\bigr]\le\rho\alpha$. The feasibility
condition at level $\alpha/\rho$ is $\alpha/\rho\ge1/(K+1)$.
(b) Under the stated factorisation the scores $S_1,\dots,S_{K+1}$ are weighted exchangeable in the
sense of Tibshirani, Barber, Cand\`es and Ramdas (2019) with weight function
$w$; their Theorem~2 gives exact $(1-\alpha)$ coverage for the weighted quantile
$\inf\{s:\sum_k p_k\mathbf 1\{S_k\le s\}\ge1-\alpha\}$,
$p_k=w(W_k)/\bigl(\sum_i w(W_i)+w(W_{K+1})\bigr)$. Estimability of $w$ from unlabeled data is the
standard two-sample density-ratio reduction: $w\propto\frac{\pi(W)}{1-\pi(W)}$ for
$\pi(W)=\Pr(\text{episode is current}\mid W)$, and $W$ contains no labels.
\end{proof}

% STATUS: PROVED (immediate corollary of Aud-A).
\begin{corollary}[The relaxation is not free]\label{epi:cor-not-free}
The conditional-stability hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-shift}(b) is not auditable from
label-free deployment data: Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-A} applies verbatim at each fixed $w$, so no
label-free statistic distinguishes $Q(\cdot\mid w)$ from any other conditional drift law with the
same observables. It is, however, \emph{falsifiable} given retrospective labels on two episode
populations, which a declared $\beta$ at a single deployment is not. That is the whole gain:
the assumption moves from unfalsifiable to falsifiable, not from unverified to verified.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Is E2 plausible? An honest answer}
\label{epi:sec-honesty}

E2 asserts that the deployment a system is about to face is exchangeable with the deployments it has
already logged. Three observations, in decreasing order of comfort.

\emph{Where it is plausible.} When the episode population is generated by a stable operational
process --- a fixed fleet of sites, a recurring seasonal cycle, a queue of customers drawn from a
stable market --- and the model, the candidate update rule, and the scoring function are held fixed,
E2 is the same assumption already made whenever a deployment team computes a historical error rate
and uses it as a forecast. It is checkable in the weak sense that its consequences (coverage of past
budgets on later episodes) can be back-tested.

\emph{Where it fails, and it is the interesting case.} E2 fails exactly when the current deployment
differs from the history in kind rather than in draw: a new corruption mechanism, a new site, a new
severity regime, a new sensor. This is the case that motivates test-time adaptation in the first
place. A deployer who could guarantee E2 could often also have retrained.

\emph{What the theory can and cannot say about that failure.} Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-shift}(a) prices
the failure if $\rho$ is known, and $\rho$ is not knowable label-free
(Corollary~\ref{epi:cor-not-free}). Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-shift}(b) removes the need for $\rho$ at the
cost of a conditional-stability assumption that is itself unverifiable at deployment. So the honest
summary is: \textbf{episode structure converts an unfalsifiable per-deployment declaration into a
falsifiable population-level one, and buys a marginal (not conditional) guarantee. It does not make
$\beta$ knowable at the deployment that needs it.} The empirical section quantifies the gap.

\subsection{Numerical validation on the K-Bound artifacts}
\label{epi:sec-numerics}

Protocol. Episodes are the $6480$ CIFAR-10-C and $405$ ImageNet-C cells (condition $\times$ method
$\times$ seed) of \S\ref{sec:experiments}; $\gamma:=\Delta-M$ in benefit units as in the
$\beta$-sweep. Each split partitions the cells into three disjoint pools: a fit pool $F$ on which the
benefit model $M(\cdot)$ is fit, a history $H$ whose labels are revealed, and a deployment set $D$
whose labels are used only for evaluation. $M$ is fit once on $F$ and applied identically to $H$ and
$D$, so the scores on $H$ and $D$ are exchangeable exactly when the episode populations are. Five
random $F/H$ splits per protocol; $\alpha=0.10$, so \textbf{the null coverage is $0.90$}: any
protocol whose deployment set is exchangeable with its history must return $\approx0.90$, and the
finding is the deviation. Artifacts:
\texttt{frontier\_sweep\_v1/beta\_estimability/\{episode\_beta.py,episode\_beta\_results.json\}}.

Four things follow.
\emph{(i) The estimator is correct.} On the exchangeable control the realised coverage is
$0.905$ and $0.912$ against a null of $0.900$, and a $K$-sweep reproduces the feasibility floor
exactly: at $\alpha=0.10$ the conformal budget is infeasible for $K\in\{3,5\}$ and first attains
$0.907\pm0.088$ at $K=9=\lceil1/\alpha\rceil-1$, matching Corollary~\ref{epi:cor-bracket}.
\emph{(ii) Nuisance axes are safe, deployment axes are not.} Coverage is within $0.04$ of nominal
when the seed, class-balance protocol or batch size changes, and falls to $0.626$ and $0.454$ when
the corruption family or severity changes. The implied $\rho$ rises from $1$ to $5.5$.
\emph{(iii) A better benefit model transfers worse.} The eleven-coordinate GBM has higher decision
yield than the four-coordinate ATC ridge ($0.647$ vs $0.446$) and strictly worse budget transfer on
every deployment axis ($0.626$ vs $0.866$ on \texttt{LOCO}, $0.454$ vs $0.595$ on \texttt{LOSEV}).
Fitting the benefit surface harder shrinks the history residuals without shrinking the residuals on
a novel episode, so the quantile it produces is too small.
\emph{(iv) Weighted conformal restores validity by destroying yield.} With label-free evidence
weights (Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-shift}(b)) the \texttt{LOSEV} coverage goes from $0.454$ to $1.000$
and the decision yield goes from $0.615$ to $0.000$: the weights concentrate on the few history
episodes that resemble the deployment, the weighted quantile runs off the end of the score list, and
the frontier abstains everywhere. This is the same trade Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-A} forces on any
label-free audit, now appearing inside a method that was supposed to escape it.

\subsection{Two consequences for the rest of the paper}

\paragraph{The $\beta$-sweep's failure was never a quantile-estimation failure.}
The $\beta$-sweep declares $\widehat\beta$ as the plug-in $q_{0.90}$ of $|\gamma|$ on dev cells;
Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-conformal} replaces that by the conformal order statistic. Across all $470$
splits the two budgets differ by a median relative gap of $0.34\%$, and the median coverage
difference is exactly $0$. At $K$ in the thousands the conformal correction moves one order
statistic and nothing else. The $\beta$-sweep's negative result therefore survives intact: what it
measured was the failure of E2, not the failure of a quantile estimator, and no conformal
machinery repairs it. Conversely, when E2 does hold the frontier operationalises: on an exchangeable
three-way split of CIFAR-10-C the episode budget delivers decision yield $0.641$ at regret $0.0019$
and $\mathrm{FA}_{\mathrm u}=0.0002$, against the shipped conformal-interval rule's $0.662$,
$0.0015$ and $0.0000$ on the same cells. On ImageNet-C it does not: yield $0.344$ versus $0.508$.

\paragraph{E2 is not verifiable, but its observable component is falsifiable.}
Corollary~\ref{epi:cor-not-free} says no label-free statistic certifies the conditional part of E2.
The \emph{marginal} part is a different matter: $\Delta_{\mathrm{sep}}=\mathrm{AUC}$ of a
cross-fitted classifier separating history from deployment episodes on the evidence $Z$ alone is
computable without any labels, and equals $\tfrac12$ under E2. Measured across the $470$ splits it
correlates with realised coverage at Pearson $r=-0.35$ ($p=1.2\times10^{-14}$), Spearman
$\rho=-0.35$, and it reads $0.497\pm0.033$ on the exchangeable control, as it must. Used as a
one-sided screen at $\Delta_{\mathrm{sep}}<0.70$: mean coverage $0.905$ with $7.7\%$ of splits below
$0.85$; above the threshold, mean coverage $0.782$ with $43.3\%$ below $0.85$. So a low separability
reading is informative and a high one is only a warning --- which is exactly the asymmetry
Corollary~\ref{epi:cor-not-free} predicts, since the residual $65\%$ of the coverage variation lives
in the conditional component that Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-A} makes invisible. We recommend reporting
$\Delta_{\mathrm{sep}}$ alongside any episode budget; we do not recommend treating it as a
certificate.

\subsection{What is left open}

\begin{conjecture}[Estimable budgets without exchangeability]\label{epi:conj-open}
Is there a structural assumption, weaker than E2 and \emph{falsifiable from retrospective labels on
the history alone}, under which $\beta^\star$ for a novel episode is identified? The two natural
candidates both fail to be that: bounded episode-law ratio (Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-shift}(a)) needs an
unknowable $\rho$; conditional stability (Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-shift}(b)) is falsifiable only with
labels from the novel population, which is what is missing. A positive answer would need a drift
functional that extrapolates off the support of the history, and
Theorem~\ref{epi:thm-ident}(b) shows no such functional can be built from unlabeled observables
alone.
\end{conjecture}

We did not pursue an anytime-valid within-episode budget from the e-process machinery. The reason is
structural rather than technical: within one episode $\gamma$ is a fixed unknown number and every
observable in the adaptation stream is label-free, so Theorem~\ref{thm:aud-A} applies to any
stopping-time-measurable statistic of that stream. Sequential machinery buys anytime validity, not
identification.
