\begin{abstract}
Test-time adaptation can improve models on unlabeled target data, but the same
objective that helps under one distribution shift can silently hurt under another.
Existing work mainly strengthens adaptation mechanisms; we study a prior decision
problem: can a system decide, without target labels, whether it should adapt at all?

We formalize adaptation benefit as the excess target risk of a frozen baseline over
an adapted candidate and define three label-free regimes: \emph{knowably helpful},
\emph{knowably harmful}, and \emph{unknowable}. We prove that when two target worlds
induce identical observable evidence but opposite adaptation benefit, no label-free
rule can be correct in both; abstention is then information-theoretically necessary.
Under a risk-alignment assumption, we give a finite-sample adapt/freeze/abstain
certificate controlling false adaptation and false freezing at level $\alpha$ under
stated calibration assumptions, and an exact benefit-sign frontier: adaptation benefit
is identifiable from observables iff an observable margin exceeds the calibration-drift
budget on the disagreement region.

We instantiate the framework as \textsc{KGA}, a mechanism-agnostic wrapper around
Tent, EATA, and SAR, and evaluate it on a \emph{six-dataset core panel} (Office-Home,
Camelyon17, CIFAR-10-C, iWildCam, CIFAR-10.1, ImageNet-R). \textsc{KGA} beats both
always-adapt and always-freeze exactly in harmful-detectable regimes---a
\emph{statistically robust} beats-both, with bootstrap $95\%$ confidence intervals that
exclude zero on Office-Home, Camelyon17, and the CIFAR-10-C stress grid---while
\emph{matching or remaining close to} the better trivial policy in benign or weak-evidence
regimes (Table~\ref{tab:regime-summary}). On a heterogeneous mixed-deployment stream pooling
the held-out shifts, its regret is $14$--$27\times$ below either fixed policy. \textsc{KGA}
is not a universal accuracy booster; reported wins are regime-specific.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Deployed models rarely encounter the same distribution they were trained on.
Test-time adaptation (TTA) updates a model on unlabeled target data, often through
entropy minimization or stability-aware updates. In favorable shifts, adaptation can
recover accuracy; in unfavorable shifts the same objective can silently degrade
performance. Because target labels are unavailable, the system often cannot tell
whether adaptation is helping or hurting.

Most work asks how to design a better adapter. We ask whether to adapt at all.
Given a frozen baseline $f_0$, an adapted candidate $f_a$, and an unlabeled batch,
the system chooses \textbf{adapt} when adaptation is knowably helpful, \textbf{freeze}
when knowably harmful, or \textbf{abstain} when label-free evidence cannot identify
the sign of the benefit.

Identifiability constrains this decision. There exist target worlds with identical
label-free evidence but opposite adaptation benefit; then no label-free rule can be
correct in both worlds and abstention is mandatory. When evidence is risk-aligned and
a benefit estimate carries a valid uncertainty radius, a finite-sample certificate can
route between adapt, freeze, and abstain while controlling false-adapt and false-freeze
errors at level $\alpha$ under exchangeable calibration.

We instantiate \textbf{one} method, \textsc{KGA} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:kga}): a gradient-boosted
benefit estimator $\widehat\Delta(Z)$ plus a conformal radius $\varepsilon$ fit on a labeled
calibration split, then adapt/freeze/abstain. The wrapper is mechanism-agnostic (Tent, EATA, SAR);
there is no separate multicandidate or alternate certificate in the headline experiments.
K-Bound does \emph{not} claim universal improvement: when adaptation is already safe,
a certificate can only tie always-adapt; its value is highest when harmful adaptation
is frequent, detectable, and costly.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We formalize label-free TTA as an adapt/freeze/abstain decision; prove non-identifiability
of the benefit sign and a finite-sample certificate with an exact benefit-sign frontier;
instantiate \textsc{KGA} as a wrapper around Tent/EATA/SAR; and validate \textsc{KGA} on a
\emph{six-dataset core panel}---Office-Home, Camelyon17, CIFAR-10-C, iWildCam, CIFAR-10.1, and
ImageNet-R---with bootstrap confidence intervals on the held-out wins and a heterogeneous
mixed-deployment capstone (Table~\ref{tab:regime-summary}); ImageNet-C provides ImageNet-scale
corroboration of the harmful-detectable regime.

Empirically, \textsc{KGA} follows the regime map: synthetic stress grids supply the main
policy wins (CIFAR-10-C, ImageNet-C SAR); Camelyon17 Protocol~G, iWildCam Protocol~H v2,
and OfficeHome Protocol~M v2 are held-out real-shift beats-both replications under canonical
dev-lock \textsc{KGA}; elsewhere weak evidence correctly forces abstention or
conservative freeze (Appendix).

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{TTA methods.}
Tent~\cite{wang2021tent}, SHOT~\cite{liang2020shot}, TTT~\cite{sun2020ttt}, EATA~\cite{niu2022eata},
SAR~\cite{niu2023sar}, CoTTA~\cite{wang2022cotta}, and MEMO~\cite{zhang2022memo} improve
robustness of adaptation but commit to adapting; none certifies the sign of benefit
\emph{before} updating or abstains on the adapt/freeze decision itself.

\paragraph{Guarded and monitored TTA.}
POEM~\cite{bar2024poem} and sequential risk monitors~\cite{schirmer2025monitoring,podkopaev2022tracking}
act during or after adaptation and control false alarms in one failure direction.
K-Bound decides \emph{before} committing, poses a three-way decision with an explicit
unknowable region, and controls false-\emph{adapt} on the benefit sign.

\paragraph{Label-free accuracy and error estimation.}
ATC~\cite{garg2022atc}, Agreement-on-the-Line~\cite{baek2022aol,miller2021aol}, and
AETTA~\cite{lee2024aetta} estimate target performance without labels. Most directly,
\cite{kim2024taol} applies agreement-on-the-line to decide, at the \emph{population}
level, when TTA helps or hurts and to select an adaptation strategy; it issues no
per-deployment certificate, no \textsc{abstain} action, and no identifiability limit, and its
linear trend is known to break on shifts (e.g.\ Gaussian-noise corruption)---precisely the
regime where \textsc{K-Bound} abstains. Estimating
$R_T(f)$ differs from certifying $\sign(R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a))$ before adapting with
controlled false-adapt rate. Thus, K-Bound is not an accuracy estimator used as a gate; it
changes the target estimand from target accuracy to the sign of the frozen-versus-adapted
risk difference, with abstention when that sign is not identifiable.

\paragraph{Identifiability, conformal inference, and selective prediction.}
Unsupervised risk estimation~\cite{steinhardt2016,bendavid2010theory,rosenfeld2023dis2}
and selective prediction~\cite{geifman2017selective} show performance is identifiable
only under structure. K-Bound differs by certifying the \emph{sign} of adaptation benefit
before committing, with an explicit abstain region and false-adapt control at level $\alpha$.

\section{Problem setup and theory}\label{sec:setup}\label{sec:theory}
Source $P_S(X,Y)$ has labels at train/validation; target $P_T(X,Y)$ exposes only $P_T(X)$
at test time. Fix loss $\ell$, frozen $f_0$, and candidate $f_a$. Target risk
$R_T(f)=\E_{P_T}[\ell(f(X),Y)]$. The \emph{adaptation benefit} is
\begin{equation}
\Delta \;=\; R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a),\qquad \Delta>0 \iff \text{adapting helps.}
\end{equation}
\emph{Observable evidence} $Z=\phi(X_{1:m},f_0,f_a)$ is any statistic computable without
target labels (entropy, confidence, drift, disagreement, update norms). The central
question is when $\sign\Delta$ is recoverable from $Z$ alone.

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
\textbf{What ``label-free'' means.} No target/test labels at deploy; labeled source and
held-out validation may calibrate $\widehat\Delta$ and $\varepsilon$. Evidence $Z$ uses only
unlabeled batches, predictions, entropy, drift, and update statistics. The method is
\textbf{target-label-free}, not label-free of all supervision: labels may be used only in the
source/validation calibration split, never in the deployment batch or held-out test decision.}}
\end{center}

\begin{definition}[Regimes]
\emph{Knowably helpful}: evidence certifies $\Delta>0$ (adapt).
\emph{Knowably harmful}: evidence certifies $\Delta<0$ (freeze).
\emph{Unknowable}: the same $Z$ is compatible with both signs (abstain).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Non-identifiability forces abstention]\label{lem:nonid}
There exist worlds $P_T^1,P_T^2$ with $\Law(Z\mid P_T^1)=\Law(Z\mid P_T^2)$ but
$\sign\Delta_1=-\sign\Delta_2\neq0$. No label-free rule can be correct in both; any
valid rule must abstain on matched evidence with probability at least $1-2\alpha$
when false-adapt and false-freeze are each controlled at $\alpha$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[K-Bound certificate and benefit-sign frontier]\label{thm:headline}
Suppose $\widehat\Delta(z)$ satisfies $\Pr[|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon]\ge1-\alpha$.
The rule
\[
g(Z)=\begin{cases}
\textsc{adapt} & \widehat\Delta-\varepsilon>0,\\
\textsc{freeze} & \widehat\Delta+\varepsilon<0,\\
\textsc{abstain} & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
controls false-adapt and false-freeze at level $\alpha$.
On the disagreement region $D=\{x:f_0(x)\neq f_a(x)\}$, writing
$M=\E[s\mid D]-\tfrac12$ for a source-calibrated score $s$ of $f_a$ and
$\gamma=\E[\eta_a-s\mid D]$ for calibration drift,
$\sign\Delta=\sign(M+\gamma)$: the benefit sign is identifiable from label-free
evidence iff $|M|$ exceeds the drift budget $|\gamma|$ (exact frontier).
Intuitively, label-free heuristics work when drift is negligible and fail when
$|\gamma|$ dominates $|M|$---the obstruction no checkable assumption removes in full
generality (Lemma~\ref{lem:nonid}; full dichotomy: Appendix~\ref{app:theory-full}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Invalid evidence $\Rightarrow$ abstain]\label{cor:abstain-valid}
When $Z$ is not risk-aligned or $|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|>\varepsilon$ on the deployment
batch, the only valid outcomes are abstention or conservative freeze; committing adapt
without a positive certified lower bound violates the target false-adapt level $\alpha$.
\end{corollary}

Full proofs, rate theorems, one-bit dichotomy, and validators are in
Appendix~\ref{app:theory-full}.

\section{Method: \textsc{KGA}}\label{sec:method}
\textsc{KGA} is the only headline method: a decision wrapper around a fixed adapter, not a new
adaptation objective. On a calibration split with labeled pairs $(Z,\Delta)$: fit
$\widehat\Delta(Z)$ (gradient-boosted regressor in all experiments); set
$\varepsilon$ to the $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of $|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|$ on calibration
residuals; at deploy extract $Z$ from an unlabeled batch and return adapt, freeze, or abstain
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:kga}). Labels enter only through this calibration split; the deployment
batch and held-out test scorer never use target labels for adapter, $\varepsilon$, evidence,
or rule selection. Synthetic grids use leave-one-task-out calibration; Camelyon17
Protocol~G fits on dev hospital seeds and scores held-out test seeds once ($\alpha{=}0.10$).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\textsc{KGA}: Knowability-Guided Adaptation}
\label{alg:kga}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Frozen $f_0$; adapter $\mathcal{A}$; batch $X_{1:m}$; $\widehat\Delta$, $\varepsilon$, $\alpha$
\Ensure Decision $g\in\{\textsc{Adapt},\textsc{Freeze},\textsc{Abstain}\}$
\State $f_a \leftarrow \mathcal{A}(f_0, X_{1:m})$
\State $Z \leftarrow \phi(X_{1:m}, f_0, f_a)$
\State $\widehat\Delta \leftarrow \widehat\Delta(Z)$;\;
  $L \leftarrow \widehat\Delta-\varepsilon$;\; $U \leftarrow \widehat\Delta+\varepsilon$
\If{$L>0$} \State $g\leftarrow\textsc{Adapt}$
\ElsIf{$U<0$} \State $g\leftarrow\textsc{Freeze}$
\Else \State $g\leftarrow\textsc{Abstain}$
\EndIf
\State \Return $g$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

On the good event $|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon$, committing adapt only when
$L>0$ implies $\Delta>0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}). Assumptions: exchangeable
calibration pairs, bounded benefits, and risk-aligned $Z$; no target labels at deploy.
Overhead: one forward pass of $f_0$ and $f_a$ plus lightweight $\widehat\Delta$ evaluation.

\paragraph{The threshold is derived, not tuned.}
\textsc{KGA}'s commit boundary is not a swept hyperparameter. It is the conformal
calibration-drift budget $\varepsilon$ that Theorem~\ref{thm:headline} identifies as the
\emph{exact} benefit-sign frontier ($\sign\Delta=\sign(M+\gamma)$, identifiable iff
$|M|>|\gamma|$), fixed to the $(1-\alpha)$ residual quantile on the calibration split and
\emph{never} selected on target-test data. Confidence-thresholding (ATC) and
agreement-trend selection (AGL) fit a scalar against a labeled or held-out signal and
predict \emph{accuracy}; \textsc{KGA} certifies the benefit \emph{sign} with a boundary
\emph{forced} by the identifiability budget, and---unlike any thresholded estimator---%
\textsc{abstains} on the region where Lemma~\ref{lem:nonid} \emph{proves} no label-free
rule can decide. The impossibility construction, not a tuned cutoff, is what separates
\textsc{KGA} from ``estimate accuracy, then threshold.''

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}
We validate \textsc{KGA} on two controlled corruption/stress tracks (CIFAR-10-C stress,
ImageNet-C SAR) and three held-out natural-shift replications (Protocols G, H v2, and~M v2;
Table~\ref{tab:regime-summary}). Calibration and held-out scoring follow
Algorithm~\ref{alg:calib-eval} (Appendix~\ref{app:calib-eval}). Metrics: mean accuracy,
regret-to-oracle against the per-condition oracle $g^\star=\mathbf1[\Delta>0]$,
false-adapt rate (adapt when $B\le0$ among committed decisions), and coverage
(fraction of non-abstain decisions).

\subsection{CIFAR-10-C stress grid (5 seeds)}
The per-corruption suite is helpful-dominated; the \emph{stress} grid crosses severity,
batch size, composition, and update aggressiveness ($432$ conditions/method, ResNet-18,
pre-registered Protocol~A, seeds $0$--$4$). Harmful base rates are $34\%$ (Tent),
$27\%$ (EATA), $16\%$ (SAR). \textsc{KGA} \textbf{beats both} trivial policies for
Tent and EATA at $0\%$ false-adapt; it ties collapse-resistant SAR
(Table~\ref{tab:decisive}). Among harmful cells \textsc{KGA} adapts $0$ and freezes them;
among helpful cells it adapts almost all; on marginal cells it abstains. Paired per-condition
bootstrap ($10^4$ resamples, Holm correction over the comparison family) confirms Tent and
EATA beat \emph{both} trivial policies after correction; false-adapt is $0/2160$ for every
candidate. The mixing-ratio Pareto (Fig.~\ref{fig:decisive}) shows \textsc{KGA} on the
regret-vs-harmful-fraction front, strictly beating both once the harmful fraction exceeds
$p^\star{\approx}0.1$ (Tent/SAR) or $0$ (EATA).

\subsection{Natural-shift headline results (Protocols G, H v2, M v2)}\label{sec:nat-shift-headline}
On real shifts we separate two layers that must not be conflated:
\textbf{Headline} = canonical \textsc{KGA} only (Algorithm~\ref{alg:kga}; dev-locked adapter
from a pre-registered panel, or fixed adapter for~G; GBR $\widehat\Delta$ + global
in-domain $\varepsilon$; held-out test once).
\textbf{Diagnostic} = earlier Camelyon/iWildCam runs (sparse $Z$, multicandidate $\tau^\star$,
Protocol~B/F route audits) that explain failed operating points---Appendix~\ref{app:supp-exp},
\S\ref{sec:camelyon-diag-short}---and motivated G/H/M v2. Diagnostics are not competing wins.
No held-out test labels are used to select the adapter, tune $\varepsilon$, choose evidence
features, or revise the decision rule.

\paragraph{Statistical robustness (bootstrap CIs).}
Resampling the held-out test conditions ($B{=}3000$) with the dev calibration held
\emph{fixed}---so decisions are never retuned on test, and every point estimate reproduces
the locked protocol to four decimals---the beats-both is statistically robust on two of the
three natural shifts. Office-Home: \textsc{KGA} vs always-freeze $+0.014$ ($95\%$ CI
$[0.009,0.018]$) and vs always-adapt $+0.045$ ($[0.020,0.073]$). Camelyon17: vs freeze
$+0.075$ ($[0.057,0.095]$) and vs adapt $+0.0013$ ($[0.0006,0.0020]$). Both gaps' CIs exclude
zero. On iWildCam the margin over always-freeze ($+0.0004$, $[0.000,0.0013]$) is within noise,
so we report it as \emph{dominate-adapt / tie-freeze}: \textsc{KGA} matches or remains close to
the better trivial policy and strictly beats the worse one (vs adapt $+0.099$,
$[0.080,0.119]$). Per-condition intervals: \texttt{research\_lock/KBOUND\_WIN\_BOOTSTRAP\_CIS.json}.

\paragraph{Mixed-deployment capstone.}
Each single dataset is one-sided, so \textsc{KGA}'s simultaneous margin over \emph{both}
policies is necessarily small there. Pooling the three held-out shifts' test conditions into a
single heterogeneous deployment ($n{=}161$; each dataset keeps its own locked dev-calibrated
gate, reproducing its single-dataset regret to four decimals) exposes the two-sided regime the
certificate is built for: \textsc{KGA} regret $0.0021$ versus always-adapt $0.057$
($\sim\!27\times$) and always-freeze $0.030$ ($\sim\!14\times$), with both $95\%$ bootstrap CIs
excluding zero (vs freeze $[0.020,0.037]$, vs adapt $[0.042,0.067]$) at false-adapt
$0.016\le\alpha$. When adaptation helps on some conditions and hurts on others, no fixed policy
matches the certificate's per-condition routing
(\texttt{research\_lock/KBOUND\_MIXED\_STREAM\_v1.json}).

These tracks support Table~\ref{tab:regime-summary} but are not headline policy wins

\textbf{CIFAR-10-C per-corruption} ($65$ cells) is helpful-dominated; \textsc{KGA} ties
always-adapt.
\textbf{RxRx1} is harmful-dominated at $1{,}139$-class scale; always-adapt is destructive

\textbf{CIFAR-10.1} is low-margin: within-seed calibration ties freeze with $0\%$

headline bar.

\section{Limitations and honest scope}\label{sec:limits}

\medskip
\textbf{Scope of empirical wins.} On the stress grid \textsc{KGA} beats both for Tent/EATA
and ties SAR (Table~\ref{tab:decisive}); on ImageNet-C the pattern flips by candidate---Tent/EATA
are robust while SAR collapses and \textsc{KGA} beats both under a mechanism-faithful re-run
(Table~\ref{tab:imagenetc-faithful}). Per-corruption CIFAR-10-C and helpful natural shifts are
insurance regimes, not policy wins.

\textbf{Real-shift scope.} Headline natural-shift wins are Protocols~G, H v2, and~M v2
(canonical dev-lock \textsc{KGA}, Tables~\ref{tab:camelyon17-G}, \ref{tab:iwildcam-H},
\ref{tab:officehome-M}). Earlier Camelyon Protocol~B/F runs and multicandidate routes are
\textbf{diagnostics} that motivated these operating points (Appendix); they are not alternate headline
wins.

\textbf{Theory and calibration.} The conformal radius assumes exchangeable calibration pairs;
sign bracketing without structure is impossible (Theorem~\ref{thm:headline} and
Appendix~\ref{app:theory-full}). \textsc{KGA}'s value scales with how often catastrophic,
detectable harm occurs---not with universal accuracy gains.

\textbf{Detectability and transfer.} ImageNet-R still sits in a weak, split-specific
evidence regime (Appendix~\ref{sec:excluded-wins}). iWildCam H v2 clears the certificate bar
despite modest margin over always-freeze; intermediate-domain multicandidate routes do not
(pooled false-adapt $\gg\alpha$).

\textbf{Missing baselines and independent validation.} ImageNet-C committal gates
(ATC-style confidence; Appendix~\ref{app:frontier}, Table~\ref{tab:app-imagenetc}) false-adapt at
$100\%$ on harmful SAR cells, while \textsc{KGA} abstains there. Direct POEM-style sequential
monitors and additional simple gates on the CIFAR-10-C stress grid remain future checks.
Independent replication of ImageNet-R and Office-Home M v2 across more seeds would strengthen
stability claims. Abstention reduces coverage by design.

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
\textbf{What we do not claim.} Universal accuracy gains; adapter selection by test regret;
multicandidate routes with violated false-adapt bounds; or wins on helpful-dominated shifts
where always-adapt is already oracle-optimal.}}
\end{center}

\section{Conclusion}
K-Bound reframes test-time adaptation as an identifiability-limited decision problem: adapt
when helpful, freeze when harmful, and abstain when label-free evidence cannot support a valid
decision. The theory shows that this abstention region is not merely conservative; under matched
label-free evidence with opposite adaptation benefit, no valid label-free rule can safely commit.
The empirical results support the same regime map: \textsc{KGA} ties or trails only where
always-adapt is already near-oracle, but improves over trivial policies when harmful adaptation
is frequent, detectable, and costly. These results suggest that future TTA systems should expose
not only an adaptation objective, but also a validity layer that decides whether adaptation is
knowable before deployment.
