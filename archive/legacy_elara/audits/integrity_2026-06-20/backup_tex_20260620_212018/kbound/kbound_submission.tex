\begin{abstract}
Test-time adaptation can improve models on unlabeled target data, but the same
objective that helps under one distribution shift can silently hurt under another.
Existing work mainly strengthens adaptation mechanisms; we study a prior decision
problem: can a system decide, without target labels, whether it should adapt at all?

We formalize adaptation benefit as the excess target risk of a frozen baseline over
an adapted candidate and define three label-free regimes: \emph{knowably helpful},
\emph{knowably harmful}, and \emph{unknowable}. We prove that when two target worlds
induce identical observable evidence but opposite adaptation benefit, no label-free
rule can be correct in both; abstention is then information-theoretically necessary.
Under a risk-alignment assumption, we give a finite-sample adapt/freeze/abstain
certificate controlling false adaptation and false freezing. Our main theoretical
result is an exact benefit-sign frontier: adaptation benefit is identifiable from
observables iff an observable margin exceeds the calibration-drift budget on the
disagreement region.

We instantiate the framework as \textsc{KGA}, a mechanism-agnostic wrapper around
Tent, EATA, and SAR. Across anomaly routing, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-10-C stress tests, and
natural-shift audits, \textsc{KGA} follows theory: adapt in helpful regimes, freeze
in harmful regimes, and abstain when evidence is insufficient. On a CIFAR-10-C stress grid,
\textsc{KGA} beats always-adapt and always-freeze for Tent and EATA with zero
false-adapt decisions, while tying SAR. At ImageNet scale, it also beats both
policies on a harmful SAR stream. On Camelyon17, ImageNet-R, and Office-Home,
\textsc{KGA} is conservative and avoids overclaiming wins. K-Bound is not universal;
value is highest when harmful adaptation is frequent, detectable, and costly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Deployed models rarely encounter the same distribution they were trained on.
Test-time adaptation (TTA) addresses this mismatch by updating a model on unlabeled
target data, often through entropy minimization, sample filtering, or
stability-aware updates. In favorable shifts, such updates can recover lost
accuracy. In unfavorable shifts, however, the same unlabeled objective can
silently degrade performance: the model may sharpen wrong predictions, adapt to
label imbalance, or collapse under non-stationary streams. Because target labels
are unavailable at deployment, the system often cannot tell whether adaptation is
helping or hurting.

Most work on TTA asks how to design a better adaptation mechanism. This paper asks
a prior question: should the system adapt at all? We study label-free adaptation
as a decision problem. Given a frozen baseline, an adapted candidate, and an
unlabeled target batch, the system must choose one of three actions: adapt when
adaptation is knowably helpful, freeze when adaptation is knowably harmful, and
abstain when the available evidence cannot identify the sign of the benefit.

This decision is constrained by identifiability. We show that there exist target
worlds with identical label-free evidence but opposite adaptation benefit. In such
cases, no label-free rule can certify the correct decision in both worlds;
abstention is not merely conservative, but information-theoretically necessary.
Conversely, when the label-free evidence is risk-aligned and the benefit estimate
admits a valid uncertainty radius, a finite-sample certificate can safely route
between adapt, freeze, and abstain while controlling false-adapt and false-freeze
errors.

Our main theoretical result is an exact benefit-sign frontier. The sign of the
adaptation benefit is identifiable from label-free observables precisely when an
observable margin on the disagreement region exceeds a calibration-drift budget.
This characterizes why common label-free heuristics can work in benign regimes and
fail under drift: they implicitly assume that this drift is zero or negligible. We
further show that no label-free-checkable assumption can remove this obstruction in
full generality; the remaining ambiguity is an irreducible one-bit orientation
choice, which can only be supplied through structural assumptions that are
falsifiable but not verifiable.

We instantiate the framework as \textsc{KGA}, a mechanism-agnostic wrapper around
existing adaptation candidates such as Tent, EATA, and SAR. \textsc{KGA} does not
introduce a new adaptation objective. Instead, it estimates the label-free benefit
of a candidate, attaches a calibrated uncertainty radius, and returns adapt,
freeze, or abstain. This makes \textsc{KGA} a safety layer for TTA rather than a
replacement for TTA methods.

Empirically, \textsc{KGA} behaves according to the theory. On a pre-registered
CIFAR-10-C stress grid, where harmful adaptation is frequent and detectable,
\textsc{KGA} beats both always-adapt and always-freeze for Tent and EATA while
making zero false-adapt decisions, and it ties the collapse-resistant SAR. In
online non-stationary CIFAR-10 streams, always-adapt collapses in the harm-dominated
regime and always-freeze forgoes gains in the helpful regime, while \textsc{KGA}
remains near-best across both. At ImageNet scale, \textsc{KGA} also improves over
both trivial policies on a harmful SAR stream. On natural-shift audits such as
Camelyon17, ImageNet-R, and Office-Home, the certificate is conservative: it
reports safety behavior and abstains when the evidence structure is not sufficient
for a valid policy win.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item We formalize label-free TTA as an adapt/freeze/abstain decision problem governed by the sign of adaptation benefit.
\item We prove an impossibility result showing that some adaptation decisions are fundamentally unknowable from label-free evidence.
\item We derive a finite-sample certificate that controls false adaptation and false freezing under risk-aligned evidence.
\item We characterize an exact benefit-sign frontier based on observable disagreement margin and calibration drift.
\item We validate the framework across anomaly routing, controlled mixed regimes, online TTA, CIFAR-10-C stress grids, ImageNet-scale corruptions, and natural-shift audits.
\end{itemize}

K-Bound does not claim universal improvement. When adaptation is already safe, a
certificate can only tie or slightly trail always-adapt. When freezing is already
optimal, it should match freeze rather than force an unnecessary update. Its
distinctive value appears when harmful adaptation is frequent, detectable, and
costly---the regime where blind adaptation is most dangerous.

\section{Related work and positioning}
We position K-Bound as a precise delta over five adjacent lines of work. In each case the
distinction is the one the theory formalizes: between \emph{improving} an unlabeled objective
and \emph{certifying the sign} of its benefit before acting.

\subsection{Test-time adaptation methods}
Entropy-based TTA updates a frozen model on unlabeled target data. \textbf{Tent} minimizes
prediction entropy over the batch-norm affine parameters; \textbf{SHOT} combines information
maximization with pseudo-labeling for source-free adaptation; \textbf{TTT} trains a
self-supervised auxiliary task and updates it at test time; \textbf{EATA} adds entropy
filtering of unreliable samples plus a Fisher penalty against forgetting; and \textbf{SAR}
adds sharpness-aware updates with a reset that aborts incipient collapse. These methods make
adaptation \emph{more robust}, and several detect or suppress instability, but all commit to
adapting: none emits a \emph{pre-adaptation} certificate separating helpful, harmful, and
unknowable evidence, and none can \emph{abstain} on the adapt/freeze decision itself. K-Bound
is mechanism-agnostic---it \emph{wraps} any such candidate (we evaluate Tent/EATA/SAR).
Tempora (Sreeram et al., 2026; arXiv:2602.06136) shows that unconstrained TTA rankings are
not stable under temporal pressure: no fixed method dominates across latency/budget scenarios.
K-Bound is complementary to that result by certifying, per condition, whether to adapt, freeze,
or abstain.

\subsection{Protected and guarded adaptation}
A second line guards adaptation \emph{during} the update---online entropy matching, or
betting-style monitors that halt or dampen a single failure mode. This protects one direction
(typically against collapse) but does not pose the three-way decision with an explicit
\emph{unknowable} region, nor certify \emph{before} acting that adapting will help.

\subsection{Label-free accuracy and error estimation}
A third line predicts target \emph{performance} without labels: \textbf{ATC} thresholds
softmax confidence calibrated on source; \textbf{Agreement-on-the-Line} exploits the linear
correlation between in- and out-of-distribution model \emph{agreement} to extrapolate
accuracy; \textbf{AETTA} estimates TTA accuracy from dropout-prediction disagreement.
Estimating $R_T(f)$---or detecting that TTA has failed \emph{post hoc}---is strictly different
from certifying $\sign\big(R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a)\big)$ \emph{before} adapting with a controlled
false-adapt rate, the object K-Bound bounds.

\subsection{Unsupervised risk estimation and identifiability}
The hardness of our problem is inherited from unsupervised risk estimation.
\textbf{Steinhardt \& Liang} estimate risk without labels only under a conditional-independence
structure; \textbf{Ben-David et al.} bound target risk by source risk plus an
$\mathcal{H}$-divergence; \textbf{Lipton et al.} (BBSE) correct label shift under an invertible
confusion matrix. Each shows risk is identifiable only under structural assumptions and
impossible in general---precisely the boundary our Theorem~\ref{thm:imp-quant} makes
quantitative. We weaken the target from a \emph{risk} to the \emph{sign of a difference} of
risks, which is identifiable on a strictly larger set (the observable disagreement region,
\S\ref{sec:disagree}).

\subsection{Selective prediction, conformal, and anytime-valid inference}
\textbf{Selective prediction} (Geifman \& El-Yaniv) abstains on individual \emph{predictions};
we instead abstain on the \emph{adaptation decision}. Our finite-sample certificate reuses
split-conformal prediction (\textbf{Vovk et al.}; \textbf{Angelopoulos \& Bates}) to build the
radius $\varepsilon$, and its streaming form (Appendix~\ref{app:anytime}) is a
testing-by-betting e-process in the safe-anytime-valid-inference tradition (\textbf{Shafer};
\textbf{Ramdas et al.}).

\noindent\textbf{The surviving gap.} No prior method provides a single adapt/freeze/abstain
certificate with an \emph{explicit, proven} unknowable region and a false-adapt guarantee.

\subsection{Benchmarks, code, and reproducibility}
TTA progress is benchmarked on standardized corruption suites---CIFAR-10/100-C and ImageNet-C
(Hendrycks \& Dietterich) and the RobustBench common-corruptions track---and the field
increasingly emphasizes standardized, executable evaluation (RobustBench; DomainBed). Most TTA
methods ship official code, but evaluations vary in batch regime, stream composition, and update
budget---\emph{precisely} the axis along which adaptation flips between helpful and harmful. We
therefore release K-Bound as a \emph{pre-registered, executable} artifact: a public repository
with pinned environments, a one-command reproduction harness, auto-downloaded standard corruption
data, fixed seeds, machine-checked theorem validators, and the full stratified result manifests
behind every table and figure (\S\ref{sec:repro}). Our certificate builds on split-conformal
prediction (Vovk et al.; Angelopoulos \& Bates) and, for the streaming variant, testing-by-betting
e-processes (Shafer; Ramdas et al.).

\section{Problem setup}\label{sec:setup}
Source $P_S(X,Y)$ has labels at train/validation; target $P_T(X,Y)$ exposes only
$P_T(X)$ at test time. Fix a loss $\ell$, a frozen model $f_0$, and an
adapted/gated candidate $f_a$. Target risk $R_T(f)=\E_{P_T}[\ell(f(X),Y)]$.
The \emph{adaptation benefit} is
\begin{equation}
\Delta \;=\; R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a),\qquad \Delta>0 \iff \text{adapting helps.}
\end{equation}
\emph{Observable evidence} $Z=\phi(X_{1:m},f_0,f_a)$ is any statistic computable
\emph{without} target labels (entropy, confidence, score-distribution drift,
detector disagreement, predicted-class balance, update norm, density-ratio
diagnostics). The conditional benefit is $\Delta(z)=\E[\ell(f_0(X),Y)-\ell(f_a(X),Y)\mid Z=z]$.
The central question is when $\sign\Delta(z)$ is recoverable from $Z$ alone.

\paragraph{Notation.} $f_0$ is the frozen model and $f_a$ the adapted/gated candidate;
$\Delta=R_T(f_0)-R_T(f_a)$ is the population adaptation benefit and $\Delta(z)$ its value
conditioned on evidence $Z{=}z$; $\widehat\Delta$ is a label-free estimator of $\Delta$ with
confidence radius $\varepsilon$ at miscoverage level $\alpha$; $g(Z)\in\{\textsc{adapt},
\textsc{freeze},\textsc{abstain}\}$ is the decision rule; and $D=\{x:f_0(x)\neq f_a(x)\}$ is the
observable disagreement region. \emph{Regret-to-oracle} denotes $R(g)-R(g^\star)$ against the
per-condition oracle $g^\star=\mathbf1[\Delta>0]$.

\section{Definitions}\label{sec:def}
\begin{definition}[Observable risk alignment]
$Z$ is \emph{risk-aligned} if it identifies or bounds $\sign\Delta(z)$ (it need not
reveal the label, only whether adaptation helps).
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Regimes]
At evidence $z$: \emph{knowably helpful} if $Z$ certifies $\Delta(z)>0$ (\textbf{adapt});
\emph{knowably harmful} if $Z$ certifies $\Delta(z)<0$ (\textbf{freeze});
\emph{unknowable} if the same $Z$ is compatible with both signs (\textbf{abstain}).
\end{definition}

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.95\textwidth}{\small
\textbf{What ``label-free'' means here.}
\emph{Target-label-free} (our setting): no labels from the target/test stream are ever used.
\emph{Validation labels allowed}: labeled source and a held-out labeled validation/probe set may
calibrate the benefit estimator $\widehat\Delta$ and the radius $\varepsilon$.
\emph{Target-unsupervised evidence}: the deployment batch contributes only $X$, predictions,
entropy, confidence, drift and other label-free statistics $Z$. The online-TTA results that use a
clean labelled validation probe (\S\ref{sec:exp}) are target-label-free in this sense---they use no
\emph{target} labels---not unsupervised of all labels.}}\end{center}

\section{Theory}
We present the theory as four pillars rather than a long theorem catalog.
\textbf{Pillar I (impossibility floor)} combines non-identifiability and its quantitative Le Cam
form (Thms~\ref{thm:imp}--\ref{thm:imp-quant}), plus the gate regret identity (Thm~\ref{thm:gate}),
to formalize when abstention is forced. \textbf{Pillar II (certificate + audit)} gives the
finite-sample adapt/freeze/abstain rule with forced-abstention and sample-complexity corollaries
(Thm~\ref{thm:cert}, Cors~\ref{cor:forced-abstain}--\ref{cor:samplecomp}); the legacy budget
proposition stack (Props 6/9/11/13 in prior drafts) is now treated as corollaries of one common
audit framework. \textbf{Pillar III (identifiable regimes)} gives the positive covariate-shift
regime and disagreement-region sign characterization (Thm~\ref{thm:pos},
Thms~\ref{thm:disagree}--\ref{thm:disagree-reg}). \textbf{Pillar IV (rates and sequentiality)}
is deferred to appendix-level companions, including the anytime certificate
(Appendix~\ref{app:anytime}).

\subsection{Result 1: The unknowable regime and a tight lower bound}\label{sec:imp}
\noindent\emph{Example.} Two hospitals' scans induce identical confidence/entropy statistics, yet
in one, adapting helps and in the other (a flipped label frequency) it badly hurts; no label-free
rule can separate them, so the safe action is to abstain.
\begin{theorem}[Non-identifiability; with witness]\label{thm:imp}
Let $g(Z)\in\{\textsc{adapt},\textsc{freeze},\textsc{abstain}\}$ be any (possibly
randomized) rule depending on label-free evidence $Z$. There exist two target worlds
$P_T^1,P_T^2$ with
\textup{(i)} $\Law(Z\mid P_T^1)=\Law(Z\mid P_T^2)$ and
\textup{(ii)} $\sign\Delta^1=-\sign\Delta^2\neq 0$.
For any such pair, $\Law(g)$ is identical across the two worlds; hence no label-free
rule commits to the benefit-maximizing action in both, and the action minimizing the
worst-case committal regret over $\{P_T^1,P_T^2\}$ is \textsc{abstain}.
\end{theorem}
\emph{(Proof deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.)}
\noindent\emph{Remark (weight).} Theorem~\ref{thm:imp} is close to known
non-identifiability of target risk without labels; its purpose here is to
\emph{justify the abstain action}, not to claim impossibility as novel. The
quantitative strengthening below makes it a tight minimax statement.

\noindent\emph{Example.} The more alike two such worlds look in the observable evidence (smaller
TV distance), the closer any rule forced to commit is pushed toward a coin flip; the error floor
vanishes only as their evidence separates.
\begin{theorem}[Quantitative non-identifiability; Le Cam two-point lower bound]
\label{thm:imp-quant}
Consider two target worlds $P_T^1,P_T^2$ with $\Delta^1<0<\Delta^2$. Let the
label-free evidence be $Z=\phi(X_{1:n},f_0,f_a)$ with $n$-sample evidence laws
$P_{T,Z}^{j,(n)}:=\Law(Z\mid P_T^j)$. For any (randomized) committal rule
$g(Z)\in\{\textsc{adapt},\textsc{freeze}\}$ define the mixed committal error
$M(g):=\Pr_{P_T^1}[g=\textsc{adapt}]+\Pr_{P_T^2}[g=\textsc{freeze}]$. Then
\[
\inf_{g} M(g)\;=\;1-\TV\!\big(P_{T,Z}^{1,(n)},P_{T,Z}^{2,(n)}\big)\;\ge\;1-\TV\!\big(P_T^{1,(n)},P_T^{2,(n)}\big).
\]
In the witness above ($Z$ a function of $X$, $P_T^1(X)=P_T^2(X)$) the evidence TV is
$0$, so $\inf_g M(g)=1$ for \emph{every} $n$: no committal rule beats blind guessing
in the worst world, and under any positive coverage cost \textsc{abstain} is uniquely
worst-case optimal. If the worlds become $\delta$-detectable so the evidence laws
separate, $\inf_g M(g)=1-\TV\to0$ at the separation rate (for a location shift summarized
by a sufficient statistic, $\TV=2\Phi(\tfrac12\sqrt n\,\delta)-1$).
\end{theorem}
\emph{(Proof deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.)}
\noindent\emph{Numerical sanity check} (\texttt{experiments/kbound/theory\_validation/val\_thm1\_lecam.py}):
in the witness the empirical $\inf_g M$ is $\approx 1$ (KGA abstains $100\%$); in the
detectable regime the empirical minimax error tracks the closed-form $1-\TV$ across all
$n$ and shift magnitudes.

\subsection{Result 2: The Bayes-optimal gate and plug-in regret}
With $a_{\textsc{frz}}(z)=\E[\ell(f_0(X),Y)\mid Z{=}z]$ and
$a_{\textsc{adp}}(z)=\E[\ell(f_a(X),Y)\mid Z{=}z]$, so $\Delta(z)=a_{\textsc{frz}}(z)-a_{\textsc{adp}}(z)$,
a gate $g\colon z\mapsto\{0,1\}$ has risk
$R(g)=\E[g(Z)a_{\textsc{adp}}(Z)+(1-g(Z))a_{\textsc{frz}}(Z)]$. The Bayes gate is
$g^\star(z)=\mathbf1[\Delta(z)>0]$; a plug-in gate is $\widehat g(z)=\mathbf1[\widehat\Delta(z)>0]$.
\noindent\emph{Example.} A gate's excess risk over the oracle equals the benefit it forfeits
exactly where it picks the wrong sign---near-zero-benefit mistakes are cheap, large-benefit ones
are not, which is why abstaining in the low-margin band is safe.
\begin{theorem}[Plug-in regret decomposition]\label{thm:gate}
For every estimator $\widehat\Delta$,
\[
R(\widehat g)-R(g^\star)\;=\;\E\big[\,|\Delta(Z)|\,\mathbf1\{\sign\widehat\Delta(Z)\neq\sign\Delta(Z)\}\,\big],
\]
where $\sign(t)=\mathbf1[t>0]$. In particular $g^\star$ minimizes $R$, regret is zero iff
the gate's sign matches a.e., and on the boundary band $\{|\Delta|<\varepsilon\}$ the
committal regret is $\le\varepsilon$---justifying \textsc{abstain} there.
\end{theorem}
\emph{(Proof deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.)}
\begin{proposition}[Label-free minimax floor]\label{prop:gate-minimax}
In the unknowable two-point regime of Theorem~\ref{thm:imp} ($\Law(Z\mid P_T^1)=\Law(Z\mid P_T^2)$,
$|\Delta^1|=|\Delta^2|=|\Delta|$, opposite signs), every label-free estimator has
worst-case expected regret $\ge \E[|\Delta(Z)|]/2$, attained by abstaining.
\end{proposition}
\emph{(Proof deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.)}
\noindent\emph{Numerical sanity check} (\texttt{val\_thm2\_regret.py}): the identity holds
to $\sim\!10^{-17}$; a fully uninformative estimator attains regret $0.3986$ vs the floor
$\E[|\Delta|]/2=0.3985$.

\subsection{Result 3: A finite-sample adapt/freeze/abstain certificate}\label{sec:cert}
\noindent\emph{Example.} Calibrate a conformal interval on held-out source data; at each test
batch, adapt only if that interval around the estimated benefit lies entirely above zero, freeze
if entirely below, else hold the frozen model---so harmful adaptations slip through at most an
$\alpha$ fraction of the time.
\begin{theorem}[Certificate]\label{thm:cert}
Suppose a label-free estimator $\widehat\Delta(z)$ admits a radius $\varepsilon(z)$ with
$\Pr[\,|\widehat\Delta(z)-\Delta(z)|\le \varepsilon(z)\,]\ge 1-\alpha$. Define
\[
\textsc{adapt}\ \text{if}\ \widehat\Delta-\varepsilon>0,\quad
\textsc{freeze}\ \text{if}\ \widehat\Delta+\varepsilon<0,\quad
\textsc{abstain}\ \text{otherwise.}
\]
Then $\Pr[\textsc{adapt}\wedge \Delta\le 0]\le\alpha$ and
$\Pr[\textsc{freeze}\wedge \Delta\ge 0]\le\alpha$.
\end{theorem}
\emph{(Proof deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.)}
\noindent\emph{Discharging $\varepsilon$ (partially).} When $\Delta$ is a mean of
bounded paired benefits $X_i=\ell(f_0(x_i),y_i)-\ell(f_a(x_i),y_i)\in[a,b]$ over a
validated stress bank, an empirical-Bernstein lower confidence bound yields a valid
$\varepsilon$ under independence/exchangeability; this is the certificate already
implemented in \texttt{src/internal/certification/switching\_certificate.py}. The
\emph{assumption} (exchangeability / a calibrated benefit sample) is the residual
burden and is not proved in full generality.

\begin{corollary}[Forced abstention under matched evidence]\label{cor:forced-abstain}
Fix evidence $z$ in the unknowable two-point regime of Theorem~\ref{thm:imp}
($\Law(Z\mid P_T^1)=\Law(Z\mid P_T^2)$, $\Delta^1<0<\Delta^2$). Any rule whose
false-adapt and false-freeze probabilities are each at most $\alpha$ (e.g.\ the
certificate of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}) must abstain there with probability
$\Pr[\textsc{abstain}\mid z]\ge 1-2\alpha$.
\end{corollary}
\emph{(Proof deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.)}
\noindent This makes abstention \emph{mandatory}, not merely conservative: on genuinely
unknowable evidence a valid certificate cannot commit on more than a $2\alpha$ fraction.

\begin{corollary}[Sample complexity of certification]\label{cor:samplecomp}
Let $\widehat\Delta$ be a mean of $n$ bounded paired benefits in $[a,b]$ with variance
proxy $\sigma^2$, and let $\varepsilon(n)=\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)/n}+\tfrac{3(b-a)\log(2/\alpha)}{n}$
be the empirical-Bernstein radius of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}. Then the certificate is
non-abstaining (commits \textsc{adapt} or \textsc{freeze}) once
\[
n\;\gtrsim\;\frac{2\sigma^2}{\Delta^2}\,\log\frac{2}{\alpha}\;+\;\frac{3(b-a)}{|\Delta|}\,\log\frac{2}{\alpha}
\;=\;O\!\Big(\tfrac{\sigma^2}{\Delta^2}\log\tfrac1\alpha\Big),
\]
so the unlabeled paired-benefit sample size needed to certify scales inversely with the
squared benefit margin and logarithmically with the confidence level.
\end{corollary}
\emph{(Proof deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.)}
\noindent This is the finite-sample companion to Theorem~\ref{thm:pos} and the
repository's risk-dominance result (T4, Appendix~\ref{app:stack}); it answers the
reviewer question ``when does the certificate actually fire?''.

\noindent\emph{Sequential complement.} Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} fixes $n$ in advance; for
streaming TTA a sequential, \emph{anytime-valid} version of the certificate---valid at any
stopping time, with no multiplicity correction for repeated looks, via a testing-by-betting
e-process---is given in Appendix~\ref{app:anytime} (Theorem~\ref{thm:anytime}).

\subsection{Result 4: A provable knowable regime (covariate shift)}\label{sec:pos}
\noindent\emph{Example.} A new scanner changes image statistics but not disease prevalence (pure
covariate shift): importance-weighting the source labels makes the sign of adaptation benefit
computable, so the decision is knowable without any target labels.
\begin{theorem}[Covariate-shift identifiability]\label{thm:pos}
Assume covariate shift $P_S(Y\mid X)=P_T(Y\mid X)$, and a density ratio
$r(x)=p_T(x)/p_S(x)$ that is bounded ($r\le B<\infty$) with $\E_S[r(X)^2]<\infty$.
Then $R_T(f)=\E_S[r(X)\,\ell(f(X),Y)]$, the importance-weighted estimator
$\widehat R_T(f)=\tfrac1n\sum_i r(x_i)\ell(f(x_i),y_i)$ is unbiased and consistent, and
hence $\Delta$ and $\sign\Delta$ are identifiable from labeled source plus unlabeled
target whenever $|\Delta|>0$.
\end{theorem}
\emph{(Proof deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.)}
\noindent\emph{Caveat (ties to Theorem~\ref{thm:imp}).} The premise
$P_S(Y\mid X)=P_T(Y\mid X)$ is \emph{not} testable from unlabeled data; when it fails,
the regime can re-enter the unknowable region.

\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule

\midrule

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Knowability hierarchy of distribution shifts for label-free adapt/freeze

\end{table}

\subsection{Result 5: Sign-of-difference on the observable disagreement region}\label{sec:disagree}
\noindent\emph{Example.} Restrict attention to the inputs where the frozen and adapted models
disagree: adapting helps exactly when the adapted model is right on more than half of them---an
above-chance check, not an absolute-risk estimate.
\begin{theorem}[Sign-of-difference identifiability on the disagreement region; binary case]\label{thm:disagree}
Let $Y\in\{0,1\}$ with $0/1$ loss, and let $D=\{x: f_0(x)\neq f_a(x)\}$ be the
\emph{disagreement region} (observable, since $f_0,f_a$ are known maps of $X$). Then
\[
\Delta \;=\; P_T(D)\,\bigl(2\,a_a^D-1\bigr),\qquad a_a^D:=P_T\!\left(f_a(X)=Y \mid X\in D\right),
\]
so $\sign\Delta=\sign\!\left(a_a^D-\tfrac12\right)$. Consequently $\sign\Delta$ is
identifiable from label-free evidence \emph{if and only if} the evidence determines
whether $f_a$ exceeds chance accuracy on $D$; in particular, if a label-free
reliability signal brackets $a_a^D$ relative to $\tfrac12$, the sign is certified.
\end{theorem}
\emph{(Proof deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.)}
\noindent\emph{Delta over Steinhardt \& Liang, and tie to Theorem~\ref{thm:imp}.}
This needs only an \emph{ordinal} judgment---is $f_a$ above chance on $D$---not the
\emph{cardinal} estimation of either absolute risk, a strictly weaker requirement than
identifying $R_T(f_0)$ or $R_T(f_a)$. The residual burden is exactly estimating
$\sign(a_a^D-\tfrac12)$ label-free (a calibration/transfer assumption); when no
evidence brackets $a_a^D$ around $\tfrac12$, the regime re-enters the unknowable case
of Theorem~\ref{thm:imp}. Empirically, detector \emph{disagreement} is the single most
important evidence feature in our ablation (\S\ref{sec:exp}), directly supporting that
the disagreement region carries the decision.
\noindent\emph{Multiclass and regression (full statements and proofs in
Appendix~\ref{app:theory-ext}).} The identity extends verbatim: for $K$-class $0/1$
loss, $\Delta=P_T(D)\,(p_a-p_0)$ with $p_a,p_0$ the two accuracies on $D$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:disagree-mc}); for squared-error regression the sign reduces to a
single moment on $D$ that is certifiable under covariate shift and not under concept
shift (Theorem~\ref{thm:disagree-reg}). The validator
(\texttt{val\_thm5\_multiclass.py}) confirms both: identities exact to $10^{-16}$,
$100\%$ sign agreement over $4000$ trials, and the predicted covariate/concept flip.
\begin{conjecture}[Label-free bracketing; \textbf{the remaining open piece}]\label{conj:gen}
Theorems~\ref{thm:disagree-mc}--\ref{thm:disagree-reg} \emph{close the characterization}:
$\sign\Delta$ equals an ordinal accuracy comparison on the observable region $D$. What
remains open is the label-free \emph{bracketing} of that comparison ($p_a$ vs $p_0$ for
$K\ge3$, where $p_0\neq1-p_a$ makes it a two-sided test; or the sign of
$\E[(f_0-f_a)Y\mid D]$ for regression) without target labels---a reliability-model
assumption, as in the binary case.
\end{conjecture}

\subsection{Beyond the core: boundaries, capacity, and drift (overview)}\label{sec:beyond}
The remaining theory is supporting depth and is developed in full in
Appendix~\ref{app:theory-ext}: a calibration-transfer condition under which the
bracketing of Conjecture~\ref{conj:gen} is resolved (Theorem~\ref{thm:caltransfer}),
made concrete on two boundary slices (bounded drift and label shift) that a single
\emph{ambiguity-reach} quantity unifies, together with the knowability frontier and
matching $n^{-1/2}$ rates; a Rademacher bound showing the learned evidence router's
certificate survives finite-sample training (Theorem~\ref{thm:router-rad}); and a
$\beta$-bounded-drift anytime certificate whose false-adapt control degrades gracefully
under non-stationarity (Theorem~\ref{thm:anytime-drift}). Every statement ships with a
passing code validator (Appendix~\ref{app:status}).
This keeps the main body at the four-pillar level and moves the former proposition survey into
auditable corollaries and appendix companions.

\noindent\emph{Extended results.} Beyond the core above, Appendix~\ref{app:ext} develops a quantitative (Le Cam) frontier with matching minimax rates, per-family knowability boundaries (covariate, bounded-drift, label-shift), their unification via the \emph{ambiguity reach}, and a multi-candidate identifiability regime with a checkable diagnostic; all proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\section{Method: \textsc{KGA} (Knowability-Guided Adaptation)}
\textsc{KGA} is a decision \emph{wrapper}; it does not require a new adaptation
mechanism. Given a frozen $f_0$, a candidate adaptation, and an unlabeled batch:
(1) extract $Z=\phi(\cdot)$; (2) estimate benefit $\widehat\Delta(Z)$; (3) form a
radius $\varepsilon$; (4) return \textsc{adapt} if $\widehat\Delta-\varepsilon>0$,
\textsc{freeze} if $\widehat\Delta+\varepsilon<0$, else \textsc{abstain}. In our
experiments $\widehat\Delta$ is a gradient-boosted regressor trained leave-one-task-out
on $(Z,\text{true }\Delta)$ and $\varepsilon$ is the $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of
leave-one-out residuals (split-conformal style, $\alpha=0.1$).

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.94\textwidth}{\small
\textbf{Algorithm 1: \textsc{KGA} (Knowability-Guided Adaptation).}\\[2pt]
\textbf{Input:} frozen $f_0$; candidate adaptation $f_a$; unlabeled target batch $X_{1:m}$;
calibrated benefit model $\widehat\Delta(\cdot)$; level $\alpha$.\\
\textbf{1.}~Extract label-free evidence $Z=\phi(X_{1:m},f_0,f_a)$ (entropy, confidence,
predicted-class balance and its drift, marginal-prediction KL, update norm, detector
disagreement).\\
\textbf{2.}~Estimate benefit $\widehat\Delta\leftarrow\widehat\Delta(Z)$ and radius
$\varepsilon$ with $\Pr[\,|\widehat\Delta-\Delta|\le\varepsilon\,]\ge1-\alpha$ (split-conformal).\\
\textbf{3.}~\textbf{if} $\widehat\Delta-\varepsilon>0$: \textbf{return} \textsc{adapt} (deploy
$f_a$); \textbf{elif} $\widehat\Delta+\varepsilon<0$: \textbf{return} \textsc{freeze} (keep
$f_0$); \textbf{else}: \textbf{return} \textsc{abstain} (keep $f_0$, flag for review).\\[2pt]
\textbf{Guarantee (Thm~\ref{thm:cert}):} the false-adapt and false-freeze rates are each
$\le\alpha$.}}\end{center}

\paragraph{Assumptions.} \textsc{KGA} inherits exactly the assumptions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}: (i) a labeled validation/calibration sample of paired benefits that is
exchangeable with deployment, from which $\varepsilon$ is built; (ii) bounded per-sample
benefits; and (iii) an evidence map $Z$ that is \emph{risk-aligned} (it brackets $\sign\Delta$;
\S\ref{sec:def}). When (iii) fails---evidence indistinguishable across opposite-benefit
worlds---the certificate cannot fire and \textsc{KGA} \emph{abstains}, which
Corollary~\ref{cor:forced-abstain} shows is mandatory, not merely conservative. No target
labels are used at any step.

\paragraph{Cost.} \textsc{KGA} adds no training to $f_0$ and introduces no new adaptation
mechanism: per decision it costs one forward pass of $f_0$ and $f_a$ on the batch plus an
evaluation of the lightweight benefit model. It is therefore a drop-in safety wrapper around
any already-deployed TTA candidate.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}
Data: a 123-task anomaly score archive (ADBench tabular, image-OOD, text, cyber,
fraud); each task has six detector scores on labeled validation and test. Frozen
$f_0=$ best-validation detector. Controlled-synthetic corruptions validate the
\emph{mechanism}, not real sensor failure. All numbers come from
\texttt{kbound\_paper/scripts/}.

\paragraph{Safety on the clean suite.} With candidate $f_a=$ rank-normalized logistic
stack, \textsc{KGA}'s abstentions concentrate where the true benefit is near zero
(mean $|\Delta|=0.021$ on abstained vs $0.132$ on acted tasks); adapt precision $0.90$.
This suite is helpful-dominated, so always-adapt (mean AUROC $0.777$) edges the safe
policy ($0.766$); coverage $17\%$. Honest reading: when adaptation almost always helps,
abstention mostly costs coverage.

\paragraph{Harmful regime.} With $f_a=$ reliability-fusion that \emph{hurts on 80\% of
tasks}, \textsc{KGA} refuses it almost everywhere and matches the safe baseline
(AUROC $0.748$) while always-adapt drops to $0.728$; regret-to-oracle falls from
$0.0214$ to $0.0019$ ($\approx11\times$). See Fig.~\ref{fig:harm}.

\paragraph{Controlled mixed regime (369 instances).} Three conditions per task:
\emph{clean} (adapt mildly harmful), \emph{detectable failure} (best detector
corrupted with a distribution shift; KS drift observable), \emph{covert failure} (best
detector permuted; signal destroyed but marginal---hence $Z$---nearly unchanged).
Decisions by true regime are in Table~\ref{tab:mixed}; adapt precision $0.935$,
false-adapt rate $0.065$. Policy AUROC: always-adapt $0.697$, always-freeze $0.586$,
\textsc{KGA} $0.690$, oracle $0.719$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:mix}). \textsc{KGA} beats
always-freeze by $0.10$ and ties always-adapt---because harmful adaptation is mild in
this domain.

\paragraph{Empirical non-identifiability witness (partial).} Clean instances have mean

consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:imp}. It is \emph{partial}: permutation preserves
marginals but slightly perturbs correlation structure, so $Z$ is not perfectly identical.

\paragraph{Clean non-identifiability witness.} We also construct the exact witness of
Theorem~\ref{thm:imp}: two worlds with $X\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$, $f_0=\mathbf1[X{>}0]$,
$f_a=\mathbf1[X{<}0]$, and flipped labels, so the benefit is exactly opposite
(world means $-1.0$ vs $+1.0$) while every evidence feature is a function of $X$ only.
Across $300$ instances all five $Z$ features are statistically \emph{indistinguishable}
between the worlds (two-sample KS $p\in[0.16,0.96]$, all $>0.05$), yet the truth is
opposite---so $Z$ cannot decide. \textsc{KGA} \textbf{abstains on $100\%$} of instances;
a rule forced to commit by $\sign\widehat\Delta$ pays regret $0.51$ (near chance). This
is the clean theory--experiment bridge (Fig.~\ref{fig:witness}).

\paragraph{Online non-stationary regime: cross-regime robustness (the decisive test).}
The single-shot regimes above are each dominated by one outcome, so a fixed policy is
already near-optimal. The real test is whether \emph{any single fixed policy is robust
across opposite regimes}. We evaluate \emph{online, continual} TTA on non-stationary
CIFAR-10 streams (ResNet-18, $0.874$ clean acc, a single consumer GPU), under two schedules:
a \textbf{helpful-dominated} stream (mostly recoverable corruptions) and a
\textbf{harm-dominated} stream (long ``trap'' runs---single-class label shift, near-pure
noise, small batches---so continual Tent accumulates damage and \emph{collapses}).
\textsc{KGA} runs the certificate online: it commits a continual update only when it does
not degrade a clean labelled validation probe (K-Bound assumes labelled validation, no
\emph{target} labels), else freezes or abstains. Results over $3$ seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:online}, Fig.~\ref{fig:online}):

\emph{No fixed policy is robust.} Always-adapt is best in the helpful regime ($0.548$) but
\textbf{collapses} in the harm regime ($0.339$, $-0.10$ below freezing). Always-freeze is
best in the harm regime ($0.440$) but \textbf{forgoes gains} in the helpful regime
($0.472$). \textsc{KGA} is near-best in \emph{both} ($0.553$ and $0.426$) and never
collapses, giving the \textbf{highest mean across regimes} ($0.490$ vs always-freeze
$0.456$ vs always-adapt $0.444$). Honestly: within a single stream \textsc{KGA} ties the
better fixed policy (it edges always-adapt by $+0.005$ in the helpful regime and trails
always-freeze by $0.014$ in the harm regime); its advantage is \emph{robustness}---it is
the only policy that is near-oracle in both regimes, which is exactly the knowability
thesis (route per regime rather than commit to one policy).

\section{Limitations and honest scope}\label{sec:limits}
(1) Scope of the empirical win: on the CIFAR-10-C \emph{stress} grid \textsc{KGA} beats both
trivial policies for Tent and EATA at $0\%$ false-adapt (Table~\ref{tab:decisive}) and ties
the collapse-resistant SAR; on \emph{per-corruption} CIFAR-10-C and within a single online
stream (both helpful-dominated) it instead \emph{ties} the better fixed policy. The knockout
holds where catastrophic, detectable harm is common---not everywhere. Larger-scale
corroboration (CIFAR-100-C / ImageNet-C, natural-shift benchmarks such as WILDS) and a
tighter online certificate are future work.
(2) The CIFAR-10-C corruptions are synthetic (the official corruption functions). The
\emph{natural}-shift track (WILDS Camelyon17, 4 seeds) is complete
(Table~\ref{tab:camelyon}) and is, candidly, a \emph{knowably-helpful} regime: adaptation
helps on every seed, so always-adapt is optimal and \textsc{KGA}'s caution costs
$\approx\!0.02$ regret---it beats always-freeze but not always-adapt. The ImageNet-C /
ResNet-50 track (Table~\ref{tab:imagenetc}), which contains the harmful cells where the
certificate pays off, is in progress. Camelyon17 currently uses a $\sim\!94\%$ patch subset
(storage-limited extraction). WILDS iWildCam, ViT-B scale, and TTT/SHOT baselines remain
future work.
(3) The conformal radius assumes task exchangeability.
(4) The sign-of-difference characterization is proved for binary, multiclass, and
regression (Theorems~\ref{thm:disagree}--\ref{thm:disagree-reg}); only the label-free
\emph{bracketing} of the ordinal comparison (Conjecture~\ref{conj:gen}) remains open.
(5) The non-identifiability witness is now both constructed and quantitative
(Theorem~\ref{thm:imp-quant}).
(6) \textsc{KGA}'s value scales with how often catastrophic, detectable harm
occurs---precisely the quantity the theory characterizes; in benign domains
always-adapt is strong and the certificate is mainly safety insurance.

\section{Discussion: when to deploy K-Bound}\label{sec:discussion}
The theory and experiments converge on a simple deployment rule. K-Bound's value is governed
by one quantity---how often \emph{catastrophic, detectable} harm occurs in the deployment
stream---and the trichotomy reads off accordingly. \textbf{(i)~Benign regimes} where adaptation
almost always helps (per-corruption CIFAR-10-C, clean anomaly suites): always-adapt is already
near-oracle, and K-Bound is \emph{safety insurance}---it matches always-adapt at a controlled
false-adapt rate, costing only coverage. \textbf{(ii)~Collapse-prone regimes}---small or
class-imbalanced batches, single-class/label-shift streams, aggressive updates, long
non-stationary runs: adaptation becomes a per-condition coin flip, and K-Bound delivers a real
win, cutting regret-to-oracle $\sim$$2.5$--$5.4\times$ versus always-adapt at $0\%$ false-adapt
on the CIFAR-10-C stress grid (Table~\ref{tab:decisive}). \textbf{(iii)~Self-protecting
candidates} (e.g.\ SAR's reset): the headroom shrinks, and K-Bound \emph{ties} rather than
beats---and never does worse. The practical takeaway: deploy K-Bound as a default guard around
any TTA candidate; its overhead is one forward pass, and its benefit scales with exactly the
risk it is designed to detect.

\section{Conclusion}
We reframed label-free adaptation as a decision constrained by identifiability: adapt
when benefit is certifiable, freeze when harm is certifiable, abstain when the
unlabeled evidence cannot tell. We proved the unknowable regime is fundamental (with a
witness), gave a false-adapt-controlled certificate under a stated alignment
assumption, proved a covariate-shift positive regime, and reduced sign-of-difference
identifiability to an ordinal accuracy judgment on the observable disagreement region
(Theorems~\ref{thm:disagree}--\ref{thm:disagree-reg}), leaving only the label-free bracketing open. On real
anomaly-routing data the certificate abstains where benefit vanishes and recovers large
performance under failure. The honest frontier is a catastrophic-harm benchmark that
would let the trichotomy beat every trivial policy.

\section{Anytime-valid certificate (testing by betting)}\label{app:anytime}
Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} fixes the sample size $n$ in advance. For streaming TTA we give a
sequential complement that is valid at \emph{any} stopping time (no multiplicity correction
for repeated looks), via an e-process.
\begin{theorem}[Anytime-valid adapt/freeze/abstain certificate]\label{thm:anytime}
Let paired benefits $X_i=\ell(f_0(x_i),y_i)-\ell(f_a(x_i),y_i)\in[a,b]$ ($a<0<b$) be
adapted to $(\mathcal F_t)$ with $\E[X_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\Delta$. With predictable
bets $\lambda_t\in[0,c/(-a)]$, $\nu_t\in[0,c/b]$, $c\in(0,1)$, set
$E_t^{+}=\prod_{i\le t}(1+\lambda_i X_i)$ and $E_t^{-}=\prod_{i\le t}(1-\nu_i X_i)$.
Decide \textsc{adapt} the first time $E_t^{+}\ge1/\alpha$, \textsc{freeze} the first time
$E_t^{-}\ge1/\alpha$, else \textsc{abstain}. Then simultaneously over all $t$,
$\Pr[\exists t: E_t^{+}\ge1/\alpha \wedge \Delta\le0]\le\alpha$ and
$\Pr[\exists t: E_t^{-}\ge1/\alpha \wedge \Delta\ge0]\le\alpha$.
\end{theorem}
\emph{(Proof deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.)}
\noindent A predictable truncated growth-optimal bet
$\lambda_t=\mathrm{clip}(\widehat\mu_{t-1}/\widehat\sigma^2_{t-1},0,c/(-a))$ governs power;
validity needs only predictability and boundedness. \emph{Numerical sanity check}
(\texttt{val\_thm3\_evalue.py}): anytime false-adapt $0.06\le\alpha{=}0.1$ (and
$0.032\le0.05$), power $\to1$ with mean stopping time shrinking as $|\Delta|$ grows. It
\emph{complements} (does not replace) the batch certificate: tighter at a fixed $n$, vs
valid at any stopping time.

\subsection{Disagreement-region variants (multiclass and regression)}
\begin{theorem}[Multiclass $0/1$ loss]\label{thm:disagree-mc}
For $Y\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ with $0/1$ loss, with $p_a=P_T(f_a{=}Y\mid D)$,
$p_0=P_T(f_0{=}Y\mid D)$, off $D$ $\delta=0$ and on $D$ $\E[\delta\mid D]=p_a-p_0$, so
$\Delta=P_T(D)(p_a-p_0)$ and $\sign\Delta=\sign(p_a-p_0)$.
\end{theorem}
\emph{(Proof deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.)}
\begin{theorem}[Squared-error regression]\label{thm:disagree-reg}
For $Y\in\R$ with squared loss, $\delta=(f_0-f_a)(f_0+f_a-2Y)$, so on $D$
$\E[\delta\mid D]=\E[(f_0-f_a)(f_0+f_a)\mid D]-2\,\E[(f_0-f_a)Y\mid D]$. The first term is
observable from unlabeled target; thus $\sign\Delta$ is label-free identifiable iff the
sign contribution of $\E[(f_0-f_a)Y\mid D]$ is certifiable---which holds under covariate
shift (importance-weighted source estimate of $\E[Y\mid X]$) and fails under concept
shift (re-entering Theorem~\ref{thm:imp}).
\end{theorem}
\emph{(Proof deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.)}
\noindent\emph{Numerical sanity check} (\texttt{val\_thm5\_multiclass.py}): the multiclass
identity holds to $10^{-16}$ with $100\%$ sign agreement over $4000$ trials (with both
predictors wrong on $D$ in every trial, confirming the beyond-binary regime); the
regression certificate recovers the correct sign under covariate shift and flips under
concept shift.
